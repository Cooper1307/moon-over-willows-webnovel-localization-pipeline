## Chapter 317: Demanding to Meet the Poetess

She found Prince Yi standing atop the wooden platform, gazing off toward the Divine Willow.

Matron Zou climbed the steps to join him. “Your Highness, I questioned Xilan. She was afraid you’d scold her, which is why she held back—Princess Consort Yi really did suffer a terrible fright yesterday.”

Shen Jianchen spun around anxiously. “What scared her?”

Matron Zou rolled out her rehearsed story smoothly. “After the ladies’ gathering, Her Highness strolled down the street for a bit and stumbled right into two men hacking at each other with knives—one even died on the spot. You wouldn’t remember, Your Highness, yet since childhood Her Highness has been fainthearted; she can’t stand blades and bloodshed, especially seeing someone killed.”

Shen Jianchen bought every word. Having no memory of Mengrong’s childhood, he never doubted Matron Zou for a second. “It’s my fault. If I’d gone to pick her up myself, none of this would have happened.”

“Don’t blame yourself, Your Highness—everything runs by fate. All I pray for is that you and Her Highness stay well from here on.” Tears welled in Matron Zou’s eyes again; she’d fooled the prince, yes, yet Her Highness had brought ruin on herself—if His Highness ever learned the truth, he might loathe her forever.

“Don’t cry, Matron. No matter what happens to Mengrong, I’ll never cast her aside.” Shen Jianchen looked back toward the Divine Willow as if bidding farewell to the woman he loved. “Once Mengrong recovers, I plan to return to Bianliang. I’m no longer Military Commissioner of Lin’an anyway—what’s the point hanging around here?”

Matron Zou nodded warmly. “You couldn’t make a wiser choice, Your Highness. Prince Yi’s Manor in Bianliang is your true home.”

Meanwhile, at the Chen family tea shop, while clerks bustled behind the counter, Lin Jue marched up with two military training officers and a platoon of soldiers to surround the building, then strode inside alone.

Chen Jiangmu happened to be at the shop. Seeing Commander Lin arrive with armed troops, he stepped forward in bewilderment. “What brings Commander Lin to our tea shop with so many men?”

Back when Zhu Xiyan held office, Lin Jue bent over backward flattering all three Chen brothers to curry favor with the prefect. Now that Zhu Xiyan was out, Lin Jue dropped all pretense and put on his sharpest official airs. “Someone reported to the yamen that the Chen family is peddling counterfeit tea. Newly appointed Prefect Li sent me to seal and inspect the shop.”

It took Chen Jiangmu a beat to grasp the sudden change in tone, then he signaled the shop manager right away.

Pulling a pouch of silver from beneath the counter, the manager passed it to Chen Jiangmu.

Stepping close to Lin Jue, Chen Jiangmu slipped the pouch discreetly into his hand. “The Chen tea shop has traded in Lin’an for years—surely Commander Lin knows our reputation better than anyone? Somebody must be slandering us.”

Feeling the hefty pouch, Lin Jue tucked it straight into his robe—his attitude flipped in an instant. Drawing Chen Jiangmu aside, he murmured, “Counterfeit tea is just an excuse. Take my advice: go pay your respects to Imperial Attendant Liang sooner rather than later, or tomorrow I’ll be marching into your silk shop claiming you sell fake goods too.”

Chen Jiangmu blinked. “Why is Imperial Attendant Liang targeting the Chen family?”

“Can’t say the exact reason—only that Imperial Attendant Liang loves silver. Most likely he wants to squeeze a little profit out of the Chens.”

Chen Jiangmu cupped his hands. “Thank you for the tip, Commander Lin. I’ll go talk this over with my eldest brother at once.”

Pocketing his bribe with a pleased grin, Lin Jue marched his soldiers away.

Before setting out, Prefect Li had instructed him to shake down the Chen family’s shops until they came crawling to pay homage to Imperial Attendant Liang. Fresh in Lin’an and unfamiliar with local ways, Prefect Li expected a drawn-out fuss—little knowing Lin Jue could wrap it up cleanly in two moves. Lin Jue hadn’t argued with Prefect Li either; not knowing the new prefect’s temper yet, keeping his mouth shut kept him out of trouble.

Getting results mattered most. Lin Jue bet the Chen family would be knocking on Liu’an Villa’s door before nightfall.

Hurrying back to Chen Manor, Chen Jiangmu told his eldest brother Chen Jianglin everything that happened at the tea shop.

Thinking it through, Chen Jianglin agreed Liang Shicheng likely just wanted silver—yet how huge was the man’s appetite? How much in silver notes should they bring? Stumped, he decided to consult Zhu Xiyan first; as a veteran official who had once buttered up Imperial Attendant Liang himself, his in-law would know the man’s measure.

Arriving at Zhu Manor, Chen Jianglin found Zhu Xiyan in his study composing poetry. Free from official headaches, the former prefect had spent the last two days savoring how light a man felt without a black gauze cap weighing on his head.

Seeing his in-law arrive, Zhu Xiyan set down his brush and welcomed him warmly.

After brief pleasantries, Chen Jianglin recounted Lin Jue’s raid on the tea shop.

Zhu Xiyan sighed. “At last I see Lin Jue’s true colors. I never should have married Shuzhen to him—I ought to have wed her straight to Haoyu from the start. While I held office, he called at my gate every single day. Two days out of office and he hasn’t shown his face once—I hear he’s already fawning over Li Ke instead. Because I can’t shield the Chen family anymore, he dares come make trouble at your door.”

Chen Jianglin shook his head. “That’s officialdom for you—the tea goes cold the moment the guest leaves. Honestly, In-law, you look much better off now, free of worries and living at ease.”

Zhu Xiyan chuckled. “True enough. When you hold office, you think you can’t let go of this or that. Only when someone forces your hand do you realize you can let go of all of it—and breathe easier. Truthfully, I ought to thank Imperial Attendant Liang for helping me see I prefer life just like this.”

At the mention of Liang, Chen Jianglin stated his purpose. “Even though Lin Jue no longer shows us deference, he dropped a hint before leaving: Imperial Attendant Liang is greedy and wants a cut from the Chen family. Tomorrow I plan to take Haoyu to call on him, only I haven’t a clue how much silver to bring. Give me some guidance, In-law.”

“Greedy doesn’t cover half of it—and his appetite keeps swelling. A few tens of thousands of taels won’t satisfy him.” Zhu Xiyan held up five fingers. “At least this much.”

“Five hundred thousand taels?”

Zhu Xiyan nodded. “Yes.”

“What a monster appetite! Well—call it buying peace to ward off disaster.”

“Five hundred thousand taels of silver is steep, yet worth every coin.”

“How so, In-law?”

“Liang Shicheng holds His Majesty’s absolute trust—one hand of his covers the sky in Bianliang. Use five hundred thousand taels to grease a connection with him, and you’ll never have to worry about expanding Chen businesses into the capital.”

Chen Jianglin had actually considered that angle. Not just anyone could buy their way into Liang’s circle; plenty of merchants had silver and nowhere to send it. Sending a signal through Lin Jue meant Liang at least considered the Chens worth his time.

Still, an odd unease nagged at Chen Jianglin. He didn’t dare dream of cracking the Bianliang market through Liang—he only prayed the lofty demon would pocket the silver and leave his family in peace.

“Let’s hope it turns out just as you say, In-law.”

Back at Chen Manor, Chen Jianglin sent word summoning Chen Haoyu home, saying urgent family business required his presence.

Nowadays Chen Haoyu handled business on his own, making daily rounds through the family’s shops, teahouses, wine shops, and restaurants. Chen Jianglin had promised that after one more year of training, he himself would retire completely to spend every day with Yao Qingyan.

Chen Haoyu had his own plans too: hurry up and have a son, raise him to take over the reins, and then spend every waking hour with his own wife in domestic bliss.

Then he remembered his promise to keep himself chaste for her for two years—sighing, he shelved the thought of a son for now. Counting on his fingers, less than a year and a half remained, and he counted down the days with quiet longing.

Once Chen Haoyu reached home, Chen Jianglin explained the situation and told him to pack five hundred thousand taels in silver notes to accompany him to Liu’an Villa.

With everything ready, father and son rode their carriage straight to Liu’an Villa.

After stating their business at the gate and receiving permission, Chen Haoyu carried the wooden chest of silver notes himself behind his father as they stepped inside.

Led into the reception hall, they saw Liang Shicheng sitting upright in proper robes at the seat of honor, sipping tea.

The attendant announced, “Lord Attendant, Master Chen and his son have arrived.”

Before Liang could speak, Chen Jianglin dropped to his knees; seeing his father kneel, Chen Haoyu knelt beside him, chest still cradled in his arms.

“We pay our respects to the Lord Attendant.”

“Up, up, quickly,” Liang Shicheng said, setting down his tea bowl with a painted smile. “Seats for Master Chen and his son.”

Father and son rose and took seats along the side.

With a flick of his fingers, Liang Shicheng dismissed the attendant.

He opened with generous praise. “I’ve long heard the Chen family are the wealthiest merchants in Lin’an—and righteous merchants at that, doing much good for the local people. When I return to Bianliang, I’ll be sure to put in a good word for your family before His Majesty.”

Not catching the hidden hook yet, Chen Jianglin bowed his head modestly. “Praise from Imperial Attendant Liang honors our house beyond measure. ‘Wealthiest in Lin’an’ is merely empty flattery from neighbors. Only because His Majesty governs a peaceful realm can the Chens trade at all. Having earned a modest living, giving back to His Majesty’s subjects is simply doing our small bit for the throne.”

Liang Shicheng marveled silently—*this old merchant knows how to talk*; not a single flaw to poke at. His gaze shifted onto Chen Haoyu. “And this young gentleman is?”

Chen Jianglin hurried to introduce him. “My son, Chen Haoyu.”

“Isn’t he the one who married Zhu Xiyan’s daughter—that famed Poetess of Jiangnan?”

“The very same.”

Liang Shicheng smiled slyly. “Young Master Chen is blessed with rare good fortune.”

Caught off guard by the compliment, Chen Haoyu didn’t know what to say. Seeing his father’s subtle glance, he realized it was time to present the chest.

Standing with the wooden box in his hands, he stepped before Liang Shicheng. “You traveled far from Bianliang to Lin’an, Lord Attendant. A trifling token to show our respect—please accept it with a smile.”

Eyeballing the chest held out before him, Liang Shicheng judged by its size it held a staggering stack of silver notes—easily hundreds of thousands of taels. Tempted he certainly was. Yet remembering how thoroughly Princess Consort Yi had serviced him yesterday, a deal was a deal; no matter how sweet the silver, he couldn’t take it today. He’d squeeze the Chens dry another day.

“Hehehe… you Chens are far too thoughtful, though take your gift back. Serving His Majesty means serving the people—how could I pocket gifts straight out of ordinary citizens’ hands?”

Chen Haoyu froze on the spot. Never in a hundred guesses had he expected this—stuck holding the chest mid-air, awkward whether he held it out or pulled it back.

Stunned too, Chen Jianglin thought the eunuch deemed the sum too stingy. Jumping up, he bowed deep. “If this humble gift falls short of your expectations, Lord Attendant, the Chen family will gladly prepare more.”

Rising from his seat, Liang Shicheng pushed the wooden chest firmly back into Chen Haoyu’s hands. “I don’t want your gift. I want to meet someone.”

Pulling the chest back, Chen Haoyu forced himself to ask, “Whom does the Lord Attendant wish to see?”

“Your wife—the foremost Poetess of Jiangnan.”

Father and son stared in shock.

Chen Haoyu refused fast. “Though my wife writes ci poetry, she’s merely a retiring housewife who rarely steps past her gate—hardly worthy of your audience, Lord Attendant.”

Chen Jianglin chimed in, “Shuzhen is only a woman who has never seen grand society. Were you to meet her, Lord Attendant, you’d only be disappointed.”

Seeing both men dodge him together, Liang Shicheng’s face hardened. “I’ll give you two days. If the poetess doesn’t show up at my door in two days, I won’t guarantee how long the Chens stay Lin’an’s richest family. Give it a few more days after that, and every Chen enterprise across Lin’an and beyond can close its doors for good.”

Hearing that cold threat, both men knew meeting Liang Shicheng could cost Shuzhen her life—neither dared breathe a word of agreement.

Seeing them silent, Liang Shicheng shouted toward the doors, “See our guests out!”

With a sharp flick of his sleeve, he vanished behind a standing screen, leaving father and son standing there stunned.

Only when an attendant stepped in saying, “Please leave, sirs,” did they snap out of it, Chen Haoyu still clutching the chest as he trailed behind his father.

Inside the carriage, silence hung heavy for a long stretch as both tried to fathom why Liang Shicheng insisted on seeing Shuzhen.

Unable to hold it in, Chen Haoyu burst out, “Father, why would Imperial Attendant Liang turn down all that silver and demand to see my wife instead?”

Chen Jianglin offered the most sensible guess he could find. “His Majesty has always cherished literary men and poets—he prizes writers of shi and ci above most. Likely that’s why Imperial Attendant Liang asked to meet Shuzhen.”

Chen Haoyu shook his head hard. “If he merely wanted to meet her, all he had to do was host a poetry banquet! One word to Young Master Dongfang, with his salon’s clout, and could I possibly stop my wife from attending? Why demand to see my wife alone? He’s clearly up to something crooked! Father—could Imperial Attendant Liang have designs on my wife?”

Chen Jianglin dismissed the thought at once. “He’s a palace eunuch, not even a whole man—why would he feel desire for a woman? Even if he kept some twisted itch, what beauties hasn’t he seen in the capital? He’d hardly travel all the way to Lin’an just to snatch another man’s wife and make himself a laughingstock.”

Still, Chen Haoyu’s gut twisted with dread. “My wife is gorgeous and brilliant—anyone who lays eyes on her risks losing his head. No matter what, I won’t let her walk through his door! We have two days. We’ll find a way to save the family’s business and keep my wife far away from Liang Shicheng.”

Chen Jianglin instructed, “Keep this quiet from the household for now. I’ll go consult your father-in-law right away. Take the silver notes home, lock them up safe, then ride out to see Prince Yi and ask if he has a way.”

In his heart of hearts, Chen Haoyu hated turning to Prince Yi over his wife’s troubles—yet against a monster like Liang Shicheng, aside from His Majesty, only Prince Yi had any chance of shaking him.

First the Chen carriage stopped at Zhu Manor to drop off Chen Jianglin before rolling home.

Seeing Chen Jianglin return a second time in one day baffled Zhu Xiyan.

“Back again so soon, In-law? Come sit—stay for dinner later.”

Sitting down, Chen Jianglin wasted zero breath. “Haoyu and I took five hundred thousand taels in silver notes to call on Imperial Attendant Liang. He wouldn’t even touch all that silver—instead he demanded to see Shuzhen! Nothing about it makes sense. Haoyu and I are worried sick. I sent him to ask Prince Yi for help while I came to talk it over with you.”

Furious, Zhu Xiyan slammed a fist onto the table. “Outrageous!” The tea bowls rattled so hard tea sloshed over the rims, though neither man noticed.

“I’ve heard rumors before—Liang Shicheng craves women, and worst of all, he has a sick taste for married women! Now he’s eyeing my own daughter?! How dare he!”

So Haoyu’s worst fear was dead on! Panic gripped Chen Jianglin. “Then under no circumstances can we let Shuzhen go see him! In-law, what on earth do we do?”

Zhu Xiyan’s eyes narrowed. “Dusk is falling; calling on him tonight looks wrong. First thing tomorrow morning, I’ll go face Liang Shicheng myself and demand to know why he insists on seeing my daughter!”

Chen Jianglin stood and cupped his hands. “I’m counting on you, In-law.”

“We’re one family—no need for such words.” With no heart left for dinner invitations, Zhu Xiyan saw Chen Jianglin straight to the gate, then paced back inside to plan how he would confront Liang Shicheng at dawn.
