## Chapter 122: Paying Another Call on Wangyue Pavilion

Sharing a carriage with a royal prince made Kuqiao stiff as a board, too tense to speak a word out of turn.

Yet being escorted home by a prince himself also filled her with pride—convincing her more than ever that Prince Yi was a fine man.

Mind tangled around Qianli Xun, Shen Jianchen spared the maid no thought; her sole value right now was giving him a rock-solid excuse to approach Qianli Xun.

He had lied to Mengrong about going to see Wenyuan when he was actually heading straight to Wangyue Pavilion. Now he understood: sometimes lies came from sheer helplessness.

He had tried to forget Qianli Xun and give Mengrong his whole heart, and failed flat. Knowing full well he had to wed Mengrong and could promise Qianli Xun nothing, he still couldn't stop gravitating toward her—even if she hated him for it.

Noon was approaching; waking late, Qianli Xun had to be starving. Better bring her food—Juxiang Tower came straight to mind.

"Bai Feng, swing by Juxiang Tower first."

"You got it!" At the name Juxiang Tower, Bai Feng beamed, assuming His Highness meant to treat him and Kuqiao to lunch.

When His Highness didn't take a table and ordered everything packed to go instead, Bai Feng's face fell.

Catching his droop, Shen Jianchen chuckled: "Drop me at Wangyue Pavilion, take some silver, and bring Kuqiao right back to Juxiang Tower for a feast."

Bai Feng lit up like a lantern: "Young Master thinks of everything!"

Kuqiao grew even fonder of Prince Yi—a man of such lofty rank without an ounce of arrogance was rare indeed.

Juxiang Tower's kitchen moved fast; a waiter packed the steaming dishes into food carriers, loaded them onto the carriage, and the three rolled on toward Wangyue Pavilion.

Dropped off at Wangyue Pavilion by the Youlin Retreat carriage, Qianli Xun thanked the driver and walked toward her bedchamber with heavy steps, Xiaguo trailing behind in just as grim a mood.

Shutting her bedchamber door behind her, Qianli Xun left even Xiaguo outside. Xiaguo retreated to the maids' quarters to nurse her own bruise—though one bright spark remained: at least Young Master Qiao knew her feelings now.

Instead of collapsing onto bed, Qianli Xun sat at her desk staring into space. The heavier her chest felt, the wilder her thoughts tangled as she searched for a thread through the maze.

Crossing over alone and stuck in a loveless marriage had left her hungry for real love. Meeting Wenyuan, she had thought she found it—even wondered if crossing over had been fated just to bring her to him—only for reality to slam a fist in her face.

What now? Only two roads lay open: give up, or hold on. So far she had no reason to give up—especially after promising Wenyuan just yesterday not to quit, their vows still ringing in her ears.

When life hit a wall, nobody could save you but yourself. Those four words floated back: "Right Thought, Right Mindfulness."

Was quitting right, or holding on? Too many things in life refused neat labels of right and wrong—maybe because she stood too deep inside the forest of love. Two lines of verse came to mind: "Why can't I tell the true shape of Lu-shan? Because I myself am in the mountain" [TN: Translated by Burton Watson]. That described her right this second.

Master Jingguang had said whether she became Zhu Shuzhen or Qianli Xun came down to actions, and actions sprang from mindset. Zhu Xiyan burning her manuscripts proved Qianli Xun's mindset and choices had already steered her onto Qianli Xun's own path.

Then maybe this trial with Wenyuan was a hurdle Qianli Xun had to cross. If so, she would face it head-on.

Before crossing over, she had always kept a cool head in romance—even Shen Jianchen's relentless pursuit had never rattled her. Crossing over and meeting Wenyuan had made her lose her footing in pure emotion, and reality had just dealt her a hard lesson.

She made up her mind to honor her heart while taking back her reason: "I'll wait one year for Wenyuan. If the Qiao family still won't budge after a year, I'll... let go."

"Young Lady, I heard you got back—have you eaten lunch?" Matron Wei's voice sounded outside.

Qianli Xun rose, opened the door, and returned to her desk.

Stepping in, Matron Wei scanned the empty room: "Where are those two maids? Getting lazier by the day!"

"Kuqiao hasn't returned from Youlin Retreat yet, and I sent Xiaguo to the maids' quarters—wanted some quiet."

"Did you eat lunch at the Qiao retreat?"

"No. Not hungry."

"That won't do! Assuming you ate there, the kitchen didn't cook for you—I'll go have them whip something up right now."

"No need, Matron. Sit and talk with me awhile—your words nourish me better than food."

Matron Wei smiled: "Not afraid I'll disturb your quiet?"

"You've seen the world and see straight through things, Matron—talking with you clears the fog in my head."

Taking a chair, Matron Wei asked, "More trouble weighing on you, Young Lady?"

Qianli Xun nodded. "Word of me staying at the retreat somehow reached Master Qiao. He showed up at dawn with men, tied Wenyuan up, and hauled him back—doubt they'll let him out to see me again."

"Forgive an old woman speaking blunt, Young Lady, and don't take offense: sleeping overnight at the Qiao retreat last night was a mistake. It only gave the Qiao elders more reason to look down on you."

"I didn't overthink it—Princess Jiahe pressed me so warm to stay and talk heart-to-heart, and nothing improper happened."

"Of course I know your character, Young Lady, yet outsiders—above all the Qiaos—won't see it that way. What's done is done; no use stewing, just be more careful going forward."

"Right you are, Matron. I'll watch every word and step from now on."

"The Qiaos are imperial relatives, and Young Master Qiao is the cream of Lin'an's youth—half the noble daughters in town dream of marrying in. Still, this mess showed me the Qiaos' true size: lofty gate, shallow sight, spooked by street chatter and blind to the rare jade right before their eyes! Hmph—better off not marrying into that house! A finer match awaits you."

Matron Wei's fierce loyalty brought a faint smile to Qianli Xun's lips: "You're family, Matron—of course you think I'm wonderful. Outsiders don't."

"Pay street folk's chatter no mind! They just parrot hearsay without knowing a thing about you—chasing gossip after dinner until they get bored and hunt a new thrill, tossing old rumors straight into the dust."

"Listening to you clears the clouds right out of my sky, Matron! I'll stay put inside Wangyue Pavilion until the gossip dies out."

"If you ask me, don't let folk box you in—go out when you feel like it! One lecture I will give you, though: stop putting every private thing into poetry."

"Fair criticism, Matron. From now on, what should be written gets written, and what shouldn't won't see a drop of ink."

"Now you're talking."

Eager to hear Matron Wei's take, Qianli Xun asked, "With the Qiao family acting like this, Matron, what should Wenyuan and I do?"

Matron Wei thought a beat: "I don't doubt Young Master Qiao loves you with all his heart, or that you love him right back. Pity—true love alone counts for nothing in this world. Born into this age, you have to learn to bend with it."

"So I should give up on true love?" Shadow returned to Qianli Xun's face.

"What else? Trying to slam your bare heart against the whole world is like an egg striking stone."

Grief silenced Qianli Xun.

Matron Wei softened her voice: "Since you can't bear throwing away a true heart, set yourself a deadline. If nothing changes by then, walk away clean—that way you'll never look back in regret."

Only then did Qianli Xun say, "Great minds think alike, Matron—I just gave myself a one-year deadline."

Matron Wei beamed: "Just called you a rare jade, and sure enough you already have a head on your shoulders! The Qiaos are fools to miss out—they'll live to regret it! Still, a year drags too long. How about half a year?"

After a quiet beat, Qianli Xun made up her mind: "Deal. Half a year."

Matron Wei stood up: "Rest a minute while I have the kitchen make lunch—hungry or not, you need food in your stomach."

Just as she turned for the door, Kuqiao stepped in: "Miss, Matron—Prince Yi escorted me back and even bought hot dishes from Juxiang Tower! He's waiting outside right now."

Matron Wei blinked in astonishment: "Prince Yi escorted a maid home?! Did my ears deceive me?"

"Not at all, Matron! Prince Yi was heading to see Young Master Qiao anyway, saw Miss had left without me and I had no carriage, so he kindly gave me a lift—and said since Youlin Retreat hadn't served Miss lunch, he brought dishes over."

Matron Wei mused aloud: "Prince Yi sure has a warm heart. Since we moved to Wangyue Pavilion, nobody from Zhu Manor has visited once, yet Prince Yi has called twice."

Knowing Matron Wei's razor-sharp eyes, Qianli Xun feared she might catch on and snapped, "Kuqiao, go tell Prince Yi I'm under the weather and already in bed—send him off."

Kuqiao said, "Prince Yi guessed you'd say that! He told me to tell Miss: if you want news of Young Master Qiao, you'd better let him in."

Qianli Xun fumed inside: "Shameless snake! Fresh out of Mengrong's hug and already swooping in while I'm down!"

Matron Wei urged, "Young Lady, he's Prince Yi—who dares slam the door on a royal prince? Invite him in! And since he brought lunch, it saves me bothering the kitchen."

Needing news of Wenyuan, Prince Yi was her only lifeline right now. Rising with heavy reluctance, Qianli Xun led Matron Wei and Kuqiao out to welcome the man she least wanted—yet had—to see.
