## Chapter 282: Two Stalkers, One Target

Back inside the Judicial Office, Shen Jianchen issued his orders. “Chief Constable Wen, take men out to the Chens’ country residence and comb the room where Chun Jinlan was held—we might turn up fresh evidence. Clerk Han, take constables and track down Scarface’s buddies to ask what happened that day.”

“At your command, Your Highness!” Both bowed and led their squads out.

Just then the constable who had let Chen Haodong in the night before stepped up and laid a small cloth packet on the prince’s desk. “You’re a prophet, Your Highness! Eldest Young Master Chen really did show up last night—here are the four taels of silver he slipped me, didn’t pocket a single coin.”

Shen Jianchen picked up the packet and pressed it straight back into the constable’s chest. “Since Eldest Young Master Chen gave it to you, keep it.”

Startled, thinking it a trap, the constable waved both hands. “No, no, no! A Judicial Office constable can’t take bribes!”

Shen Jianchen smiled. “Take it—consider it my reward.”

Relieved, the constable tucked the silver away, beaming. “Can Your Highness really tell the future? Eldest Young Master Chen actually went in to see Chun Jinlan too!”

“What did he say to her?”

“Voices were low and the door’s thick, so I only caught bits—sounded like Eldest Young Master Chen was the one who fetched her to Lin’an, and she even knelt begging to be his concubine.”

Everything clicked into place. If Chen Haodong brought Chun Jinlan in, ten to one he bought Scarface too—why else would Haoyu’s own lackey knife him in court? Chen Haodong was the key; somebody had to tail him.

Tailing him undetected took real light-foot skill. Wen Xin could fight yet lacked agility, and Bai Feng was out in Zhuli Village building the cottage. Shen Jianchen decided to go himself.

Dismissing the constable, he changed into plainclothes in his quarters, still found them too fine, and walked into the bustling market to pick out an ordinary dark-colored robe at a modest tailor shop. Changing inside the fitting room, he liked the simple, comfortable fit.

Stepping out, the shop clerk beamed. “A natural clothes-hanger, Young Master—even plain cloth looks high-class on you!”

Shen Jianchen smiled. “Got a scrap left over from this same fabric?”

“Lucky break—plenty of trimmings! Let me grab a piece.” Fetching a black strip from the back room, the clerk handed it over. Pleased, Shen Jianchen tucked it inside his robe, paid in silver, and added, “Have your shop deliver my changed robes to the Judicial Office.”

Staring at the silver, the clerk gaped. “Wait—aren’t you the Judicial Investigator?”

Shen Jianchen walked out without answering. The clerk muttered to himself, “Same bearing, just way too young—and buying cheap cloth like that? Couldn’t be him.”

Hiring a carriage, Shen Jianchen staked out the Chen silk shop first, saw no sign of Chen Haodong, and collared a clerk stepping out. “Excuse me, is Eldest Young Master Chen in today?”

The clerk eyed him up and down. “Who’s asking?”

Shen Jianchen bluffed on the spot. “Judicial Office runner—the Judicial Investigator wants Eldest Young Master for questioning.”

The clerk turned polite at once. “He didn’t come in this morning—heard he went to the Judicial Office.”

“He left after the hearing. Didn’t come here?”

“No, sir, though he’s been spending most days at the tea shop lately. Try there.”

Thanking him, Shen Jianchen rode over to the Chen tea shop. After a long wait, Chen Haodong finally stepped out and climbed into a Chen carriage. Just as Shen Jianchen moved to board his hired rig, a familiar figure in young-master attire strolled out of the adjacent tea stall.

*So you suspect Chen Haodong too—great minds think alike.*

Stepping right into her path, he murmured, “Dressed loud as a peacock—I spotted you in one glance, and you expect to fool Chen Haodong?”

After Matron Wei’s tip last night, Qianli Xun had suspected Chen Haodong too and slipped into one of her two sets of men’s robes to tail him. Stung that he blew her cover on sight, she fired back, “And you think cheap plainclothes hide the fact that you’re Prince Yi?”

No time for bickering. “Same target—hop in my carriage?”

“What are you standing around for? We’ll lose him!” Qianli Xun marched straight over and climbed aboard first.

Smiling to himself, Shen Jianchen climbed in after her and told the driver, “Tail that Chen carriage ahead.” Paid handsomely, the driver asked zero questions and kept a safe distance behind.

It was their first time riding together in a carriage since crossing over—so close. In the old days Shen Jianchen would have grabbed her hand and poured out sweet words. Now both were married, and he hadn’t even kept his vow of chastity; he had no right left. Besides, the second he climbed in, she shifted over to put polite space between them.

He kept it civil. “Why are you tailing Chen Haodong?”

Same goal, and he was the judge on the case—no point hiding anything. “Matron Wei spotted him drinking tea with Chun Jinlan in a private booth at Yongtai Teahouse. Figured they’re in cahoots, so I came to dig up clues.”

After a pause, he asked, “For Chen Haoyu?”

“He’s my husband,” she answered without blinking. “Helping him is helping myself.”

Words slipped out before he could stop them. “I’ll help you. So long as you’re happy, I’m happy.”

A tug of warmth hit her—then Mengrong’s bitter words echoed back, snapping her awake. “No! Don’t go out of your way for me. Put your energy into treating Mengrong right.”

Mention of Qiao Mengrong reminded him of the pamphlets. “About those pamphlets—I apologize on Mengrong’s behalf. Don’t hold it against her.”

“I never really blamed her. I just miss what we used to have—knowing our friendship can never go back.”

“My fault.”

“Not yours—just fate playing tricks.” Sitting with that helplessness a beat, she called out, “Prince Yi.”

“Why not use my name?”

“Zhao Pu.”

“You know that’s not the name I mean.”

“From now on, you’re Prince Yi, Zhao Pu, to me. That other name—I’ll lock it away in my heart and look back on it now and then.”

Silence hung a moment before he nodded. “Deal. I’ll lock ‘Qianli Xun’ away in my heart too, and savor it now and then.”

They shared a quiet smile.

Lightening the air, she asked, “How did you start suspecting Chen Haodong?”

Shen Jianchen puffed up to lift the mood. “Pure genius—razor-sharp observation, seeing right through the surface to spot Chen Haodong’s guilt at one glance.”

She rolled her eyes. “Keep bragging, then—forget I asked!”

“All right, all right. At Chen Manor I caught Chen Haodong and Chun Jinlan trading shady looks. Then last night he used delivering bedding to Haoyu as cover to sneak into Chun Jinlan’s cell. Dead giveaway.”

“Chen Haodong can’t be that dumb—meeting Chun Jinlan right in the middle of an investigation? Not scared of getting caught?”

“He’s not dumb—I’m just so brilliant I make him look dumb.”

She scoffed, secretly relieved they could still banter like old friends.

Lifting the window curtain, Shen Jianchen saw dusk settling and open countryside rolling past. “Why’s Chen Haodong heading outside the city at this hour?”

“Plays the saint in public—who knows what dirt he gets up to in the dark.”

“Why do you think he’s setting Chen Haoyu up?”

“The Chen family business inheritance, obviously.”

“How’d you figure that?”

“I’m not blind—takes two minutes of watching to see it.”

“Eldest Master Chen doesn’t seem to see it. Why not tip him off when you get home?”

“Not touching Chen business politics with a ten-foot pole. Doesn’t concern me, and Chen Haoyu has zero ambition for it anyway. All I want is this mess over so life goes back to normal.”

Concern shaded his voice. “Doesn’t Chen Haoyu’s mess upset you?”

“What’s to be upset about? I knew his track record the day I married him and braced for everything.” She wasn’t about to let Shen Jianchen think she was disillusioned with Haoyu and nurse false hopes.

Reading between the lines, he thought, *No need to put on a front with me. I won’t act reckless anymore, and I’ve stopped hunting for a tree-climbing pig. Still, if I ever stumble across one, I’m not giving up—though odds are I’ll never see a pig climb a tree.*

The carriage halted. The driver called in, “Young Master, that carriage stopped at a house ahead, and the passenger went inside.”

Both hopped down, and Shen Jianchen handed over silver. “Pull off the road and wait—we’ll need a ride back once we’re done.”

“Don’t worry, Young Master—I spotted a thick, low-branched tree on the way in. I’ll park underneath; once dark hits, nobody’ll see us.” The driver eased the rig away on quiet wheels.

“Not bad,” Qianli Xun praised. “Your driver’s got solid security instincts.”

“Look who hired him.”

“Bragging again?”

Shen Jianchen pulled the scrap of black cloth from his robe and tied it over his face. “Spying at night takes a mask so nobody spots us. Only got one—what about you?”

Qianli Xun fished out a silk handkerchief. “Covered.”

“Way too bright—and look at your loud outfit! Wait out here while I go in.”

“I’m coming too. Almost pitch dark—who’s going to notice?”

Never one to fight her stubborn streak, he gave in. “Fine—stick close.”

“Why stick close? Think I can’t climb a wall myself?” Before the words left her mouth, she sprang cleanly atop the courtyard wall with her light-foot technique. Shen Jianchen kicked off and landed right beside her.

She whispered, “Small place—just scanned it, zero servants. Think this is the Chens’ country house?”

“Doubt it,” he murmured back. “The Chens’ country house would be way bigger and staffed with servants.”

“How would you know? You’ve never seen it.”

“Chen Haoyu testified there are servants at the country house—simple deduction.”

Before she could argue, carriage wheels rumbled up outside. In sync, both vaulted onto the roof and crouched low to peek out.

Twilight still lingered enough to spot the flashy carriage—identical to the Chunxiang Brothel rig Chun Jinmo had arrived in.

“Chunxiang Brothel,” Qianli Xun muttered. “Chen Haodong’s sneaking out to the sticks to meet a courtesan—no wonder Eldest Sister-in-law despises him.”

Shen Jianchen watched in silence as a woman stepped down, pushed open the gate, and entered: Chun Jinchan.

Chen Haodong stepped out of the house and swept her into his arms, speaking loud and loose since they had the place to themselves. “At the Judicial Office and the tea shop all day, all I could think about was how wild you are—come inside and show me a good time!”

Chun Jinchan spoke just as blunt. “I turned down clients seven straight days and scrubbed myself spotless waiting for you—you’d better pay for all seven days, or the madam will pitch a fit.”

“When have I ever been stingy with silver?” Covering her face with kisses, he hauled her indoors.

Cheeks heating, Qianli Xun shifted back. “Just a sleazy tryst—we’re not learning anything useful. I’m heading back—”

Shen Jianchen caught her arm on reflex, then let go at her awkward look. “People always talk after—he might spill something vital. Stay and listen.”

“Fine. We’ll listen.”

Picking a spot above the bedchamber, Shen Jianchen lifted a roof tile and peered through—right onto Chen Haodong half-undressed and tumbling across the bed with Chun Jinchan.
