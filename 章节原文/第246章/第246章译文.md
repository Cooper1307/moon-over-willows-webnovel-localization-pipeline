## Chapter 246: Prince Yi and His Consort Return to Lin'an

The Bianliang wedding had been grand enough to satisfy Qiao Mengrong—at last she had married her brother, imperial decree sealing the bond even with his heart elsewhere.

Yet on their wedding night, he never lifted her bridal veil, leaving only cold words before walking out: "Mengrong, I can step into Zhao Pu's shoes to protect you and marry you, yet I cannot share your bed as him—because to me you have always been my little sister."

Alone in the red-draped bridal chamber, her tears burned straight into venom against Zhu Shuzhen—the thief who stole her brother's heart.

The very next morning, he rushed to depart for Lin'an, and Qiao Mengrong hurried just as fast, burning to launch her revenge. Needing a sharp ally beyond Xilan, she recruited Matron Zou—who agreed on the spot: "Staff can mind the capital manor; I'm coming to Lin'an to see what kind of vixen bewitched His Highness into abandoning his own bridal chamber!"

Battered from days on the road, Qiao Mengrong arrived back at Youlin Retreat ready to rest, sharpen her claws, and plot payback against her former confidante.

Shen Jianchen knew full well his coldness gutted Mengrong, yet closing his eyes and claiming her without love would make him feel like a beast. Maybe someday time would heal him into accepting her as a real wife—just not now, especially after promising Qianli Xun he would stay chaste for her.

"Staying chaste"—part noble, part absurd. In a rotting dynasty run by a foolish emperor and corrupt ministers where honest men stood powerless, clinging to personal honor felt noble; yet marrying one woman under fate while keeping his heart locked to another married woman sounded downright ridiculous.

All the road back from Bianliang, one resolution grew clear: he needed work. Staying cooped up at Youlin Retreat facing Mengrong drowned him in guilt, and idle hours kept Qianli Xun looping through his skull. Burying himself in real work might dull the longing—and once he let Qianli Xun go, he could finally look at Mengrong as his wife without shame.

Barely settled back at Youlin Retreat, Zhu Xiyan arrived leading a delegation of Lin'an officials. "Forgive us disturbing Your Highness after a tiring journey! Having missed seeing you off to Bianliang, we hurried over at once to beg pardon."

Annoyed internally—if you knew I was tired, why barge in?—Shen Jianchen kept his voice smooth since he had a favor of his own in mind. "No fault of yours, Prefect Zhu; I left quietly to spare everyone hassle."

Bowing lower, Zhu Xiyan presented their proposal: "The officials of Lin'an have arranged a feast tomorrow to welcome Your Highness home and celebrate your wedding with Princess Consort Yi. We beg you both to honor us with your presence."

Sick of the wasteful luxury modeled by his imperial father's court, Shen Jianchen frowned. "Bianliang's banquet was lavish enough—no need bleeding taxpayers dry for a second feast. Cancel it."

Instantly Zhu Xiyan and his entourage dropped to their knees. "We merely regretted missing Your Highness's capital wedding and began preparations the day you left! Not one copper comes from public funds—the Chen family is sponsoring every expense!"

Catching on—the wedding raid had terrified the Chens into buying peace, and refusing might leave them taking it out on Qianli Xun—Shen Jianchen relented. "Very well. Princess Consort Yi and I will attend tomorrow. Rise."

"Thank you, Your Highness!"

Waving the rest out to wait in the courtyard, Shen Jianchen spoke privately with Zhu Xiyan. "Since taking office as Military Commissioner of Lin'an, I've barely touched city affairs and feel remiss toward His Majesty's trust..."

Sweat prickled Zhu Xiyan's neck: If Prince Yi starts auditing Lin'an administration, half my skeletons spill out!

Spotting his panic—no shock in a dynasty rotten to the roots—Shen Jianchen had zero illusions about saving the empire; helping ordinary folk beat fighting hopeless court rot. "Keep managing prefectural politics, Prefect Zhu; military affairs need little of me too. I want a hands-on post dealing directly with commoners—what office fits?"

Exhaling in huge relief, Zhu Xiyan replied, "Your compassion for the people humbles me, Your Highness! There is a post—Judicial Adjutant of the Judicial Office, handling civilian criminal investigations and trials. Only rank eight-B, far too lowly for a prince..."

Perfect! "Rank eight-B falls under your transfer authority, Prefect Zhu. Move the current adjutant to a decent post—I'm taking over as Lin'an's Judicial Adjutant."

Thrilled, Zhu Xiyan bowed. "I'll arrange it today! Only, since His Majesty named you Military Commissioner, altering your title risks my head—we'll announce that Military Commissioner Yi acts concurrently as Judicial Adjutant on His Majesty's behalf to care for the people."

Credit the incompetent emperor for my work? Classic bureaucrats! Swallowing his disgust, Shen Jianchen nodded. "Fine. I report for duty right after tomorrow's feast."

Once the officials left, Bai Feng couldn't hold back: "Your Highness, haven't seen Kuqiao in ages—may I go visit her now?"

"Go," Shen Jianchen granted—then added quietly, "Ask Kuqiao how she's doing."

"Yes, Your Highness." Feeling a twinge for Princess Jiahe—still pining after a married woman right after his own wedding—Bai Feng put himself in the prince's boots: forced to marry someone other than Kuqiao, he'd probably act the same! Off he rushed to see his girl.

Needing to explain his new post before Mengrong drew wild conclusions, Shen Jianchen walked over to her courtyard.

Waking from her nap, Qiao Mengrong stood gazing bitterly around her maiden pavilion: wedded wife now, yet still parked in a side wing! She had every right ashow could they give me a hard time?"

Old Madam Xia laughed and chided him. "Talking nonsense again. Did you forget why your father-in-law forced you to marry the Zhu girl?"

"A real man doesn't rehash old humiliations! Don't bring that up, Grandmother, or if that tigress overhears, your grandson will be in deep trouble."

Wanting a fat handout, Chen Haoyu pivoted fast to butter her up. "Grandmother, I pray every day for your long life and good health. Today at Zhu Manor, I hunted down their Old Ancestor's longevity secret. Want to hear it?"

Knowing full well her grandson was spinning tall tales, the old lady played along. "Of course I do."

Chen Haoyu leaned in with mock mystery. "I circled the Zhu family's Buddhist hall for ages and heard nothing but wooden fish clacking and chanting. I figured their Old Ancestor lived this long from knocking a wooden fish and reciting sutras every day. How about I buy you a wooden fish and a string of prayer beads tomorrow so you can give it a try?"

"Save it. I'm not cut out for chanting sutras. Just drop by every day to sit with me and keep me company. Keep me happy, and longevity will take care of itself."

"Deal—I'll come keep you company more often." Casting about for a way to sic his grandmother on Zhu Shuzhen, he put on his best pitiful face. "Grandmother, you have to help me."

"Help you with what?"

"Zhu Shuzhen is bullying me."

"How?"

"She drove off my maids, stole my courtyard, seized all my property, and doles out a measly twenty taels a month! I'm practically a pauper now. Nobody outside even believes I'm a Chen."

Old Madam Xia chuckled. "That bride of yours has a razor-sharp tongue. Even I can't out-talk her. Besides, who told you to kneel and beg her to marry you, and sign some prenuptial contract? Blame yourself for gambling with Master Jiang and nearly losing the whole Chen estate."

Chen Haoyu blinked in shock. "How do you know about that?"

"Your new wife told me. Your parents know too."

Zhu Shuzhen! You didn't leave me a scrap of face in front of my own family! Just you wait! Chen Haoyu seethed inside while keeping his voice coaxing. "Gambling was my mistake, Grandmother, and I swear I'll never touch dice again. Still, you have to put Zhu Shuzhen in her place. She's walking all over my head now."

"How should I put her in her place?"

"Find excuses to give her a hard time whenever you can."

"She's your wife now. Giving her a hard time is giving you a hard time."

"Grandmother, we're husband and wife in name only."

A shadow crossed the old lady's face. "My grandson waited so long to take a wife, and this is how you two live. Breaks my heart just thinking about it."

"Don't be sad, Grandmother," Chen Haoyu wheedled. "Sooner or later I'll marry someone who pleases you."

The truth slipped right out of Old Madam Xia. "I'm already plenty pleased with the wife you have."

Seeing his scheme to weaponize his grandmother flop, Chen Haoyu went straight for the silver. "Grandmother, Zhu Shuzhen won't give me spending money, and I dare not ask Father or Mother. Could you spare me a little silver?"

She never pinched pennies with her favorite grandson. Motioning a maid to bring a wooden box, she drew out a five-hundred-tael silver note. "Spend it wise. Not a copper on courtesans or gamblers, hear me?"

Chen Haoyu snatched it up in delight and showered her cheeks with kisses until she laughed from ear to ear. After a few more minutes of banter, he tucked the note into his sleeve, dashed out of the manor, and swept Scarface and his crew back out onto the town.
