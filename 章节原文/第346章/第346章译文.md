## Chapter 346: A Storyteller Brings Good News

Chapter 346: A Storyteller Brings Good News

At daybreak, Chen Haoyu woke from Xiangrong's bed. The night before, he had let himself loose, pretending she was his wife. He knew it plainly: from now on, he had no right to long for his wife again.

Xiangrong nestled up to him. "Haoyu, I'm so happy to be your woman."

Compared with his wife, Xiangrong was far gentler; compared with other pretty girls, she sang his wife's poems the sweetest. It was only natural that she had won him over.

"Xiangrong, forgive me for not holding a wedding with you. I can have only one wedding in this life."

"No need to explain. I understand everything. If I can stay by your side and bear your children, I'll be content."

"I can't give you a wedding, but I promise you this: there'll be no other woman in my life."

Xiangrong knew he had slept with her only because he missed Youqi Jushi, and that the promise came from obligation, not love. Even so, she was moved.

From that first night on, Chen Haoyu stopped holding back. He spent nearly every night at Xiangrong's rooms.

One day Xiangrong lost her appetite and kept retching. The doctor they called confirmed it: she was pregnant. The whole household rejoiced.

With a child on the way, Chen Haoyu put aside his indulgence and decided to take up his duties as heir to the business.

Yao Qingyan and Chen Jianglin finally breathed easy. Paying three thousand taels of silver to buy Xiangrong's freedom had been worth every coin.

One evening, just after dusk, Chen Haoyu came back from the embroidery shop, walked into Moxiang Garden, entered his wife's Suxin Thatched Hall, and lay down on her bed.

"Wife, Xiangrong and I even have a child now. Will you blame me? What I wanted most was a child of our own, and that can never be…" A tear rolled from the corner of his eye.

From outside came Matron Wei's voice. "Young master, there's something I need to discuss with you."

Chen Haoyu wiped his eyes, got up, walked into the outer room, and opened the door himself. "Come in, Matron, and tell me."

Matron Wei stepped in and saw the young master's eyes were red. She guessed he had been crying. So he still couldn't let the young mistress go.

Moved as she was, she had to speak. "Young master, Miss Xiangrong is with child. It isn't right for us servants to keep occupying Moxiang Garden. Why not take this chance to move back to Zhu Manor, so Miss Xiangrong can live here?"

Chen Haoyu refused flatly. "Don't bring it up again, Matron. Unless my wife comes back to life and tells me with her own lips to take you all away, I won't hear of it."

Matron Wei sighed. "Alas, the young master's marriage to the mistress has been dissolved. We servants have no proper place here. Word will get out, and people will laugh."

"Dissolving the marriage was a last resort. A new emperor sits on the throne; the old edict can be voided. Say no more about leaving, Matron."

"Very well. I won't mention it again."

Matron Wei indeed never raised the subject again. She lived quietly in Moxiang Garden with the ten other servants, and had her hands full with Amei's upcoming wedding.

…

Shen Jianchen bore the title of prince but lived the life of a country villager, and he found it rather pleasant.

Unlike the villagers, he never had to work. Li Ke, the prefect of Lin'an, saw to it that money was delivered to him in person.

Shen Jianchen turned no one away and accepted every gift. A corrupt official's silver was there for the taking; spending it on good deeds would count as merit for them.

Li Ke's change of heart toward the Prince of Yi had one main cause: his patron at court, Liang Shicheng, had been strangled on the new emperor Zhao Huan's orders, and every last bit of his property confiscated. With his old backer gone, he had thought of the Prince of Yi.

The Prince of Yi was a prince without real power, but a prince all the same. He could get a word in with that brother of his on the throne.

While currying favor with bribes, Li Ke heard that the emperor pitied his brother wandering outside the capital. He had even told his ministers that the court was too busy with troubles at home and abroad to fetch him now, but once the troubles were over, he would surely summon him back to the capital.

With such signs in the air, Li Ke would not let a powerful man under his nose slip away. He came to the bamboo grove to pay his respects in person now and then, never without rich gifts and money.

The people of Zhuli Village had never seen a high official. Whenever the prefect came, they all crowded around to watch. Soldiers held them back while every neck craned toward the finest bamboo house in the village.

One day Li Ke delivered his silver and gifts and left, well pleased. The villagers watched him go, then stood outside the bamboo house waiting for Bai Feng to speak.

Inside the house, Shen Jianchen was dressed as a common villager, his face stubbled, looking much older than his years. He raised a wine gourd, drank, and said, "Go tell everyone there's another feast today."

Bai Feng was dressed as a villager too, but his hair was neatly combed and his face clean; he looked far sharper than the prince. He thought of his own marriage and, instead of hurrying off to tell the villagers, said, "Your Highness, promise me one thing first."

"Hmph. Since when do you bargain with me?"

"It isn't a bargain, Your Highness, it's a request. Amei married Master Jiang last week. When will Kuqiao and I have our day?"

"I'm sorry my trouble has kept you and Kuqiao waiting. I promise you this: I'll have your bamboo house built first, then bring Kuqiao here as your bride, so she won't have to stay in the Chen house with no standing."

Bai Feng beamed. "Thank you, Your Highness!"

"I'll have a proper talk with Chen Haoyu in a day or two and bring Matron Zou and the others back. She belongs to me; her servants should follow me too. The bamboo house is still your job. If the silver runs short, just ask the corrupt officials for more."

"Yes, sir."

Bai Feng walked out of the bamboo house and called to the villagers gathered outside. "Listen up, everyone. There's another Qianli feast today. Same rule as always: every one of you shout 'Qianli Xun' ten times, loud!"

The villagers had no idea why, but they shouted: "Qianli Xun! Qianli Xun! Qianli Xun!…"

Hearing the shouts from outside, Shen Jianchen drained a mouthful of wine and murmured to himself, "Only this makes me feel you're still alive. Was it cold for you outside Yanjing this winter? Spring is here. It's time I went to Yanjing and brought you back to Lin'an, and Mengrong and Xilan too. I've found a place for you, close to me. I'll stay with you to the end of my days."

When the shouting was done, Bai Feng went on. "Same as before. Whoever's doing the shopping, come to me for silver."

Ku Jue led the buyers forward, collected the silver, and went off about their tasks.

At noon the Qianli feast began, laid out in the village head's courtyard.

Shen Jianchen ate a few mouthfuls, then took his wine gourd and went off alone.

The village head was about to urge the Prince of Yi to eat more when Bai Feng stopped him. "His Highness has something on his mind. Don't disturb him."

The village head sat back down, saw to the guests at the Qianli feast, and asked the question that had been bothering him. "Captain Bai, forgive my prying. Why does the prince call this a 'Qianli feast'? And why make everyone shout 'Qianli Xun'? When I went into the city, I heard Qianli Xun was a gifted writer whose stories are wonderful—the storytellers all love to tell them. What is the prince to this Master Qianli Xun?"

"Don't ask about what's none of your business. Just remember: when the prince hears that name, he's happy. When the prince is happy, we all get a feast."

"I've got it, I've got it. No more prying. Captain Bai, let's drink a cup together."

Shen Jianchen carried his wine gourd to the village gate, where a carriage happened to be waiting. He climbed in and told the driver to take him to Qiaotouwa in the city.

At Qiaotouwa he headed straight for the storytelling house. He was luckier than Chen Haoyu: a storyteller who specialized in tales by Qianli Xun was just taking the stage.

Shen Jianchen found a seat, ordered no tea, asked for a token plate of preserved fruit, and kept drinking from his gourd.

Once the storyteller had settled himself on the stage, he sipped tea as custom required and began. "Friends, you're in luck today. I've come by a brand-new storybook by the gifted Qianli Xun—still warm from the press. Listen, and I'll tell it to you now…"

Shen Jianchen stopped drinking. His hazy eyes were full of anticipation.

Someone in the audience called out, "Don't fool us! Everyone knows the gifted Qianli Xun left for Bianliang more than two years ago. That story of yours is probably old hat in Bianliang, and now you're peddling it to us!"

The hope faded from Shen Jianchen's eyes. "She's gone. How could there be a new story?" he murmured, and lifted the gourd to drink again.

The storyteller explained, "You wrong me, sir. I wouldn't dare bring it out if it weren't fresh. The book was sent to me secretly from Yanjing by a friend. The Jin hold Yanjing; getting anything out of there is no small feat. My friend says nobody in Yanjing dares tell stories now, so the gifted Qianli Xun asked him to send the books out. They changed hands several times before they reached me. He wrote in his letter that the gifted Qianli Xun will go on writing and will keep sending them to me."

Hope flared in Shen Jianchen's eyes again. "When did the new book arrive?" he called out.

"Ten days ago," the storyteller answered.

Shen Jianchen stood up. "Ten days? From Yanjing to Lin'an is a month at the least. If it got here within a month, she wrote it a month ago. It's April now, so she likely wrote it in March—no! Even February or January would do. She's alive?"

The crowd stared at him, wondering what he was muttering about.

The storyteller replied, "The gifted Qianli Xun is alive, of course. Otherwise my friend couldn't have told me he'd keep sending me his stories."

"She's alive. She's alive. I have to go find her, I have to go…" Shen Jianchen couldn't sit still a moment longer. He turned and left, tears of joy in his eyes.

Someone in the crowd observed, "He must be a follower of the gifted Qianli Xun. He thought the writer might have died when the Jin army came south after he left Lin'an for Bianliang. Now that he hears he's in Yanjing, he's so worked up he wants to go find him in person. Yanjing is full of Jin soldiers. He'll likely never come back."

The storyteller sighed. "Don't be like that young man, friends. The gifted Qianli Xun writes fine stories; listening is enough. No need to follow him wherever he goes. But enough chatter—back to the tale…"

All the way back, Shen Jianchen was too excited to know what to do with his limbs in the carriage. "She's alive, she's alive," he kept saying under his breath.

When he got back to the bamboo house, he meant to pack at once and leave for Yanjing, but found Wenyuan waiting for him inside.

Shen Jianchen threw his arms around him, trembling. "Do you know? She's alive! Alive!"

Qiao Wenxian hugged the prince back. "That's exactly why I came. I see you've heard already."

Shen Jianchen let go of him. "How did you learn it?"

Qiao Wenxian guided him to a seat. "Early this morning, Brother Dongfang came to me. He held a poetry gathering yesterday, and a new acquaintance was there. This friend was trapped in Yanjing last year. It was only after the present emperor took the throne, and the Song ceded land to make peace with the Jin and the Jin withdrew from outside Bianliang, that the people stuck in Yanjing could come and go freely. Once free, this friend wanted to see what Yanjing looked like under Jin rule, so he stayed on. Then, unable to refuse a friend's kindness, he spent New Year's Day there. One day he was wandering the streets and stepped into a tea house hung with calligraphy and paintings. He went through them one by one, and there, to his amazement, was a new lyric in Shuzhen's hand. Do you know what date it was signed?"

"What date?"

"The sixth day of the first month of the Jingkang era."

Shen Jianchen was more agitated than ever. "When I saw her new grave, it was still the Xuanhe era. If she wrote it on the sixth day of the first month, she must be alive. I—I'm going to Yanjing now, to find her."

"Don't rush," Qiao Wenxian said. "Haste only spoils things. Think carefully. With Shuzhen's gifts, if she were free, she'd have come back to Lin'an at once. She hasn't come back. What does that tell you?"

Wenyuan's words steadied him. "That Wanyan Chi is still holding her, and she isn't free."

Qiao Wenxian nodded. "So if you charge off rashly, Wanyan Chi may play his tricks again and demand another ten million taels of silver. Where would you find that? Knowing Shuzhen is alive is reason enough for us to breathe easier. Think of a way to force Wanyan Chi to hand her over before you go to Yanjing."

Shen Jianchen saw the sense in it—but what move could strike in one blow and make Wanyan Chi let her go willingly?

He remembered how Wanyan Chi had once come to Lin'an searching for his mother, and at once he had a plan. "I have a way. I'll make him release her, willingly. But I need a favor from you."

"Why stand on ceremony with me? Just say it."

"Tell those three young masters not to breathe a word to Chen Haoyu that she's alive. I don't want any complications."

"I knew your mind before you spoke. I've already warned Brother Dongfang and the others. And with Miss Xiangrong pregnant, Chen Haoyu has his own life to live; no one wants to disturb it. It's mainly for you. You've come through so much, traveled so far, paid so dearly. If they still kept you from her, even heaven would find it hard to bear—let alone us."

Shen Jianchen smiled through tears. Bai Feng and Nan Shuang, standing to one side, were happy for the prince too.

Bai Feng had meant to tell Kuqiao that Youqi Jushi was alive. But hearing what the prince asked of Qiao Wenxian, he decided to hold his tongue. Kuqiao would tell Matron Wei, Matron Wei would tell the other servants, one of them would slip up, and word would reach Chen Haoyu—and that might ruin everything the prince had planned.

After Qiao Wenxian left, Shen Jianchen began to wash and groom. Bai Feng first shaved the stubble from the prince's face, then washed it for him, and finally combed his hair into a topknot.

Matron Zou came in from outside. "Let me attend to Your Highness."

Bai Feng stepped aside. "Your hands are better than mine, Matron. You do it."

Matron Zou took the wooden comb from Bai Feng and carefully combed the prince's hair.

Shen Jianchen made no reply. He listened with his whole heart to the words so full of feeling. One thing was certain: nothing stood between him and Qianli Xun now. He could go to her without a shadow of hesitation.

When he was done, he looked a new man.

Shen Jianchen told Bai Feng not to follow him. Bai Feng wanted to bring Kuqiao into the house as soon as possible and hurried off to get the bamboo house built. Shen Jianchen rode alone toward the Shao family escort agency in the city.
