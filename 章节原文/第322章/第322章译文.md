## Chapter 322: The Black-Draped Prisoner Cart

Once the troops marched off, Chen Haoyu didn’t pause a heartbeat—bolting out the gate, he hitched a carriage and raced after them.

Still baffled, Madam Cui asked, “Sister-in-law, why on earth would Imperial Attendant Liang want to meet Shuzhen?”

Unable to guess the real reason, Yao Qingyan only murmured bleakly, “I fear this trip spells disaster for her.”

Madam Bao chimed in, “Shuzhen barely sets foot outside the manor—she couldn’t possibly have offended Imperial Attendant Liang! Why did Commander Lin show up with an army hauling her off like a criminal?”

Too heavy-hearted to answer, everyone stayed silent. Feeling sidelined again, Madam Bao swallowed another bitter lump—her standing in this household really had hit rock bottom when nobody even bothered answering her questions.

Staring down the empty street for a long while, Old Madam Xia sighed at last. “What’s done is done. Worrying won’t help—leave it to Heaven now. Go back inside.”

Slowly the crowd dispersed back inside the manor to wait for whatever storm was coming.

Outside Liu’an Villa, Lin Jue watched Zhu Shuzhen step alone through the tall gates. Once the doors shut behind her, he let out a long breath, shaking off an unwanted pang of worry and regret, and led his soldiers away.

By the time Chen Haoyu arrived, the gates stood locked tight. He hammered on the wood, yelling until his throat went raw: “Let my wife out! Let my wife out!” Not a single voice answered from inside.

Spent and hoarse, Chen Haoyu slumped down against the front doors, refusing to budge until his wife walked back out.

Inside, a servant led Qianli Xun into a cavernous chamber and vanished, slamming the door shut behind him with a heavy *bang*. Without even glancing back, Qianli Xun swept the room with sharp, wary eyes.

Almost bare of furniture, the vast room looked weirdly empty—except for sheer pink silk curtains hanging floor to ceiling layer after layer ahead, hiding whatever lay beyond as warm, humid mist curled out from behind the drapes.

*Could Imperial Attendant Liang be taking a bath behind those curtains?* Qianli Xun wondered.

Then giggles drifted through the mist—definitely more than one woman, several by the sound of it.

Curiosity getting the better of her, Qianli Xun parted layer after layer of damp pink gauze until thick steam billowed around her—and there lay a sprawling indoor bathing pool. Amid rolling hot vapor, a handful of young women bobbed like lotus blooms in mist, laughing and splashing one another.

*Why on earth did Liang have his servant bring me here?*

Before she could work it out, a head surfaced from the water—straggly soaked gray-streaked hair plastered around an aged, lecherous face stretched in a slimy grin. Lunging his hands at the splashing girls, he cackled, “Felt every inch of you underwater already—now whichever one I catch gets a big kiss!”

Knowing all too well what needle torture waited if he caught them, the girls darted expertly through the pool, laughing brightly as if playing a game so Liang wouldn’t suspect their dread.

Well practiced at dodging, they slipped right past Liang Shicheng’s groping claws every time. Missing grab after grab, Liang’s temper was just starting to sour when through the swirling steam he spotted a striking beauty standing straight on the pool’s edge—and his lewd grin snapped back wider than ever.

“Since a fresh beauty’s arrived, I’ll let you lot off for now!”

Swimming to the steps, Liang Shicheng climbed out wearing a single soaked silk tunic clinging to his skin, dripping beside his plastered wet hair.

The girls climbed out right after him, standing there drenched and shivering without daring to change.

Revulsion curling her lip, Qianli Xun looked straight away from the repulsive old creep.

“Are you Zhu Shuzhen, the Poetess of Jiangnan?”

*As if the real Zhu Shuzhen ever ran into filth like this!* Stuck wearing her name, though, Qianli Xun answered coolly, “Close enough.”

“Why aren’t you kneeling before this official?”

“I kneel to Heaven, Earth, my parents, wise rulers, and loyal ministers. Which category does Imperial Attendant Liang fit into? If none of the above, spare yourself the breath.”

Stung by her razor-sharp insult, Liang Shicheng nearly flared up—yet admiring her lovely face, he swallowed his irritation for the moment.

“Know why I summoned you here?”

“If you don’t tell me, Lord Attendant, how would I know?”

Stepping closer across the wet tiles, he purred, “Think hard—who have you offended?”

Mengrong flashed across Qianli Xun’s mind, though she refused to name her. “I live upright and aboveboard. I haven’t wronged a soul.”

“Hmph! Offending people without even realizing it only makes you deserve death faster. Someone hired me to put you in the ground.”

“Even with all your power, Lord Attendant, you can’t just murder someone whenever you feel like it.”

“Cooking up an excuse to execute someone takes me less effort than turning over my palm.”

“Oh? And what charge do you plan to pin on me?”

Instead of answering right away, Liang Shicheng changed tack smoothly. “I used to buy that old line about talented women being plain—never guessed you’d boast brilliance *and* a face and figure that make my chest itch just looking at you! Word is you’ve wed twice—first to Lin Jue, now to the Chens. Truth be told, I’ve never tasted what a twice-married woman feels like. Keep me happy one time today, and not only do I spare your life, I send you safely home to Chen Manor.”

Qianli Xun shot back without blinking, “Hmph! I’d drop dead on the spot before entertaining scum like you!”

Choking down anger, Liang Shicheng coaxed, “One afternoon of fun to buy back a whole life—quite the bargain.”

Qianli Xun shoved a fist straight under his nose. “Got the guts to fight me one-on-one? Beat me first, then we’ll talk bargains!”

Liang Shicheng flinched backward as the wind of her punch slapped his face and blew his wet locks straight back.

Catching his balance several paces back, he hissed, “Since you’re begging for a grave, why waste my breath—”

Before the last word finished leaving his tongue, Qianli Xun’s flying kick caught him square and sent him splashing headfirst back into the pool! The soaking girls shrieked and scattered to the far wall.

Breaking the surface sputtering, Liang Shicheng roared, “Guards! Seize her!”

Vaulting light across the water’s edge, Qianli Xun drove a solid fist straight into his face, then landed smooth as a feather on the dry tiles just as the doors burst open and armed imperial guards poured in clutching swords.

Face throbbing where she’d clocked him—not even daring to wipe it with soaked sleeves—Liang Shicheng screeched, “Attempted murder of a court official! Grab her! Haul her back to Bianliang for the Court of Judicial Review—put her under the executioner’s blade!”

Swords clashed against bare fists as brutal combat erupted around the steaming pool.

At the start, Qianli Xun dodged and counterstruck with effortless grace, knocking men down left and right—yet wave after fresh wave of imperial guards kept pouring in nonstop.

Unlike sloppy mountain bandits on Mount Tianmu, these palace troops moved like seasoned killers trained to perfection.

After flooring seven or eight waves straight, exhaustion dragged at her limbs while razor-sharp blades flashed closer from every side. Still she fought on with everything she had.

Standing waist-deep in the pool watching the melee, Liang Shicheng marveled inside—*this poetess actually fights like a demon!*

Half a shichen into the brawl, Qianli Xun’s arms felt like lead and her breaths turned ragged. Seeing her falter, Liang Shicheng crowed in delight, “Take her down!”

One split second of exhaustion cost her: a blade sliced across her back, drawing hot blood. As fiery pain blinded her for a breath, seven or eight sword points locked tight around her throat.

Spent beyond fighting, thinking that if Mengrong wanted her dead so badly she might as well hand her the win today, Qianli Xun shut her eyes. “If you’ve got the spine, kill me right now!”

Climbing back out of the pool with half his face already bruised purple-black, Liang Shicheng offered smugly, “Last chance. Agree to keep me happy, and you walk free.”

Snapping her eyes wide, Qianli Xun spat straight into his face! “Ptui! Not even if I die right here!”

Saliva landed dead across his bruised cheek, stinging the fresh welt like acid. Wincing, Liang scrubbed at it with a drenched sleeve—only making the sting burn worse. Twisted with pain and fury, he roared, “Tie her up! Lock her in a prisoner cart and ship her to Bianliang right now!”

Guards hauled over heavy ropes and lashed her wrists and shoulders tight. Before they dragged her out, Qianli Xun asked evenly, “Why wait? Why not kill me on the spot?”

Nursing his throbbing cheek, Liang Shicheng gave a venomous laugh. “Oh, don’t worry—you’ll die soon enough! As a court official, I need a clean charge and proper procedure before chopping heads. And look what you just handed me: *attempted murder of an imperial official*—straight to the chopping block! Let the Court of Judicial Review stamp your death warrant in Bianliang so your death stays nice and legal!”

Qianli Xun spat straight at the floor. “Bah! Corrupt dog! Doing filthy deeds while still setting up a virtue arch for yourself! Think anyone falls for it? History will remember every rotten crime you ever pulled!”

“Get her locked in that prisoner cart—*now*!” Liang Shicheng shrieked.

Outside the gates, hours ticked past with zero sign of his wife, yet Chen Haoyu refused to leave.

Slumped gloomily by the wall, he jerked his head up at the creak of iron-rimmed wheels.

Out through Liu’an Villa’s side gate rolled a wooden prisoner cart—only unlike ordinary cages, heavy black cloth draped every side from top to bottom, hiding whoever sat inside while Villa guards marched tight around it.

Jumping up, Chen Haoyu bolted straight in front of the escort. “Why hasn’t my wife come out yet?!”

A guard kicked him hard in the stomach, knocking him sprawling. “High-priority convict inside! Get out of the road!”

Sitting bound behind the black drapes, cloth stuffed tight between her jaws, Qianli Xun couldn’t utter a sound—and even if she could, she would sooner vanish silently than watch Chen Haoyu throw his life away fighting armed guards.

Groaning through the sharp ache in his ribs, Chen Haoyu watched the black-draped cart roll slowly out of sight. A wild fear flickered—could *she* be inside? Then he shook his head: prisoner carts hauled heinous killers and traitors; how could his sweet, gentle wife ever be stuffed inside something like that?

Dragging himself back to the front gate, Chen Haoyu sat down again to wait.

Night fell pitch-dark, and still no Shuzhen walked out.

Panic boiling over, Chen Haoyu pounded the heavy gates with both fists. “Let my wife out! Let my wife out…!”

On the fifth roar, the side door finally cracked open.

Lunging forward, Chen Haoyu grabbed the servant’s collar. “Talk! Where is my wife?!”

“The Lord Attendant told me to pass word,” the servant muttered, “your wife tried to assassinate a court official—heinous treason! She’s under Court of Judicial Review jurisdiction now, already on the road to Bianliang. If you want any chance of saving her neck, it’ll take five hundred thousand taels of silver at minimum.”

“Liar!” Chen Haoyu swung a wild fist, knocking the servant straight to the dirt.

“Hitting me won’t help!” the servant yelped from the ground. “Better hurry home and gather silver instead!”

Chen Haoyu bellowed through the open doorway, “Wife! Wife! Where are you?!”

No wife answered—only a squad of armed guards surged forward.

The lead officer barked, “The poetess left Liu’an Villa hours ago! Want her, chase all the way to Bianliang!”

The black-draped prisoner cart flashed back into Chen Haoyu’s mind like lightning. Half out of his wits, he sprinted back to his carriage and screamed at the driver, “Chase north toward Bianliang—fast!”

Horses galloped hard through Lin’an’s dark streets, burst out through the north gate, and pounded north along the highway.

Miles down the dark road, the carriage slowed and rolled to a halt.

Throwing the door wide, Chen Haoyu snapped, “Why are we stopping?!”

The coachman turned anxiously. “Young Master, go much farther and we cross straight out of Lin’an prefecture! Even if you ride to Bianliang after Young Madam, you’ve got to tell the family first and pack properly! Wait—outside Liu’an Villa I heard them say five hundred thousand taels to Imperial Attendant Liang could spare Young Madam’s life! Saving her neck comes first—go back, settle the silver with him tonight, and *then* set out for Bianliang!”

Blinded by panic, Chen Haoyu hadn’t been thinking straight—the coachman’s blunt words snapped sense back into his head. Staring down the black, empty highway with zero trace of any cart ahead, even catching up alone unarmed wouldn’t accomplish a thing. Better race home and buy Liang off with silver first.

“Turn around!”

At his nod, the driver whipped the horses round for home.

Back inside Suxin Thatched Hall, Kuqiao sat lighting one stick of incense after another—counting straight to eight, yet no Miss walked through the door. Understanding at last her mistress had tricked her, she broke into quiet sobs. “Miss… you *have* to come back safe…”

Lighting a ninth stick, she whispered through tears, “Nothing will happen to Miss… she’ll be fine, I know she will…”

Two more sticks burned down into pitch night, still without a shadow of her mistress. Dropping the joss sticks, Kuqiao crumpled onto the floor and wept out loud. “Wuu…!”

Returning from errands outside, Matron Wei heard sobbing, pushed open the door, saw zero trace of her young lady, and found Kuqiao weeping alone on the floorboards.

“Why are you crying like that? Where is Miss?”

Kuqiao raised red, flooded eyes. “Commander Lin took Miss away to see Imperial Attendant Liang, and she still isn’t back! Wuu…!”

“See Imperial Attendant Liang?! Why on earth would Liang want to see her?!”

“Wuu… it’s got to be Princess Consort Yi putting Liang up to hurting Miss! He pressured the Chen family, and Miss went willingly so she wouldn’t drag them down! Wuu… she promised she’d be back after eight sticks of incense, and I’ve burned ten whole sticks already and she still isn’t home! Wuu…!”

Matron Wei slumped onto a chair, face drained of color. “Heaven forbid—something awful must have befallen her! Blame me for going out today of all days!”

Hongdou marched in with the rest of the trained servants right behind her. Stepping forward, Hongdou declared fiercely, “Matron Wei, we talked it over—tonight we’re storming Liu’an Villa to break Miss out! Even if it costs our lives, we don’t care!”

Chouya cried, “Miss treated us like family and taught us martial arts—she’s in trouble, we can’t just sit on our hands!”

Every servant echoed together, “Right! We have to save Miss!”

Seeing such fierce loyalty among the ten servants Qianli Xun trained moved Matron Wei deeply. Yet knowing her mistress would never want anyone throwing their life away for her—and that ten half-trained servants storming a minister’s guards meant walking straight onto a butcher’s block—she kept her head.

“If you truly care about Miss, stay right where you are and don’t waste everything she sacrificed! You aren’t facing mountain thugs tonight—you’re up against a man high enough to whisper one word in His Majesty’s ear and wipe out the entire Chen clan! Even *one* slip from you lot only hands him an excuse to destroy both Miss *and* the Chens!”

Asheng muttered grimly, “Put that way, we didn’t think it through… still, we can’t just sit here doing nothing!”

“Doing nothing *is* helping Miss!” Standing firm, Matron Wei ordered sharply, “Nobody steps one foot outside Chen Manor! Let the Chen masters handle this—I’m going to Eldest Master and Madam right now. Kuqiao, keep watch over them!”

Once Matron Wei hurried out, Amei whispered, “Sister Kuqiao… are we really just sitting still?”

“Listen to Matron Wei,” Kuqiao sniffled. “She worries about Miss every bit as much as we do.”
