# Chapter 30: Street Ballads and a Clash with a Rake

On the first night in the countryside, Qianli Xun slept without a care.

When she opened her eyes, it was barely dawn and the sun had not yet risen, so she decided to sleep a while longer. But sleep only brought another dream—she stood in a wedding dress, exchanging vows with Shen Jianchen as a crowd gathered to bless them.

Just as Shen Jianchen was slipping the ring onto her finger, Qianli Xun jerked awake. She tried to fall back asleep, but no matter what she did, she could not. The beautiful dream was gone just like that.

She sat up, disconsolate, and murmured to herself. *Shen Jianchen, where are you? I want so badly to be in love. Why is it that when I didn't want it, you were always hovering right in front of me—yet now that I do, I can't even catch a glimpse of your shadow?*

Then she thought again. She was already Zhu Shuzhen. Even if she met Shen Jianchen, he would never recognize her as Qianli Xun. And if Shen Jianchen had transmigrated like her, she would likely not know him either. Two people who had been close, who died in another age in an earthquake and woke in this one, yet could never find each other—surely no sorrow in life ran deeper than that.

Kuqiao got up early, first went home to make breakfast for her mistress, and carried it in a bamboo basket to the bamboo house. Hearing the sigh from inside, she knew Miss was awake. She put down the bamboo basket and went into the inner room.

"Miss is awake. Once you've eaten, I'll show you around the village—I promise you'll feel better for it."

Qianli Xun dressed and let Kuqiao simply pin her hair into a bun—no ornaments, no rouge or powder. Only then did she feel she was truly living as the locals did.

After getting ready, she came to the table. Kuqiao had already put away the brush, ink, paper, and inkstone, and set out breakfast—a bowl of soft congee with the faint scent of bamboo, and a plate of dried bamboo shoots stir-fried with pork.

Qianli Xun sat on the soft cushion, looking at that dish of meat, thinking that Kuqiao's family probably went without meat on ordinary days. This dish must have been made specially for her.

"Kuqiao, see that my food money goes to your uncle and aunt. I won't have your family spending on my account."

"Don't worry, Miss. The food money's covered. There's nothing fancy to eat out here in the countryside—I'm just afraid you won't be used to it."

"I'm not picky. I can eat anything."

Qianli Xun looked up toward the door. Before her stretched a sea of green—bamboo stalks standing straight and tall, reaching upward, radiating a quiet vitality. The emptiness the dream had left in her heart eased at the sight. A smile touched her lips, and she picked up her chopsticks to enjoy her breakfast.

She was barely halfway through breakfast when singing drifted over, now and then punctuated by the beat of a drum. The sound was muffled by distance, and she could not make out the words.

"Why is there singing outside?"

"Oh, it's a drum-verse performer. They go from village to village and sing for a bit of coin to get by."

Qianli Xun had heard of drum verses. Her interest piqued, she hurried through the rest of her food and set down her bowl and chopsticks. "Kuqiao, don't fuss with the clearing up yet. Come with me to listen."

Following the sound, they reached the spot—a small open patch at the village entrance where a crowd already sat listening. Kuqiao found a stone and spread her silk handkerchief over it. "Miss, sit here."

Qianli Xun took her seat and studied the performer. He was a blind old man perching on a wooden stool, a drum cradled under his left arm and a wooden mallet held in his right. He wove spoken prose and melodic song into a seamless whole—his spoken storytelling poured out like running water, while his sung verses resonated with a sweet, lyrical cadence. It bore little resemblance to the traditional opera she knew, and even less to modern pop music. To call it a cross between the two felt entirely wrong. It was simply a vocal style—an ancient art of storytelling—the likes of which she had never heard before.

What captivated Qianli Xun most was the sheer simplicity of the music—a single drum whose rhythmic beats laid bare the pure essence of cadence. She listened hard to the verses and realized the blind bard bearing his drum was chanting the folk tale of Cai Yong (courtesy name Bojie) from the Han Dynasty: how he took top honors in the imperial examinations, turned his back on the wife of his youth, cruel and unfeeling, and took the Prime Minister's daughter as his bride.

Qianli Xun chuckled inwardly. *This story absolutely massacres Cai Yong's good name—they've gone and fictionalized him into a Song Dynasty Chen Shimei.*

She had meant to set the record straight for Cai Yong once the old man finished. But then she thought better of it. Drum verses were literature, after all, and literature took liberties with the past all the time. Heaven knew how many real people she had fictionalized in her own web novels—next to those, these verses were small potatoes indeed. So she let the matter drop. Perhaps old Cai Yong, wherever he rested, would even be amused to find his invented exploits entertaining the common folk.

Observing the villagers around her, Qianli Xun saw the rapt attention with which they listened, caught up in outbursts of laughter or sympathetic sighs. She came to appreciate the cultural contrast: while classical poetry was the refined solace of the Song Dynasty literati, the common folk found their true joy in the vivid, accessible rhythm of popular drum-ballads.

Qianli Xun, writing poetry under Zhu Shuzhen's name, occasionally played at elegance—but she knew full well those poems were not hers.

All at once she wanted to write something of her own in this age. The blind singer's performance had lit the spark. *That's right—common folk love this kind of popular literature so much. Why not write stories of my own here, to amuse them and put my own gifts to use?*

Just as she was thinking this, a woman's voice rang out: "Grandpa! Help! The bad men are after me!"

The blind singer stopped performing, and everyone turned toward the sound.

Qianli Xun rose as well. A girl came running toward them—her clothes were patched and mended, yet none of it could dim the loveliness of her face.

The girl ran panting, four or five burly men close behind. She broke through the crowd and threw her arms around the blind old man. "Grandpa! Those brutes are trying to catch me!"

Before anyone could react, the four had already pushed into the crowd.

One of them spoke up. "Hey, blind old man, get your granddaughter to come along with Young Master Chen, and quick. Follow him and she'll eat well and want for nothing—and you can stop dragging yourself around singing for a living at your age."

The old man shielded his granddaughter and answered, heat in his voice, "I earn my keep with my own skill. That's better than selling my granddaughter to you for money that sits heavy on her conscience. You wouldn't dare snatch her in broad daylight—don't think I won't report you to the authorities!"

The onlookers joined in, cursing the thugs. "You've no law in you at all!"

"That old man and his granddaughter are misery itself—have a heart and let them be."

"You bullies only know how to pick on honest folk!"

The four flexed their arms and shook their fists at the crowd, faces twisted into snarls. The villagers fell silent. Some, unwilling to get involved, turned and went home; others shrank back, fearing for their own safety.

Qianli Xun was not afraid. She held her ground, curious to see what these men were really after—but ready to put them flat on their backs the moment they overstepped. With her mistress behind her, Kuqiao's face stayed calm as well.

One of the men leered at them. "Oi, you two—why aren't you backing off? You there, pretty little thing, want to take her place and come with Young Master Chen? He's waiting in the carriage—I'll take you right over."

Qianli Xun curled her lip in disdain. "Yours truly was going to let it slide—but you just had to come knocking for a fight. Don't say I didn't warn you!"

Catching him off guard, Qianli Xun drove a punch straight into his face. His cheek purpled and blood welled at the corner of his mouth; the force of the blow sent him staggering back more than two meters.

He steadied himself, wiped the blood from his mouth with the back of his hand, and barked at the others, "All of you—get her!"

The four closed in around her, certain that four against one would lay the meddler low. They all came at her with their fists.

She leapt—a light, seamless blur of fists and feet. Her combinations came fast and unpredictable, and one by one the four thugs dropped—each struck clean and true before they could answer.

One of them gritted his teeth and lurched back to his feet, throwing everything he had at her. Qianli Xun met him without flinching—a few quick, clean strikes sent the fool reeling, and he hit the ground a second time.

The crowd broke into cheers. "Well fought, mistress! What a thrashing! Never seen the like!"

Qianli Xun watched the four pick themselves up, each clutching a hurt, faces twisted in pain. She laid into them: "This is MY ground. You dare throw your weight around on my turf? You're asking for it! And hear this—the old man and the girl are under my protection now. Lay another hand on that girl and my fists won't be so gentle!"

Realizing they were outmatched, the four muttered among themselves, "Let's fetch Young Master Chen and let him deal with her."

"She's so strong. Young Master Chen definitely can't beat her either."

"We have to tell him even if we can't beat her. Otherwise Young Master Chen will blame us."

"Right. Let him try. Once he knows how strong that woman is, he won't blame us."

Just as the four turned to leave, a voice cut in. "What took the four of you so long to run down one girl? Making me wait like this!"

They turned—and there was Chen Haoyu himself, come in person.

Wincing, they crowded around him. "Young Master Chen, some woman jumped in out of nowhere and claimed this was her turf. She's fierce—look what she did to us!"

Qianli Xun looked closer. The "Young Master Chen" was none other than Chen Haoyu himself. Enemies meeting on a narrow road—what fate!

Chen Haoyu did not recognize Zhu Shuzhen at first, because she was dressed as a maid. Spotting Kuqiao beside her, he smiled. "Why are you everywhere? What is a young lady from a good family doing in a place like this?"

Qianli Xun curled her lip. "I was wondering who this 'Young Master Chen' was—turns out it's you, the one rat dropping that spoils every pot of soup wherever it goes!"

Called a "rat dropping," Chen Haoyu burned with anger—but he knew he stood no chance against Zhu Shuzhen, so he swallowed it.

"Today, out of regard for you, this young master will let the girl go. But don't mistake my mercy for a kindness, girl—follow me and you'd live well enough. Retreat!"

After a few steps, Chen Haoyu looked back. "Zhu Shuzhen—that's twice now. Don't let there be a third time we cross paths. Your turf, is it? We'll see whose it is tomorrow." With that he turned and strode off with his four henchmen, whistling some careless tune.

Qianli Xun truly could not fathom how anyone could be so brazen.

After this incident, the blind drummer couldn't continue performing. His granddaughter supported him as he thanked Qianli Xun. "Thank you for your help, miss. This old man has nothing to repay you with. If you like listening to songs, another day this old man will bring his granddaughter to your door to perform specially for our benefactor."

The blind drummer's granddaughter knelt on the ground and kowtowed. "Thank you, sister, for helping me."

Qianli Xun helped the girl up. "What's your name?"

"My name is Xia Guo."

"Nice name." Qianli Xun looked at Xia Guo. She was really a beauty in the making, and affection stirred in her. "Why not follow me from now on?"

Xia Guo was extremely grateful and was about to kneel again, but Qianli Xun held her. "Don't keep kneeling. I don't like it. Do you have any other family at home?"

"There's also my grandmother and parents."

"That's good. If I take you away, it won't leave the old people without care."

As Qianli Xun spoke, she gave Kuqiao a look. Kuqiao took out one tael of silver and stuffed it into the blind drummer's arms. "Old man, this is from our miss as a reward for you."

The blind drummer felt the silver and burst out, "It's too much! We don't need this much!"

Qianli Xun said, "I'm taking your granddaughter away from you. This money is still too little. Just take it, old man."

The old man replied, gratitude in his voice, "Miss letting Xia Guo stay by your side is finding a place for this child. This old man is extremely grateful. How can I take your silver?"

Kuqiao chimed in from the side. "Old man, don't be polite. If you're too polite, our miss won't take Xia Guo as a maid."

The old man said in a rush, "Alright, alright. I won't be polite."

Qianli Xun had Xia Guo first take the blind drummer home and settle him down before coming to the bamboo house to report.

Unable to continue listening to the songs, the villagers dispersed. The village entrance returned to silence, leaving only Qianli Xun and Kuqiao.

The more the merriment faded into stillness, the more readily a faint emptiness stole into the heart. Qianli Xun had never been in love. Just then she longed to fill that hollow with love. But where was the one who loved her—the one she longed to love? No one answered, and so she turned to other things, if only to dull the emptiness.

"Kuqiao, find me some storybooks."

"What does Miss want them for?"

"Staying in the bamboo house is boring. I'll use them to pass the time."

"Miss never used to read these popular stories."

"You even know the idiom 'lowbrow art'! Truly worthy of being my—" She'd been about to say Qianli Xun, but caught herself. "—truly worthy of being Zhu Shuzhen's maid. But Kuqiao, remember this: high art or low art alike, so long as ordinary people enjoy it, it's a good thing."

"Alright, I'll have Kujue go to the city to buy a few books for Miss."

Qianli Xun sighed. "I can't leave the bamboo forest now. Otherwise, I'd go to the streets myself, either to a teahouse or to the theaters and entertainment quarters, to listen to the art of storytelling in person. Now I can only trouble you two siblings."

"Miss doesn't need to be polite with me."

Qianli Xun walked around the bamboo forest again before returning to the bamboo house.

After settling Miss, Kuqiao went to find Kujue to arrange the book purchase. After arranging it, on the way back, she met Xia Guo asking about Miss's residence. She brought her back along the way and told her how to take care of Miss on ordinary days. Xia Guo committed every word to memory.
