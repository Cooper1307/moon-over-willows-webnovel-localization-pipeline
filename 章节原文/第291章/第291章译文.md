## Chapter 291: Tea Outside the Qiantang Gang

First thing next morning, right after Chen Haoyu called outside the door that he was off to the silk shop, Qianli Xun had Kuqiao summon Hongdou. Confirming the veteran hands could run the congee shed fine alone, she told Hongdou to skip it today and round up all nine courtyard servants—they were paying the Qiantang Gang a visit.

“Two went out early with Matron Wei on errands,” Hongdou reported.

“No matter—eight including you is plenty.”

Moments later Hongdou assembled seven more: Asheng, Amei, Chouya, Sanbao, Jiaxiang, Fulu, and Ayun.

“You usually go with Matron Wei, Ayun—why home today?” Qianli Xun asked.

“Matron said she didn’t need extra hands and left me for Miss to use.”

Qianli Xun nodded. “Today I’m having a long chat with Master Jiang, and fists might fly. The Qiantang Gang has numbers—anybody scared?”

“Not scared!” all eight shouted.

“Good—move out!”

Since taking over the congee shed, she came and went without asking the elders’ leave, sparing endless questions—better not worry them over a brawl anyway. Boarding two carriages with her eight servants, she rolled straight for the Qiantang Gang. She wouldn’t lay hands on Mengrong, yet Master Jiang had crossed her line and owed her a reckoning. Knowing she couldn’t fight and would only get in the way, Kuqiao stayed back tidying the quarters.

Over at the Qiantang Gang, the ambush squad had limped back bruised and empty-handed the day before. Before Master Jiang even spoke, Chun Jinmo had harped in his ear from dusk till dawn, nagging him to send his best killers to finish the poetess off.

Much as Master Jiang doted on his new wife, he didn’t lose his head over big stakes. Killing the Prefect’s daughter meant war with the yamen—bad business—and once he dug up her link to Prince Yi, he wanted zero part of it.

Since marrying Chun Jinmo, Master Jiang had quit rising early, preferring to lounge in bed holding her close.

Chun Jinmo pouted against his chest. “Princess Consort Yi backs Chen Haodong! Wipe out Youqi Jushi, hitch yourself to royalty, and who cares about the Prefect?”

Kissing her cheek, Master Jiang asked, “Tell me—who’s stronger, Prince Yi or Princess Consort Yi?”

“Prince Yi, obviously—yet she’s his wife! If he’s powerful, she is too.”

Master Jiang chuckled patient. “Living in Chunxiang Brothel, you didn’t see how Lin’an’s elite circles work. Now that you’re my wife with status, mingle with respectable ladies—gossip carries real intelligence.”

Shoving him away, Chun Jinmo rolled onto her pillow facing the wall. “Save it! The Qiantang Gang is underworld trash, and I’m ex-brothel—what noble lady would give me the time of day?”

Master Jiang wrapped his arm back around her. “Once I hook onto Prince Yi, nobody will dare look down on you.”

“Hmph! Won’t even lift a finger for Princess Consort Yi and dreaming of Prince Yi? Keep dreaming!”

“Ever hear about Prince Yi raiding Chen Haoyu’s wedding?”

Spinning back around, Chun Jinmo gaped. “Prince Yi raided the wedding—for Youqi Jushi?!”

“Now you get why Princess Consort Yi wants her dead?”

“All the more reason to kill her—stealing a royal’s husband!”

“If I want Prince Yi’s favor, touching a hair on her head is suicide. I asked around: Prince Yi picked the Princess Consort up as a kid, and His Majesty only decreed the match because she tended him while he lay sick. Without that illness she’d never have made Princess Consort—she’s got zero court backing. Hooking onto her gets us nothing.”

“Then why is Eldest Young Master Chen working for her?”

“No idea—just trust me, Youqi Jushi is untouchable! Not only is her dad Prefect, Prince Yi himself is off-limits. Know what happened when Prince Yi heard Chen Haodong paid me to trap Chen Haoyu into marrying her? He stormed into my hall in person—calling himself Young Master Shen—all to stand up for Miss Zhu! Only dug up later that he was Prince Yi. Think: how huge does a woman have to be to get a prince swinging fists for her?”

“Poor Young Master Chen—married a woman a prince won’t let go of.”

“Chen Haoyu’s problem, not ours. Me, I just keep out of Prince Yi’s crosshairs. I already brushed off the Princess Consort—sent weak scrubs who couldn’t touch the poetess so she’ll give up on my gang.” Pulling her close again, he grinned. “Bet a VIP shows up at our door today—”

Right on cue, a subordinate knocked outside. “Chief! Somebody’s at the gate making trouble—calls herself Youqi Jushi!”

“Speak of the VIP!” Master Jiang rolled out of bed. “Get dressed and come greet her—she’s Jiangnan’s top poetess and one of Lin’an’s premier ladies. Getting friendly with her opens every door in high society for you.”

Bitter over her stealing Chen Haoyu, Chun Jinmo bristled, yet couldn’t argue with his logic. Sneering, she got up to see what kind of fuss the poetess planned to kick up.

Skipping breakfast, Master Jiang marched out with only a few men, grinning ear to ear.

Outside the gate, Qianli Xun stood flanked by her servants, braced for an armed mob to pour out—only to blink in bafflement as Master Jiang strolled out beaming like a host welcoming kin.

His first line baffled her even more. “Had breakfast yet, heroine? If not, our kitchen’s got a spread ready—eat with me?”

Snapping out of her daze, Qianli Xun turned stern. “Already ate—and even starving I wouldn’t share a table with a guy who tried to kill me! Be smart: get on your knees, own up, and swear off sending thugs to murder people, and maybe we talk. Otherwise I’m pounding you flat till you beg!”

To her shock, Master Jiang dropped straight to his knees. “I was wrong, heroine! Shouldn’t have sent men after you! I swear I’ll stick to running gambling houses with my boys and quit blood-for-hire for good!”

*Why’s this mob boss folded so fast?*

Rising and stepping close, Master Jiang murmured, “Cut me some slack, heroine—had no choice yesterday, so I picked my sorriest fighters on purpose, or how else would you be standing here today?”

Half her steam deflated. “What’s your angle?”

“Just want to invite you inside for a calm chat.”

Sizing up his broad, dark face—earnest enough, though caution never hurt—she refused. “Skip going inside. Let’s sit in the pavilion right outside your gate.”

“Still don’t trust me—fair enough, your call.” Turning to a henchman, he barked, “Bring my breakfast to the pavilion, grab a jug of rice wine for the heroine, and call Madam out!”

“No alcohol—tea only,” Qianli Xun cut in.

“West Lake Longjing tea, then! After you, heroine.”

Hongdou and the other seven trailed to stand guard outside the pavilion, until Master Jiang dismissed his own thugs after tea and breakfast were set, and Qianli Xun waved her eight back to rest out of the sun inside the carriages.

Pouring her tea, Master Jiang grabbed chopsticks. “Help yourself while I grab a bite.”

“Quit calling me ‘heroine’—sounds weird.”

“Then I’ll call you Youqi Jushi like my wife does.”

Taking a polite sip, Qianli Xun cut straight to it. “What do you want to talk about, Master Jiang?”

Setting down his chopsticks, he came clean. “Word is you’re tight with Prince Yi. Could you put in a good word and introduce me to him?”

*Aha! Now the polite act makes total sense.* “You already work for Princess Consort Yi—getting to Prince Yi through her should be effortless. Why take the long way round?”

Scooping congee, Master Jiang explained. “I’ve seen Prince Yi—sharp, upright man. The dirty work Princess Consort Yi hires me for can’t see daylight; going through her would only make Prince Yi suspicious. Going through Youqi Jushi is clean, no shady deals—plus Prince Yi hangs on your every word.”

Hearing him hint at gossip buzzing behind her back soured her stomach. “If I help you after you put it like that, I just confirm the rumors! I’m happily married into the Chen family. My elders treat me well and grant me freedom—I won’t risk my good name outside. Sorry to disappoint.”

Rejected flat-out, Master Jiang stayed sunny. “No harm done! Even if you won’t make the intro, making friends with you works too—makes talking to Prince Yi easier.”

“My friends are noble ladies and scholars—never ran with gang bosses.”

Turned down twice, he still grinned. “Fair enough—sitting down for a chat today is already a great start.”

Right then Chun Jinmo’s syrupy voice cut in. “Well! Wondered who the VIP was—Youqi Jushi herself!” Having dressed and grabbed a quick bite, she had come out both on Master Jiang’s orders and to dig up Chun Jinlan’s trial results—plus take a fresh dig at her rival.

The second his wife appeared, Master Jiang hopped up and rushed to take her hand. “Watch the steps, Wife.”

Watching the tough gang boss fuss over his wife stirred unexpected warmth in Qianli Xun.

Swaying into the pavilion, Chun Jinmo sat beside her, waving a round fan and eyeing her up and down with disdain. To break the awkward stare, Qianli Xun remarked, “Lucky Madam Jiang—having your husband steady your arm over a single step.”

“Treating one’s wife right is only proper,” Master Jiang beamed. “I look rough, yet I’m tender with my woman.”

Ignoring him, Chun Jinmo sneered, “No need to envy me, Youqi Jushi—turning Young Master Chen into a saint has everyone green with envy.”

“Each person has their own path,” Master Jiang cut in gently. “Quit pining for Chen Haoyu—with him you’d only be a concubine, nowhere near being my primary wife.”

Disgust flashed across Chun Jinmo’s face as she looked away, trapped in silent bitterness.

A pang of pity hit Qianli Xun—even a rough underworld boss turned humble before the woman he loved. “All right, Master Jiang. If I run into Prince Yi, I’ll put in a word for you.”

Overjoyed, Master Jiang leaped up and bowed hands clasped. “Thank you, Youqi Jushi! Pull that off and you’re the savior of the Qiantang Gang!”

Taking another sip, she glanced sideways. “You call me savior—doubt Madam Jiang agrees.”

Sitting back down with an apologetic look at his wife’s turned back, Master Jiang murmured, “Bear with her, Youqi Jushi—newly wed, still adjusting, she’ll come around. Actually... one more tiny favor?”

*Give an inch, take a mile!* Still, moved by his devotion to his wife, she relented. “Spit it out.”

“Youqi Jushi’s fame spans Jiangnan, Prefect’s daughter, married into Lin’an’s richest clan—top tier among Lin’an’s noble ladies. My wife can’t compare, yet since she married me, I want to lift her standing. Could you take her along and ease her into high-society circles?”

Amused—yet sympathetic to a man wanting better for his wife—Qianli Xun offered a gentle reality check. “Your devotion to your wife moves me, Master Jiang. Still, think about why I move in those circles: my father is Prefect of Lin’an, and my in-laws are the wealthiest family in town. Were I an ordinary girl, nobody would let me in either. If you want Madam Jiang accepted, you’ll have to earn that standing yourself.”
