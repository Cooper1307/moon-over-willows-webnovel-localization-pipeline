## Chapter 291: Tea Outside the Qiantang Gang

Next morning, checking that the congee shed was running smoothly on its own, Qianli Xun left Kuqiao behind and rounded up eight of her martial servants—Hongdou, Asheng, Amei, Chouya, Sanbao, Jiaxiang, Fulu, and Ayun (the other two were out on errands with Matron Wei)—and rolled straight to the Qiantang Gang in two carriages. She wouldn't strike Mengrong, yet Master Jiang owed her an explanation!

Meanwhile, lounging in bed with Chun Jinmo—who nagged him nonstop to send real killers after Youqi Jushi—Master Jiang calmly schooled his bride on real Lin'an politics: Princess Consort Yi was an orphan with zero court backing, whereas Prince Yi had personally stormed the Qiantang Gang over Zhu Shuzhen and crashed her wedding! Crossing Prince Yi meant suicide—that was why he'd sent low-tier thugs to deliberately botch Mengrong's hit so she'd quit pestering him!

Just then a runner reported Youqi Jushi waiting outside for a fight. Grinning—"Speak of VIPs!"—Master Jiang dragged Jinmo up to dress and strolled straight out beaming with zero weapons, asking if "Lady Hero" had eaten breakfast yet!

Shocked when he cheerfully dropped to his knees swearing off murder for good—"Sent my weakest boys on purpose so you wouldn't get hurt!"—Qianli Xun stayed wary, turning down entering his compound yet agreeing to sip West Lake Longjing tea at his front pavilion while her eight servants waited in the carriages.

Over tea, Master Jiang laid his cards flat: he wanted Youqi Jushi to introduce him to Prince Yi so he could go legit! Flatly refusing at first—helping him would only fuel filthy gossip about her and Prince Yi—Qianli Xun watched Chun Jinmo sway out, saw rough Master Jiang leap up tenderly guiding his bride down the steps even as Jinmo rolled her eyes at him, and felt an unexpected tug of pity and respect.

"Fine, Master Jiang—next chance I get, I'll put in a word with Prince Yi."

Overjoyed, Master Jiang pushed his luck—begging her to bring his bride into Lin'an's noble ladies' circle—until Qianli Xun gently pointed out that respect came from his own standing, not coat-tailing.
