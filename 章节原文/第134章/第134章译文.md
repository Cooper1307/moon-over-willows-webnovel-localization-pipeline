## Chapter 134: Hunger Strike Fails to Undo the Betrothal

Seeing both elders rise took a tiny weight off Qianli Xun—if they had stayed kneeling, she might really have lost ten years off her lifespan!

Never showing their faces until today, and the second they walked in, they struck straight at her softest spot.

Pointless as asking was, one question burned inside her: "Master Qiao, Wenyuan and I love each other—why tear us apart over false rumors?"

Qiao Shouren spoke blunt: "Even before rumors broke, I opposed the match because you had been through a mutual divorce. Only Prince Yi talked me round into proposing to the Zhu house—then rumors exploded. True or false, Wenyuan can never marry a woman mocked by the whole city."

Prince Yi had stepped up for her and Wenyuan back then too. So no human villain had ruined them—they were fated to meet and never meant to stay.

"Leave now. Tell Wenyuan to meet me beneath the Divine Willow outside the city tomorrow at the hour of wei. You two will have your answer."

Qiao Shouren hated letting his son see Miss Zhu alone, yet had no better option: "See that Miss Zhu keeps the word you gave us today."

Qianli Xun offered only a dry, bitter smile.

Matron Wei snapped, "Our Young Lady spoke plain as day—still don't trust her, Master Qiao? Leave at once! You're not welcome at Wangyue Pavilion; don't show up here again!"

Driven out by Matron Wei's sharp tongue, Qiao Shouren and Madam Qian had no face to linger and left.

Every drop of strength drained from Qianli Xun; she sank limp into her chair without a word.

Heart aching for her, Matron Wei urged, "Go lie down in your room, Young Lady—a bed beats a hard chair."

Supported by both maids, Qianli Xun summoned the last of her strength to walk back to her chamber, collapse onto bed, and pull the quilt over her head...

After the Miao betrothal freed him from lockup, Qiao Wenxian could not face Shuzhen until he broke off the Miao engagement, so he had waged war demanding to annul it.

Yet neither argument nor pleading moved his elders; when Father threatened suicide again, he fell quiet—locking himself in his own room without needing Father's padlock.

With the spring wedding to Miao Zhiyu looming closer as the winter chill deepened, desperation drove him to a hunger strike.

Truth be told, it was half theater for his parents—Nan Shuang slipped him snacks on the quiet so his health would not collapse, remembering Shuzhen's plea to take care of himself so she would not see a wreck once he won.

Still, stolen snacks could not replace hot meals; he dropped another size thinner and played up his frailty in front of his parents until tears streamed down their faces.

Guilt tugged at him watching them weep, yet for a life with Shuzhen, he steeled himself and refused to budge.

Even pushed this far, Father would not yield—how could they break a sealed betrothal and ruin Miss Miao's reputation?—ordering him to forget Miss Zhu.

So Qiao Wenxian lay weak and resolute: "Unless Shuzhen tells me to my face she's giving up, I won't stop."

And that had sent Qiao Shouren and Madam Qian trudging through snow to Wangyue Pavilion.

Back at Qiao Manor, Qiao Shouren and Madam Qian entered their son's courtyard, stepped inside, and handed their snowy capes to waiting maids.

Entering the inner bedchamber and eyeing their gaunt, listless son sprawled on the bed, Qiao Shouren sighed: "Look at the state of you—was it worth it? Miss Zhu looked livelier than you by a mile!"

At Shuzhen's name, life sparked in Qiao Wenxian as he propped himself up: "Father saw Shuzhen?!"

Before Qiao Shouren spoke, Madam Qian sank tearful onto the bedside: "Your father and I saw her. Not a trace of sadness on her—it's clear she doesn't care about you at all! She sent word for you to meet her beneath the Divine Willow tomorrow at the hour of wei."

Qiao Wenxian bolted upright: "Shuzhen wants to see me?! Did Father and Mother agree to cancel the Miao betrothal?!"

Qiao Shouren cut cold: "Breaking the betrothal is out of the question! We're letting you see her because we saw straight through her—she has no feelings left for you! Go hear it from her own lips and snap out of it."

Alarm flashed across Qiao Wenxian: "What did you say to Shuzhen?!"

Madam Qian lied smooth: "With our standing, would we ever bully her? Seeing how devoted you are, we even offered—for love's sake—to let her enter as your concubine! Yet she refused flat, said everything between you two is finished, and called you out tomorrow to make a clean break..."

"No! How dare you ask her to be a concubine?! Shuzhen only spoke cold out of anger—I'm going to find her right now!" Pushing past Madam Qian, Qiao Wenxian swung out of bed, caught himself half-dressed, and barked, "Nan Shuang, dress me—bring my red robe! I can't let Shuzhen see me looking like a ghost..."

Just as Nan Shuang reached for the robe, Qiao Shouren thundered: "Enough! How long will this circus drag on?! Stop! Are you weaker than a woman?! Miss Zhu sat cool as ice, while here you are starving yourself, demanding annulments, fretting she'll cry seeing you pale—are you even a son of the Qiao house, a scholar admired across Lin'an?!"

His father's lash combined with days of half-starvation made Qiao Wenxian sway, near collapse.

Nan Shuang jumped in to catch him and ease him into a chair.

Madam Qian sobbed: "My boy! She isn't worth wrecking yourself over! Waaah!"

Face blank as stone, Qiao Wenxian murmured chill: "You two forced me to this."

Qiao Shouren ordered, "Quit crying and come with me—leave him alone to think straight."

Dabbing her eyes, Madam Qian walked out.

Reaching the outer room, Qiao Shouren paused, stepped back in, and softened his voice: "The Miao wedding stands. Miss Zhu has already moved on, so act like a man. Still, your mother and I will take one step back: if you can talk her into being your concubine tomorrow, both wife and concubine enter Qiao Manor together come spring."

With that, Qiao Shouren threw on his cape and strode out into the snow, Madam Qian trailing behind.

Snow fell thicker outside, blanketing the stones where Qiao Shouren and Madam Qian left crisp footprints.

Beyond the courtyard gate, Madam Qian wiped tears against the swirling flakes and asked, "Husband, would you really let Miss Zhu enter as Wenyuan's concubine?"

Qiao Shouren stopped dead: "Her, Wenyuan's concubine? In her next life! With a reputation that filthy?!"

Puzzled, Madam Qian asked, "Then why tell him that?"

Qiao Shouren smiled sly: "First, toss Wenyuan a scrap of hope so he doesn't collapse or duck tomorrow's meeting out of fear. Second, from what I saw of Miss Zhu today, she'd die before accepting concubine—safe as houses making that promise!"

"Nobody outsmarts you, Husband—just breaks my heart for Wenyuan." Fresh tears brimmed in Madam Qian's eyes.

Inside, aching for his master, Nan Shuang urged, "Let me fetch food from the kitchen, Young Master—get some strength back before seeing Youqi Jushi tomorrow."

Not a day had passed without Qiao Wenxian yearning for Shuzhen; he had held off visiting only hoping to fix everything first, then rush to her beaming, "Shuzhen, let's get married!"

Now he had to face her with nothing fixed—knowing tomorrow's meeting might end everything between them.

Equal parts desperate to see her and terrified to see her, he let out a bitter laugh at himself: "Hmph—engaged to another woman, what right do you have asking her to wait? If she doesn't quit now, when would she? Expect her to grow old alone waiting on you?"

Not catching his master's muttering, Nan Shuang asked again, "What do you feel like eating, Young Master? I'll tell the kitchen."

Dropping the hunger strike—he could not look like a corpse facing Shuzhen tomorrow—Qiao Wenxian waved a hand: "Whatever's ready."

Nan Shuang lit up: "Right away! I'll run to the kitchen!"

Stepping into the yard, Nan Shuang bumped right into Concubine Mi arriving beneath an umbrella held by one maid while another carried a food carrier.

Concubine Mi chided him: "Where are you racing off to?"

Nan Shuang answered fast: "Concubine Mi! Young Master wants food—I was running to the kitchen."

"Save your legs—didn't you see my maid carrying a food carrier?"

Stepping indoors, Concubine Mi had the umbrella-holding maid fold the umbrella by the doorway and take her snowy cape.

Left with nothing to fetch, Nan Shuang trailed right back indoors to wait on his master.

Entering the inner chamber and seeing her worn-out son, Concubine Mi's eyes reddened at once: "Quit torturing yourself, Wenyuan. Mama stewed bird's-nest porridge to build your strength back."

Lifting his eyes to the woman who gave him birth, Qiao Wenxian asked, "Mama, you carried me ten months—you ought to want me happy. Why are even you against me marrying Shuzhen?"

"Silly boy, it's because I gave birth to you that I want the best for you—and that means never letting you marry a divorced woman with a ruined name!"

"Only Shuzhen is the one I want to marry."

"You're too young to grasp what marriage really is. Listen to your elders—we won't steer you wrong."

"Young? I've traveled wide, seen Bianliang, attended His Majesty's banquets—what haven't I seen? Don't treat me like a child."

"See the whole world and you're still green until you've been wed. Why is Miss Zhu twice as coolheaded as you? Because she's been married and divorced once—she knows what marriage really takes."

Concubine Mi's remark pierced straight through Qiao Wenxian—could Shuzhen's love never have burned as fierce as he believed?

"Enough brooding. Eat up, make a clean break with Miss Zhu tomorrow, and come home to live a good life."

Another twist of the knife inside him: "Fine, I'll eat. Maybe a full stomach hurts less."

Setting the steaming bird's-nest porridge on the table, the maid stepped back while Nan Shuang helped his master to a chair.

Lifting his spoon, memories of sweet days with Shuzhen rose up, bringing a faint smile to his lips. He refused to believe Shuzhen had stopped loving him—his parents had to be lying! Tomorrow, face to face, he would know the truth.
