## Chapter 303: Her First Praise for His Tea

Shao Zhengye had paid redemption copper to spare Peng Jingxin's life and visited him with wine every year since. When Yueting bore a son, furious Shao Zhengye carried the newborn all the way to the Jin capital of Huining, tossed the infant safely into palace guards' arms atop Wanyan Wuqimai's manor roof—"Tell him to stay away from Han girls!"—cut down two intercepting guards, and carried a severed pinky back to lie that father and child were both dead, driving grieving Yueting to shave her head as a mountain nun.

Twenty years later, that baby—now **Wanyan Chi**—had marched into Lin'an seeking his birth mother using a fake revenge story! Unwilling to mix with hostile Jin royalty or shatter his sister's quiet vows, Shao Zhengye had driven him off—confirming Wanyan Chi fought using Shen Jianchen's own *Shen Fist* (meaning the martial art might trace six hundred years back to Jin roots!) and had sworn he'd trade his own life just for one glimpse of his mother. Respecting family honor, Shen Jianchen set the case aside and drank the two jars dry with father and son.

Over at Chen Manor, freshly graduated Chen Haoyu marched straight to Suxin Thatched Hall's tea table, demanding brand-new utensils, a fresh whisk, and freshly ground tea powder—prompting pouting Kuqiao to sulk while Qianli Xun laughed watching his unexpectedly deft, focused hands.

Beating thick white foam, Haoyu dipped a wooden scoop into darker tea paste and executed authentic *chabaixi* tea-foam art: drawing the Divine Willow—and Qianli Xun's slender back beneath it! Taking a slow sip, Qianli Xun praised its rich, mellow smoothness and high fragrance—thrilling Haoyu into claiming exclusive morning-and-evening tea duty from now on!

Then he asked to read her poetry so he could understand her heart. Handing him a volume of Zhu Shuzhen's verse, she added shyly that listening to Master Qianli Xun's stories would help him know her even better.

Back in Boya Study that night, Haoyu opened the book straight to the poem she'd quoted on their return-visit carriage ride:

*"Gulls and mandarin ducks swim in the same pond, / Everyone knows they don't belong together. / The Lord of the East has failed as master of flowers, / Why must we be intertwined branches all our lives?"* [TN: Translated by Ronald Egan]

*We weren't on the same path before,* Haoyu vowed softly, *yet from now on I'll walk straight down yours.* Telling yawning Qinggua he was joining him in the counting house tomorrow (and brushing off Qinggua's hunch that Haoyu would run the Chen empire better than Brother Dong), Haoyu turned in.

Deep in the dead of night, seven or eight dark silhouettes scaled Chen Manor's walls, slid long bamboo tubes through every window—masters' halls and servants' rooms alike—and blew thick sleeping smoke until the entire household dropped unconscious, before unbolting the main gates for a flood of masked raiders.
