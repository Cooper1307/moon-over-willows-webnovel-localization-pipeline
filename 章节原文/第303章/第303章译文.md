## Chapter 303: Praise for His First Bowl of Tea

Outraged clan elders had dragged Peng Jingxin to the Judicial Office, where Song law mandated hanging. Shao Zhengye put up the silver for Peng’s family to commute it to life imprisonment—visiting him yearly with fine wine and bribing the jailers so Peng lived comfortably behind bars.

“Then those Jin fighters who attacked your escort hall earlier...?” Shen Jianchen asked.

Trusting the prince as a true friend, Shao Zhengye held nothing back. When Shao Yueting bore a boy, refusing to keep a Jin bastard, Shao Zhengye snatched the newborn and rode nonstop to the Jin capital of Huining. Half hoping the rough road would kill the infant—yet buying goat’s milk when the tough little tyke survived—he left him in an open inn room on a grim wager: if anyone stole the baby, he’d assassinate Wanyan Wuqimai; if not, Wuqimai lived. Finding the infant resting peacefully after locating Wuqimai’s princely palace, Shao Zhengye crept over the palace wall at dusk, thrust the baby straight into patrolling guards’ arms yelling “Wanyan Wuqimai’s bastard—keep him off Han girls!”, cut down two pursuing guards, and sliced off one’s pinky finger.

Back in Lin’an, he lied to his sister that the baby died on the road and Wuqimai lay dead by his blade. Heartbroken, Shao Yueting shaved her head and entered a mountain nunnery.

Twenty years later, that baby—now grown into Wanyan Chi—came to Lin’an posing as a grieving son hunting his father’s killers, tracked down Shao Escort Agency, and came demanding his birth mother, sparking the brawl when Shao Zhengye refused to yield.

“A son seeking his mother is human nature,” Shen Jianchen remarked.

“With Song-Jin tensions razor-thin, the Shaos can’t touch Jin royalty! Besides, my sister’s lived in peaceful seclusion twenty years—I won’t let him shatter her peace! He swore he’d even trade his life for one look at her, yet I’ll never let him see her—never!”

Remembering Shao Zhengye’s earlier remark about similar martial styles, Shen Jianchen asked, “The Jin fighter using moves like mine—that was Wanyan Chi?”

“Yes.”

*So Shen Fist traces back six hundred years—maybe born in Jurchen lands!* Settling the Peng Jingxin mystery in his mind, Shen Jianchen drank both jugs dry with father and son trading martial tales.

Over at Moxiang Courtyard, Chen Haoyu graduated from his tea lessons at Yongtai Teahouse and marched straight to the tea table—rejecting the old utensils, rejecting the worn tea whisk, and ordering Kuqiao to dump all her tea powder!

“Haven’t even made a cup and already acting picky!” Kuqiao griped.

“Great tea starts in the details—proves you’ve been slapping sloppy tea together for my wife!”

“Hear that, Miss?! Years of careful whisking and he calls it sloppy!”

Putting away her huaben manuscript—no longer hiding it since Chen Haoyu respectfully kept clear of her desk—Qianli Xun smiled. “I know how good you are to me; ignore him.”

Pouting, Kuqiao dumped the powder into her own jar next door in Yuening Room, cleaned the caddy, and returned to see her miss smiling beside the tea table watching Young Master grind fresh powder—slipping quietly inside to leave them be.

“Never thought you’d have the patience for fine work like this,” Qianli Xun teased.

“For my wife, no work is too fine.”

A sudden rush of real warmth made her wonder if she was actually falling for him. Watching his crisp, graceful movements as he whisked water into creamy foam—smoother than Kuqiao’s even on his first try—she leaned closer in delight as he dipped the wooden spoon handle into thick tea paste to paint across the white froth.

“You really can do tea-foam painting!”

“Chabaixi—anything to make my wife smile.”

Slowly a willow took shape—the Divine Willow! “You’ve never been there with me—how’d you draw it?”

“Heard servants say you visit it often, so I rode out twice myself to memorize every branch.” Adding a slender female silhouette beneath the boughs, he looked up hopeful. “That’s you. Close?”

“Seven parts likeness—too pretty to drink! What do I do?”

“Drink it—that’s what I made it for!”

Taking a proper sip, Qianli Xun’s eyes widened. “Rich and mellow with zero bitterness, pure aroma—true mastery!”

Praise lit Chen Haoyu brighter than any praise at the silk shop. Stepping out, Kuqiao pretended to sulk—“All tea duty goes to Young Master from now on!”—before strolling out to chat with the other servants.

“Morning bowl and evening bowl, every day?” Chen Haoyu beamed.

“After tasting yours, I’m spoiled—deal!”

Walking after her toward the desk, thrilled he’d mastered a skill she couldn’t do without, he ventured, “Wife, I want to read your poetry.”

Spinning around in surprise, she studied his face. “Pinch me—am I dreaming?”

“Dead serious. They say poetry mirrors the heart—I want to understand what’s inside yours.”

Touched to the core, she picked a bound volume off the desk and handed it over. “Here—everything Zhu Shuzhen wrote.”

“Funny how you say that like you’re not Zhu Shuzhen.”

Caught half off guard, she added, “One more way to understand me—listen to Scholar Qianli Xun’s storybooks.”

“Love storytelling! Only how do his tales show me your heart?”

“I love his stories—means we think alike! Don’t ask why, just listen alongside the poems!”

“Whatever my wife says!”

Beaming back to Boya Study, Chen Haoyu opened the poetry collection—flipping right to a familiar verse and murmuring aloud: “Gulls and mandarin ducks swim in the same pond, everyone knows they don’t belong together. The Lord of the East has failed as master of flowers, why must we be intertwined branches all our lives?” [TN: Translated by Ronald Egan]

The poem she had recited in the carriage on their post-wedding visit—saying they walked different worlds. “Maybe we weren’t on the same road before,” he vowed softly, “yet from now on I’ll walk right onto yours.”

Qinggua wandered in assuming he was studying ledgers, teasing him for turning scholarly, griping when Haoyu complained about being neglected for shop clerking and Chouya, and lighting up when Haoyu ordered him into the accounting room alongside him tomorrow.

“Keep learning and maybe when Young Master runs the Chen house I’ll make steward!”

“Hush—only Brother Dong has the chops to run Chen Manor!”

“Dunno, Young Master—feels way safer in your hands,” Qinggua whispered before yawning him off to bed.

Deep into the dead of night, as crickets chirped over sleeping Chen Manor, seven or eight dark shadows vaulted the outer wall. Knowing the layout cold, they slipped long bamboo tubes through every window—even the servants’ quarters—blowing thick knockout smoke through every room until the entire household dropped senseless, then unbarred the front gate to let a horde of masked bandits pour inside.
