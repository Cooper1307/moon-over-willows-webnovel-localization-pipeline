## Chapter 182: A Teahouse Meeting at the Hour of the Rooster

With Bai Feng busy getting acquainted with the piglet and plotting how to train it to climb trees, Shen Jianchen went to Yongtai Teahouse alone.

Stepping inside for the very first time, he saw how different it was from ordinary tea shops: refined antique decor, walls hung with calligraphy and painting, and lovely tea mistresses whisking tea with flowing grace. Patrons sipped tea while admiring scrolls, watched tea performances, or spoke in hushed clusters—every face looking scholarly and cultivated.

A waiter stepped up. "Are you taking tea alone, sir, or meeting someone?"

"Youqi Jushi," Shen Jianchen answered.

"Right upstairs to the second-floor private room, sir—the plaque reads *Could Such Feeling Wait to Become a Memory*."

Climbing the stairs, Shen Jianchen found the plaque and pushed the door open. The woman on his mind sat waiting at the table; spotting him, she rose and curtsied. "Greetings, Your Highness Prince Yi."

"You know who I am—why act so distant?" Shen Jianchen frowned.

Hardly had the words left his lips when Qianli Xun shot a fist straight at him. Shen Jianchen caught it on reflex. After trading several crisp exchanges, he blocked her wrist. "Can we talk like normal people instead of swinging fists the second we meet?"

"I am giving Mengrong's brother a lesson on her behalf!" Qianli Xun threw another punch.

Blocking two more blows, Shen Jianchen glanced at Kuqiao standing beside them. "What are you standing there like a post for? Your mistress and I need to talk—step outside!"

Curtsying, Kuqiao stepped out and pulled the door shut. She did not worry over their sparring: Miss was a strong fighter, and Prince Yi loved her too much to hurt a hair on her head.

When Qianli Xun raised her fists again, Shen Jianchen stayed her. "Call a truce. Let us sit down and talk this out."

Lowering her hands, Qianli Xun returned to the table. Like it or not, he wore Prince Yi's rank, so she left the seat of honor open. Watching him swagger straight into the host's seat drew a half-scoff, half-chuckle from her. "Sure know how to put on princely airs."

Sitting down and meeting her eyes, Shen Jianchen smiled back. "You strut around playing the great literary talent—cannot I put on a few princely airs?"

Something in his gaze pulled Qianli Xun back to the earthquake: him charging through falling rubble straight toward her without a thought for his own life, the exact second she grasped how deep this man's love ran—deep enough to lay down his life. Her nose stung, and moisture pooled in her eyes.

"What is wrong? Why do you look ready to cry?" Shen Jianchen asked in surprise.

Forcing a bright smile to beat back the tears, she murmured, "During the earthquake, running straight toward me through all that danger—how could you be that dumb, getting yourself..."

"Turned out great, did it not? We landed in the same dynasty and found each other again. Proof you can never slip out of my palm."

Unwilling to bark him down like before, Qianli Xun spoke in earnest. "Have you ever considered that fate only wrote us down as friends?"

"In my heart, you will only ever be mine."

"Hmph. Full of yourself."

"Not full of myself—dead serious."

His steady gaze stirred her chest; forcing thoughts of Mengrong back to the front, she steadied her voice. "I called you here today to make my heart plain. Before we transmigrated, I kept turning you down, and only when the earthquake hit did I regret never accepting you..."

"About time you saw how good I am."

"When I first arrived in this world, I missed you every single day, thinking I would never see you again. Starved for love, I met Wenyuan—so gentle, so wonderful—and fell for him before I knew it. Then you showed up with a betrothed little sister by your side. Then the Zhu and Chen families sealed my engagement, and ONLY THEN did you tell me you were Shen Jianchen..."

"What is the point of rehearsing all that?" Shen Jianchen cut in. "I already know every word."

"The point is for you to see how many walls block us from ever being together! Do you know what that proves?"

Afraid of the answer, Shen Jianchen stayed mute.

"It proves we were never meant to end up together!" she answered herself. "Kill the hope right now and stop chasing illusions. If I could move on and love Wenyuan, why can you not love Mengrong?"

Cherry-picking only the pieces he wanted to hear, Shen Jianchen smiled. "Now I understand your heart at last! You DID fall for me—you only turned to Wenyuan thinking you would never find me again! And now you tell me to let go only because of our two betrothals—right?"

Qianli Xun almost had to hand it to him: grinning through impossible odds like that stripped away all her armor, softening her voice despite herself. "Without Mengrong—and without the lingering ache for Wenyuan—I would have said yes to you. You are the very first man willing to trade his life for mine, and my heart truly moved for you. Yet the walls between us cannot be crossed. Let it go, and let us be friends for life."

Unused to hearing her speak so gently, Shen Jianchen felt his heart melt soft. "Who says they cannot be crossed? Just say you will be with me, and leave the rest to me."

After all that breath, he had not budged an inch! Hardening her tone again, Qianli Xun snapped, "I told you before—when pigs climb trees, I will marry you! Did you forget?!"

Unable to keep his secret in, Shen Jianchen let it slip. "Not a word! I already had Bai Feng buy a piglet and start training it to climb trees. Once it makes it up, no backing out!"

"Are you sick in the head?! Could you not tell I was brushing you off?!"

"I do not care—I am stripping away every excuse you have to say no."

Just then, the door swung open and a tea mistress stepped in. "Youqi Jushi, I am here to prepare tea for you and this young gentleman."

Their bickering paused at once. "Much obliged, Tea Mistress."

Behind her entered a male qin player. Taking seats on the floor—she by the tea stand, he behind his zither—they began in unison: the crisp notes of the qin wove around the graceful sweep of her wrists as she prepared the tea. Dropping their standoff, both Qianli Xun and Shen Jianchen surrendered to the music and art before them.

Kuqiao slipped inside to stand behind her mistress, eager to steal pointers from a master. Every step matched Kuqiao's own routine—grinding tea powder, spooning it in, pouring hot water, whisking and whisking again—yet the tea mistress turned every motion into pure poetry, topping it off by painting a delicate scene across the creamy froth, a feat Kuqiao knew she could never match.

Right on the final chord of the qin, the tea was complete. An attendant set the two bowls before the guests; tea mistress and musician bowed and withdrew, and tactful Kuqiao stepped right back outside and closed the door.

"Sharp maid you have got there," Shen Jianchen remarked.

Lifting her bowl, Qianli Xun took a sip without hesitating over spoiling the tea painting like she had on her first visit. "Look who her mistress is."

Glancing down at his own bowl—where the foam depicted a faint range of distant hills—Shen Jianchen paid the artwork little mind and lifted his eyes. "I know you came today to lecture me for Mengrong's sake. My mind is locked in: nothing you say will sway me."

A tremor ran through Qianli Xun. What girl on earth could stay untouched when a prince pursued her with everything he had—especially when that prince was Shen Jianchen? Taking another sip to hide her face, she realized a truth she had been dodging: since learning Prince Yi was Shen Jianchen, he had crept back into her thoughts day after day. At first she had blamed rebound heartbreak over Wenyuan; now she saw that as Wenyuan faded, she had fallen for Shen Jianchen all over again. Was she fickle? No—Wenyuan was already married; she had every right to love again. Could she swallow her pride and be his concubine? In the face of love, part of her actually wavered—until Mengrong's tear-streaked face snapped her cold sober. If Mengrong ever discovered her dearest friend had stolen her brother, she would never forgive her, and Qianli Xun could never forgive herself either. Sharing a husband with her best friend and watching sisterhood rot into venom was a nightmare she refused to live.

Draining the bowl in one gulp, she set it down hard. "I have said my piece. Listen or not, your call—do whatever you please, I am done meddling, and I will never play along." Rising to the doorway, she glanced back with a dry scoff. "Heh—getting a pig up a tree? Wait for your next life! No, not even then!"

Pushing the door open, she walked straight out without looking back.

Far from angry, Shen Jianchen knew Mengrong was the sole wall holding Qianli Xun back. While Mengrong stood between them, they had no path forward. Lifting his tea bowl like a cup of hard liquor, he swallowed the distant hills in one big gulp.

Down on the street, the servant Xilan had bribed had trailed Prince Yi's carriage to West Lake and shadowed him on foot to Yongtai Teahouse. Hiding across the lane to watch the entrance, he saw Youqi Jushi exit with her maid about an hour later—followed moments after by Prince Yi himself.

"Wait a second," the servant muttered under his breath. "Was Prince Yi meeting Youqi Jushi in there? Oh boy, what a fireworks show!" Waiting until twilight fell and Prince Yi had ridden off, he hopped back into his carriage and hurried to Youlin Retreat.

Inside his own rolling carriage, Shen Jianchen wrestled with how to untangle his betrothal to Mengrong. Regretting telling her last night that he would still marry her, he decided to go back, convince her to accept he was not Zhao Pu and let go of marrying him, and then ride to Bianliang to ask His Majesty face-to-face to rescind the marriage decree.
