## Chapter 202: A Smooth Property Transfer

After Qianli Xun finished serving tea to the elders and withdrew, the older generation chatted a while longer before going their separate ways.

Before long, Butler Wang came to report to Chen Jianglin. “Master, a tricky matter has come up, and I need instructions from you and Madam.”

Chen Jianglin had just set out the Go stones, not yet starting his match against Yao Qingyan. “What is it?”

Butler Wang gave it to him straight. “Young Madam wants to dismiss every single maid in Second Young Master’s courtyard and replace them with her own people. Yesterday I couldn’t well turn her down to her face—she only just married into the family. Thinking it over afterward, I realized I had to ask Master and Madam first.”

Yao Qingyan plucked a stone and set it at random on the board. “Those maids in Haoyu’s courtyard have been an eyesore to me for ages—who even knows where he dug them up? I’ve wanted him to send them packing for a long time. Better to take this chance and clear them all out.”

Chen Jianglin placed a stone of his own. “My wife is right. Since Shuzhen wants it done, do as she says.”

Yao Qingyan nodded and asked the butler, “How did Haoyu take it?”

“Second Young Master was none too pleased, of course,” Butler Wang replied, “yet he didn’t seem able to do a thing about Young Madam, so he agreed in the end.”

Chen Jianglin laughed. “Looks like we picked the right daughter-in-law after all. Just follow Shuzhen’s orders, Butler Wang.”

Butler Wang assented, then hesitated. “Master, Madam, there is one more awkward problem to report.”

Chen Jianglin spoke without lifting his eyes from the board. “Go on.”

“Half the maids from Second Young Master’s quarters refuse to leave Chen Manor and will have to be reassigned to other courtyards. I wasn’t sure where to put them. On my way over I ran into Second Master and Third Master and mentioned it in passing, and both were eager to take them. Then I ran into Second Madam and Third Madam, who flat-out refused, saying the maids from Second Young Master’s courtyard look far too seductive and can’t be kept.”

Yao Qingyan paused with a stone between her fingers, then snapped it onto the board. “If you ask me, dismiss the whole lot of them before they stir up trouble in the other courtyards.”

Chen Jianglin agreed without hesitation. “Dismiss them all.”

“Yes, Master.” Butler Wang bowed and withdrew.

Once the butler left, the couple went back to their game. Winning or losing mattered little; what mattered was each other’s company.

Noticing Chen Jianglin letting her have the upper hand again, Yao Qingyan put on a pout. “You let me win every time we play. How boring.”

“Not at all,” he said. “I find watching my wife play delightful—most of all when you win and your whole face lights up.”

Yao Qingyan’s thoughts turned back to their son. “Earlier, we thought about dismissing Haoyu’s maids ourselves, only to drop the idea for fear the Old Madam would step in to spoil her grandson. If the Old Madam finds out Shuzhen cleared out every last maid in his courtyard, do you think she’ll make a fuss?”

“Wait and see,” Chen Jianglin said. “We’ll take it one step at a time.”

They played on until Yao Qingyan took the match, then strolled out into the garden.

Chen Manor’s garden boasted rows of prized flowers and shrubs, bursting with fresh spring vigor. Once their feet grew tired, the two rested inside a pavilion.

They had barely sat down when Steward Fang, who kept the accounts for the Chen businesses, hurried up to report. “Master, first thing this morning, Young Madam’s Matron Wei came over demanding that every property under Second Young Master’s name be transferred into Young Madam’s name. This is too big for me to decide on my own, so I told Matron Wei I had to ask Master before making any move.”

Transferring Chen family assets was a far cry from dismissing a handful of maids. Unable to decide on the spot, Chen Jianglin and Yao Qingyan knew they needed the full story before weighing their answer. They told Steward Fang to hold off for now.

Their mood for strolling gone, Yao Qingyan sent her personal maid Yingxue to summon Matron Wei to her quarters for a talk.

The couple had no sooner returned to their room and taken their seats than Yingxue stepped in to report. “Master, Madam, Matron Wei has arrived and is waiting outside.”

“Invite Matron Wei in at once,” Yao Qingyan said.

Yingxue stepped back out to bring her in. The moment Matron Wei crossed the threshold, Yao Qingyan rose to greet her and took her by the hand. “Now that you’re living in Chen Manor, Matron, you must drop by my quarters often.”

Even with her hand clasped, Matron Wei did not skip proper etiquette. “My greetings to Master and Madam.”

“No need for ceremony, Matron Wei,” Chen Jianglin said. “Please take the upper seat.”

Matron Wei declined with polite poise. “I am a servant—how could I ever take a seat of honor?”

“Don’t talk like that,” Yao Qingyan insisted. “We’ve heard all about your standing in the Zhu household. What noble young lady in Lin’an doesn’t envy Miss Zhu for having someone as capable as you by her side?”

Yao Qingyan tried to draw her toward the upper seat, yet Matron Wei stood firm and chose a lower chair instead. Yao Qingyan stopped pressing.

Once seated, Matron Wei spoke with modesty. “Everyone flatters me with an empty reputation. All I ask is to stay by my young lady’s side and handle the mundane chores so she has time to compose poems and ci lyrics without wasting the talent Heaven gave her.”

Chen Jianglin smiled. “All of Jiangnan knows Youqi Jushi as a renowned poetess, yet few realize half that fame belongs to our daughter-in-law’s gift and the other half to Matron Wei.”

Matron Wei chuckled. “Now that is something I love to hear, Master. I’m a plain woman who can’t write a line of verse, yet I love watching my miss write. Every time people praise her poetry, I feel as proud as if I had written it myself.”

The three shared a warm laugh, the air in the room light and cordial.

Matron Wei guessed her hosts had called her in over Young Master’s properties. Since they felt awkward broaching it first, she cut straight to the point. “Master and Madam must have something important to discuss with me. If I’m not mistaken, it concerns transferring Young Master’s assets to my young lady?”

“A sharp woman indeed,” Chen Jianglin praised. “Yes, that’s it—not that it’s any great crisis. It just came out of the blue, and Qingyan and I don’t know the full background, so we hoped to hear the ins and outs from you.”

Matron Wei pulled a folded sheet of paper from her sleeve and opened it. Yingxue took it and passed it to Madam.

Yao Qingyan read it through, let out a sigh, and handed it to Chen Jianglin.

Chen Jianglin read every word, his brows knitting tight, and sighed too. “So Miss Zhu only agreed to marry into the Chen family because of what’s written on this sheet.”

“Don’t feel disheartened, Master and Madam,” Matron Wei said. “Marriages are arranged by Heaven. Whatever the reason, my miss has married into the Chen household, and that makes her one of the Chens.”

Yao Qingyan nodded and asked, “Did the two of them consummate the marriage last night?”

“Both my miss and Young Master opposed this match at first,” Matron Wei answered, “yet Heaven brought them together anyway, and here they are, wed. Don’t rush them, Master and Madam. Give the two of them time. We can’t foresee everything that lies ahead, yet I’m certain it will all turn out for the good.”

Her answer made it clear they had not consummated the marriage.

Even so, Matron Wei’s words smoothed the crease between Chen Jianglin’s brows. “Now that Shuzhen has married in, consummation is only a matter of time—no sense rushing it. As for this contract, Haoyu drew it up with our daughter-in-law before the wedding; as elders, we can’t help him break his own word. Besides, whether Haoyu’s assets sit under his name or hers, they remain Chen family property. The fertile water isn’t flowing into an outsider’s field.”

Glancing down at the contract once more, Chen Jianglin broke into a grin. “There’s one clause here I like very much: our daughter-in-law only agreed to give Haoyu twenty taels of silver a month for spending money. That Haoyu was willing to sign his name and press his thumbprint for a mere twenty taels—now that is rare.”

He handed the paper back to Yingxue, who returned it to Matron Wei to tuck away.

“Well said, Master—no fertile water flows to outsiders,” Matron Wei replied. “Once my miss married in, she became a Chen, so transferring Young Master’s assets to her keeps them right inside the family. Besides, my miss isn’t greedy for wealth. She only used those properties as a pretext to stop fighting fate and step into this marriage with an easier mind. Master hit the bull’s-eye just now: my miss is Young Master’s nemesis in the best way. Whatever brought her into this house, she will only bring the Chens good fortune.”

Her words struck straight to the couple’s hearts.

“How sharp you are, Matron—you saw right through the reason Jianglin and I agreed to this match,” Yao Qingyan said, smiling at last. She turned to her husband. “In that case, there’s nothing left to hesitate over.”

Chen Jianglin nodded. “Go straight to Xinglong Pavilion and have Steward Fang handle the transfer, Matron. I’ll send someone over to pass the word along.”

Xinglong Pavilion was the very hall where the wedding had taken place, now back to business as usual.

Built right inside Chen Manor to let the family manage its ventures and confer over major deals, the pavilion housed a combined staff of more than two hundred stewards and clerks. All two hundred lived, ate, and worked along the west wing of the manor, while the Chen family mostly lived on the north and east sides.

The manor’s south-facing main gate served the Chen family, whereas a separate west gate served the stewards and clerks. Even living inside the same compound, the two groups crossed paths very seldom.

The ground floor of Xinglong Pavilion was cavernous—the wedding banquet had filled it the day before. Though Matron Wei had already visited that morning, stepping back inside still made her look all around.

Gone was the sea of festive red from the wedding; in its place hung an atmosphere of brisk, solemn industry. The overhead lanterns stayed lit even in broad daylight because the hall was too vast for sunlight to reach every corner.

Matron Wei walked down the central aisle flanked by busy desks divided into neat sections. In one area clerks bent over sheets of paper writing; in another clerks hurried back and forth delivering ledgers; in a third they sorted and bound stacks of documents into volumes.

She headed straight for the innermost section, where the crisp clack of abacuses grew louder with every step. Several clerks sat behind abacuses there, checking account books while nimble fingers flicked the wooden beads. Steward Fang sat at the main desk—no abacus in front of him, just a ledger he was studying with focus.

Matron Wei stepped up with a greeting. “Steward Fang, I’m back again.”

Steward Fang set down the ledger and rose with a jovial smile. “Master just sent word over. I’ll take care of it for you right away.”

Matron Wei put on a look of mock astonishment. “I came straight here from Master and Madam’s courtyard without stopping once and never saw a messenger. Don’t tell me you and Master can send messages through thin air?”

“Nothing magical about it,” he laughed. “You must have missed him as he passed. Master’s runners are fleet-footed—he walked out the moment before you stepped in.” Steward Fang pulled up a chair himself. “Please, sit.”

Matron Wei took the seat without standing on ceremony. “Now that Master and Madam have approved it, get it done quick, Steward Fang. I’ve got plenty of other things waiting on me.”

Steward Fang sat down and brought out a volume labeled “Property Register,” though instead of opening it right away, he struck up a chat. “Young Madam is truly blessed to marry Second Young Master. You may not know this, Matron, but Song law requires a family’s scattered properties to be registered under a single household head, so all Chen assets stand under Eldest Master’s name. Only because the Old Madam dotes on Second Young Master did she pull strings with the Prefect to split several properties off into his name. Who would’ve thought Second Young Master dotes on Young Madam so much he’d hand every last one over? Remarkable.”

Matron Wei knew about the single-household registration rule, though she had not thought that far into it. Hearing Steward Fang explain it made her think even higher of the Chens—no wonder their business had grown so massive. “Tell me, Steward Fang, is it much trouble to transfer Young Master’s properties into my miss’s name?”

Steward Fang opened the register. “No trouble at all. First I log it here, stamp it with the main seal of the Chen firm plus the personal seals of Eldest Master and Second Young Master, and then take it to the local yamen to register.”

“Much obliged, Steward Fang.”

He dipped his brush, wrote across the ledger for a while, and set the brush down. “All logged. In a moment I’ll head up to the second floor to the seal-keeper and get the three seals stamped—that finishes the first step.”

Only then did Matron Wei realize Xinglong Pavilion had more than one story. Glancing around, she spotted a wooden staircase whose steps were too numerous to count at a glance.

“One more thing I need to clarify with you, Matron.”

She turned back. “Ask away, Steward Fang.”

“All the properties under Second Young Master’s name have been run by people Eldest Master appointed. Now that they belong to Young Madam, will you put your own people in charge, or keep the current managers? Don’t take offense—I have to ask so I can report back to Master.”

Getting Chen family assets signed over to her miss was already a windfall, Matron Wei reflected; why quibble over who managed them? Leaving Master’s people in place would reassure him the wealth stayed inside the family and spare her miss any future friction. Besides, Matron Wei already had her hands full managing her miss’s own dowry and estates.

“Keep the same managers,” she said. “I’ll drop by on my miss’s behalf when I have time, and skip it when I don’t.”

Steward Fang beamed. “That works out best of all.”
