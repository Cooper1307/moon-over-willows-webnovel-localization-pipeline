## Chapter 223: The Culprit Is the Groom Himself

Seated on the edge of her couch, Matron Wei weighed the dilemma. Keep quiet, and since even servants knew, her young mistress would find out sooner or later anyway; tell her now, and her grudge against the groom would explode, making their married life unbearable.

Her mind drifted back to running into Yao Qingyan in the garden just before coming home.

Walking back through Chen Manor after her errands, she had heard Yingxue calling from a pavilion where Eldest Madam sat resting and waving her over with a smile. Detouring into the pavilion, Matron Wei bowed. "Madam."

"You're such a busy woman, Matron—hard to catch you out in the garden," Yao Qingyan greeted her warmly. "Lucky I bumped into you today. Sit and chat a moment."

Guessing Eldest Madam had waited there on purpose, Matron Wei sat at the stone table. "Busy as I am, I can't compare with the Chen masters."

"I owe you thanks for helping Shuzhen—and helping me in the bargain."

Having forgotten her offhand remark, Matron Wei blinked. "Which matter do you mean, Madam?"

"You help Shuzhen so often it's no wonder you lose track. Winning over Old Madam Xia!"

Understanding dawned. "Ah, that. I only dropped a passing hint, and Miss took it to heart and acted on it. Credit belongs to her."

"You have sharp judgment, Matron, and Shuzhen has a good heart." Then Yao Qingyan's brow furrowed. "There's another matter I need your brains on."

"What troubles you, Madam?"

"Haoyu and Shuzhen, of course. Married, yet unconsummated, living separate lives—him roaming wild outside, her going about her days acting like he doesn't exist. At this rate, will I ever live to hold a plump grandson?"

Matron Wei had expected this sooner or later; a household might humor some clauses of a prenuptial contract, never all of them. "No rushing these things, Madam. It takes time."

"True enough. Set grandchildren aside for now—the urgent thing is getting Haoyu reined in. Old Madam Xia, Jianglin, and I all agreed to leave disciplining Haoyu to Shuzhen. We asked her, she nodded politely, yet hasn't cared one bit. We feel awkward bringing it up again, so I'm begging you, Matron—figure out a way to make Shuzhen willing to take Haoyu in hand."

At last Matron Wei saw why Yao Qingyan had staked out the pavilion. "Normally parents raise their own children. Having a new bride take on educating her husband—folks outside would hardly believe it."

"You're right, and as his mother I'm ashamed to dump our failure on my daughter-in-law," Yao Qingyan confessed. "Yet Old Madam Xia, Jianglin, and I talked it over and saw huge benefits—above all, knocking heads constantly might just strike sparks between them! Bang two boulders together enough and even stone sparks, let alone two living people. Don't you agree, Matron?"

Matron Wei found the whole scheme half laughable, yet understandable. Old Madam Xia spoiled him too much to be harsh; Yao Qingyan lacked the heart; and Chen Jianglin could not stay stern with both women softening every blow. Handing the baton to Shuzhen let all three stay on good terms while potentially turning Haoyu around and sparking romance in the process. Neat math—and honestly, molding a decent husband with her own hands was not a bad deal for her mistress either. The only catch was that Shuzhen respected her contract to the letter and had zero urge to meddle with him.

"A clever plan, Madam, except Miss won't agree."

"That's why I'm coming to you, Matron! Find a way to make her willing."

"I never force Miss to do anything she dislikes—"

Yao Qingyan rose and stepped in front of her. "If you don't say yes, Matron, I'll kneel right here until you do!"

Horrified—a mistress kneeling to a servant broke every law of propriety—Matron Wei jumped up to catch her arms. "You'll shorten my life, Madam! I'll try my best, though I can't promise Miss will agree."

Overjoyed, Yao Qingyan gripped her hands. "With you stepping in, you're worth two of us!"

Replaying that scene now, Matron Wei saw the whole board click into place. Tell her young mistress the truth, and she would fly into a rage and never let Chen Haoyu off the hook—ending his carefree gallivanting overnight. Even without wanting to "discipline" him, her wrath would accomplish the exact same thing. And Matron Wei knew her mistress's character: fierce fists aside, she would never cripple or kill him, only make him sweat—which the reckless playboy had long overdue. As for hope of him turning good, one only had to look at his father Chen Jianglin's devotion to Yao Qingyan; raised in a home like that, the boy had decent roots buried underneath. If Chen Haoyu ever treated her mistress half as well as Chen Jianglin treated his wife, Matron Wei would wake up smiling in her sleep.

Mind made up, she stepped out of her room and glanced across the back courtyard at Boya Study. "If Miss can kick the groom out here to live across from me, what can't she handle? Helping her whip herself up a decent husband—nothing left to hesitate over."

Inside Suxin Thatched Hall, Qianli Xun was bent over her desk writing. Having wrapped up her previous serial, she had decided to quit adapting modern web novels and write an original tale—inspired by her own heartache and seeing Ye Anya and Cao Ban tying red silk beneath the Divine Willow, a story about star-crossed lovers breaking free of miserable marriages to chase happiness. Words flowed off her brush across three or four pages before Matron Wei stepped inside.

Spotting her, Qianli Xun set down her brush and rose to welcome her. "Same courtyard, yet I haven't seen you in days, Matron! Missed you."

Matron Wei eyed the desk with a teasing smile. "Not telling Kuqiao to hide your manuscript today, Miss?"

Qianli Xun pulled her into a chair. "You knew all along and never said a peep, making me put on an act for nothing!"

Weighing how to break the news, Matron Wei stayed silent for a beat, letting her young mistress ask first.

"Why so quiet, Matron?"

Right on cue. Matron Wei spoke with measured care. "There is something I turned over for a long while, and decided you have a right to know."

"What is it?" Qianli Xun leaned forward in curiosity.

"Last year's rumors about you."

"Those? Ancient history—don't tell me people started gossiping again?"

"The culprit behind those rumors has surfaced."

Before Qianli Xun could open her mouth, Kuqiao bristled. "You know who did it, Matron? Name him right now so I can march over and curse him out until his ears bleed!"

"Who is it, Matron? And how did you find out?" Qianli Xun asked.

Matron Wei grew even more cautious. "How I found out doesn't matter. What matters is: once I tell you the name, what will you do?"

"Need you ask?" Qianli Xun declared. "That bastard put me through hell. One word: payback!"

"Yes! Payback!" Kuqiao echoed.

"Spit it out, Matron. Who?"

Steeling herself, Matron Wei uttered two words: "Young Master."

Qianli Xun stared. "Chen Haoyu?"

Matron Wei nodded.

Kuqiao froze in silence, watching shock darken into fury on her mistress's face. Qianli Xun's fingers clamped onto the wooden armrest—crack!—snapping the solid camphorwood clean in half.

Young Master is dead meat! Kuqiao winced inside.

Qianli Xun shot to her feet, paced two tight steps, and whirled around. "If even you know, Matron, with my father's reach he must have known ages ago—and still shoved me into this marriage blind! I'm marching back to Zhu Manor to have it out with him!"

Matron Wei rose to block her. "What good does arguing with Prefect Zhu do now, Miss? What's done is done. Better figure out how to deal with Young Master."

That cooled her head a fraction. Raising a storm at Zhu Manor would change nothing. "Fine, my father gets a pass for now. Anyone else I can forgive, never Chen Haoyu! I'm collecting every ounce of payback for making me the laughingstock of Lin'an! Kuqiao!"

"Here!"

"Fetch Hongdou!"

Moments later, Hongdou rushed in.

"Send men through every brothel and gambling den in Lin'an to track down Chen Haoyu," Qianli Xun snapped. "Report straight back—I'm going myself to beat him black and blue and haul him home in ropes!"

"No need to search, Miss," Hongdou answered. "I already know where he is."

"Chunxiang Brothel?"

Hongdou nodded.

"Then grab the men and follow me to Chunxiang Brothel!" Qianli Xun stormed straight out of Suxin Thatched Hall.

Matron Wei let out a sigh. "Well, things are about to get lively."

"Why do you look like you're enjoying the show, Matron?" Kuqiao blinked.

"Nonsense! Gather those manuscript pages off the desk before anyone sees them, and swap out that broken chair."

Moving to clean up, Kuqiao caught Matron Wei's sleeve as she turned to leave. "Matron, I'm scared—Miss already went through one mutual divorce. You don't think she's headed for a second one?"

"Can't you wish your mistress well instead of harping on divorce?" Matron Wei chided. "Young Master may be a mess right now, yet this second marriage is worlds apart from the first because the Chens are nothing like the Lins. Just sit back and watch."

Matron Wei walked out, leaving Kuqiao scratching her head. "Matron always talks in riddles—still, she doesn't look worried, so Miss must be fine. Right, he's the one in the wrong, and Miss's fists will beat his backside into ribbons!" Relieved, she tidied the manuscript and fetched a spare chair.

Leading ten servants—three maids and seven men—Qianli Xun marched out of Moxiang Courtyard toward the front gates, drawing curious stares and whispers from every passing servant.

Near the main gate, she ran straight into Chen Haodong returning from outside. Sighting her war party, Chen Haodong knew his bait had landed and stepped up with a bright smile. "Off somewhere with such a crowd, Sister-in-law?"

With zero reason to hide it from the Chens, Qianli Xun said straight, "Going to fetch Chen Haoyu."

"Why go yourself with a small army when you can send servants?" he baited her.

A sudden question hit Qianli Xun: did the entire Chen family know about Chen Haoyu spreading those rumors, leaving her the lone fool kept in the dark? "Eldest Cousin-in-law, do you know about Chen Haoyu spreading slander about me last year?"

Bullseye! Concealing his glee, Chen Haodong poured oil on the fire. "Since you already know, I won't hide it—everyone in Chen Manor knows. That's the exact reason the Chens never minded your damaged reputation or past divorce. Haoyu's a man; he made the mess, so he had to own up to it. Besides, last year Prefect Zhu showed up at our gate with a horsewhip, hauling Haoyu tied like a pig, and forced Eldest Uncle and Aunt to seal the betrothal on the spot. They had no choice."

Whatever Chen Haodong's angle, he had laid bare the full truth: Zhu Xiyan had known all along and hidden it just to offload a "damaged" daughter fast—caring solely about Zhu Manor's face, never once about her happiness.

Too furious to waste another syllable on Chen Haodong, Qianli Xun brushed right past him out the gate.

Grinning wider, Chen Haodong called after her, "Haoyu's at Chunxiang Brothel, Sister-in-law! Don't go to the wrong place!"

Job done, he strolled back to his courtyard in high spirits to await the fireworks.
