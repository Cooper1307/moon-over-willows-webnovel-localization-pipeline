## Chapter 222: A Tedious Chat with Xiaguo

Chatting with Xiaguo promised to be dull enough; doing it indoors without scenery would make it unbearable. "Let's talk outside under the hehuan tree."

Matron Wei had already placed a rattan chair and wooden table beneath the silk tree. Hongdou brought out an extra chair for Xiaguo, Kuqiao set down a platter of seasonal fruit and stood by to attend, and Hongdou slipped off to the West Courtyard to watch Qinggua drill—by now he had the move down well enough to dodge his master's kicks with ease.

Though summer had not officially started, the midday sun already carried heat. Sitting in the dappled shade beneath the leaves spared a woman's delicate skin while letting gentle shafts of sunlight through, pleasing Xiaguo immensely.

She began by praising the yard. "I noticed the courtyard on your wedding day, Miss—not half as lovely as this, without a single tree if I recall right. Now look at it: your favorite chrysanthemums everywhere, lotus sprouting in the pond, and this lush hehuan tree. Yard and hall alike scream that you rule the roost here."

"Chen Haoyu promised me this courtyard before the wedding. Since it's my turf, I fix it up how I like—why stare at an eyesore every day?"

Xiaguo sighed. "Nothing beats being a primary wife. A concubine like me in Qiao Manor could never dream of treatment like this."

"At least you married the man you love," Qianli Xun offered.

That hit the sweet spot. Formidable and admired as her former mistress was, she was stuck married to someone she couldn't stand, making Xiaguo feel far luckier by comparison. A pleased smile curved her lips. "There's one thing you ought to be happy for me about."

Leaning back in the rattan chair with eyes closed, soaking in the fresh scent of leaves and warm patches of sun, Qianli Xun asked, "What's that?"

"Wenyuan has consummated our marriage," Xiaguo confessed with a shy flush.

Without opening her eyes, Qianli Xun let a small smile tug at her mouth. "Sharing bedroom secrets right in front of me? Have you no shame?"

"I treat you like family, Miss, or I wouldn't say it. Mind you, Wenyuan also consummated his marriage with Miao Zhiyu—fair as a level bowl of water. Miao Zhiyu acts a bit nicer toward me now, though her words still pack hidden thorns."

With zero interest in another household's drama, Qianli Xun stayed silent.

"You wouldn't believe it, Miss," Xiaguo pressed on. "Miao Zhiyu plays the poised gentlewoman outside, yet her heart isn't half so pretty. Every few days she finds an excuse to pick on me. Lucky for me I'm no fool, and Wenyuan mostly sides with me, so she can't touch me."

Feeling obliged to say something, Qianli Xun spoke with her eyes still shut. "Good or bad in a person is hard to judge—depends where you stand. At poetry salons, Miao Zhiyu struck me as gracious and cultured; during last year's rumors, she spoke up for me behind closed doors, so I thought well of her. As for now, I don't see her daily like you do, so I won't jump to conclusions."

Taking that as validation, Xiaguo warmed up even more. "She may share salon memories with you, Miss, yet she doesn't truly like you. When Wenyuan sent Nan Shuang every few days to ask for your poems, Miao Zhiyu fumed—even grilled Wenyuan yesterday asking if he still wasn't over Youqi Jushi!"

Dismayed that a handful of Zhu Shuzhen's lyrics were stirring trouble in Wenyuan's home, Qianli Xun opened her eyes and gazed across the garden, trying to tune out the noise.

Xiaguo rolled right on. "Wenyuan told her he's moved on, and I believe him, yet Miao Zhiyu doesn't. She demanded he stop sending Nan Shuang for poems, and he refused. Given our bond, of course I backed Wenyuan, so lately he's been spending most nights in my room! Miao Zhiyu is livid—and the madder she gets, the happier I am."

Staring up at the new spring shoots on the hehuan branches, Qianli Xun tried to picture them in bloom, yet every word of Xiaguo's chatter drilled straight through her ears. She wanted this visit over. "Xiaguo, pass word to Wenyuan for me: tell him to stop sending Nan Shuang for poems."

"No way!" Xiaguo refused on the spot. "That's his one hobby—I won't spoil his mood!"

"Then next time Nan Shuang shows up, I just won't give him any." Qianli Xun shut her eyes again.

"No way!" Xiaguo protested again. "You've always lived free and unbothered, Miss—why let Miao Zhiyu dictate what you do? Just proves she was faking her love of your poems all along! Guess who she's chummy with these days?"

On second thought, Qianli Xun realized Wenyuan was hardly the only person requesting her verses, yet no other household fought over them. The fault lay in human jealousy, not Zhu Shuzhen's poetry. Someone looking for offense could twist anything; her own conscience was clear—she was only passing along Zhu Shuzhen's literary legacy, not secret love notes. She dropped the idea of cutting Nan Shuang off and tossed out one word: "Who?"

"Lin Jue's wife, Bai Meiruo!" Xiaguo wrinkled her nose at Lin Jue's name, still remembering how he had groped and tied her up when she was a maid. "She visits Qiao Manor constantly, and Miao Zhiyu even drags her around to meet all her close lady friends. Don't ask me why, I can't stand Bai Meiruo—whereas Lin Jue's new concubine actually seems decent, even from one meeting."

Qianli Xun's eyes popped open. Barely ten days had passed since she bumped into Bai Meiruo, and Lin Jue already had a concubine? "Don't tell me it's Chun Jinqiao from Chunxiang Brothel?"

Xiaguo gasped. "You heard already? Word travels fast!"

Fast? I'm the one who handed her the playbook! Qianli Xun thought.

"Bai Meiruo may be awful, yet she dotes on Lin Jue," Xiaguo went on. "Used her own dowry money three days ago to buy Chun Jinqiao's freedom and install her as his concubine. Yesterday Miao Zhiyu dragged me to a banquet where every household was told to bring their concubines. Thought she was being nice—turned out to be a pack of primary wives ganging up to mock and lecture the concubines! That's where I met Chun Jinqiao."

Qianli Xun closed her eyes a third time, breathing in the spring air. "People actually throw banquets like that? Stay clear next time."

"Caught me off guard once—next time Miao Zhiyu tries dragging me along, I'll say no flat out. Still, silver lining: I met the concubines from all the big families in Lin'an and found out they hold private gatherings of their own! I chipped in to join their circle, so now I'll never run short of invitations." A genuine, settled smile lit Xiaguo's face—she had found her tribe, women she could chat with and vent about primary wives to.

With eyes still shut, Qianli Xun murmured, "Old Master Laozi got it right: 'Good fortune leans against misfortune, and misfortune lurks within good fortune.'" [TN: Translator's rendering]

Baffled by the quote, Xiaguo was casting about for a reply when a maid from Old Madam Xia's courtyard arrived.

"Young Madam, the old gentleman finished his ballad, and Old Madam Xia wants to chat with him and keep him for lunch. She sent me to invite you and Concubine Xia to join them."

Grateful for an escape from Lin'an's wife-versus-concubine soap opera, Qianli Xun stood right up. "Come on, let's go eat with Grandmother."

Over the lively meal in Old Madam Xia's courtyard, Xiaguo had zero chance to launch back into her gossip.

After lunch, Qianli Xun personally saw the blind ballad singer and Xiaguo to the front gate. Loaded down with enough silver from the generous Chen household to cover a whole year's living without trudging village to village in wind and sun, the old man climbed into the carriage sighing in gratitude.

"No need to sigh, Grandfather," Xiaguo chided. "Even without Chen silver, what I send home covers the family. Stop wandering the roads singing."

"Your own path in Qiao Manor isn't easy," her grandfather warned. "Quit sending us so much and stash silver away for yourself so you can stand tall in that house. Remember to stay on good terms with your in-laws and the primary wife—harmony brings prosperity."

"You've told me a dozen times, Grandfather. I remember."

Returning to Moxiang Courtyard, Qianli Xun spotted Asheng pacing uneasily in the yard. "Something on your mind, Asheng?"

He spun around, mouth opening as if to spill something, then hesitated. "Nothing, Miss. Heading to the West Courtyard to help Qinggua drill." He hurried away looking weighed down.

"What's gotten into Asheng?" Qianli Xun wondered.

"Word is he likes Hongdou," Kuqiao replied. "Probably wanted to ask you to betroth her to him and lost his nerve."

Qianli Xun laughed. "He likes Hongdou? If he's too shy to ask, let's pretend we didn't notice." Putting it out of her mind, she went back inside to write.

Halfway to the West Courtyard, Asheng ran straight into Hongdou. Unable to hold the secret in another second, he grabbed her sleeve and pulled her into a quiet corner.

"What are you dragging me over here for?"

"I'm bursting!"

Hongdou teased, "Go use the latrine then—you'll feel right as rain!"

"Not that kind of bursting! I'm sitting on a secret that's killing me!"

Having noticed Asheng's crush ages ago, Hongdou felt her cheeks flush, convinced a confession was coming. "Go ahead, I'm listening."

Asheng dropped his voice. "Remember those awful rumors about Miss last year?"

Disappointed it was not a confession, Hongdou deflated. "Of course. Why bring up ancient history?"

Glancing around to ensure the coast was clear, Asheng whispered, "A servant from Eldest Young Master's courtyard told me today—our own young master was the one who spread those rumors!"

Disappointment flipped to shock. "What? Second Young Master spread them?"

"Keep your voice down!"

"What do we do?" Hongdou fretted. "My heart aches for Miss already. She never wanted to marry him, and if she finds out he's the one who ruined her match with Young Master Qiao with slander, she'll be crushed! Yet leaving her in the dark like a fool feels cruel too."

"That's why I'm stuck. Tell Miss or not? I'll go with whatever you think."

"I don't know either..."

"What are you two whispering about over here?" a voice cut in.

Both spun around to see Matron Wei, just back from checking the shops. Trading a look, they knew at once: nobody was wiser than Matron Wei. Bowing, Hongdou said, "Please come inside first, Matron. Asheng and I have something urgent to tell you."

Inside Matron Wei's room, before she even sat down, the two spilled every word.

Matron Wei sank onto her couch in stunned silence. Clever as she was, she had never dreamed the groom himself stood behind last year's slander.

Yet the pieces clicked fast. Back during the betrothal, the Chen family overlooking a divorce made sense, yet shrugging off citywide scandal had puzzled her. Now it all made sense. Prefect Zhu cared fiercely about face and would never let rumors slide—Madam Xiang had even let slip once at Wangyue Pavilion that he was investigating the culprit. Then the probe went dead silent, followed out of nowhere by a sudden match with the Chen family. Prefect Zhu had clearly uncovered the truth and forced a marriage as a neat fix: saving Zhu face while landing his daughter a wealthy husband.

Only his daughter had been kept completely in the dark. Poor girl.

"Go back to your duties, both of you," Matron Wei said. "Let me think this through."

"Yes, Matron." Together they stepped outside.

With the weight off his shoulders, Asheng breathed out in relief. "Handed the headache to Matron Wei—feel light as a feather now!"

Still stinging from her dashed hope of a confession, Hongdou snapped, "Enjoy feeling light, then! I've got work to do!" and marched off.

Asheng stared after her, scratching his head. "Weird—she was fine a second ago. What set her off?"
