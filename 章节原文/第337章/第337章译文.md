## Chapter 337: Burying Mengrong, Scouting the Prince's Mansion

When Bai Feng unleashed that chain of kicks, Wanyan Chi's face filled with surprise. He murmured to himself, 'Thunderbolt Chain Kick! My master spent a whole year working out this set of fist and foot techniques. He never had time to teach it to me. How could he know it?'

When he heard Bai Feng call the man on the ground 'Your Highness,' Wanyan Chi ordered the generals who had not been knocked down and were about to thrust with their swords, 'All of you, back off!'

The generals sheathed their swords and stepped aside.

Wanyan Chi walked up. 'You call him "Your Highness." Is he a son of the Song emperor?'

Bai Feng saw that the mere title had stopped their hands. The Jin, it seemed, feared royalty too. So he told the truth. 'His Highness is the thirteenth son of the Emperor, Prince Yi, named Zhao Pu.'

'Ha! I never expected you to be a prince like me. Then I'll let you live a while longer. You can go back to Bianliang and tell the Song emperor this: when my Jin army reaches the Eastern Capital, he had better hand over a letter of surrender, quietly. That will spare his people the misery of war.' Wanyan Chi turned to his troops. 'Withdraw!'

Shen Jianchen's heart was locked on Qianli Xun. He pushed himself up with his hands, struggling to rise. 'Stay...!'

Wanyan Chi had taken only a few steps. Hearing the Song prince's voice, he turned easily. 'What? You want to die?'

'Let... her go!'

'Hmph. Who are you to order me? Why should I listen? She is nothing to you now. Worry about yourself.'

Wanyan Chi saw no point in arguing with a man he had already beaten. He led his generals and soldiers away at once.

His life was safe. Only then did Shen Jianchen let his body go slack and lie flat on the ground. One person still twisted his heart: Qianli Xun.

Bai Feng asked, worried. 'Your Highness, are you all right?'

'I'm fine. Just tired. The Jin... came in waves. My strength... ran out. A bit more rest... and I'll be good.'

Bai Feng wanted to speak of Princess Yi, but he did not dare.

As he hesitated, the prince asked, 'Did you... take the princess and Xilan... back to the inn?'

Bai Feng started crying before he said a word. 'Waaah...'

'Why are you... crying... like a woman?'

'Waaah, I failed to protect the princess. She, she, she and Xilan were killed by the Jin. Waaah...!'

Shen Jianchen forgot all about resting. He grabbed Bai Feng. 'What did you... say? They're... dead?'

Tear streaks covered Bai Feng's face as he nodded. 'We were going out through the alley. Jin soldiers appeared everywhere. I couldn't handle them alone. Then I was slashed. That's why I failed Princess Yi and Xilan. Waaah...!'

Shen Jianchen forgot his fear for Qianli Xun. He helped Bai Feng to his feet.

'Where... are they now?'

Bai Feng steadied the prince. 'I put their bodies in an empty room. It's in the alley over there.'

'Take me... there.'

One was wounded. One had no strength left. They leaned on each other and headed for the alley.

They entered the alley and walked a long way before reaching the courtyard with the empty room. The gate stood open. Bai Feng had lit an oil lamp before leaving, so from the yard Shen Jianchen could clearly see two figures lying on the bed in the main room. From their clothes, one had to be Mengrong.

Grief drove him. Shen Jianchen pushed Bai Feng aside and walked on alone. A few steps later, one leg gave way. He nearly fell.

Bai Feng rushed in and caught him. He saw two lines of tears on the prince's face.

Shen Jianchen no longer pushed him away. Step by step, he went into the room. He thought of the times he had spent with Mengrong, and found, to his surprise, that some of them had been good.

Mengrong and Xilan lay side by side on the bed, their faces pale. A small smile seemed to rest on Mengrong's lips. She had not suffered at the end. The guilt in Shen Jianchen's chest eased.

'Mengrong, whatever you did before, I won't blame you again. In the next world, I hope you find someone whose heart beats with yours.'

...

The two kept watch over the bodies through a long night of grief. The next day Shen Jianchen sent Bai Feng into the street to buy two coffins, hire a few porters, and get offerings and paper money.

Bai Feng asked, 'Are we to bury Princess Yi in Yanjing?'

'Bodies cannot wait. We must bury them soon, so they may rest in the earth. When things are settled, I'll move Mengrong's bones back to Bianliang. He thought of how soon Bianliang would be lost, and changed his words at once. No. To Lin'an.'

Bai Feng said no more. He went out to do what the prince had ordered.

The Jin army had only just entered Yanjing. The people who had not fled hid in fear of being killed. No one dared come out. Bai Feng was the only soul moving in the street.

At last he spotted a coffin shop. He went up and pounded on the door. 'Shopkeeper! Open up. I need two coffins.'

He called several times before the shopkeeper opened the door a cautious crack. 'Come in and speak.'

Bai Feng slipped inside. Several finished coffins stood in the shop.

'The Jin are in Yanjing,' the shopkeeper said. 'Aren't you afraid of being killed? Coming out to buy coffins?'

'Someone in my house was killed by the Jin,' Bai Feng answered. 'That's why I'm here.'

'Pity, poor soul. Life is hard in these times.' The shopkeeper sighed and pointed. 'They're all over there. Take your pick.'

Bai Feng chose two coffins but did not pay yet. 'I'm a stranger here. No kin in Yanjing. My people are dead. They need to be laid in the earth at once. I also need you to find some men to carry the coffins out of the city.'

The shopkeeper looked troubled. 'The city is full of Jin. No one will go out.'

'I just came along the street. I saw no Jin. Please find some men for me.' Bai Feng took out a banknote and held it out. 'You'll be paid extra.'

The shopkeeper took the note. It was worth more than a dozen coffins. He agreed at once. 'My shop often handles funerals. A few strong fellows hiding in the back don't dare go home with the Jin in the city. I'll have them carry the coffins. I'll throw in some paper money for free.'

Bai Feng led the borrowed porters to the alley with the two coffins. They placed Princess Yi and Xilan inside.

Once the lids were nailed shut, Shen Jianchen and Bai Feng walked ahead of the carriers, heading out of Yanjing.

The way was quiet enough. But Yanjing had turned cold and empty since the day before, like a city with no one left in it.

Only at the gate did they see the Jin guards. The carriers grew nervous. One spoke for the rest. 'You two must guarantee our safety.'

'Nothing will happen,' Bai Feng said. 'Once we're outside and the burial is done, you'll be paid.'

Silver still waited at the end. The carriers found their courage again.

Shen Jianchen was not afraid. A night's rest had brought much of his strength back. Worst case, he would kill a few more Jin and call it revenge for Mengrong and Xilan. He ignored the guards and walked straight out.

A guard drew his sword and blocked his path. 'Nobody leaves!'

Shen Jianchen itched to snap the hand thrust at him. He held back. Words first, force later. 'Do you see the dead behind me? They have to be buried.'

'I don't care if you're alive or dead,' the guard said. 'Not one of you leaves.'

He was about to knock the man down with one punch when a voice came from the tower. 'Order from the young prince! Let him pass!'

The guard pulled back his sword. Shen Jianchen unclenched his fist and walked on through the gate.

When the second coffin was carried out, the officer on the tower shouted, 'Prince of Song! The young prince says: stop dreaming of beauties! Go back to Bianliang and tell your emperor to line the streets and welcome the Wei Prince of great Jin! Hahaha...!'

Everyone on the tower joined in. 'Hahaha...!'

On any other day he would have leapt up and dragged every mocking face down. Mengrong was not yet in the ground. The dead come first. As for those men, there would be time to settle with them later.

Shen Jianchen walked on, leaving the Jin laughter behind him.

The porters led him to a stretch of wild land outside the walls. They knew their work. They borrowed tools from a nearby village, dug a pit just wide enough for two coffins, buried the dead, and scattered paper money over the mound. Then they took Bai Feng's silver and went to stay with relatives outside the city, well pleased.

Bai Feng saw the prince standing at the grave, as if speaking with his heart to the woman beneath the earth. Since last night he had understood something: the prince had not loved his wife, but he had cared for her, and treated her like family.

Bai Feng came close and said carefully, 'Your Highness, the princess and Xilan are in the ground. Youqi Jushi has been taken by the Jin. Do we return to Bianliang, or stay and save her?'

He already knew the answer. He only spoke to pull the prince out of his grief for a moment.

'We stay, of course. We save her.'

'Jin hold the gate. We can't walk in openly. After dark, you and I will slip inside.'

'There's no other way.'

Bai Feng's stomach growled twice. Not one warm meal since the night before. 'Your Highness, shall we find something to eat in the villages nearby?'

Grief had taken his hunger, but he could not ignore Bai Feng. He said his last goodbye to Mengrong. 'Rest here, Mengrong. One day I'll take you home.'

Reluctantly they left the grave and found a farmhouse to stay the night. The yard and the rooms were bare. But at least they would eat something warm.

The farmer set out the meal: two coarse buns, two bowls of porridge, and a dish of old pickled vegetables. These were hard times. No one cared about finer points. The two ate what was put before them.

When night came, they asked the farmer for two pieces of black cloth and covered their faces. At the foot of the wall, they leapt up with qinggong, knocked out the guards clean and quick, and dropped inside. They slipped along the empty main street, deeper into the city.

Footsteps sounded. They sprang onto the rooftops. From up high they saw a patrol of Jin soldiers moving below.

Bai Feng whispered, 'Where can they be holding Youqi Jushi? We can't stumble around like flies with no heads.'

Shen Jianchen glanced down the street. 'Go down. Grab the last man in the patrol. Ask him.'

Bai Feng moved like a shadow. He struck the acupoint of the last soldier in line, and before the others noticed, he had hauled the man off in another direction.

The patrol heard a sound behind them and turned. Nothing there. One man said someone was missing. Only then did the others notice. They searched everywhere and found nothing.

Someone said the soldier must have slipped off to avoid patrol duty. Everyone agreed. They went back to their rounds, easy in mind.

In a dark corner of another street, Bai Feng pressed another acupoint. The soldier woke, tried to fight, and could not move.

Shen Jianchen asked, 'Where does Wanyan Chi live?'

The soldier stuck to his guns. 'Why should I tell you?'

'You won't talk?' Bai Feng swung his fists again and again until the Jin man begged for mercy.

'Spare me! Spare me! I'll talk! I'll talk!'

Shen Jianchen pressed him again. 'Where does he live?'

'The young prince lives in a mansion not far from the Yanjing government offices.'

'Which direction, exactly?'

'Follow the main street north.'

Bai Feng drew the dagger hidden on him and drove it into the soldier's vital spot. The man died at once. He pulled the blade out and wiped the blood clean on the dead man's coat. 'Call it revenge for Princess Yi and Xilan.'

They slipped north along the main street. Patrols passed now and then. One day had been enough for the Jin to spread their men across the whole city.

They dodged them neatly, but grew more careful with every step.

It was deep night when they reached the mansion. Everyone slept. They vaulted inside and split up, slipping past the guards on patrol. They searched nearly every room that could hold a prisoner. Not a trace of Qianli Xun.

From a roof, Shen Jianchen even saw Wanyan Chi sprawled on a bed, snoring, an arm around a girl. He stared hard. The girl in those arms was not Qianli Xun.

Bai Feng met the prince outside the mansion. They looked at each other and both shook their heads, disappointed.

A carriage rolled by, heading away from the Yanjing government offices, by the look of it. The men escorting it suggested the official inside was Han.

'Let's pay this man a call.'

They landed with qinggong in front of the carriage and blocked its way.

The driver pulled hard on the reins. The soldiers behind rushed forward. 'Who are you? How dare you stop Lord Cai's carriage?'

Bai Feng glanced at the prince for permission, then gave their names. 'Before you stands the thirteenth son of the Emperor, Prince Yi. He has come to meet your lord.'

Lord Cai heard him from inside. He pushed the door open and stuck out his head. 'You are Prince Yi?'

'No doubt about it,' Shen Jianchen answered. He took off his waist token as he spoke.

The soldier took it and passed it inside the carriage.

Soon Lord Cai's voice came from within. 'If you are Prince Yi, come aboard and talk.'

Shen Jianchen climbed in and sat down.

Lord Cai said to his followers, 'Remember this: not a word of today goes outside.'

Then he ordered the driver, 'Home!'

Shen Jianchen pulled the black cloth from his face. 'May I ask your honorable name, Lord Cai?'

When Lord Cai saw the face, he knew it: the man who had saved a Song officer on the street the day before. He handed back the token. 'Cai Jing, your humble servant. My poor name is not worth Your Highness's notice.'

Shen Jianchen took the token and said with a sneer, 'You serve the Jin now, Lord Cai. "Humble servant" does not sit well in my presence.'

Cai Jing squeezed out a few tears and pretended to wipe them away. 'You do not know, Prince. I never wanted to surrender. It was Guo Yaoshi. He held me and forced me. I am only a civil official. I could not lift a chicken, let alone a sword. I had no choice but to do as I was told.'

The emperor was dull, the court rotten. Men without backbone were only to be expected. Shen Jianchen did not care to judge him too harshly. Besides, he needed a favor. 'You let me into your carriage, Lord Cai. That says the Song is still in your heart.'

'How could it not be?' Another tear slipped from Cai Jing. 'Prince, do not stay in Yanjing. Return to Lin'an soon. As I judge it, the Jin cannot take Bianliang at once. But Yanjing is already in their pocket. Your Highness would be in danger here.'

'A lifetime and I still can't remember it...'

Cai Jing sensed at once that the prince had stopped him for that very matter. 'May I ask what holds Your Highness here?'
