## Chapter 181: Buying Home a Little Dim-Witted Pig

After parting with Kuqiao, Bai Feng made a lap through the pig market, seeing nothing but huge, grimy, fat hogs that turned his stomach. Just as he turned to leave, a middle-aged farmer rolled up pushing a barrow full of piglets and began hawking: "Fresh piglets straight off our sow! Dirt cheap—eighty wen apiece!"

Bai Feng walked over and looked them over. Clean enough and easy on the eyes. "I will take one."

Delighted to score a sale the second he parked, the farmer grinned. "Take your pick, young man—home-raised, cheaper than anyone else on the street!"

Knowing nothing about pigs, Bai Feng pointed at random. "That one. Tie a rope on it so I can lead it home."

Looping a cord around the piglet's neck, the farmer lifted it off the barrow. Free on four hooves, the piglet tried bolting—only for Bai Feng to haul the rope taut. "Trying to run? Let us see how far you get!" Pulled short, the piglet flopped meekly onto the dirt.

"Eighty wen, young man," the seller reminded him.

"Do not worry, you will not be shortchanged." Gripping the rope in one hand, Bai Feng fished eighty copper coins from his pouch, paid the man, and led the piglet away.

Hiring a carriage to haul himself and the piglet back to Youlin Retreat, Bai Feng drew a cluster of curious servants the moment they stepped through the gate. "Guard Bai, what did you buy a pig for?"

"To teach it a skill," Bai Feng sighed.

"Not teaching it kung fu, are you?" a servant joked.

"Shoo! Beat it, all of you! His Highness ordered it—he has his reasons."

As the servants scattered, Butler Li walked up with a chuckle. "Well now! Guard Bai craving Dongpo Pork so bad you plan to fatten your own pig for slaughter?"

"Update your view of me, Butler Li," Bai Feng retorted, keeping a tight grip on the rope lest the piglet bolt. "Eating at His Highness's table every day, I got sick of Dongpo Pork ages ago—I prefer light dishes now."

"Then why buy a pig?"

"His Highness's orders. Quit prying and get back to work." Leading the piglet off to report, Bai Feng left Butler Li muttering after him, "Prince Yi's orders? What on earth does a prince want with a pig? Beats me."

Tying the piglet to a tree in the main courtyard, Bai Feng turned to knock—only to find Prince Yi already standing in the doorway, drawn out by the grunting.

"What does Your Highness think of this pig?"

Stepping close, Shen Jianchen looked it over. Clean and pale enough, yet still thoroughly pig-faced and unlovable. "One pig looks like the next—long as it is clean."

Right on cue, the piglet let loose a stream of urine followed by a steaming pile of dung, sending a sharp stench wafting across the yard.

Pinching his nose, Shen Jianchen barked, "Get this mess cleaned up and tie it somewhere else!"

Turning to fetch a servant, Bai Feng stopped at the gate and looked back. "Your Highness, Youqi Jushi invites you to meet her at Yongtai Teahouse by West Lake at the Hour of the Rooster."

Qianli Xun initiating a meeting?! Thrilled enough to forget the stench, Shen Jianchen dropped his hand. "Understood."

Seeing the undisguised grin light up his prince's face, Bai Feng fretted for Princess Jiahe and added, "Kuqiao told me Her Highness visited Wangyue Pavilion first thing this morning."

The grin vanished off Shen Jianchen's face at once—guessing immediately why Qianli Xun had called him out. "Cut the chatter and get someone to clean this up! Starting today, every spare hour outside fist drills, train that pig!"

"Your Highness has not forgotten your promise, right? Extra silver and visiting Kuqiao whenever I want?"

Without answering, Shen Jianchen spun around and went back inside.

Bai Feng kicked himself for mentioning the princess's visit and souring his master's mood—what if last night's promises went up in smoke? Then again, His Highness was generous and always kept his word. Cheering back up, he went to fetch a servant to scrub the courtyard and scouted out an empty corner in the back yard to pen the piglet under a servant's watch.

Once the yard was cleaned, Shen Jianchen picked up a book inside merely to kill the hours. Even guessing why Qianli Xun had asked to meet, anticipation still tugged at him. After laying his heart bare to Mengrong last night, he was done hiding his feelings. Part of him wondered if honesty was selfish—hurting Mengrong and risking Qianli Xun's friendship with her—yet he could not force himself to live a lie. He talked himself around: in this world, having him was enough for Qianli Xun; she did not need anyone else's affection, friendship included. They had known each other before transmigrating, crossed time together, and found each other again—if fate that deep did not unite them, what justice existed under Heaven? Today, he resolved to persuade Qianli Xun to choose him willingly. Say the word, and once Mengrong was provided for, he would cast off the title of Prince Yi, leave Lin'an with her, and never set foot in Bianliang again.

Back from Wangyue Pavilion, Qiao Mengrong felt marginally calmer, yet every thought of her brother loving someone else twisted a knife in her chest. Used to seeing him every day, staying away made the world feel drained of color. Tidying her makeup, she stepped out of her room and walked hesitantly toward his courtyard, Xilan trailing behind with a heavy heart—no words could fix this; her mistress had to find her own footing.

Reading inside, Shen Jianchen sensed a shadow at the door and looked up. Mengrong stood there, eyes faintly red, brows knitted in sorrow; dipping into a curtsy without a word, she took a chair.

Guilt pricked him, yet not regret. Setting his book down, he said, "Go out more often when you have time, Mengrong—get some air, take outings with friends. Do not waste this fine spring."

Hearing his breezy tone—without a shred of reluctance to part with her—deepened her ache. "Where Brother goes, I go. If Brother stays, I stay."

Catching the heavy hurt in her voice left him at a loss. Gauging the hour, he stood up. "I have an errand and need to head out."

He started toward the door, leaving her sitting there.

Qiao Mengrong shot to her feet. "Is Brother going to see the woman in your heart?"

Nailed on the spot, Shen Jianchen almost said yes—yet without Qianli Xun's consent, he still had to shield her. "Just heading out on business."

"Why will Brother not tell me who she is?"

"When the time is right, I will."

"Last night—could Brother have been teasing me?"

She hung on his words, praying for a yes, only to hear clear as ice: "Why would I joke about my own heart? Rest easy, Mengrong—I will take good care of you."

Breaking down, Qiao Mengrong lunged forward and wrapped her arms around him, tears pouring free. "I do not want Brother's care! I want Brother's love—just like when you were sick! Wuuu!"

After a beat of hesitation, Shen Jianchen pried her arms off him. "Do not throw yourself into my arms anymore, Mengrong. You are a young lady—if folks see, they will think you lack modesty."

His rebuff cut her to the bone. "I am about to marry Brother!" she choked out. "Hugging my own betrothed—why should I care what tongues wag?!"

The betrothal remained a wall he could not scale. Giving no answer and mindful of the hour, he strode straight out the doorway without looking back.

His cold retreating back opened the floodgates all over again. Sobbing uncontrollably, Qiao Mengrong gasped out, "Xilan... find a servant... give him plenty of silver... have him tail Brother... find out... who he is going to see!"

Heart aching for her mistress, Xilan dashed out at once, grabbed a sharp-eyed servant eager to win the princess's favor, pressed silver into his palm, and whispered instructions. Grinning ear to ear, the servant swore to pull it off. Leading him out to the front gate just as Prince Yi's carriage rolled away, Xilan flagged a coachman, bribed him for silence, and ordered him to trail Prince Yi's carriage without getting spotted.

Only after watching the tailing carriage pull out did Xilan return to her mistress's room. Back in her chambers weeping quietly, Qiao Mengrong merely nodded at the report and sank into bitter memory. Brother had once held her hand and sworn never to betray her devotion—yet he had betrayed her all the same. Better she had never prayed for his recovery! Sick, even dying, at least his heart had belonged wholly to her. Now he was cold to her and held another woman inside, shattering every dream she had cherished since childhood.

The longer she brooded, the sharper her hatred grew—hatred for the woman who had stolen her brother's heart. Track her down, and she would drive the interloper straight out of his life!
