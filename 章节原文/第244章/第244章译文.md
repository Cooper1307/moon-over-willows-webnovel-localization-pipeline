## Chapter 244: Eldest Cousin's Plot Goes Up in Smoke

Within twenty-four hours, news of Chen Haoyu's sudden transformation reached Second and Third Brothers. At Old Madam Xia's courtyard after the storyteller left, the old woman and Yao Qingyan beamed nonstop praising Shuzhen and calling Haoyu's turnaround a blessing for the whole clan.

When Chen Jiangmu came home after dark, Madam Cui sat sulking with her lip thrust out until he pried out why.

"Great news!" he laughed. "Another capable Chen son pitching into business!"

"Means I have to write my surname upside down!" she grumbled.

"Huh?"

"Swore inside my head that if the Zhu girl reformed Haoyu, I'd write 'Cui' upside down!"

"Say it out loud?"

"No—just in my head!"

"Nobody heard—why stress yourself?" he chuckled. "Help me change; long day."

Relaxing as she unfastened his robe, she sighed, "Lesson learned—never underestimate folks again. Turned Haoyu around in days! That girl's got real tricks."

"Her fists scare him straight—never dreamed a wife could whip a husband into shape!"

"Makes sense though," Chen Jiangmu mused sitting on the bed. "Divorced once, can't afford a second divorce, so she rolled up her sleeves and forged herself a decent husband. Plus Chen men treat their wives right in their bones, or even she couldn't have turned him this fast."

Hanging his robe, Madam Cui countered softly, "Most Chen men—not Haodong! Passed Anya today and saw fresh marks on her neck."

"Pity—Haodong shines everywhere else, yet beating his wife is shameful. Still, Second Brother's branch; not our place to meddle." Snuffing the lamp, they turned in.

Over in Chen Jiangshu's rooms, Chen Haodong burst in late at night reeling from the reversal. Madam Bao sat untouched at her dressing table while Chen Jiangshu brooded on the bedside.

"Father, is it true Haoyu reports to Steward Qiu at the silk house tomorrow?!"

"True enough," Madam Bao snapped. "Old Madam and Eldest Sister-in-law crowed over it all afternoon! Pushing Haoyu into that marriage backfired!"

"My fault for getting greedy wanting him tortured by a fierce wife," Chen Haodong cursed himself.

Chen Jiangshu looked up calmly. "So he joins the silk house—you've traded since boyhood; scared he'll catch up? Relax!"

"Catch up? Never!" Chen Haodong scoffed, then frowned. "Just baffled how he flipped in two days! Think the tigress broke him so bad he's putting on an act for the elders?"

"Could be—Haoyu's always been a smooth actor," Madam Bao agreed.

"Quit guessing," Chen Jiangshu advised. "Ride with him to the silk house tomorrow and sound him out—he trusts you blind and spills everything. Then plan our next move."

Agreed, Chen Haodong returned to his own courtyard, where Xiaocui met him bowing. "Young Master, Miss asks to speak with you."

Rarely summoned by his wife, a flicker of hope stirred as he hurried inside—only to freeze at Ye Anya's icy stare as she rose. "What do you want?"

"My mind is made up. Give me a mutual divorce."

Thunder from a clear sky! Lunging forward, he seized her collar. "Barren hen! Haven't cast you out yet and you dare demand mutual divorce?! Never!"

Two tears slipped down Ye Anya's closed lashes. "Then write a letter of repudiation and cast me out."

Suspicion flared cold. "Hanging around Haoyu's divorced bride lately—did she put this poison in your head?!"

"This is between you and me—nobody else." Ready to burn every bridge, she stood unyielding.

He shoved her back. "Not casting you out yet—wait till I feel like it!"

"What does it take for you to cast me out?" she asked flat and steady.

Her frozen tone snuffed every spark of warmth inside him. Stepping close, he hissed, "Play the spotless, obedient wife and keep your mouth shut until next year when the clan names the Chen heir! Once I claim the estate, maybe I'll be generous and grant you a mutual divorce."

"Swear it?"

The flash of joy on her face stabbed straight through his ego. "That desperate to get away from me?!"

"Every waking breath I want out."

Already raw over Haoyu joining the silk house, that knife-twist broke his last restraint. Yanking her onto the bed, he struck her over and over while outside the closed door Xiaocui wept silently: When will this hell end?

At daybreak, Chen Haoyu rose bright and early for his first day at the silk house. Stopping outside Suxin Thatched Hall's shut door, he called loud through the wood: "Off to the silk house, Wife! First day might run long—won't be back till dark! Don't miss me too much!"

Doing her hair at the mirror, Qianli Xun ignored him.

"Pretty thoughtful, saying goodbye before work," Kuqiao smiled.

Sliding a hairpin home, Qianli Xun snapped, "His business, not mine! Go tell him to stop filing attendance reports at my door!"

Cracking the door, Kuqiao relayed, "Miss says no need to check in whenever you come or go, Young Master."

"Just her being considerate—all the more reason I must check in so she never worries I'm chasing skirts outside! When her heart's at ease, mine is too!"

Kuqiao hid a giggle as Qianli Xun marched to the doorway scowling. "Quit saying 'Wife'—gives me the creeps! Beat it to work and quit bugging me!"

Seeing her freshly groomed and glowing, Chen Haoyu beamed. "Seeing my gorgeous wife at dawn feels like a spring breeze—won't get tired all day!"

"More cheesy poetry and you eat knuckles!"

"Easy, easy—leaving now!" Bang! The door slammed right in his face.

Chanting silently—Not mad, not mad at all!—he strolled out with Qinggua trailing in amazement. "She slams the door in your face and you aren't even mad, Young Master?"

"Mad at my own wife? Chen men never lose their tempers with their wives—look at my father!"

"Exceptions exist," Qinggua muttered. "Word is Eldest Young Master beats Eldest Young Madam nonstop."

Chen Haoyu halted. "Where'd you hear that? News to me!"

"Always out partying—when did you ever notice home drama?"

"If that's true, I'm giving Brother Dong a talking-to!"

Outside the main gates stood Chen Haodong waiting beside his carriage with a bright smile. "Thrilled you're joining the silk house, Haoyu—waited to ride over together!"

"Awesome!" Climbing aboard with Qinggua driving, Chen Haoyu grinned while Chen Haodong studied him keenly.

"Seriously let Sister-in-law whip you into shape?"

"Nobody whipped me—I was solid all along!"

Relaxing at that familiar cocky swagger, Chen Haodong tested him. "If the silk house gets dull, slip out and play—I'll cover for you."

"Thanks, Brother Dong—always had my back since we were kids! Thing is, married now, can't bum around forever. She's the Poetess of Jiangnan—if I stay a deadbeat, folks will mock her for marrying a loser."

Tension crept back into Chen Haodong's gut. "Reforming for real? Not faking to dodge her fists?"

"Think I'm that good an actor?" Leaning in, Chen Haoyu grinned sheepish. "Never guess it in a million years, Brother Dong—I actually fell head-over-heels for the tigress! First time truly loving someone—feels amazing! Gotta level up so she falls for me too! Hehe!"

Half his master plan just went up in flames! Envy bit bitter inside Chen Haodong—how did this airhead stumble into love with his own wife while he, who secretly wanted Ye Anya, ended up beating her in cold rage?! Masking his venom, he probed, "Swear you're not messing with me?"

"Swear to Heaven—dead serious!"

Certain he meant every word, Chen Haodong iced over inside: Fall in love all you want—I'll dredge up enough skeletons from your dirty past to blow your marriage sky-high!

"Say, Brother Dong," Chen Haoyu lowered his voice, "heard a rumor you hit Sister-in-law Anya—true?"

"Pure servant gossip," Chen Haodong brushed it off smooth. "Don't listen to trash."

Accepting it—how could polished Brother Dong beat his wife?—Chen Haoyu dropped it.

Back in Moxiang Courtyard after breakfast, Qianli Xun tried writing her huaben about star-crossed lovers, only for her own sorrow to choke the ink dry. Setting down her brush, she had Kuqiao stow the pages and fetch the wooden box with the peeing clay doll.

Studying the little figurine, memories of Shen Jianchen brought a soft smile that quickly died. Surrounded by maids, guards, warm elders, and Chen Haoyu hovering daily, a raw chill of loneliness still hollowed her out. This room was a bridal chamber meant for a loving pair, yet she sat solo because she refused to betray her heart by sharing a bed with a man she didn't love. Even watching Chen Haoyu transform, loving him felt miles away.

Trapped in the same century, tasting Zhu Shuzhen's exact ache, she tucked the doll back in its box, unrolled fresh paper, and penned a ci lyric:

"Alone I walk, alone I sit, / Alone I sing, alone I drink, alone I lie down. / Standing idle, spirit bruised, / Helpless as a light chill touches my skin. / Who sees this feeling? / Tears wash away more than half my fading rouge. / Sorrow and sickness follow close— / Snuffing the cold lamp's wick, no dream will come." —"Jianzi Mulan Hua: Spring Resentment" [TN: Translator's rendering]
