## Chapter 220: Three Elders Plot Who Holds the Reins

When Chen Jianglin returned from the silk house, Yao Qingyan told him everything that had happened. Enlightenment hit him like a thunderbolt: he had misread his mother for decades, talking about filial duty without grasping what it really meant.

Together, he and Yao Qingyan went straight to call on Old Madam Xia. Though Yao Qingyan had just left her courtyard a short while ago, she came gladly now that the old lady greeted her with warmth.

Expecting her eldest son, Old Madam Xia sat on her couch flipping through a volume of poetry—picking out her granddaughter-in-law's verses and frowning in puzzlement. "Shuzhen's poems read so mournful and brokenhearted. Doesn't match her spirited temper at all."

"You know what Young Madam went through before marrying into our house, Old Madam," a maid beside her observed. "After all that, how could anyone feel cheerful?"

"True enough," Old Madam Xia nodded. "Anyone in her shoes would hurt. Now that she's in the Chen family, things will turn around."

Just then Chen Jianglin and Yao Qingyan walked in. Old Madam Xia laid the book aside. "Back already? Tiring day?"

Overjoyed—this was the first warm welcome his mother had given him at first sight since Wenxin's birth—Chen Jianglin drew Yao Qingyan forward to bow. "Working for our own family is no hardship, Mother."

As Yao Qingyan bent to curtsy, Old Madam Xia waved her upright. "You already bowed to me today, Qingyan. Sit right here across from me and let him do the bowing."

Yao Qingyan straightened without hesitation and settled opposite her mother-in-law, while Chen Jianglin completed his bow and took a side chair.

"You two got yourselves a gem of a daughter-in-law, and I got a gem of a granddaughter-in-law," Old Madam Xia declared. "If she hadn't bothered reading my mind, you two might never have figured out what I wanted."

"Forgive your unfilial son, Mother," Chen Jianglin said. "I was too thickheaded to understand you and misread you for years. Shuzhen solved it the moment she arrived—which only shames me more for overlooking something so simple as head of the household."

"Stop beating yourself up. Just drop by often to keep my courtyard lively, and I'll be content enough to live a few extra years."

"Whenever business allows, Qingyan and I will come keep you company every day so this yard stays full of cheer—and you'll live to be a hundred!"

Laughter filled the room.

When the chuckles died down, Old Madam Xia sighed. "We've patched things up, yet my grandson is still out gallivanting, never home, and hasn't even consummated his marriage. This can't go on."

Emboldened by the truce, Yao Qingyan spoke up. "If neither you nor I step in to shield him, Mother, the moment Haoyu comes home Jianglin can give him a proper thrashing and lock him inside. Let's see him run wild then!"

Far from taking offense, Old Madam Xia blamed herself. "I spoiled him because he looks just like his late grandfather, and back then he was the only one who knew how to cheer me up. Because you two didn't get me, I flew off the handle whenever Jianglin tried to discipline him. Looking back, I only hurt the boy."

"Not all on you, Mother," Yao Qingyan admitted. "I worried Jianglin would beat him too hard and shielded him too."

Chen Jianglin grinned. "Sounds like you both approve of me cracking down on that rascal?"

"I proposed it, of course I approve!" Yao Qingyan declared. "Discipline him however you like—I won't step in."

Old Madam Xia smiled mysteriously. "I doubt it will even fall to the three of us to whip Haoyu into shape."

Understanding flashed across Yao Qingyan's face. "You mean Shuzhen, Mother?"

"Precisely. She's the ideal candidate."

"Earlier, Qingyan and I asked Shuzhen to handle him only because she wasn't afraid of you blocking her, Mother," Chen Jianglin said. "Now that we're all on the same page and you'll let me discipline him, why dump the burden on Shuzhen?"

"Fair point, yet letting Shuzhen handle him brings three big gains," Old Madam Xia counted off. "First, we already handed her the job, and taking it back looks bad. Second, it saves the three of us the headache. Third, and most important—while she disciplines Haoyu, they'll be rubbing elbows nonstop. Bang two rocks together enough times and sparks are bound to fly!"

That third point won Chen Jianglin over on the spot. "Brilliant as always, Mother. Let's do it."

"One snag," Yao Qingyan fretted. "They signed that prenuptial contract agreeing not to meddle in each other's lives. We told Shuzhen to rein him in, yet she hasn't lifted a finger. Haoyu's been gone for days and she acts like he doesn't exist, focused solely on entertaining you, Mother."

Guilt washed over Old Madam Xia. "My fault. The day of the return visit, he came whining he was broke, sweet-talked me a bit, and I caved and handed him a five-hundred-tael banknote. That's why he vanished again."

Chen Jianglin smiled knowingly. "Don't blame yourself, Mother. That actually proves Shuzhen already has him on a leash without lifting a finger. Steward Fang told me Haoyu tried to draw silver from the ledger that day, and when Fang demanded Shuzhen's written order, Haoyu didn't even dare ask her—so he ran straight to you."

"Transferring his properties to Shuzhen really was the right call," Yao Qingyan agreed.

"Controlling his purse strings is step one," Old Madam Xia chuckled. "Give her time and she'll have him trained."

"No more slipping him silver from now on, Mother!" Yao Qingyan cautioned.

"Not a copper more, I promise."

Just then Chen Jiangshu and Chen Jiangmu walked in together. Briefed by their wives on the old lady's secret craving for attention, neither wanted their eldest brother hogging all the filial credit; bumping into each other at the gate, they came in side by side, bowed to Old Madam Xia, greeted their eldest brother and sister-in-law, and took seats.

"Mother, whenever you want something from now on, don't bottle it up and make us guess," Chen Jiangshu spoke first. "Just say the word—would any of us three sons dare refuse?"

"Exactly, Mother," Chen Jiangmu chimed in. "Eldest Brother, Second Brother, and I spend all day buried in family business and overlook things. We're men, not subtle enough to guess what's turning over in your head."

Any other day, those complaints would have set Old Madam Xia's temper flaring; today she felt too happy to care. "When you three were babies unable to talk, I spent over a year guessing every single grunt and cry from each of you! Now I ask you to guess my mind a little, and you complain?"

All three brothers chorused, "We're glad to guess, Mother!"

Old Madam Xia turned to her second son first. "You raised Haodong into a capable young man, which pleases me—yet you need to teach him how to treat his wife. When each of you married, I told you to cherish your wives, and all three of you listened. Why didn't you pass that lesson to your own son? I hear Haodong beats Anya regularly."

"Rest assured, Mother," Chen Jiangshu promised. "I'll give Haodong a stern talking-to tonight and make him treat Anya better."

She turned next to her third son. "Haofei is a fine lad, devoted to his studies. If he wins an official degree, our ancestors will smile down on us. Still, don't let him bury his nose in books alone—he needs to mingle and learn to handle people. Real learning isn't just on paper; knowing how to converse and deal with people isn't taught in textbooks."

"Wise advice, Mother," Chen Jiangmu agreed. "Actually, he's improved lately. Haoyu's bride gifted him a few poems, and he's been thrilled, heading out to poetry salons every couple of days and mixing with scholars and fellow exam candidates."

Old Madam Xia nodded, even firmer in her belief that they had picked the right bride.

The family chatted until Old Madam Xia grew drowsy, then dispersed to their separate courtyards.

Walking home, Yao Qingyan asked, "Should we send men to fetch Haoyu back?"

"He always vanishes for days at a stretch," Chen Jianglin said. "We never chased him before, and we won't start now. Once his silver runs dry, he'll crawl home on his own, and we'll back Shuzhen up in teaching him a lesson."

Seven or eight days slipped by. Young lotus leaves broke the surface of the Moxiang Courtyard pond, small yet breathing fresh green life into the yard.

All week, Qianli Xun split her time between keeping Old Madam Xia company and writing her huaben, forgetting Chen Haoyu existed—as if she had moved into Chen Manor just to play daughter and granddaughter. Only when Yao Qingyan or Old Madam Xia casually asked whether Haoyu had come home did she remember she was married to the man she detested. Whenever they hinted she should send men to haul him back and straighten him out, she nodded on the outside while rolling her eyes inside. The Chens were great folks, yet they overestimated her willingness to reform a playboy she could not stand. Still, watching the quiet disappointment cross their faces when she did nothing pricked her conscience.

Meanwhile, after seven or eight days of recovery, Master Jiang's wounds had healed enough to get moving. Settling a few gang matters, he figured Chen Haoyu would still be hanging around Chun Jinmo and marched straight into Chunxiang Brothel.

He brought only five men this time; making too big a scene last time had drawn Commandant Lin's troops with nothing to show for it. Keeping the crew small avoided attracting official eyes.

Even with five men, Master Jiang swaggered in with enough menace to turn every head in the room.

The brothel madam hurried over. "Welcome, Master Jiang! Right this way!"

Settling into a table, Master Jiang leered up at Chun Jinmo dancing on stage. "When she finishes, send her down to keep me company."

The madam pulled an apologetic face. "I'm afraid I can't, Master Jiang. Young Master Chen has booked her exclusively."

Master Jiang stood and strode straight over to Chen Haoyu's table.

Chen Haoyu had spotted him walking in—with just five thugs. Backed by his three hired bodyguards, he did not bat an eye, lounging in his chair watching Chun Jinmo dance.

Master Jiang dropped into a seat across from him. "Still coming after Chun Jinmo, Young Master Chen?"

With his promissory note reduced to ash, Chen Haoyu had zero fear left. "Heh! All that fancy gambling trap you laid went up in smoke, huh, Master Jiang? Sure, I married a tigress—too bad for you I have a knack for taming tigresses! She's sweet as a kitten now, lets me roam free, purrs at me every day, gentle as silk. I go wherever I please. Jealous?"

Rage flashed across Master Jiang's face. "Hand Chun Jinmo over right now, Chen Haoyu, or I'll knock your teeth down your throat!"

At his sharp glance, his five thugs closed in. Paid to earn their keep, Chen Haoyu's three hired fighters stepped between them.

Every patron in the hall swiveled toward the showdown, nearby tables scrambling back. On stage, Chun Jinmo froze mid-step, staring down in alarm.

Upstairs, Scarface and his cronies broke off flirting with courtesans and leaned over the balcony rail.

"Another brawl's kicking off," one lackey muttered.

"We should talk Young Master Chen into dropping Chun Jinmo," said another. "Plenty of prettier girls right here in Chunxiang Brothel."

"What do you idiots know?" Scarface scoffed. "Young Master Chen is loyal in love—doesn't switch women on a whim."

"That counts as loyal in love?" the two cronies echoed. "First time hearing that!"

"Shut up and watch the fight!"

Downstairs, fists flew. Chen Haoyu and Master Jiang stayed seated, glaring daggers at each other across the table.

"Level with me," Chen Haoyu demanded. "Did Prefect Zhu grease your palm to set me up and force me to marry his leftover daughter?"

Stunned by that wild guess, Master Jiang decided to roll with it—both to mess with his head and scare him away from Chun Jinmo. "Sharp as a tack, Young Master Chen! Think just because I run in the underworld I don't have friends in office? Prefect Zhu is my backer—who else would I help? He ordered me to straighten you out and told me to warn you: stay out of Chunxiang Brothel and play dutiful husband at home, or you'll regret it!"

Infuriated, Chen Haoyu jumped to his feet. "Big deal, he's the prefect! Who gives him the right to meddle in my private life? I'll come here every single day just to drive him mad—"

He cut himself off as his three fighters sent Master Jiang's men reeling. His temper flipped straight into glee. "Look at your pathetic thugs! Wiped out by my three bodyguards! Hahaha!"

Master Jiang rose too. Seeing his five men battered backward, unwilling to lose more face in public, he barked, "Hold it!"

Both sides broke off. Master Jiang jabbed a finger at Chen Haoyu. "I'll let you off today. Next time your luck runs out!" Turning toward the stage, he roared, "Chun Jinmo! Sooner or later you're mine!"

Before leaving, he stepped up to the three hired fighters and gave them a thumbs-up. "Nice skills, boys. The Qiantang Gang's doors are always open to you. See you around!"
