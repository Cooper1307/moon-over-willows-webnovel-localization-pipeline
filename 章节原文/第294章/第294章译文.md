## Chapter 294: Bringing Her Husband Along

Chen Haoyu got home after dusk and checked in outside Suxin Thatched Hall as usual. “I’m back, Wife! Steward Qiu says I move to the accounting room day after tomorrow to learn bookkeeping. Plus the female tea master at Yongtai Teahouse praised my progress—give me a few more days and I’ll whisk tea for you! Oh, and good news: Yongtai Teahouse belongs to the Chens now, hung with fresh paintings and calligraphy if you want to browse sometime.”

The door swung open, his wife standing inside. “Come in—need to talk to you.”

Eager to step in, Chen Haoyu felt Qinggua yank his hem. “Can I skip going in, Young Master?”

“Nobody cares if you come in.”

“Go see Chouya?”

“Scram.”

“Scramming!”

Odd how cursing Qinggua felt warm now—less temper, more affection.

Inside, Kuqiao handed Chen Haoyu a freshly whisked bowl of tea. Peering into the bowl, he started nitpicking right away. “Foam’s neither thick nor white enough.” Taking a sip, he tutted. “Taste is passable, only the water-to-powder ratio is off—too much water, and the powder’s coarse.”

Kuqiao stuck out her lip. “I’ve whisked tea for Miss for years without one complaint! Young Master’s studied three days, hasn’t even made a bowl yet, and already acts like a critic—never making tea for you again!”

“Gotta take honest feedback or you’ll never improve! Three days or not, I’m already better than you.”

“Big talk! Whisk one for Miss right now and prove it!”

“Not yet—wait a few more days and I’ll blow my wife’s mind.”

Griping all the while, he still drained the bowl—which Kuqiao snatched straight out of his hand.

“Watch the attitude, girl!”

“Draw a picture on tea foam, then we’ll talk!” Setting the bowl down at the tea table, Kuqiao pinched some tea powder between her fingers—okay, maybe a hair coarse—and sat down to re-grind it.

Smug his jab worked, Chen Haoyu turned to his wife, who had watched their bickering from her desk with an amused smile. “Actually, Wife, I really can paint on tea foam—just need more polish.”

Skipping tea talk, Qianli Xun held up the invitation. “Look at this.”

Taking it open, he blinked. “Going to a poetry salon?”

“Mm. Mind?”

“Who am I to mind? Go wherever you please.”

“Mostly men—not worried?”

Closing the card on her desk, he said earnest, “Course I worry—yet after living with you, I know your character. You keep strict boundaries with other men.”

A warm rush caught her off guard, prompting words she hadn’t even planned. “Ask Steward Qiu for tomorrow off and come with me?”

His eyes lit up. “Really? I can come?”

“With my clout in Lin’an’s poetry circle, bringing one guest is nothing.”

“Only... I don’t know a thing about poetry—what if I embarrass you?”

“Not bringing you to compose verse—just stand beside me and it shuts down half the gossip.”

Remembering his own rumor-mongering, he winced. “Ignore what people wag their tongues about.”

“Don’t want to come?”

“Course I do!”

“Then quit jabbering!”

“Yes, ma’am!” Grinning ear to ear, he backed toward the door. “Rest early, Wife—I’m heading to the rear courtyard.”

Watching him go, Kuqiao spooned fresh-ground powder back into the caddy. “Bringing Young Master to the salon is the best idea ever, Miss.”

Stretching her arms overhead, Qianli Xun teased, “Why re-grind the whole batch over one comment from him?”

“Well, even Miss calls him ‘Young Master’ now—how dare I ignore him?”

“Sleepy—time for bed.” Heading into the inner chamber, she let Kuqiao wrap up and help her turn in.

Meanwhile, backed by Prince Yi’s generous purse, Dongfang Chenze had gone all-out with Cao Ban and An Mingxuan, inviting Lin’an’s top officials and every noted poet regardless of age or gender under a bold new banner: the Dongfang Poetry Salon. Aiming to swallow every rival gathering in town, he even won over his own official father, who had once griped about his son wasting silver on salons yet now beamed with pride over Prince Yi’s backing and pitched in to help.

Salon morning arrived. Qianli Xun skipped makeup altogether—no powder, no rouge—believing respect came from conduct, not paint, and knowing a painted woman at a male-heavy literary gathering invited snide remarks. She picked a plain, refined gown designed to blend in rather than steal eyes.

Right on cue, Chen Haoyu called outside, “All set, Wife! Ready to roll?”

Opening the door, Qianli Xun blinked—Chen Haoyu stood dressed head to toe in vibrant crimson, setting off his fair skin and sharp features.

“Not a groom today—why red?”

“Not wedding robes—a casual outfit I tailored two years back and only wore once! If I’m accompanying my wife in public, folks need to know who I belong to—wear bright red and everybody spots the Poetess of Jiangnan’s husband!” Smug pride practically glowed off him.

Amused despite herself, she asked, “Where’s Qinggua?”

“Sent him to the silk shop to request my leave—plus he’s so hooked on clerking I worry he’ll ditch being my valet.”

“Going from kicking-post lackey to shop clerk is a huge step up for Qinggua,” she teased, striding ahead.

He trotted alongside. “Give me credit for growth! Haven’t kicked him in ages—plus since you taught him that phantom-step dodge, I couldn’t land a kick if I tried!”

Picturing Qinggua sidestepping and tripping Haoyu flat on his rear drew a quiet laugh from her.

Spotting the pair heading out together, Yao Qingyan’s maid Yingxue bolted straight back to her mistress’s quarters. “Madam! Madam! Huge news!”

Right as Yao Qingyan set a Go stone against Chen Jianglin, the shout scattered her whole strategy. “What’s the panic?”

“Second Young Master and Second Young Madam just went out together!”

Dropping the game on the spot, Yao Qingyan beamed. “Together?!”

Yingxue bobbed her head like a rattle-drum. “Saw them with my own eyes!”

“Here we were fretting Shuzhen would freeze him out, and look at the lovebirds!”

Gathering his black stones, Chen Jianglin smiled. “Kids sort out their own blessings. Shuzhen’s got a level head—she knows what’s best for her future.”

“Acting like you didn’t fret too! Who asked me last night whether Shuzhen would dump Haoyu?”

Chen Jianglin chuckled sheepish. “Allowed to worry once in a while.”

“She’s off to that poetry salon—must be taking Haoyu along!”

“Ten to one.”

“Wait till I tell Mother—she’ll be over the moon!” Slipping on her shoes, Yao Qingyan hurried out with Chen Jianglin tagging along.

Outside Chen Manor’s south gate, Chen Haofei waited beside a hitched carriage and stepped up as they emerged. “Carriage is ready, Second Sister-in-law—let’s go.”

Chen Haoyu stuck out his chest and sidled right up to his wife. “Blind to your second brother? Not ‘you two’—‘us’!”

Chen Haofei blinked. “Second Brother’s coming too?”

“Why not? With Jiangnan’s poetess bringing me, who dares say peep?”

“Ah—fair enough! Haven’t caught up with you in ages anyway, Second Brother—we’ll chat on the ride!”

“Chat any other day, not today! I’m riding with my wife—go hitch your own carriage.”

Flushing, Chen Haofei murmured, “My oversight—I’ll grab another rig.”

Qianli Xun grinned. “I like company—three in one carriage chatting and laughing suits me fine.”

Chen Haoyu flipped his tone in half a second. “Whatever my wife says goes! Skip the second carriage, Haofei—hop in!”

Speechless, Chen Haofei decided the old saying about every beast having its tamer was gospel truth.

Back inside, Old Madam Xia laughed till her eyes crinkled at Yingxue’s report. “Splendid! Bringing Haoyu proves Shuzhen truly accepts him—and sets our minds at rest that attending a salon won’t spark gossip.” Turning to business, she asked Chen Jianglin, “When’s the heir selection?”

“Clan elders approved moving it up a year and left the date to me—I’m setting it for next month.”

“Next month it is. Looks like Haodong has the best shot—trained under you since childhood and the eldest. Only flaw is treating Anya poorly; lean on him to do better.”

“I will, Mother.” Chen Jianglin thought briefly of Chun Jinlan—sabotaging a cousin was a major strike, yet Haoyu wasn’t ready and Haofei had no head for trade, leaving Haodong the sole viable heir. Keeping that stain hidden and locking the selection down early was the only way to keep the clan united.

Inside the rolling carriage, Chen Haoyu ignored Haofei completely, staring at his wife like he’d never seen her before. “Why dress so plain today—not even a dab of powder?”

“Not a beauty pageant—why cake on powder?”

“Bare-faced suits you even better—grows prettier every second.”

Feeling invisible as a ghost, Chen Haofei stared out the window regretting his choice of ride.

Noticing his awkwardness, Qianli Xun drew him in. “When do you head to the capital for the exams, Haofei?”

Grateful, he turned back. “Heading to Bianliang this winter for the spring metropolitan exam—pass that, and it’s on to the palace exam.”

“And after the palace exam you become a court official?”

“Top three tiers earn court posts.”

“I bet you take first place!”

“First place is a stretch—third tier is well within reach.”

A burst of awe lit her face—prouder than sitting next to a prince! “So I’m riding right beside a future court official!” Then remembering the rot of late Northern Song politics, she turned serious. “Once you wear official robes, Haofei, never become a corrupt crook.”

“Count on it, Sister-in-law—nothing I hate more than corrupt officials!” Blushing, he added, “Talking like I already passed!”
