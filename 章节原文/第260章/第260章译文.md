## Chapter 260: Both Schemes and Trials Run Smoothly

A carriage rolled to a halt outside the Chen Tea Emporium. Stepping down, Matron Zou marveled at the grand facade, then walked straight into the attached teahouse. Part of the same estate, the parlor was hushed and refined, its walls hung with literati paintings and calligraphy. Plenty of patrons sat sipping tea and admiring art without loud chatter—only the gentle ripple of a guqin filled the air.

Matron Zou picked a tucked-away table and sat down. A waiter stepped right over. "What tea would you like?"

Knowing nothing about tea varieties, Matron Zou glanced around and spotted a male tea master whisking tea with fluid grace. "Have him prepare a bowl for me."

"Right away, Madam—just a moment."

Once the waiter left, Matron Zou murmured instructions into her maid's ear. The girl nodded, stepped out of the teahouse, and walked into the tea shop right next door. Inside, spacious, handsome counters displayed canisters of every prized leaf imaginable.

A clerk hurried up. "Welcome! Looking for any particular leaf? Feel free to look around—the Chen Tea Emporium stocks every grade there is."

"I'm not buying tea," the maid said. "Kindly pass word that Matron Zou from Prince Yi's household wishes to speak with Eldest Young Master Chen—she's waiting in the teahouse next door."

At the words *Prince Yi*, the clerk jumped to attention. "One moment—I'll notify Eldest Young Master right away!"

Slipping back into the teahouse, the maid whispered briefly to Matron Zou and took her stand behind her chair.

Today happened to be Chen Haodong's day at the tea emporium; he was in the reception parlor hosting merchants when the clerk slipped in to whisper that Prince Yi's Matron Zou was waiting next door. Haodong blinked in surprise—he had zero ties with Prince Yi, so what could his wet nurse want with him? Still, nobody kept someone from a prince's household waiting. Wrapping up his meeting cleanly and seeing the merchants off, he cut through the back door into the teahouse.

The teahouse manager hurried over. "Eldest Young Master! Care for a bowl of tea? I'll have our tea master whisk one up."

"Is there a matron sitting here?" Haodong asked, skipping the tea.

Usually the teahouse drew artsy scholars; a royal matron stood out like a crane in a chicken coop. The manager pointed toward a secluded corner. "Right over there, Eldest Young Master."

"Put her tea on my tab."

"Yes, Eldest Young Master."

Striding straight to Matron Zou's table, Haodong bowed with smooth courtesy. "May I ask what brings a distinguished visitor to seek me out?"

Glancing up at a tall, well-built man with decent features, Matron Zou knew she had Chen Haodong. "I'm no distinguished visitor—merely a servant to royal masters. Please sit, Eldest Young Master Chen."

No sooner had Haodong sat across from her than the male tea master arrived, set down two bowls of whisked tea, bowed, and withdrew. Admiring the delicate pattern drawn across the creamy foam, Matron Zou murmured, "Who knew the Chen teahouse's tea masters could create art right inside a tea bowl?"

"If Matron enjoys it, I'll have him whisk several more bowls."

"No need. One bowl is enough to savor its finest flavor; too many only dull the palate."

"Wise words, Matron."

Beneath the polite banter, Haodong's mind worked overtime—surely she hadn't summoned him just to chat over tea! Reading his thoughts, Matron Zou steered gently toward her goal rather than blurting it raw.

"Back in Bianliang, I saw the Chen family's tea shop too—nowhere near as grand as this one, and business looked pretty slow. I always wondered why the Chen family doesn't thrive in the capital."

Was she probing their capital operations on Prince Yi's behalf? Either way, Haodong answered straight. "Bianliang is packed with noble patrons. We Chens can't muscle in on aristocrats' turf, so we keep a low profile."

"Does your family have any ambition to expand in Bianliang?"

Chen Jianglin had mentioned that exact dream to Haodong once, only to shelve it for lack of backing in the capital. Haodong had suggested asking Zhu Xiyan, since the Prefect had court ties, yet his uncle had shaken his head—Zhu Xiyan helped them in Lin'an, sure, yet his capital connections were strictly guarded for protecting his own official seat, never loaned out to merchants. Could Prince Yi be offering to back the Chen family in Bianliang?! Nothing could beat that!

"To be candid with you, Matron Zou, we do hope to expand there—only lacking a patron in the capital has kept those plans on ice."

"Eldest Young Master may not know this," Matron Zou said smoothly, "but under His Majesty's original decree, Prince Yi and Princess Consort Yi weren't to wed until Mid-Autumn Festival. Yet the wedding moved all the way up to spring, simply because the Princess Consort wished to marry sooner. How many souls in the empire can alter an imperial decree? Princess Consort Yi did. Care to guess how?"

Baffled why she was rambling on about the royal wedding, Haodong still listened with sharp ears and shook his head. "How could an ordinary commoner like me know the workings of royalty?"

"Because the patron behind Princess Consort Yi is Imperial Attendant Liang—His Majesty's right-hand man. One letter from her, and Imperial Attendant Liang had His Majesty amend the decree, then rode all the way to Lin'an himself to deliver it! That's how Princess Jiahe became Princess Consort Yi in the blink of an eye."

Half the picture clicked into place—she was flaunting Princess Consort Yi's powerhouse backer, Imperial Attendant Liang. Still, why pitch this to *him*?

Catching the question in his eyes, Matron Zou laid her cards on the table. "If Eldest Young Master links up with Princess Consort Yi, scaling up the Chen family business in Bianliang won't be a problem at all."

So the tie was Princess Consort Yi, not Prince Yi—either way, royal backing was royal backing! Having a golden ladder dropped straight into his lap thrilled him; if he cracked open the Bianliang market, the helm of the entire Chen empire was guaranteed to be his! Still, Haodong wasn't naive enough to believe free pies fell straight from the sky onto his head. Nobody handed out favors this big for free.

"Earning Princess Consort Yi's favor is a dream come true," Haodong said smoothly. "May I ask what Her Highness wishes me to do in return?"

A satisfied smile curved Matron Zou's lips. "Dealing with a sharp mind saves so much breath! There is someone inside Chen Manor who is a thorn in Princess Consort Yi's flesh and eye. Until that thorn is pulled out, Her Highness will know no peace—and she needs Eldest Young Master's help."

Haodong knew in half a second she meant Haoyu's wife—who in Lin'an didn't know Prince Yi had crashed the wedding over her and wounded Princess Jiahe? Still, plucking that thorn wouldn't be a walk in the park. "That won't be easy—her fists are wicked."

"No rush—we take it step by step." Matron Zou crooked a finger; as Haodong leaned across the table, she dropped her voice to a whisper. "First, stir up a little trouble like this, and then..."

By the time she finished outlining the plot, a grin spread over Chen Haodong's face. "Splendid! I won't let Her Highness down."

Sitting back upright, Matron Zou concluded, "Once it's done, Her Highness will grant your deepest wish. Don't jump the gun yet, though—wait patiently for her signal."

"Deal!" Haodong crowed inwardly. What luck—Princess Consort Yi's goals dovetailed straight into his own! Good thing business had kept him too busy to launch his own scheme these past few days; now his ready-made plan would kill two birds with one stone!

Back at the Judicial Office, Shen Jianchen sat high behind the magistrate's bench presiding over court. Bai Feng and Wen Xin flanked him, Han Gu sat at the clerk's desk taking notes, and constables stood lined along both sides of the hall.

Wen Xin had hauled Sun Er down from the hills; bound with ropes, the prisoner knelt on the flagstones with his head hanging low.

"State your name!" Shen Jianchen barked.

"Sun Er, Your Honor."

A constable stepped forward holding the murder weapon on a wooden tray before Sun Er's eyes. One glance told Sun Er the game was up, and his chin sank back to his chest.

With a wave of Shen Jianchen's hand, the constable withdrew the dagger. "This is the blade you used to murder Fang Jiyuan's wife. Do you confess?"

Sun Er didn't even try to squirm. "I killed Auntie—only I had no choice! Blame Fang Jiyuan!"

An effortless confession lifted a load off Shen Jianchen's chest. "Explain why you killed her and how it happened."

"Your Honor, could you call Fang Jiyuan in? I want to say it straight to his face."

Fortunately, Fang Jiyuan was already waiting outside—no way he was missing the trial of his wife's killer.

"Summon Fang Jiyuan!" Shen Jianchen called out.

A constable at the doors shouted outside, "Fang Jiyuan, His Honor summons you!"

Stepping in behind the runner, Fang Jiyuan spotted Sun Er kneeling in ropes and surged with the urge to beat him bloody, holding himself back only out of awe for the court as he knelt and kowtowed.

"Fang Jiyuan is here, Sun Er," Shen Jianchen said. "Speak."

Sun Er began his confession. "I worked at Uncle and Auntie's house, and they treated me well. I was grateful and truly meant to repay them with honest work. One day Auntie handed me a bundle of clothes and silver and asked me to carry it to her parents' house behind Uncle's back—said Uncle was too tightfisted to let her help her own family. Feeling sorry for her, I took it over."

Fang Jiyuan pounded his own head and wailed, "Her family's a bottomless pit! No amount of silver ever filled it—how dare she call me tightfisted! *Sob*..."

Sun Er went on. "Another time, when Uncle was out, she asked me to make another run. As I took the bundle, my hand brushed hers, and dirty thoughts crept into my head. When I got back from the errand, Uncle still wasn't home and Auntie was tending the kitchen fire. I grabbed her from behind. She fought me off, so I threatened to rat her out to Uncle about the secret bundles. She had no choice—and I was stronger than her anyway—so that's how we had our first time..."

Snapping clean past his limit, Fang Jiyuan sprang to his feet and tackled Sun Er with flailing fists. Nearby constables moved to pull him off, yet Shen Jianchen raised a hand to hold them back—after a betrayal like this, letting the grieving husband blow off steam was only human.

"You ungrateful, cold-blooded beast!" Fang Jiyuan screamed, swinging wildly. "Pulling filth like that under my own roof! I'll beat you to death right here!"

Still, Fang Jiyuan had neither the muscle nor stamina to kill anyone; within minutes he collapsed gasping onto the floor, sobbing his heart out.

Bruised black and blue across the face yet oddly unbothered, Sun Er kept talking. "After that I forced Auntie whenever the urge hit me. Finally she couldn't take it anymore and threatened to tell Uncle everything I'd done. Scared out of my wits, I pulled a dagger and swore if she breathed a word, I'd stop helping her parents and gut her on the spot. She said she'd had enough of living like that—she'd stop helping her parents and tell Uncle the truth even if it killed her! Half terrified, half furious, I plunged the blade straight into her chest..."

With another anguished howl, Fang Jiyuan scrambled up to hammer at him again. Bound hand and foot, Sun Er couldn't swing back, yet his tongue stayed venomous. "It's all on *you*! If you hadn't been such a cheapskate, none of this would've happened! At the end of the day, *you* killed Auntie!"

Fang Jiyuan slumped back onto the stones, broken. "Wife... I was wrong! I shouldn't have been so stingy... *sob*..."

"Afterward," Sun Er finished, "I hid the dagger in the firewood stack, rented a carriage, stuffed Auntie's body inside, and lied to Uncle that she'd gone to her parents' house. Once he fell asleep, I drove out to the bamboo grove, dumped her by the stream, and fled back to the hills the next day."

Case closed. Murder carried no pardon; Sun Er was hauled off to the cells to await execution after the autumn assizes. Clerk Han escorted Fang Jiyuan to claim his wife's body, everyone else returned to their posts, and silence settled back over the Judicial Office.

Shen Jianchen meant to review old case dossiers, yet the trial lingered heavy in his gut, leaving him too restless to read. Fang Jiyuan's petty stinginess over his wife's family had planted the seed of domestic ruin—and what unseen disaster was Shen Jianchen planting by clinging to Qianli Xun and refusing to consummate his marriage with Mengrong?

Yesterday he had promised Wenyuan he would return to Youlin Retreat and consummate the marriage once this case closed—purely to stop his stubbornness from bringing harm down on Qianli Xun. Since he had given his word, why put it off any longer?

Handing off his court affairs, he ordered Bai Feng to hitch the carriage. Hearing His Highness was heading home to see the Princess Consort filled Bai Feng with quiet relief on her behalf.

Just before the carriage reached Youlin Retreat's main gate, Shen Jianchen called a halt and hopped down. "I'm going to walk under the Divine Willow for a bit," he told Bai Feng. "Go inside and tell the Princess Consort I'm staying the night at Youlin Retreat." Without looking back, he disappeared into the woods.

Bai Feng blinked after him for several moments, stumped. What was strange about His Highness sleeping at his own villa? Why make a special point of announcing it to the Princess Consort?

Only after stabling the horses and stepping inside did the penny drop: Princess Consort Yi had decked out the prince's quarters like a wedding chamber and moved right in—saying he would spend the night meant he meant to consummate the marriage at last! Grinning in relief for both of them, Bai Feng hurried inside to relay the message.

Hearing Brother's exact words from Bai Feng, Qiao Mengrong lit up with radiant joy and ordered Xilan to draw hot water at once—she was going to bathe and dress for her husband.
