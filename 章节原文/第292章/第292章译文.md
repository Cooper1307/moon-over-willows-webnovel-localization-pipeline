## Chapter 292: An Invitation Arrives

Catching the hint—an underworld gang had zero respectability—Master Jiang nodded. “Dead right. Ever since marrying my wife, I’ve wanted to turn the Qiantang Gang legit, earn clean silver, and give her real face when she steps outside.”

Touched all over again, Qianli Xun softened. “Truth is, I rarely attend society teas—hate gossip and empty feasting. Poetry salons, though, I go to plenty. If Madam Jiang wants, next time I’m invited, I don’t mind bringing her along.”

“Thank you!” Thrilled, Master Jiang nudged his wife. “Thank Youqi Jushi, Wife!”

Chun Jinmo shot it down cold. “I’ve got someone else to bring me into high society—and zero interest in poetry salons.”

Flushing awkward, Master Jiang apologized. “Forgive me, Youqi Jushi—spoke out of turn without asking my wife.”

Relieved—dragging an unwilling rival to poetry gatherings would’ve been torture—Qianli Xun smiled easy. “Even better if she already has a sponsor.”

Chun Jinmo turned to her husband. “Give Youqi Jushi and me a moment alone.”

His goal already met, Master Jiang rose without hesitation. “My wife speaks, I obey! Excuse me, Youqi Jushi.”

Watching him stroll off, Qianli Xun marveled: came here spoiling for a brawl, wound up sipping tea and discovering the mob boss was a hopeless romantic.

Once Master Jiang was out of earshot, Chun Jinmo cut straight to business. “Looking pretty cheerful—so Chun Jinlan didn’t become Young Master Chen’s concubine?”

“Sorry to disappoint, Madam Jiang. Chen Haoyu was acquitted, and Chun Jinlan killed herself in court out of shame.”

“What?! Dead?! Wasn’t Young Master Chen the one who ruined her?”

“Scarface confessed to the whole thing.”

A shadow crossed Chun Jinmo’s eyes. “A courtesan’s life—worth less than dirt, gone in a blink.”

“Cherish what you have, Madam Jiang. Master Jiang treats you sincerely.”

“Too bad I don’t love him—I had no choice.”

“Don’t you think marrying for love in this era is a rare luxury anyway?”

Chun Jinmo jumped on the opening with a smirk. “Still pining for Prince Yi, Youqi Jushi? Nearly cost you your neck already—quit trying to steal a royal’s man!”

Kicking herself for getting philosophical with someone incapable of real talk, Qianli Xun stood. “No idea where you pick up such gutter talk. Enjoy talking to yourself—I’m leaving.”

Chun Jinmo called after her, “Don’t think dodging one bullet means you’re safe! Princess Consort Yi won’t let you off!”

Pausing without turning, Qianli Xun replied, “Too bad your husband already did.”

“Hmph! My husband’s a spineless coward! Plenty of killers out there ten times deadlier, and Princess Consort Yi has oceans of silver to hire them!”

Turning back one last time, Qianli Xun said coolly, “Whoever she hires, bring them on—I’m not scared. Just treasure the man right in front of you and stop spitting on Master Jiang’s heart.”

Walking back to her carriages, she pitied Master Jiang—then shrugged it off: the guy had done plenty of dirt in his life, maybe an unloving wife was his karma. Behind her, Chun Jinmo spat, “Cocky bitch! Wait till you get what’s coming!”

Funny how life twisted—marched out to beat up a gang boss and ended up volunteering to play matchmaker with Prince Yi. At least Master Jiang was off Mengrong’s payroll now; maybe Mengrong would finally cool down.

Rolling back up to Chen Manor’s south gate, Qianli Xun spotted Dongfang Chenze’s page boy Huayu peeking in with an invitation card.

*Another poetry salon?*

Stepping down with Hongdou’s help, she greeted Huayu as he hurried over. “Lucky timing—saved waiting for Sister Kuqiao! Young Master is hosting a poetry salon and sent me to deliver your invitation personally.”

Opening it, Qianli Xun saw the venue: Juxiang Tower. “The usual crowd?”

“Not this time! Only poets with real standing in Lin’an’s literary circles—plus high-ranking Lin’an officials. From the old crowd, Young Master Qiao is invited of course, and Young Masters Cao and An made names for themselves critiquing your verse, so they’re in—and Youqi Jushi goes without saying. Madam Miao has no literary standing, so she wasn’t invited.”

Clearly a major event—no reason to decline. “Tell your master I’ll be there on time.”

“Young Master also asks that you bring a brand-new ci lyric for the occasion.”

“Of course.”

Seeing Huayu off, Qianli Xun tapped the card against her palm. Even though her in-laws granted her freedom, this salon would be packed with men—proper respect meant clearing it with the elders and checking with Chen Haoyu too, since he reported his comings and goings to her every day. Sending the servants back to Moxiang Courtyard, she walked straight to Old Madam Xia’s courtyard where Yao Qingyan would be sitting.

With the storyteller out sick two days running, the three daughters-in-law were keeping Old Madam Xia company, everyone in high spirits today.

Seated on the couch, Old Madam Xia looked over all three. “Haoyu came to see me last night. Only a few days and he’s matured leaps and bounds—swore he’ll never pull foolish stunts again. Good came out of bad: our most worrisome boy turned into a new man! Remember, all three of you: teach the three cousins to stick together and pull as one.”

“Yes, Mother!” all three chorused.

“One more thing so you aren’t caught off guard.” Sipping tea, Old Madam Xia went on, “Yesterday Jianglin asked to name the business heir this year instead of next. I approved—no reason to object.”

Joy flared inside Madam Bao—just as her husband predicted, the courtesan scandal pushed Eldest Brother to act fast, and her son had the clan elders locked up! Hiding her smirk, she played puzzled. “Isn’t there still a full year left? Why rush?”

“Jianglin has his reasons—just go along without questioning. None of you hold a deciding vote anyway; just know it’s happening.” That shut Madam Bao right up.

Unbothered since her bookish son wasn’t competing—once Haofei passed the civil exams and took court office, the Chens would be leaning on him anyway—Madam Cui still chimed in to stay visible. “Has Eldest Brother picked a date yet, Mother?”

“Not yet. He’ll sound out the clan elders and pick an auspicious day together.”

Just then a maid announced Second Young Madam. Inviting her in, Old Madam Xia listened as Qianli Xun curtsied and presented the invitation. “Grandmother, Mother-in-law, Young Master Dongfang just sent an invitation to a poetry salon tomorrow. Huayu says every notable figure in town is going, so I accepted tentatively to ask your leave first—with mostly men attending, I wouldn’t want to stir up gossip.”

Old Madam Xia didn’t even glance at the card. “No need to show me! Inviting you honors both you and Chen Manor—of course you go! Mostly men? Let tongues wag! Grandmother backs you.”

“Go right ahead, Shuzhen,” Yao Qingyan agreed. “No need to ask us over poetry salons—decide for yourself. Come sit.”

Taking a seat beside Yao Qingyan, Qianli Xun smiled inside: sure she knew they’d say yes, yet asking first showed respect and won even more trust.

Madam Cui leaned over eager. “Shuzhen, is that salon at Juxiang Tower?”

“Yes, Third Aunt.”

Madam Cui beamed. “Haofei got an invitation this morning too! Same salon—look out for him tomorrow, will you?”

“Of course—we’re family.”

Old Madam Xia sighed fondly. “Used to envy literary salons when I was young, only I don’t know a rhyme from a radish! Now Chen Manor has two guests invited—Shuzhen goes without saying, and Haofei’s made a name for himself too. Wonderful news!”

Puffed with pride, Madam Cui added, “Guess who’s bankrolling tomorrow’s salon, Mother!”

Old Madam Xia played along. “By that grin, don’t tell me it’s Prince Yi?”

“Bull’s-eye! Haofei says Prince Yi put up the silver to make Young Master Dongfang’s salon number one in Lin’an! With Prince Yi attending, what Lin’an official dares skip? Having Haofei invited makes me proud as punch!”

Sour envy twisted Madam Bao’s stomach. *Poetry salon—big deal! Wait till Haofei flunks the exams and see who’s crying!*

Qianli Xun frowned inside: why was Prince Yi funding Dongfang Chenze’s salon? Worse, if Mengrong showed up, she’d have to steer wide clear.

“All the more reason not to decline if Prince Yi’s behind it,” Old Madam Xia said, then changed topics. “Just back from the congee shed, Shuzhen?”

Unwilling to worry them with the truth, Qianli Xun fibbed, “Yes... just back.”

“Prince Yi’s relief project has the whole city cheering—before long our congee shed can wind down. Father-in-law says the Prefect suggested shutting it now to put the silver toward building cottages and cave dwellings, and I said no! Until those roofs are up, homeless folks still need hot meals. Chen Manor will cover the government’s share down to the last copper without starving the poor. Sorry to keep you busy a while longer.”

Respect for the Chens’ integrity swelled in Qianli Xun’s chest. “Since the Lazybones Village folks went back to the hills, the shed runs smooth even without me—no trouble at all.”

“Good.” Old Madam Xia shot Yao Qingyan a glance.

Catching her cue, Yao Qingyan said gently, “Shuzhen, Haoyu’s messes put you through plenty of stress and fright these past days. Now that peace is back, Grandmother, Father-in-law, and I agreed we won’t meddle between you and Haoyu anymore. Live your marriage your way—so long as you’re building toward good days, nothing matters more.”

Emotion tightened Qianli Xun’s throat. She had fretted over how the elders would react to her two-year chastity pact, and instead they handed her total trust—making her want to stay in this family and give Haoyu a real shot more than ever. “Shuzhen thanks Grandmother and Mother-in-law—I’ll take every word to heart.”
