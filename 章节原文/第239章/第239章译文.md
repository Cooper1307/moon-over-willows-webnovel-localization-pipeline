## Chapter 239: Lightness After Letting Go

Where the mountain path began, the driver halted and set down the stepping stool. Kuqiao hopped down first and extended her hand. Reading her mistress's long silence throughout the ride, Kuqiao felt certain of the reason: Young Master turning sweet had bottled up all Miss's bitterness over last year's rumors, driving her back to the Divine Willow.

Seeing her maid's cautious gesture, Qianli Xun rested her palm on Kuqiao's to reassure her and stepped down.

Pilgrims of every age streamed up and down the trail bound for the hillside nunnery. Before transmigrating, Qianli Xun had scoffed at superstition; now she no longer judged. Everybody needed an emotional anchor—ordinary folks burned incense at temples, while she came sitting beneath a thousand-year-old willow whenever her heart knotted up.

Walking toward the Divine Willow, she noticed far fewer catkins drifting now. Counting days, Prince Yi and Mengrong had been gone over half a month, the same stretch she had lived in Chen Manor. Wedding underway or already heading back, she hoped joy helped Mengrong let go of her hatred.

Wait—why do I hope Mengrong lets go of hating me, while I refuse to let go of hating Chen Haoyu? All the way here she had fantasized crueler tortures: hoisting him in a well three days straight, burying him neck-deep in a field for passersby to mock, stripping him to his undershorts with soot smeared across his face and acupoints locked to beg on the street! All for putting her through hell last year!

Ahead beneath the willow, four young couples were knotting red ribbons onto boughs—nearly half again as many ribbons as last visit, spring fever blooming everywhere. A sudden ache of envy tightened her throat. When would someone hold her hand to tie a ribbon here? She almost regretted forgetting to buy one during her sweet days with Wenyuan—maybe the Divine Willow would have shielded them, sparing her this bitter tug-of-war.

As the couples wandered away, quiet settled back over the pond. Stepping close, cool air radiating from bark and leaves washed over Qianli Xun's skin, soothing the tight knot in her chest—easing the anger, yet not erasing it. Out loud, she addressed the ancient tree.

"Tell me what to do, Divine Willow. Marrying someone I loathed was bad enough; finding out he started those rumors makes me want to slice him into pieces..."

Called it! Kuqiao thought silently.

"...yet overnight he flipped into a different person—stopped fighting, took every punishment smiling, even..." Skipping his confession, she finished, "...leaving me unable to swing hard, yet walking away with my grudge unsettled leaves me choking!"

A warm breeze swept past, setting willow fronds and crimson ribbons swaying in the slanting afternoon sun, silk strands tangling softly around slender twigs. Zhu Shuzhen's verse rose unbidden to her lips:

"High strands, ten thousand threads of slender warm wind, / Veiled in mist and haze east of the market bridge. / Knotted into hidden sorrow beneath the slanting sun, / Snapped off as parting grief amid the fine rain." —"Willows" [TN: Translator's rendering]

"Fine poem!" a familiar voice called behind her.

Turning, Qianli Xun recognized the elderly nun from the nunnery—still unaware she was Master Jingguang—and bowed respectfully. "Master! Fate brings us together again."

Fingering her prayer beads with palms pressed together, Master Jingguang murmured, "Amitabha. Fine verse, young donor, yet hardly matching the scene. This willow stands by a pond outside town, so why 'east of the market bridge'? Clear bright skies today, so why 'parting grief amid the fine rain'? You're not parting from anyone here."

"Just recited whatever popped into my head, Master—no need to dissect it."

"Not quite random. 'Knotted into hidden sorrow beneath the slanting sun'—you carry resentment inside and hoped poetry would vent it. Yet venting never cures a heart poisoned by anger."

Struck straight in the bullseye, Qianli Xun asked, "Then what is the cure? Please enlighten me, Master."

"Merely passing along the Buddha's wisdom. The true remedy against anger is two characters: forbearance."

"Just swallow it? No! I can't let the man who ruined me walk off scot-free! I want payback until he's on his knees begging!"

"You refuse forbearance because you have not grasped the unarisen nature of anger," Master Jingguang said calmly. "Anger arises only from conditions; anything bound to conditions is illusory. Why let an illusory emotion torment you?"

"Illusory?!" Qianli Xun protested. "My rage sits solid in my chest, and his slander rang across all of Lin'an! Too deep for a mortal woman like me—I can't pretend it's fake."

"Have you taken revenge on the person who sparked this anger?"

"Sure did—only I'm too softhearted, my punishments weren't nasty enough, and he ended up enjoying them! Leaves me itching to go nastier, yet my heart holds back."

Standing beside her, Kuqiao silently prayed the nun's words would reach her mistress.

"Because your nature is kind," Master Jingguang said gently, "which is why karma brought us beneath this willow so you might hear the Dharma. Tell me: after exacting your revenge, did you feel happy?"

Searching her own chest—from hauling him out of the brothel in ropes, slapping his cheeks, making him apologize to maids, down to the stinking chamber pot at dawn—not one genuine laugh had crossed her lips. "Honestly? Not happy for one second."

"If mild revenge brought you no joy, harsher cruelty will only bring deeper misery. Acting out of anger never brings peace. True wisdom lies in forbearance and total release—only letting go sets you free."

"Letting go sounds easy on your tongue, Master. Why should I let go when he trashed my name, wrecked my marriage, and turned my happiness to dust?"

"Have you considered that the happiness you mourn was never yours to begin with?"

A quiet ache passed through her—maybe Wenyuan and she were never meant to be—yet she still pushed back. "By that logic, if victims endure and forgive, doesn't every villain roam free until the world collapses?"

"Tell me: is the man you hate an irredeemable monster?"

That stopped her cold. Scoundrel, sure—yet an irredeemable monster? Flashing back to him apologizing sincerely to Qinggua, sweeping the yard with real focus, smiling through every scolding... "He's just a good-for-nothing jerk."

"Not an irredeemable monster, then. Step back one pace, and sea and sky open wide."

"Isn't he getting off way too easy?"

"Do you truly care about yourself, donor?"

"Of course!"

"Then why obsess over another man at the expense of your own peace? Someone who truly cherishes herself has no hours to waste nursing hatred. Life spans barely a few decades—why pour your days into a poison that wounds you before it ever touches him?"

That hit clean through every wall. Who was suffering right now? Chen Haoyu was grinning through chores back in Moxiang Courtyard, while she was brooding out in the hills! "Harm your foe a thousand, bleed eight hundred yourself"—pure truth. Not for his sake, purely for her own lightness and joy, she let the grudge drop. Last year's rumors: wiped clean.

The instant she released it, weight lifted clean off her shoulders. "Thank you for your patience and wisdom, Master. I'm letting it go."

"Amitabha. Letting go brings great merit. May your days be peaceful and bright." With a bow, Master Jingguang turned and walked up the hill.

Qianli Xun bowed after her retreating back. "Thank you, Master!"

Beaming bright, Kuqiao asked, "Miss, does 'letting go' mean you're done punishing Young Master?"

Qianli Xun arched a playful brow. "Since when did Chen Haoyu buy you off too?"

"Never!" Kuqiao protested eagerly. "I just hated seeing you miserable ever since you found out—even punishing him made you glum! Hearing the Master talk made everything click!"

The first bright, unburdened smile in days lit Qianli Xun's face. "Got real wisdom in that head after all—worthy of being my maid! Fine, clean slate with Chen Haoyu. Back to normal for all of you, so quit scheming to feed me compliments about him—think I didn't notice?!"

"Nobody outsmarts you, Miss!" Kuqiao giggled.

Lighthearted again, Qianli Xun wandered the lakeside picking wildflowers with Kuqiao and weaving matching flower crowns for their heads. Whenever young couples strolled up to tie red silk, her eyes flicked over each pair—Kuqiao thinking she was people-watching, only Qianli Xun knowing she was half scanning for two familiar figures, torn between longing and dread of seeing them.

As dusk softened the hills, she sat a short while beneath the willow before riding home with Kuqiao.
