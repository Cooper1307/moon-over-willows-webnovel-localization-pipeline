## Chapter 249: How Hard It Is to Let Someone Go

Scarface swaggered into the silk emporium with a handful of lackeys at his heels. Thinking ruffians had come to stir up trouble, the clerks hung back while shoppers, men and women alike, edged toward the walls. Only when Scarface grinned ear to ear and greeted Chen Haoyu as "Young Master Chen" did everyone exhale and go back to their business. Seeing his pupil had visitors, Ergui excused himself to greet incoming customers.

Annoyed that they had spooked the shoppers, Haoyu barked, "What are you doing barging in here? If you've got something to say, take it outside."

Once they stepped onto the street, Haoyu tore into them before Scarface could get a word out. "Look at the lot of you, slouching around like street bums—and you still have the nerve to come looking for me? Don't come hanging around me anymore. I'm busy!"

Scarface blinked. "Only a few days since we saw you last, and you're a whole different man, playing shop clerk at the silk emporium? Don't tell me the Young Madam beat the sense clean out of you?"

"Watch your mouth!" Haoyu snapped. "My wife treats me great."

Qinggua bristled with old resentment. These were the very loafers who used to drag his master off to carouse while leaving him behind. Since Young Master wanted nothing to do with them now, why not pile on? Stepping forward, he puffed out his chest. "Hey! Clear off, before I tell the Young Madam and she comes out to beat you all crying for your mothers!"

Scarface and his crew ignored Qinggua as though a mosquito had buzzed past. One of the cronies called out, "Wait—don't you miss Chun Jinmo at all, Young Master Chen?"

A twinge of guilt tugged at Haoyu at the sound of her name. "How is she?"

Scarface pounced on the opening. "How could she be, without you watching out for her? Cries her eyes out every single day. The very day after your wife hauled you home, Master Jiang marched into Chunxiang Brothel. Chun Jinmo swore she'd rather die than yield, and even the madam couldn't budge her. Lucky for her, Master Jiang didn't force her—he just bought out her exclusive patronage and says he'll redeem her contract once she has a change of heart. As long as silver keeps rolling in, the madam leaves her alone, but she's still pining for you morning and night."

Thinking of his wife back home, Haoyu steeled his heart. "Pass a message to Chun Jinmo for me. Tell her I used to be bored out of my mind with nobody I truly cared for, so I was only fooling around with her. Tell her not to take it to heart. I won't be setting foot in Chunxiang Brothel again, so she'd better stop pining for me."

Scarface saw then that Chen Haoyu meant it; the days of freeloading off his purse were over.

Another lackey whined, "Young Master Chen, if you stop taking us out, we won't have anyone left to lean on."

An idea struck Haoyu. "Instead of loafing around doing nothing all day, why not earn honest silver and feed yourselves? I can put in a word with Brother Dong and get you boys heavy labor hauling freight."

Accustomed to eating and drinking for free, every single one of them pulled a sour face at the mention of honest sweat.

Haoyu sneered. "Look at your lazy mugs. Don't bother me again unless you've got real business."

He pivoted to head back inside to his clerk duties, only for a voice to stop him in his tracks.

"Young Master Chen."

Turning around, he spotted Guard Bai—and right beside him sat Prince Yi's carriage. Haoyu blinked. He hadn't expected the prince back from Bianliang so soon. "Oh, Guard Bai. What brings you here?"

"His Highness invites you into the carriage for a word."

Who dared refuse Prince Yi? Haoyu obediently climbed aboard.

Scarface and his crew had not cleared out yet. They didn't recognize Bai Feng, but hearing the word *Prince* was enough—who in Lin'an didn't know Prince Yi had taken up residence in town? Sticking out their tongues in fright at the thought of offending royalty, they scattered in a hurry.

Qinggua, who had seen firsthand how lethal Bai Feng was, stayed well back at a safe distance while waiting for his master.

Inside the carriage, Chen Haoyu sat down and bowed. "Had I known Your Highness had returned, I ought to have paid a formal call myself. Making you come all this way to see me is unforgivable. Still, how did you know I was at the silk emporium?"

"When I want to find someone or learn something, no obstacle stays difficult for long," Shen Jianchen said. "Besides, the Chen Silk Emporium is famous enough in Lin'an—hardly a secret."

"I've already seen what Your Highness is capable of. May I ask what brings you here today?"

Shen Jianchen looked over Haoyu's plain tunic. "Why are you dressed like a shop clerk?"

"To be honest with Your Highness, I've decided to turn over a new leaf. I'm going to help run the Chen family business, starting from the bottom as a clerk."

"You truly mean to change?"

"Would I lie about that? That gang outside just came trying to lure me back to Chunxiang Brothel, and I tore strips off them until they tucked their tails and ran."

"And her... is she happy?"

Realization dawned on Haoyu—Prince Yi had come solely for Zhu Shuzhen. "She... I can't tell yet. Still, I believe if I keep at it, she'll come around one day. She's married to me, after all."

Studying the prince's face as he spoke, Haoyu noticed his lowered eyes and heavy gloom.

After a long pause, Shen Jianchen asked, "Do you remember what you said to me outside the Qiantang Gang before I left for Bianliang?"

Haoyu had banished those words clean from his mind, and the reminder hit him like a thunderbolt—he had promised to hand Zhu Shuzhen back to Prince Yi! Regret flooded him, followed by panic that the prince might charge him with breaking his word. He slid off the bench onto his knees—the first time in his life he had ever knelt to anyone inside a carriage.

"I hadn't fallen for Shuzhen back then, so I was just running my mouth," he pleaded. "Please don't hold me to it, Your Highness! With your noble status, you only have to raise a hand and any girl you want will come running. Why fight a commoner like me over my wife?"

"Is that how you see me?"

*Isn't it true?* Haoyu fumed silently. *You crashed my wedding to steal my bride, and even after marrying Princess Jiahe you're still hung up on my wife! If you weren't a prince, I'd brawl with you right here. Look at Qiao Wenxian—ever since Shuzhen married me, he's settled down and left all that old romance behind!*

"Chen Haoyu."

"Yes, Your Highness."

"I came today to speak plainly with you. My feelings for her—past, present, and future—will never change. Since fate led her to marry you, I respect her choice. But if you ever go running back to brothels or do anything to wrong her, I will not forgive you."

"If I ever pull a stunt like that again, Your Highness won't even get the chance—my wife's fists will flatten me first."

Hearing Haoyu call her "my wife" with such easy warmth sent an ache straight through Shen Jianchen's chest. Still, he forced himself to accept reality. "Tell her for me that I'm stepping back for her own good. I hope... I hope... you two grow old together in peace."

Hearing the prince's blessing made Haoyu's spirits soar. "Thank you, Your Highness!"

"You may go."

Haoyu got to his feet with care, stepped down from the carriage, and watched it roll away before hurrying back into the emporium to resume his lessons under Ergui.

When the carriage halted outside Youlin Retreat, Shen Jianchen stepped out and headed straight into the secluded woods rather than his quarters. Knowing his master wanted to visit the Divine Willow, Bai Feng fell in behind him without a word.

In his heart, Shen Jianchen could not let Qianli Xun go, no matter how loud reason insisted he must. Letting fate take its course without clinging sounded effortless on paper; living it was torture. His heart refused to align with the path fate had handed him, tearing him raw from within. Yet a man couldn't sit down and sob like a girl—he could only bury his tears inside. Maybe sitting beneath the Divine Willow long enough would wear his heart down until he surrendered to reason.

He cut through the thick grove and followed the pond toward the great tree. Since his trip to Bianliang, the willow's boughs had grown even lusher, and far more red silk ribbons fluttered from its branches. Plenty of lovesick souls out in the world, it seemed.

As he neared the tree, a man's and a woman's voices drifted through the foliage.

"He said he'll only consider a heli—a mutual divorce—after he inherits the family estates," the woman said.

"How long will that take?" the man asked.

"A year."

"I'm willing to wait."

"I can't wait that long! I wish I could leave him this very minute."

"We can't rush something this serious. We need to plan it through."

"Even after I get my heli, your family will accept me even less."

"That doesn't matter. I have plenty of brothers and sisters—my parents won't miss one son. When the time comes, I'll take you to the ends of the earth so we can live free together."

Another pair of star-crossed lovers, clearly—a married woman desperate to divorce her husband and run off with the man she loved. Something familiar in the man's voice made Shen Jianchen part the tall brush. It was Cao Ban, holding a woman Shen Jianchen did not recognize—Ye Anya. After lingering in an embrace, the two clasped hands and slipped away.

Once they were out of earshot, Bai Feng ventured, "Your Highness, who would've thought Young Master Cao was in love with a married woman?" The words were barely out before he realized his blunder—wasn't his own master in the exact same boat? Worse, even: at least Cao Ban was unmarried, whereas Prince Yi had been wed to Princess Jiahe by imperial decree.

Shen Jianchen paid him no mind. Waiting until the couple vanished, he walked up to the Divine Willow and sat in silence on his usual stone. Maybe Qianli Xun had come and sat right here while he was gone. He pictured her perched on this rock, half-convinced a trace of her warmth still lingered in the stone, and closed his eyes to hold onto that illusion.

After a spell, he opened his eyes. "Bai Feng, I want to find another piglet."

Bai Feng stared, dumbfounded all over again. "Your Highness, pigs simply cannot climb trees! The last piglet is buried right over there, still glaring at the sky in protest! You want to train another one? At this rate, the Divine Willow is going to turn into a piglet graveyard."

Shen Jianchen had come here hoping to talk himself into cold reason, yet the exact opposite had happened—perhaps watching that couple refuse to give up had sparked one last stubborn ember inside him. "I'm not giving up on training a pig to climb a tree, and as my guard, neither can you. Don't just grab any dim-witted pig this time. Keep your eyes open—who knows, maybe one day we really will stumble across a piglet that can climb."

That eased Bai Feng's mind. "As long as no more piglets get hurt, I'll keep an eye out for Your Highness. Though... does a tree-climbing pig even exist in this world?"

"Whether it exists or not, I want to try. I want to leave myself one shred of hope."

Bai Feng had a vague hunch this obsession with tree-climbing pigs had something to do with Youqi Jushi, though he still couldn't connect the dots.

Just then Qiao Wenxian arrived beneath the willow with Nan Shuang in tow. "So you really are out here!"

At the sound of his friend's voice, Shen Jianchen rose and broke into a genuine smile. Qiao Wenxian strode over and threw his arms around him. "When I reached the gate of Youlin Retreat, Butler Li told me you'd gone into the woods. I guessed you'd be right here, and sure enough, caught you red-handed."

Shen Jianchen hugged him tight. "I planned to look for you after tomorrow's banquet, and here you are delivering yourself straight to me."

Qiao Wenxian stepped back. "You leave without a word, you come back without a word—if the Prefect hadn't sent an invitation to tomorrow's banquet, I wouldn't even have known you were back! Some friend you are."

"I don't like making a spectacle of myself, and having a crowd of officials hovering around me makes my skin crawl."

"Like it or not, there's no dodging tomorrow. Every official in Lin'an will be there."

"All thanks to being Prince Yi and Military Commissioner."

"Now that you're back," Qiao Wenxian urged, "don't stay hands-off like before. Put that Military Commissioner title to some real use."

"True enough. I need something to keep busy so the long days and nights don't drag forever."

Hearing the weary resignation beneath his friend's words, Qiao Wenxian understood. "We're brothers in misfortune, you and I. You ought to let go of what's past, just like I did."

"I can still picture you heartbroken right under this willow last year, Wenyuan. Didn't think you'd let go so fast."

"What choice did I have? Her heart belonged to someone else—my best friend, no less."

Shen Jianchen let out a sigh. "And what good did that do? She still married another man. Even as a prince, I couldn't stop it."

"Which is why I'm telling you to let go like I did. You and Princess Jiahe make a fine pair—stop reaching for someone who isn't yours."

Unwilling to dwell on letting go, Shen Jianchen shifted tracks. "Care to guess what I plan to do now that I'm back in Lin'an?"

"What else? You're the Military Commissioner—do what a Military Commissioner does."

"When the court at the top is rotten, how clean can local officials be? Even acting as Military Commissioner wouldn't fix a thing. Better leave that to history—history will sweep it all away soon enough. Instead, I asked the Prefect for the post of Judicial Investigator so I can handle ordinary folks' troubles. At least this trip to Lin'an won't go to waste."

"Judicial Investigator? That's a tiny rank! If His Majesty catches wind of it, he'll tear the Prefect to shreds."

"Relax. I'm still Military Commissioner on paper—I'm just taking on the Judicial Investigator's workload. Never underestimate how skilled local officials are at keeping the capital in the dark. Besides, His Majesty has his hands full negotiating with the Jin over the Sixteen Prefectures of Yanyun; he doesn't have time to fuss over me."

"If your mind's made up, I won't argue. Still, the Judicial Investigator's yamen is shabby as dirt. Aren't you worried about making Princess Jiahe suffer there?"

"I've already arranged it with Mengrong. She'll stay here at Youlin Retreat, I'll live at the yamen, and I'll come back once a week to check on her."

Qiao Wenxian went silent. A newlywed prince skipping out on his bride to sleep alone at a shabby yamen? Nothing could be plainer—and who knew how badly that must be breaking Princess Consort Yi's heart? Had he not witnessed Princess Jiahe's devotion with his own eyes, Qiao Wenxian could never have blamed the prince for loving Shuzhen—she was extraordinary, after all. Yet he had seen firsthand how tirelessly Mengrong had nursed the sick prince back then. Seeing the prince turn cold on her now made Qiao Wenxian's blood stir on her behalf.
