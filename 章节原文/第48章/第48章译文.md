# Chapter 48: Mistaken Identity in Excitement

Night had fallen by the time they returned to Youlin Retreat.

Back from West Third Alley, Bai Feng came straight to report. Spotting Prince Yi drilling forms in the yard, he stood quiet watching in awe—wondering how many years till his own fists looked half that sharp!

Catching Bai Feng out of the corner of his eye, Shen Jianchen lowered his guard crisp: "Any luck today?"

"Courier didn't show today—though I hit it off fast with the storyteller! Even invited me to dinner and lets me wait indoors!"

Shen Jianchen chuckled: "Making friends wherever you go—good, beats freezing on the curb! Normal he skipped a day; try again tomorrow."

"Yes, Your Highness! Ready to turn in?"

Wide awake still, Shen Jianchen shook his head: "Walk up to the tree platform with me."

Climbing up above the woods together, they noticed lamplight glowing through Wenyuan's window—still reading by candlelight! Guy wasn't even taking imperial exams; why grind so hard?

Then it clicked: zero phones, zero movies in the Song dynasty—books were the best entertainment going!

Gazing out through moonlight toward the Divine Willow, he saw the moon fuller than days ago—nearly round. Far off, dwarfed against dark hills yet towering inside Shen Jianchen's heart, stood the serene willow.

Every ounce of his love for Qianli Xun crossed time with him, witnessed by that willow; even their duel beneath its branches before the quake held love in every clash!

Yet if Qianli Xun now walked in a male scholar's shoes, bitterness flared toward the willow—why shatter his heart like this?

Watching his master brood silent over the treetops, Bai Feng broke the quiet: "Your Highness, the storyteller mentioned the courier looked like a country fellow, not a city servant. Could the person you're hunting live out in the countryside?"

"Countryside?" Shen Jianchen murmured—figuring if Qianli Xun was a broke scholar, of course any helper wore rough country cloth! Still, check Dongren Street first thing tomorrow! "Let's head back down."

Easy enough to find—Qiao Manor's coachman knew Dongren Street well and drove Shen Jianchen into town at sunrise.

Qiao Wenxian stayed holed up indoors nursing Zhu Shuzhen's heartbreak; Shen Jianchen got it—feeling the same gnawing ache worrying Qianli Xun lived in poverty!

Qiao Mengrong asked to come along, yet Shen Jianchen turned her down gentle—Dongren Street teemed with broke male scholars, no place for a princess!

Knowing Brother was looking for a male friend, Qiao Mengrong took the refusal calm without worry.

The instant they cleared the city gate, Bai Feng hopped down and sprinted straight for West Third Alley on stakeout duty.

Rolling past bustling avenues and turning east, the carriage entered ever poorer lanes.

Halting at a narrow crossroad, the driver called: "Dongren Street ahead, Young Master Shen—too tight for the carriage, gotta walk from here."

Hopping down, Shen Jianchen instructed: "Wait right here."

Narrow indeed, flanked by sagging shacks, littered with refuse and puddles reeking sour.

Not just scholars lived here—women and kids crowded the doorways too.

Grimy-faced youngsters played right amid street scraps.

Women sat on stoops wearing faded homespun—worlds apart from Qiao Mengrong or Miao Zhiyu—dull eyes, hair knotted bare without pins, weather-beaten skin untouched by powder.

Shabby scholars shuffled past eyes down, hugging worn book bundles in threadbare robes.

Heart aching at the sight, Shen Jianchen stopped one passing scholar: "Pardon me, sir—does a scholar named Qianli Xun live around here?"

Shaking his head: "Never heard of him."

Next he tried a woman sewing outside her door: "Excuse me, sister—know anyone named Qianli Xun here?"

Glancing up blank, she went straight back to her stitches without a word.

A pack of kids dashed up, one beaming cheeky: "Big Brother! Fancy clothes—you're loaded! Buy us candy?!"

An instant plan clicked! Crouching to grin at them: "Find someone named Qianli Xun for Big Brother, and I buy every single one of you mountains of treats!"

"Deal!" the leader cheered.

"Deal! Waiting right back at the crossroad—find him and run tell me!"

Like sparks from a flint, the pack scattered down every alley.

Walking back to the crossroad, Shen Jianchen handed his driver loose silver to buy candies and snacks.

Soon the driver returned with two bulging paper bags stowed on the carriage seat.

While waiting for the kids, Shen Jianchen climbed inside and shut his eyes to rest.

Soon eager shouts rang out: "Where's Big Brother?!"

Popping straight out of the carriage: "Found him?!"

"Found him! Guy named Qian Lixun!"

Thrilled yet wary, Shen Jianchen told the driver to pass out all the candies and treats.

Screaming with joy like New Year's Day, the kids stuffed their pockets while an older boy grabbed Shen Jianchen's hand: "Come on, Big Brother—I'll show you his house!"

Surrounded by chattering kids, he wound deeper down Dongren Street.

Halting before a worn door, the boy pointed: "Right here, Big Brother!"—then thumped twice on the wood with his fist.

A woman cradling an infant pulled the door open and snapped at the kids: "Who told you brats to pound on my door?!"

Giggling, the kids darted off down the lane.

Stepping up polite, Shen Jianchen asked: "Excuse me—does Qianli Xun live here?"

Sizing up his fine silks, the woman hollered inside: "Old Qian! Somebody asking for you!"

A scholarly-looking man nearing thirty stepped out clutching a book: "Who is it?"

His wife nagged sharp: "Buried in books sunrise to sunset—what good's that?! Go hawk goods on the street and feed us!"

"Guest standing right here—ease off a bit," the man murmured.

Stepping inside the tiny yard, Shen Jianchen stared hard: "Are you Qianli Xun?"

"I am indeed Qian Lixun—how may I help you, young master?"

Hearing yes blew every dam inside Shen Jianchen! Lunging forward, he wrapped both arms tight around the stunned man: "I FINALLY found you!"

Baffled stiff, the man stammered: "Hold on—I don't even know you, young master!"

Squeezing tighter, Shen Jianchen choked out: "It's me—Shen Jianchen! The Divine Willow dragged us both here! Never dreamed you ended up like this—married with a kid and all!"

Weirded out completely, the man shoved him back: "Quit hugging me—creepy! Shen who? Divine what? Haven't a clue what you're talking about!"

Staggering back, cold sense slapped Shen Jianchen awake! Wait—crossing over turned HIM into Zhao Pu; why on earth would Qianli Xun keep her exact original name?! Wait—then why did the storyteller know the name Qianli Xun?!

"Hold on—are you the Qianli Xun supplying huaben scripts to the storyteller in West Third Alley?!"

"Never written a huaben in my life—thought about trying, haven't started yet!"

"How do you write your name?!"

"Qian (千) as in a vast army, Li (离) as in leaving home, Xun (循) as in following rules!"

Pure homophone—not Qianli Xun at all!

Exhaling sheepish relief, Shen Jianchen bowed: "Wrong person—so sorry for barging in!"

Pulling out one tael of silver: "For your trouble—please accept this!"

His wife swooped in snatching the silver with a dazzling grin: "Thank you, rich generous young master!"

Relieved it wasn't her—yet bummed all over again—Shen Jianchen swore off wild goose chases before making an even bigger fool of himself!

Stick to West Third Alley! Names might match by fluke, yet NOBODY on earth wrote "Hongxiang and Xuanying" except Qianli Xun!

Meanwhile, bored stiff and starving at the storyteller's place, Bai Feng slipped out to a noodle stall, slurped down a steaming bowl of soup noodles, paid up, patted his full belly, and strolled back toward West Third Alley.

Right at the alley mouth he brushed shoulder to shoulder past Kujue without giving the tanned country lad a second glance—then rapped on the storyteller's door!

Swinging open the door, the storyteller blurted: "Courier just came and left!"

"What'd he look like?!"

"Same guy—tanned, stocky, short!"

"Name?!"

"Told you already he won't give his name! Quit talking—chase him!"

Bolting like a hound off a leash, Bai Feng realized he had just passed the exact guy at the alley mouth and sprinted straight toward the city gates!

Scanning tight through bustling crowds while weaving fast, he locked eyes onto a stocky, tanned back ahead! Closing in, he clamped an iron grip on the lad's shoulder: "Hey! You the guy who just dropped a script in West Third Alley?!"

Trapped fast—strong as Kujue was, this slender kid's iron fingers refused to budge!—"Who are you to grab me?!"

Hauling Kujue into a quiet nook without letting go, Bai Feng pressed: "Answer my question first, then I let go!"

Too honest to spin smooth lies—yet remembering the former Proprietor's strict rule never to reveal her name—Kujue hesitated before admitting: "Yeah, I dropped it."

Still holding tight: "Good—come take a quick trip with me!"

Dragging him to a carriage hire stand, Bai Feng flagged a rig and shoved Kujue inside: "Relax—I'm one of the good guys, won't hurt you! Just taking you to answer a gentleman's questions, then you're free!"

Terrified—simple-minded maybe, yet nobody stayed calm getting kidnapped out of town!—Kujue scrambled for an escape!

Seeing city walls fall behind spiked pure panic—bandits?! Gotta bolt NOW!

Squirming toward Bai Feng: "Little master... gotta pee so bad I'm bursting!"

"Trying to run, huh? Told you I'm a good guy—won't hurt you!"

Fat chance Kujue believed that! "Really can't hold it—let me step off two seconds!"

Ordering the driver to stop, Bai Feng escorted him behind roadside bushes.

Pretending to fumble his sash while eyeing Bai Feng hovering two feet away, Kujue pleaded: "Can't go with you staring right at me, little master—turn your head!"

Figuring nobody could outrun him from two feet off, Bai Feng glanced sideways.

The instant his eyes turned, Kujue tore off like a spooked deer!

Cursing, Bai Feng sprinted right behind! Bursting onto the main road inches from grabbing Kujue's collar—a passing carriage screeched to a halt, yanked Kujue straight inside, and thundered forward! Stranded, Bai Feng dashed back to his own rig yelling for his driver to give full chase!

Pure luck: that passing carriage belonged to the village head returning from town errands—spotting Kujue chased by a stranger, he scooped him straight aboard!

Gasping endless thanks inside, Kujue felt he'd just dodged death!

"Word is bandits roam outside town lately—keep your eyes peeled!" the village head warned.

Kujue bobbed his head nonstop.

Shadowing tight behind, Bai Feng watched the lead carriage vanish straight into a dense bamboo forest!

Checking with his driver: "Folks live inside that bamboo grove?"

"Yep—a bamboo-cutting village tucked inside."

"Just one village?"

"Only one! That rig's from there for sure."

Only one village—piece of cake to track down! Big breakthrough at last—no more staking out West Third Alley! Time to race back and report to His Highness!
