## Chapter 164: Spotting Kites in the Sky

Qiao Wenxian stepped across the newly sprouted grass toward the group. Sharp-eyed Wenhui spotted her brother first and rose to meet him. "You came too, Brother. Why did you not bring Sister Miao?"

Unwilling to speak Miao Zhiyu's name, Qiao Wenxian brushed past the question and chided her instead. "You sneak off to a pleasant spot like this and do not even call your brother?"

"I tried calling you! The moment you stepped onto the chuiwan grounds, you wandered around with Nan Shuang as if your soul had left your body. I shouted several times and you never even heard me." Mentioning Nan Shuang, she peered behind him. "Where is Nan Shuang anyway?"

An Mingxuan pointed a short distance off. "Look over there. The second he arrived, he ran off to hijack someone else's kite."

Sure enough, Nan Shuang was taking a kite spool from Huayu and launching into play, looking nothing like a lad who had just lost his sweetheart.

Wenhui laughed and scolded, "Brother, stop spoiling Nan Shuang, or he will forget he is supposed to be waiting on you."

Qiao Wenxian turned the scolding right back on her. "A young lady sitting out here among a pack of young gentlemen is hardly proper. Head back to the pavilion to keep Mother company, or go play with Wenxiu."

"Fine, I am going." Wenhui cast a parting smile back at Dongfang Chenze on the grass, bade her brother farewell, and strolled toward the pavilions.

The three young men walked over to Dongfang Chenze, and the other gentlemen around the table gave up their spots to go fly kites. Settling onto the cloth, the quartet soaked up the spring sun.

Dongfang Chenze poured Qiao Wenxian a cup of sweet rice wine. "You made it at last, Brother Qiao. I thought you were so busy getting ready to be a groom that you tossed the rest of us out of your mind."

Qiao Wenxian knocked the cup back in one gulp. "Do not mention being a groom to me. You all know where my heart lies."

"It is time to step forward, Brother Qiao," Dongfang Chenze urged. "Let the past stay in the past."

"Easier said than done. I just cannot let go. I envy you and Wenhui—lovers united at last. Why can I not marry the woman I love?" He poured himself another cup and drank.

Cao Ban chimed in to comfort him. "I just saw Youqi Jushi teamed up with Prince Yi for chuiwan. She looked cheerful enough—she has probably moved on. As a man, Brother Qiao, you ought to take it in stride."

Qiao Wenxian fell mute. The brighter Shuzhen smiled, the deeper his heart ached. He poured another cup and downed it hard.

An Mingxuan shot Cao Ban a sharp look, and Cao Ban realized he had put his foot in his mouth. "Listen to my big mouth, always saying the wrong thing. I will drink a penalty cup." He poured one out and raised it in apology.

Qiao Wenxian paid no attention, lost in gloom.

Dongfang Chenze cast about for a fresh subject. "Poetry gatherings have been popping up all over Lin'an lately. On top of the usual annual salons, I hear at least ten new ones have sprouted up, each drawing over fifty guests. When will a poetry gathering hosted by Dongfang Chenze make a name for itself across Lin'an? That would make me content."

"You always swore quality mattered more than headcount," An Mingxuan pointed out. "Why envy big crowds all of a sudden?"

"Turns out my old view was dead wrong. I figured having Brother Qiao and Youqi Jushi headline my salon would put it on the map, yet how many people in Lin'an even know I host one? Nobody is clawing over one another for my invitations. So yes, numbers are the key."

Dongfang Chenze felt pleased with his change of topic; even if Brother Qiao remained quiet, maybe listening would pull his thoughts away from heartbreak.

Catching his friend's intent, Cao Ban kept the ball rolling. "Here is an idea, Brother Dongfang. Since last Mid-Autumn Festival, you have collected a fair stack of Youqi Jushi's poems and lyrics. Bring those out and send invitations far and wide. Half the city will flock to your door on reputation alone."

"Brilliant! Consider it done." Dongfang Chenze turned to Qiao Wenxian. "Brother Qiao, you have to come and back me up when the time comes."

Qiao Wenxian drank yet another cup of sweet rice wine. "Count me in. If it features her verses, of course I will go—if only to honor the days when we called each other kindred spirits."

The three friends exchanged helpless glances and carried on with idle chatter.

Back on the chuiwan field, both teams had fought through the Middle Tally. When Chen Wenxin sank her ball into the opposing hole, the match stayed deadlocked, pushing them into the Grand Tally.

The referee set the ball on the pad and gestured toward a distant hole perched on high ground with no flag beside it. "One stroke each. This decides the match."

Seeing the steep rise, Chen Wenxin nearly gave up. "Maybe I should concede right now. Getting this far is already plenty. I will never clear a slope that high."

"Do not talk defeat before we swing, Sis," Chen Haoyu protested. "We will not know who wins until we play."

Buoyed by her brother, Chen Wenxin decided to give it a try. Qianli Xun caught Shen Jianchen's questioning gaze and gave a crisp nod to show she had no trouble at all.

More onlookers gathered at the pavilion rails now that the final round had arrived, eager to see who would claim the prize.

Prince Yi stepped up first. Setting his stance and gauging the distance, he struck with measured force. The ball traced a clean arc through the air and dropped neatly into the elevated hole.

Qiao Mengrong's heart had climbed into her throat; the instant it landed, she blurted out, "I knew Brother could do it!"

"Truly His Majesty's son!" Madam Qian applauded. "What a gorgeous shot!"

Having left his bride behind, Lin Jue had wandered over to the pavilion of Lin'an's eminent men—Qiao Shouren and Zhu Jingyuan among them—where wine cups clinked back and forth. Every man turned to watch the field and erupted in praise at Prince Yi's stroke.

Chen Wenxin stepped up next. Knowing her odds were slim, she gave it her best swing anyway, yet either her force fell short or the slope proved too steep; the ball kissed the rim of the hole and rolled back down.

"Young Madam Zhu is formidable among women players," Qiao Shouren remarked. "Reaching this stage is already impressive."

"Master Qiao is kind," Zhu Jingyuan replied. "Still, Wenxin's skill falls far short of Prince Yi's."

Qianli Xun came third. If Shen Jianchen could make the shot, she certainly could—yet sinking it plain and simple felt too dull. Why not put on a little show? Channeling her martial internal strength down the club shaft into the ball, she swung.

The ball shot skyward and began spinning in tight circles midair, holding every spectator spellbound.

Madam Qian gaped. "I have never seen a stroke like that! Matron Wei, Miss Zhu is extraordinary—do you think it will drop in?"

"If my mistress hit it that way," Matron Wei said with pride, "in it goes."

Out on the field, Shen Jianchen smiled to himself. You always have to outdo everyone else—even turning chuiwan into a stunt show.

Chen Haoyu snorted. "Show off like that, Zhu Shuzhen, and it will drop straight short—worse than my sister's shot."

"Nobody asked for your commentary!" Qianli Xun shot back.

"Hold your tongue, Haoyu!" Chen Wenxin intervened before they could start snapping at each other.

Even as Chen Wenxin spoke, Qianli Xun flicked a pebble she had palmed beforehand. The stone struck the spinning ball dead on in midair; knocked out of its spin by the impact, the ball shot forward as if guided by design and dropped straight into the hole.

A chorus of cheers rolled across the pavilions: "Bravo!"

"The match is decided," the referee announced. "No need to continue."

Chen Haoyu balked. "Decided or not, I still want my final swing so everyone can admire my form."

Since the grounds belonged to the Chen family, the referee gave Young Master Chen his face. "Go right ahead, Young Master."

Stepping to the pad, Chen Haoyu swung hard. The ball soared high—only to plummet halfway short of the target.

Shen Jianchen scoffed inwardly. Hah! Skill like that, and he thinks he can marry the woman I love? If she had not turned me down, you would be out of an engagement by now!

Never one to miss a chance to needle Chen Haoyu, Qianli Xun jeered, "Your shot was miles worse than my sister-in-law's, Chen Haoyu."

Chen Haoyu took it in stride. "Both Prince Yi and you are monsters out here. Losing to the two of you is no shame at all."

Glancing up by chance, Qianli Xun spotted kites bobbing in the distant sky and brightened. "Look! People are flying kites!"

Shen Jianchen stepped beside her. "Shall we go fly kites in a bit?"

Before Qianli Xun could answer, Chen Wenxin stepped up and curtsied. "Your Highness, the match is over and the prize awaits. I must take Shuzhen back now, so I will leave the prize to you."

Realizing he had pushed too close and caught Young Madam Zhu's suspicion, Shen Jianchen gave a polite nod. "As you wish."

Chen Wenxin took Shuzhen's hand and called, "Haoyu!" Handing his club to the referee, Chen Haoyu bowed to Prince Yi and trailed after his sister.

Watching the three walk off together—looking every inch a real family—sent a cold pang through Shen Jianchen. Unwilling to let Qianli Xun slip away so soon, he moved to wrap up the prize and think of a plan.

Not that he had to fetch it himself. Having wrapped up his chat with Kuqiao and returned just in time to see his master win, Bai Feng dashed onto the field, claimed the gold ruyi scepter, and hoisted it gleaming into the air.

"Prince Yi's team takes the Grand Tally! Victory!" the referee proclaimed.

Already changed into playing clothes with a club in hand, Madam Qian approached Prince Yi. "Your Highness, would you and Miss Zhu care to pair up again for a match against me?"

Having lost all interest in chuiwan and thinking only of how to get Qianli Xun out to the kite meadow, Shen Jianchen turned her down straight away. "I would not dare refuse Grandmother's gracious invitation, yet I am a bit winded, and Miss Zhu mentioned wanting a rest too. Better I return to the pavilion and admire Grandmother's form from there."

"As Your Highness pleases," Madam Qian said, turning to look for another opponent.

Walking back toward the pavilion, Chen Wenxin could not shake her unease. Throughout the match, Prince Yi had acted odd around Shuzhen, grabbing every chance to edge close and dropping remarks ill-fitting for a prince. Worried he might harbor untoward designs on Shuzhen, she scolded her younger brother along the way. "Haoyu, your wedding to Shuzhen is right around the corner, and you do not even think to take her for a stroll?"

"No thanks!" Qianli Xun jumped in at once. "I have zero desire to walk anywhere with Chen Haoyu."

"See?" Chen Haoyu grinned. "Not my fault—she is the one turning me down. I am off to look around elsewhere. Enjoy yourselves."

Chen Wenxin sighed in defeat. Push as she might, neither of the two cared a whit.

Back in the pavilion, Matron Wei had already returned and heaped praise on her young mistress. "Miss played like nobody before or since!"

"Since Matron is family, I will accept the compliment without false modesty."

Laughter rippled through the pavilion.

Just as Qianli Xun moved to sit, she spotted Mengrong approaching with Xilan. "Mengrong is coming for me again. I will go meet her." Stepping down from the pavilion, she greeted her friend. "Coming to drag me out for another walk, Mengrong?"

"Coming to invite you, yes, though not for a walk. I want to fly kites—will you come with me?"

Overhearing every word, Chen Wenxin stepped out of the pavilion. "Princess Jiahe is inviting our Shuzhen to fly kites—is Prince Yi going too?"

"Brother accompanies me wherever I go, so of course he is coming."

The answer poked at Chen Wenxin's nerves again, yet she talked herself down. Prince Yi and Princess Jiahe were betrothed; if the princess saw nothing amiss, why was she fretting over shadows? Perhaps Prince Yi treated Shuzhen with special favor simply because she was the princess's closest friend.

"Since Her Highness invited you in person, Shuzhen, go have fun for a while."

"Thanks, Sister-in-Law!" Qianli Xun linked arms with Mengrong. "Let us go. Where is the kite meadow?"

"Brother says it is on the big meadow right behind the chuiwan grounds."

The two headed off in bright spirits. All three of Shuzhen's maids moved to follow, only for Matron Wei to step in front of them. "Hongdou alone will do. You two stay here."

Hongdou hurried after her mistress at once.

"Why stop us, Matron?" Kuqiao asked in puzzlement.

"Use your eyes," Matron Wei replied. "Her Highness brings only one maid, and Prince Yi's guard is still standing in his pavilion. If our young mistress brings three attendants, would that not look like she is trying to outshine royalty?"

Noticing Bai Feng had stayed put, Kuqiao let the matter drop.

Xiaguo, however, frowned. "Miss always brought both Kuqiao and me along before, and Her Highness never minded—they stayed close as ever. Matron is being way too fussy."

Matron Wei fixed Xiaguo with a sharp glare. "Kuqiao did not complain, so what are you mouthing off about when you are about to become another family's concubine? Bringing two maids was passable before, yet marching around with three is flashy and begs for gossip."

Unconvinced, Xiaguo pushed back. "If you feel that way, Matron, why did you not stop Miss from taking Hongdou on? Or tell her to leave Hongdou home today instead of blocking the two of us now?"

"Sharp little tongue you have there," Matron Wei retorted. "Miss need not worry about you getting bullied at Qiao Manor. As for my reasons, that is none of your concern. Bottom line: when Miss goes to fly kites, the two of you stay put."

Left with nothing to say, Xiaguo sat back down. Her own future was locked in anyway; none of the rest mattered to her.
