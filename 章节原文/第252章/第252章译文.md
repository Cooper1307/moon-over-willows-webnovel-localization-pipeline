## Chapter 252: Investigating in the Bamboo Grove and Meeting Kujue

After keeping Old Madam Xia and Yao Qingyan company, Qianli Xun was strolling back toward Moxiang Courtyard when a gatekeeper from Chen Manor's south gate hurried up to meet her.

"Second Young Madam, someone is outside delivering an invitation card and insists you receive it in person."

Qianli Xun raised a brow. Invitations were always passed inside through the manor servants—who was putting on such airs as to make the mistress walk out to the gate herself? "Who's the messenger?"

"A maidservant. Calls herself Xilan."

"Xilan." Qianli Xun echoed the name under her breath. It had to be Mengrong sending her—could Mengrong have forgiven her? Without asking another question, she hurried straight toward the south gate.

Sure enough, Xilan stood waiting outside.

"Mengrong... how is she?" Qianli Xun asked.

Xilan offered a perfunctory curtsy. "Thank you for your concern, Youqi Jushi. Princess Consort Yi is quite well. Tomorrow Her Highness is hosting a poetry salon at Youlin Retreat for her closest female friends only. Out of high regard for Youqi Jushi, she sent me to deliver this invitation in person and hopes you will honor her with your presence."

Xilan held out the card, and Kuqiao stepped forward to accept it.

Turning Xilan's words over in her mind, Qianli Xun felt a warm surge of hope—inviting only her closest friends meant Mengrong still counted her as one! "Please tell Mengrong I'll be there right on time tomorrow."

"Youqi Jushi would do well not to call Her Highness by her given name anymore," Xilan reminded her. "She is Princess Consort Yi now—her rank stands far higher than before."

Overjoyed just to be invited, Qianli Xun read nothing sinister into the reminder, taking it as a well-meaning nudge about etiquette. Between old friends like her and Mengrong, what was a title? "Thanks for the reminder. I'll be more careful from now on."

Once Xilan departed, Qianli Xun took the invitation from Kuqiao, opened it, and read every stroke with care. She had glimpsed Mengrong's handwriting once before at Youlin Retreat—delicate, graceful, unmistakable, identical to the calligraphy in her hands. Mengrong had even written her card with her own brush! What else could that mean besides forgiveness?

Reluctant to hand the card back to Kuqiao, she kept it cradled in her palm all the way home, mulling over how to act if she ran into Shen Jianchen so she wouldn't hurt Mengrong again. Love was already beyond her reach; that only made her friendship with Mengrong twice as precious. With any luck, tomorrow's salon would mend the rift and give them a fresh start.

Meanwhile, Shen Jianchen led his men from the Judicial Office down to the stream beside the bamboo grove. A cluster of villagers stood watching and murmuring from a distance, not one daring to edge closer—and at the sight of yamen runners, they backed away even farther.

The coroner knelt to examine the corpse first, then rose to report. "Your Honor, the deceased is a woman of about forty. Cause of death is a dagger thrust straight into the heart."

Without waiting for orders, Wen Xin already had constables fanning out to sweep the area for the murder weapon.

"There is one more check," the coroner added, clearing his throat. "Her lower body needs to be examined, and as a man, it isn't proper for me to..."

Before he could finish, a woman's crisp voice rang out. "I'll do it! I'll do it!"

Shen Jianchen turned to see a woman in her fifties striding up. Skipping any bow, she shook out a bolt of coarse cloth from under her arm and called to the nearest constables, "Lend a hand here, boys—hold this screen up!"

Two constables, clearly old hands at this, stretched the cloth wide to block everyone's view and turned their heads aside so they wouldn't peek. The woman stepped behind the screen to check the lower half of the body.

"Who is she, Clerk Han?" Shen Jianchen asked.

"That's Granny Jiu, Your Honor. She works as a midwife and female examiner—whenever we turn up a female corpse, we call her in. Both she and the coroner are regulars at our yamen."

Wen Xin strode back to report. "Your Highness, no weapon turned up anywhere around here."

Shen Jianchen nodded and beckoned to the village headman, who hurried over. "What can I do for Your Honor?"

"You saw the body before reporting it. Is she from your village?"

"I know every soul in Zhuli Village, Your Honor. Never laid eyes on her before—she's definitely not one of ours."

Granny Jiu finished her check, folded up her cloth, and stepped forward to report. "Your Honor, the woman had no relations with any man before she died. We can rule out a crime of passion."

Shen Jianchen gave a faint smile. "Ruling out a crime of passion from that alone is jumping the gun." Turning back to his officers, he issued his orders. "Chief Constable Wen, have the body carried back to the morgue at the Judicial Office and tell the coroner to perform a thorough autopsy—he may spot something we missed out here. Clerk Han, check whether any household has reported a missing person lately."

"Yes, Your Honor!" Wen Xin and Han Gu answered together.

Gazing at the bamboo flanking the stream, Shen Jianchen remembered his very first chance encounter with Qianli Xun right after arriving in Lin'an—back when he didn't even know Youqi Jushi was her, and they had brushed past each other without a single word. Remembering it now stirred a tender ache in his chest.

Sending the rest of the team back to the Judicial Office ahead of him, he stayed behind for a walk through the grove. He followed the stream until he found the narrow path where he had first laid eyes on her, stood gazing at the boulder she had sat on, and drifted down the lane into Zhuli Village until he reached the village entrance—lingering right where they had exchanged that quiet nod.

Bai Feng had no patience for his master's lovelorn brooding. The bamboo grove and village were gorgeous, and he had rushed through last time without taking in the sights; might as well feast his eyes now. As he scanned the towering green stalks, a figure ducked behind a cluster of bamboo.

In a flash of light-foot technique, Bai Feng swooped over, seized the lurker, and dragged him out—only to blink and loosen his grip. It was Kujue, Kuqiao's younger brother, whom he had collared once before.

"What are you sneaking around behind the bamboo for?"

Unaware of his sister's romance with Bai Feng, Kujue eyed him with caution. "So that gentleman is the Investigator... don't tell me you're an official too?"

"I'm Bai Feng. Hasn't your sister ever mentioned me?"

Kujue shook his head. "Never heard my sis say a word about you. You know my sis?"

A stab of hurt hit Bai Feng—Kuqiao had never once mentioned him to her family! He puffed out his chest. "Remember this good and clear: from now on I'm your brother-in-law. Whenever you see me, you call me Brother-in-law."

Kujue broke into a goofy grin. "Hehe! Never dreamed I'd land a brother-in-law this impressive! Wait till Ma and Pa hear—they'll be over the moon!"

Validated, Bai Feng let his sulk vanish. "You still haven't told me why you were skulking behind the bamboo."

"I've got something important to tell the Investigator—about the dead woman."

A witness! Bai Feng snapped straight to business. "Come with me and tell His Highness every single detail."

"His Highness?" Kujue's jaw dropped. "A real prince came to Zhuli Village? And the prince's right-hand man is my brother-in-law? Am I dreaming?"

Basking in a few seconds of sheer glory, Bai Feng marched Kujue over to Shen Jianchen. "Your Highness, Kujue here knows something about that corpse."

Pulled out of his reverie, Shen Jianchen turned to face the earnest young villager. "You're Kujue? Kuqiao's brother?"

Kujue grinned bashfully, tickled pink that even a prince knew his sister—she must be doing mighty well in the city to bring the family such face. He wondered if he ought to call the noble gentleman "Your Highness" like Bai Feng did, yet the words stuck in his throat. At last he blurted, "Reporting to the Investigator—yes, I'm Kuqiao's brother Kujue. That woman was murdered, hauled out here in a carriage, and dumped by the stream."

Landing a murder case on his very first day as Investigator had left Shen Jianchen racking his brains back by the creek over how to crack it. Yet the moment he paused to walk in Qianli Xun's footsteps and think of her, an eyewitness fell straight into his lap. Stubbornly, he decided Qianli Xun had brought him this luck.

"Tell me everything you saw, from start to finish."

"Would Your Honor care to sit at my house? I can tell you the whole story there."

Glancing at the villagers passing the entrance now and then, Shen Jianchen agreed. "Lead the way to your bamboo house."

Since marrying, Kujue had moved into a newer bamboo cottage—the exact one Qianli Xun had stayed in during her retreat here. Re-furnished for newlyweds, its spare, poetic elegance had given way to cozy country clutter. Kujue led them into the yard, where a low bamboo table sat surrounded by four bamboo stools. "Please sit, Your Honor."

Shen Jianchen chose a stool. Looking up, lush bamboo filled the sky, though he set sightseeing aside for the case. "Both of you sit down, too. Now tell me everything."

Seeing Bai Feng pull up a stool without ceremony, Kujue sat down and gathered his memories. "My mother had a craving for carp, and midnight is the best hour to catch them. There's a carp pond just outside the bamboo grove, so I headed out after dark..."

Around midnight, Kujue had landed his carp, packed them in his bamboo basket, and started back toward the village when the rattle of a carriage caught his ear. *Who's driving a carriage out at this hour?* Peering through the moonlight, he saw it was coming *toward* the village from outside, thought nothing of it, and kept walking.

A few paces on, the wheels went silent. Looking back, he saw the carriage had halted. Curious why someone headed for the village would stop short of the entrance, he ducked behind cover to watch.

In the dark, a man dragged something bulky out of the carriage and hoisted it onto his shoulder—a large cloth sack, though Kujue couldn't guess what was stuffed inside. His curiosity piqued by the man's furtive air, he crept after him to the stream and hid behind a boulder.

When the man pulled the sack off, moonlight revealed a woman's corpse. Terror froze Kujue in place; he cursed himself for following, yet running now would only give him away, so he stayed crouched behind the rock.

Rolling up the sack, the man spoke straight to the dead woman: *"I'm the one who killed you, sure, but don't blame me—blame Fang Jiyuan."* With that, he hurried off.

"After he left," Kujue finished, "I crept over for one look, nearly jumped out of my skin, and bolted straight home. Next morning the headman found her too and went to report it."

Shen Jianchen repeated the name under his breath. "Fang Jiyuan... I don't know which characters write it yet, but having a name moves this case forward by leaps and bounds. All thanks to you, Kujue."

Kujue scratched his head with a bashful grin. "Glad it helps, Your Honor. Since you know my sister, you must know our Proprietor too. Next time you see her, tell her to come visit the bamboo grove whenever she has time—everyone in the village remembers her kindness and misses her."

"Proprietor?" Shen Jianchen blinked, baffled.

"The lady my sister serves—Miss Zhu, who married into the Chen family! Funny how things turned out: went all the way around in a circle, and the bamboo grove ended up belonging to her again. Whole village was thrilled!"

Shen Jianchen couldn't follow all the twists behind that remark, yet he knew at once who Kujue meant. "Where did Miss Zhu stay when she came to the bamboo grove?"

"Right here in this bamboo house! After she went back to the city, I got married and moved in with my wife."

So *this* was where Qianli Xun had lived. Shen Jianchen lifted his eyes to the dense sea of bamboo swaying overhead, imagining her gaze resting on these same leaves. Back then she had only just woken up as Zhu Shuzhen, still trapped in her marriage to Lin Jue, escaping out here for breathing room.

Seeing his master sink back into silent longing, Bai Feng got up, pulled Kujue aside, and dropped his voice. "Since I'm your future brother-in-law and I'm already here, why not introduce me to my future father- and mother-in-law?"

Kujue looked sheepish. "I'd love to, except they went over to my maternal grandfather's place today, and my wife went along to meet the relatives."

Bai Feng deflated. "Your wife went and you stayed home by yourself?"

"Hehe—somebody's gotta stay behind and do the chores. That's me."

Still, Bai Feng cheered up fast; at least he'd met his future brother-in-law. His thoughts swung back to his master—aside from brief flashes of focus on the murder case, the prince's whole mind had wandered straight back to Youqi Jushi. How on earth was he going to build a life with Princess Jiahe like this?

After taking in the bamboo grove a while longer, Shen Jianchen stood up. "Bai Feng, give Kujue the entire pouch of silver you're carrying."

Glad to put money in Kuqiao's family's hands, Bai Feng pulled out a heavy pouch and pressed it on Kujue. "Take it—a reward from His Highness."

Honest to the bone, Kujue waved both hands in alarm. "No, no, no! I don't want silver just for telling what I saw!"

"It isn't payment for your testimony," Shen Jianchen said. "I want to build a bamboo cottage here in the grove. Pick out a spot with a fine view and hire builders to put it up for me. Running errands, securing the plot, hiring hands—all of that takes silver. Take this for now, and if it's not enough, I'll have Bai Feng bring more."

Only then did Kujue accept the pouch with relief. "Leave it to me, Your Honor—I'll see it done right!"

Bai Feng leaned in to whisper, "I'll be swinging by the grove plenty from now on. I'll meet my future in-laws sooner or later."
