## Chapter 263: Trouble with Lazybones Village at the Congee Shed

With Xilan off to the Judicial Office, Qiao Mengrong tossed her book aside and sat beaming at the empty air, reliving Brother's touch.

Matron Zou stepped in, took one look at the glowing newlywed, and thought, *Look at her—hooked even deeper now. Better give her a word of advice.* Bowing, she smiled. "Missing His Highness already?"

"I started missing him the instant he stepped out the gate," Mengrong admitted without a blush. "I wish he stayed here with me all day like he used to."

"That won't do, Your Highness. A man needs affairs outside the home."

Noticing the older woman still on her feet, Mengrong gestured to a chair. "Sit and talk with me, Matron."

Settling down, Matron Zou spoke with motherly candor. "I'm overjoyed His Highness finally took you to bed, yet you must spare his health—don't wear him out too often."

Mengrong flushed. "I'll keep that in mind, Matron."

"His Highness is deeply devoted by nature. Now that you've consummated the marriage, his heart will belong to you. As for that Second Young Madam Chen, let her go—live your own lives and stay out of each other's path."

Mengrong straightened slightly. "My hatred for her has ebbed a little, yet it's still there. I'm not letting her off that easy."

"Then stir up a little trouble for her and leave it at that," Matron Zou suggested. "Drop the idea of making her vanish."

Mengrong frowned in fresh dread. "Matron... what if Brother sneaks off to meet her in secret? Plenty of people carry on illicit affairs."

Seeing that consummating the marriage had only sharpened her mistress's insecurity, Matron Zou sighed inwardly. "So you still won't let that poetess go?"

Mengrong hesitated. "First we'll make her life uncomfortable and see how she behaves. If she stays shameless and keeps eyeing Brother, I still won't spare her."

Having watched Mengrong devote her youth to nursing a dying prince, Matron Zou couldn't bear crossing her. "We'll do as Your Highness wishes. Only let me say one more thing: if His Highness remains devoted to you alone, that's your good fortune; yet even if he ever takes a secondary consort or two, Your Highness should stay magnanimous, keep the inner court in order, and guard your seat as primary consort rather than wasting energy on lesser women."

"You don't understand, Matron. Zhu Shuzhen isn't some passing fancy—she's a scar branded onto Brother's heart."

Realizing her mistress hadn't heard a word of her broader caution, Matron Zou sighed quietly. "Whatever Your Highness decides, I'll back you to the end." A pleased smile touched Mengrong's lips.

Over in Lin'an's east quarter, the Chen family's congee shed was a lifeline for every homeless pauper in town, famous for hot gruel and solid steamed buns that actually filled an empty belly. Word had spread so far that vagrants hiked in from rural counties just for a meal.

The compound held three timber canopies: a cooking shed with four massive cauldrons tended daily by two porridge cooks and two bun steamers—no watery swill here, only thick, tender rice porridge and hefty buns where one alone killed hunger; a serving shed with a long counter holding twin wooden tubs of gruel and twin bamboo baskets of buns, where clerks ladled two generous scoops and handed out a bun to every battered bowl held up in line; and a dining shed where vagrants sat on the dirt eating with relish, some even bedding down there at night so long as they didn't foul the floor.

Leaving Kuqiao back in Moxiang Courtyard, Qianli Xun arrived early with her ten martial servants to find the line running in decent order.

Steward Wang, the rotating manager on duty, hurried out in relief. "At last Second Young Madam is here! Now I can head back to the tavern."

"Not so fast," Qianli Xun barred his way. "That's pretty sloppy management, Steward Wang."

Blink-staring in bewilderment, he stammered, "Whatever do you mean, Second Young Madam?"

"I'm taking over fresh, and you're bolting without briefing me on a single detail of how this place runs—call that doing your job?"

"Don't misunderstand, Second Young Madam! There's nothing to brief—one look tells you everything—and the tavern's swamped today."

Seeing right through his half-hearted attitude toward charity work, Qianli Xun decided to teach him a crisp lesson: every Chen assignment deserved real effort. "Oh? Then tell me: how many vagrants eat here daily? How many men, women, elders, and children? How many measures of rice go into the cauldrons each day, and how many bowls does that yield? How many steamer tiers of buns, and how many buns total? And how many freeloaders are showing up to mooch?"

Hit by a rapid-fire barrage, Steward Wang gaped helplessly. "Second Young Madam has sharp eyes—I'm just a rough fellow who never tracked numbers, and I've only been here a few days. Every steward rotates through for a short stint; doubt a single one knows those figures. Please forgive me."

She wasn't out to punish him, just rattle his cage. "Remember from now on: big task or small, anything Master Chen puts in your hands gets done right. Go on back to your tavern—I've got it from here."

Thanking her hastily, Steward Wang scrambled into his carriage muttering to himself, *Second Young Madam is no joke! Hit me with a firing squad of questions right off the bat—spitting image of Old Madam Xia in her youth! No wonder the Chens ignored her divorce and snapped her up—and word is she whipped Second Young Master into shape too. Formidable!*

Watching the ragged, grimy line shuffle forward—men, women, young, old, nothing suspicious yet—Qianli Xun had Hongdou keep order and watch for troublemakers. Hongdou split the ten servants into two teams of five under herself and Asheng to patrol both lines.

Qianli Xun strolled through the cooking shed, returning the cooks' respectful greetings with a nod, then stepped into the dining shed. Huddled against a wall sat a gaunt, ghostly-pale old woman while a boy of seven or eight carefully spooned porridge from a chipped bowl to her lips.

"One more bite, Grandma—you'll get better."

Qianli Xun walked over and laid a hand on the old woman's forehead. "She's burning up with fever. Gruel won't cure this—she needs a doctor."

The boy burst into sobs. "We don't have a single copper! No doctor will look at her!"

Pity squeezed Qianli Xun's chest. "Chouya!"

Chouya jogged right over. "Yes, Miss?"

"Take this grandmother to a clinic right now." Pressing a one-tael ingot into Chouya's palm, Qianli Xun watched Chouya gently hoist the old woman up while the little boy trailed behind sobbing endless thanks.

No sooner did Qianli Xun turn to leave the dining shed than a dozen hands shot out around her.

"Help me too, lady!"

"Spare some silver!"

"I've got sores on my hands—send me to a doctor too!"

"My old mother's sick at home—have a heart!"

They demanded handouts with shameless entitlement, as though refusing them were a crime. Too many destitute souls crowded this era; dumping every tael she owned into their palms would only breed a swarm of idle parasites. Somehow they needed a way to stand on their own two feet.

Just then a fresh mob poured in—men, women, even sturdy older youths, not a single frail elder or toddler among them. Dressed in the same grimy rags, yet bursting with robust vigor, they hauled an old cart loaded with a giant wooden barrel and an enormous bamboo basket.

The instant they appeared, every vagrant in line shrank aside—even the beggars crowding Qianli Xun wilted, grabbing their bowls to bolt out of the dining shed.

Qianli Xun snatched one fleeing man's arm. "Just now you were bold enough to beg silver—one look at them and your knees turn to jelly?"

Wincing under her iron grip, he gasped, "I won't beg anymore, just let me go! If they catch me blocking their way, they'll beat me black and blue!"

"Explain who they are, and I'll let go."

"They're from Lazybones Village! Moochers all year round! Every big tavern has hired toughs to kick them out, so they muscle in on us beggars instead. Used to hog the old Anji Fang charity infirmary; once that closed, they took over the congee shed!"

"Where is Lazybones Village?"

"Up in the hills."

"Sounds far from town—they haul themselves back and forth every day?"

"Not too far, not too close—a full day's round trip. All the able-bodied men and sturdy women come down here while the old folks and little kids wait back home, living off whatever free grub they haul back."

"Got it." Releasing him, Qianli Xun watched him bolt out the gate.

The Lazybones villagers filed into the dining shed, barely glancing at the pretty young lady standing there before squatting down to wolf down porridge and buns. Qianli Xun held her fire, strolling over to the serving shed where the two clerks glared daggers while ladling bowls. Crooking a finger, she whispered brief orders to Hongdou; moments later, eight servants split into two squads of four and sealed off both exits. Qianli Xun settled calmly into the manager's chair to wait for the show.

The villagers noticed the sudden guards right away. One nudged his leader inside the dining shed. "Headman, look—did the Chens hire toughs to block us? Think we're getting any food back to the village today?"

"Only a handful of them, and there's a hundred of us!" the headman scoffed. "Pile on ten to one and we'll squash 'em flat. Eat up!"

Once they licked their bowls clean, the headman swaggered straight into the cooking shed. "These two cauldrons of porridge and two steamers of buns—all ours!"

The cooks scowled yet dared not utter a peep. Clearly previous managers had caved rather than risk a brawl, inflating these bullies' egos sky-high. Time for a wake-up call. Qianli Xun tipped Hongdou a glance.

Hongdou stepped right in front of the headman. "You've got it backwards, pal. This congee shed feeds wandering paupers—nobody hauls off whole cauldrons."

The headman snorted down his nose. "Well, well! Where'd this little wench crawl out from, bossing me around? We're wandering paupers too—what're you gonna do if I take the pots?"

"My mistress runs this shed today, and you aren't hauling off a single ladle."

Shooting a contemptuous look at Qianli Xun in her chair, the headman jeered, "The Chens used to send men here and even they didn't dare cross us! Some fresh little skirt wants to play hero? Butt out before you bite off more than you can chew!"

Checking her mistress's nod, Hongdou whipped a crisp punch straight into his cheek.

Staggering back with half his face already turning purple, the headman roared, "Oh, feisty little wench! No wonder you've got a loud mouth!" He waved an arm, and every able-bodied man from Lazybones Village swarmed around Hongdou. Over at the gate, Asheng tensed in alarm, pinned to his post.

With a sweep of light-foot technique, Qianli Xun vaulted clean through the air and landed dead center in the ring, sending the stunned villagers stumbling back several paces.

"So long as I'm standing here today, not one drop of porridge or one bun leaves this shed!"
