整体来看，这一章的翻译质量**依旧非常优秀**！译者在处理大段对话、解释古代背景以及主角的现代心理活动时，游刃有余。尤其是那句突如其来的英文 **"Yes!"**，把男主穿越后发现自己年轻了十几岁的窃喜表现得淋漓尽致，极具网文的趣味性。

针对你提出的审校维度，这章在**“特定文化词汇的海外适配”、“称呼的英文习惯”以及“场景描写的流畅度”**上，可以做进一步的打磨。

以下是具体的审校意见与优化建议：

### 1. 表达是否正确且地道？（核心修改点）

*   **P8 中式第三人称对话**：`"Brother feels that half the construction is enough for living..."`
    *   **问题**：中文里妹妹习惯说“哥哥觉得……”，但在英文里，对着本人用第三人称（Brother feels）听起来像是在谈论一个不在场的人，显得极其生硬。
    *   **修改**：应转化为过去时的引述或者第二人称：`"You used to say that building on half the land was enough..."` 或者 `"Brother, you felt that..."`
*   **P11-12 皇室专用词**：`The Sovereign's word is law.`
    *   **问题**：“官家”是宋代对皇帝的特有称呼。翻译成 Sovereign 语法没错，但在英文小说里，Sovereign 往往指代模糊的“君主”，缺乏皇权压迫感。
    *   **修改**：建议直接用 **the Emperor**。`The Emperor's word is law.` 更加直白有力。
*   **P29 塞钱的委婉表达**：`This is a small token of appreciation, nothing more.`
    *   **问题**：翻译得非常棒！完美契合了“不成敬意”。
*   **P33 表达稍显平淡**：`The Inner Attendant is close to the Sovereign...`
    *   **优化**：这里乔梦容是在解释“太监能在皇帝耳边吹风”。英文中形容这种能在掌权者耳边说话的影响力，有个极地道的表达：**have the Emperor's ear**。
    *   **修改**：`"These attendants have the Emperor's ear."`
*   **P57 情感深度的传达**：`Otherwise, who would her feelings be attached to?`
    *   **优化**：“感情找谁依托”翻译得略显直白。可以用更具文学感和网文言情味的词：**anchor**（锚定/寄托）。
    *   **修改**：`Otherwise, where would she anchor her affections?`

### 2. 句子是否过长？表达是否过于重复？

*   **“Inner Attendant” 与 “Eunuch” 的交替**：
    *   原文为了让男主有个“恍然大悟”的过程，前面用了“内侍”，后面男主内心吐槽“原来是太监”。
    *   英文处理得很好，但为了避免满篇都是大写的 `Inner Attendant`，在男主顿悟后，叙述文中可以适当地直接使用 `the eunuch`，这样能减少重复感，加快阅读节奏。
*   **P44 长句切分**：`Before, Prince Yi only ate in his sickbed. The kitchen prepared his meals separately, steaming and boiling them until they were soft and mushy, so they wouldn't harm his body.`
    *   **优化**：这句翻译得已经很好了，`soft and mushy` 极度贴切。

### 3. 标点使用是否合乎网文习惯？
*   整体标点非常规范。特别是破折号（em-dash）的使用，如 `The Sovereign's word is law. Even if Brother hadn't woken up, I had already made up my mind—to remain loyal to Brother and guard Prince Yi's Manor for life.`，非常符合英文原生小说的节奏。

### 🌟 综合精修版英文参考 (Polished Version)

以下是吸收了上述建议后的精修版。我在不改变你原本意思的基础上，让角色的对白更加自然，内心吐槽更犀利，更贴合英文古言/穿越文的语境：

**(P1-7: Exploring the Manor)**
Prince Yi's Manor was quite expansive—about the size of the public parks Shen Jianchen used to stroll through in the modern city.
There weren't many buildings; instead, the estate was defined by its breathtaking scenic landscapes. Fully half of the land remained undeveloped, intentionally left to overgrow with wildflowers, tall grass, and ancient trees. Reaching this untamed section, Shen Jianchen paused to take in the sight.
Qiao Mengrong had been trailing behind him the entire time. Clearly, her newly awakened brother wasn't as affectionate toward her as he used to be. In the past, he would have held her hand, fussed over her every need, and looked at her with adoring eyes. Even though he had been perpetually bedridden back then and rarely left his quarters, those few precious interactions had enveloped her in his deep warmth.
Her brother, who had miraculously recovered, remembered nothing. It seemed he had even forgotten the deep feelings they shared. Qiao Mengrong silently cursed Mengpo's Soup, but then reconsidered—if not for that soup, how could her brother be standing here, healthy and whole? Their love had been profound, but given his frailty, even if she married him, she would have inevitably become a young widow.
Thinking of this, she realized she couldn't ask for too much. Though his frame was just as slender as before, he now radiated a vibrant, masculine energy. Even his silhouette seemed infused with strength—it was the exact image of the brother she had envisioned in her dreams.
She couldn't help but step closer. Just as she closed the distance, the figure suddenly turned around. Qiao Mengrong froze, rooting herself to the spot with a shy flush.
"Princess Jiahe, why is half the manor's land left uncultivated?" Shen Jianchen asked.

**(P8-21: The Betrothal and the Age Discovery)**
"You used to say that building on half the land was enough for us to live in," she replied softly. "The other half was kept in its natural state simply because you loved the scenery."
After explaining, Qiao Mengrong ventured a small request. "Brother, you never called me 'Princess Jiahe' before. You always just called me Mengrong. Could you... call me that again?"
Looking at the delicate, blushing girl before him, Shen Jianchen couldn't bear to hurt her feelings. After all, she and the original Zhao Pu had been deeply in love and were engaged. "Alright, I'll call you Mengrong. But I really don't remember much. About that betrothal..."
Hearing him call her name like old times, her face lit up with joy. "As for the betrothal, there's no need to rush. The Emperor's decree states we are to be married next year during the Mid-Autumn Festival."
Shen Jianchen studied her again. Shy and gentle, she was exactly the type of girl many men dreamt of. "But if I hadn't woken up, wouldn't the engagement have been voided?"
"How could it be voided? The Emperor's word is absolute law. Even if you hadn't woken up, I had already made up my mind—I would have remained a widow in name and guarded Prince Yi's Manor for the rest of my life."
Moved on the original prince's behalf, Shen Jianchen couldn't help but ask, "You look like you're only fifteen or sixteen, the perfect age to just enjoy life. Why marry so early?"
"I'm already sixteen, which is the proper age for a girl to wed. If I delay any longer, I'm afraid no one will want me."
Shen Jianchen suddenly remembered this was ancient times, where both men and women married extremely young. Piqued by curiosity about his own body, he asked, "Mengrong, tell me... how old am I exactly?"
"Brother just turned eighteen."
*After transmigrating, I'm actually twelve years younger than before!* Shen Jianchen couldn't suppress a fist pump and blurted out in English, "Yes!"
Seeing the utterly baffled expressions on Princess Jiahe and the maids, he quickly coughed and explained, "I mean, I'm just too happy! I'm still so young. I got my wish—a second chance at life."
Qiao Mengrong didn't entirely understand. Just as she was about to ask what he meant by "a second chance," a servant rushed over to report: "Your Highness, Princess! An Inner Attendant from the palace has arrived to see the prince."
"Brother, a court attendant is here. We must go receive him," she urged.
"An Inner Attendant? What's that?" Shen Jianchen was still fuzzy on ancient titles.
"They are the servants who attend directly to the Emperor. He must have been sent by His Majesty."

**(P22-36: The Eunuch's Visit)**
Hearing this, Shen Jianchen realized the visitor carried royal authority, so he hurried back to the Main Hall with Mengrong.
Three people were waiting inside. The one in the center was significantly older, flanked by two younger lackeys dressed in identical uniforms. Their appearance instantly reminded Shen Jianchen of the eunuchs from historical TV dramas. *Ah, so "Inner Attendants" are just eunuchs,* he realized.
The elder eunuch stepped forward, beaming. "Truly miraculous! Your Highness is indeed a new man. When the Emperor first heard of your unfortunate passing, he was grief-stricken. But then the joyful news arrived that you had returned to life, completely cured of your ailments. He sent me to see for myself. And just from your stride, one can tell the sickness has entirely left your body!"
Though unfamiliar with Prince Yi's life, Shen Jianchen was a worldly man and handled the situation smoothly. "I am deeply grateful for Father Emperor's concern, and I appreciate you making the trip in person. However, while I was fortunate enough to survive and shed my illness, my past memories have vanished completely. If not for Princess Jiahe and Nurse Zou answering my questions, I wouldn't even know who I am."
"Is that so?" The eunuch looked intrigued, glancing at Princess Jiahe.
Qiao Mengrong quickly interjected, "What my brother says is true. When he first opened his eyes, he didn't even recognize me. Please relay this to the Emperor—should my brother make any missteps in protocol in the future, we beg for His Majesty's understanding."
The eunuch chuckled. "Forgotten memories are but a small price to pay. What matters is that he is alive and well. This is a joyous occasion. Princess Jiahe is very fortunate indeed."
Blushing, Qiao Mengrong accepted a purse from her maid and approached the eunuch. "Thank you for taking the time to visit. Just a small token of appreciation, nothing more."
The eunuch slipped it into his sleeve with practiced ease. "The Princess is as courteous as ever. I shall report Prince Yi's excellent recovery to the Emperor upon my return. Farewell."
After bowing, the eunuch and his lackeys departed.
Watching their retreating backs, Shen Jianchen frowned. "Why did you just give him money?"
Qiao Mengrong sighed. "These attendants have the Emperor's ear. Giving them some silver ensures they'll put in a good word for you. Even if they don't praise you, at least they won't speak ill of you."
Shen Jianchen was annoyed. "Aren't I the Emperor's own son? Why do I need a eunuch to put in a good word for me?"
"It seems you truly remember nothing," Mengrong explained patiently. "You may be the Emperor's son, but you have never been favored. When you were on your deathbed, His Majesty never visited you once, nor did he ever summon you. He only ever sent attendants with messages. Everything he knows about you comes from their mouths. They can spin the truth however they like. Bribing them is the only way to guarantee they won't slander you."
Only then did the reality sink in: Zhao Pu was a completely neglected prince. But Shen Jianchen didn't mind. Being unfavored had its perks—fewer political targets on his back and absolute freedom.
"From now on, when they come, don't give them a single copper," he declared. "Let them tell the Emperor whatever they want."

**(P37-45: The First Meal Together)**
"Absolutely not!" Mengrong immediately refused. "The silver must be paid. I don't care if the Emperor values you or not; I only care about your safety."
Touched by her fierce, devoted gaze, Shen Jianchen relented. "Fine, whatever you want. Anyway, I'm starving. Is there anything to eat?"
Nurse Zou happened to enter the hall just then. Hearing the prince complain of hunger, her face lit up. "The meal is already set, Your Highness. We were just waiting for you."
Arriving at the dining hall, Shen Jianchen stared at a table overflowing with dishes. He recognized some, while others were entirely foreign. He couldn't help but marvel—even an unfavored royal ate like a king.
Nurse Zou and Qiao Mengrong sat at the table with him. It had been an eternity since they had felt this happy.
Before, Prince Yi had only ever eaten in his sickbed. The kitchen had to prepare his meals separately, boiling and steaming everything until it was soft and mushy so his fragile stomach could handle it.
But now, he was sitting right in front of them, vibrantly alive, scarfing down a feast with relish—no different from a healthy, normal man.
"If you like it, eat more, Your Highness. You've been sick for so long and lost too much weight. You need some meat on your bones," Nurse Zou said, placing a choice piece of food in his bowl.
Qiao Mengrong simply sat there, mesmerized by the sight of her brother eating, as if she had never witnessed it before. 
And the truth was, she hadn't. When she was six, her birth parents had abandoned her on the side of the road. Prince Yi's carriage had happened to pass by. Hearing her cries, he stopped, took pity on her, and brought her back to the manor to raise as his own sister.
From the very first day she met him, he had been ill. He never dined with her. Even as they grew older and fell in love, they had never once sat across from each other at a dining table like this. It was a scene she had dreamt of countless times, never daring to believe it would actually come true.

**(P46-59: Revealing the Truth)**
Feeling their intense stares, Shen Jianchen paused awkwardly. "Aren't you two going to eat?" He picked up some food with his chopsticks and dropped it into both of their bowls.
Tears welled in Nurse Zou's eyes. "To see Your Highness restored to health in my lifetime... even if I were to drop dead this very instant, I would die completely content."
Mengrong lightly scolded her with a smile, "Nurse Zou, don't say such inauspicious things! You're going to live to a hundred and enjoy Brother's blessings for a long time."
With that, they finally picked up their chopsticks and ate happily.
Shen Jianchen could see how fiercely, unconditionally loyal these two women were to Zhao Pu. He felt a heavy pang of guilt and decided he owed them the truth. Turning to the maids standing nearby, he ordered, "You may all step down. We don't need serving."
The maids bowed and filed out of the room one by one.
Puzzled, Mengrong asked, "Brother, why did you send them away? They need to serve you."
"I have something important to tell you both. It's not for outsiders' ears."
Nurse Zou waited until the last maid closed the doors before nodding. "We are alone, Your Highness. You may speak freely."
Shen Jianchen set down his chopsticks, his expression dead serious. "Actually, I am not Zhao Pu. My real name is Shen Jianchen. I come from another era. I died in a massive earthquake and somehow ended up here. I saw it with my own eyes—Zhao Pu's soul was taken away by a golden light and celestial music. And then, I woke up inside his body..."
Qiao Mengrong giggled. "Brother, I never knew you were so good at making up stories."
"I'm not making this up. Every single word is the truth."
Looking into his resolute, unwavering eyes, Mengrong felt a sudden chill. She realized he might actually mean it. Panic flickering in her chest, she turned to Nurse Zou.
Nurse Zou's expression remained remarkably calm. "No matter who you are, since you awakened in this manor, inside Prince Yi's body, you can only be Prince Yi. You cannot be anyone else. I implore you, Your Highness—never mention this to another living soul."
Mengrong vehemently refused to accept that the man sitting before her was a stranger. If he wasn't her brother, where would she anchor her affections? If he had simply never woken up today, she could have grieved and moved on. But he *did* wake up—healthy, vibrant, exactly as she had prayed for every night. How could she let a single sentence like "I am not Zhao Pu" shatter her perfect reality?
"No! You *are* my brother!" she insisted, her voice trembling slightly. "You just lost your memory and can't think straight right now, that's all."
Shen Jianchen sighed helplessly. Thinking it over, they were right. In this era, "Shen Jianchen" didn't exist. He had taken over Zhao Pu's life, and to the rest of the world, he would only ever be Zhao Pu.
"Forget it. What's the point of arguing when no one will believe me anyway?" He picked up his chopsticks again. "Fine. I am Zhao Pu. And from now on, I will fully embrace my role as the Emperor's neglected, unfavored prince."
Nurse Zou and Qiao Mengrong exchanged a relieved smile, picked up their bowls, and happily resumed their meal.
