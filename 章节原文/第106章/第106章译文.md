## Chapter 106: Leaving Zhu Manor Amid Family Warmth

Once the maids finished packing, they stacked bundles and wooden chests around the room and called male servants to haul everything out to carriages waiting beyond the gate.

Unwilling to be hurried by the maids any longer, Qianli Xun walked out toward the gate on her own—reluctant to leave, yet resolute all the same.

Burning the manuscripts was supposed to happen after Zhu Shuzhen's soul slipped away—her final exit from Zhu Manor through death.

Now the burning had come early, and so had Qianli Xun's departure. Only by walking away could she step onto the destiny that belonged to Qianli Xun.

Eyeing her mistress's scraped knuckles and picturing the whip marks across her back, Kuqiao wondered which deity on earth her poor mistress had offended to get hurt twice in a single day.

"Once we settle in, Miss, go burn incense at the nunnery to pray for protection—or go see Master Jingguang again."

Qianli Xun saw right through Kuqiao: feeling sorry for her mistress and out of ideas, she was grasping at incense sticks in a pinch.

Kuqiao's mention of Master Jingguang reminded Qianli Xun of the Old Ancestor. Ever since returning to Zhu Manor, she had stayed away out of respect for the elder's quiet devotion. Leaving now, she ought to say goodbye.

Halfway down the path, both maids watched their mistress stop in her tracks and turn back.

Kuqiao asked, "Miss, where are you—"

"I'm going to bid the Old Ancestor farewell."

Without looking back, Qianli Xun headed straight for the Buddhist shrine.

Both maids hurried after her.

Qianli Xun knew every path in Zhu Manor by heart now. Just as she had grown used to calling it home, she had to leave—it was hard not to feel a tug of sorrow.

Maybe her crossed-over life was meant to be a drifting one: from Lin Manor back to Zhu Manor, from Zhu Manor to Wangyue Pavilion, and who knew where after that. Still, anywhere beat dying; as long as she drew breath, hope remained.

Qianli Xun treasured this second shot at life Heaven had handed her. She meant to make every step count and face whatever storm came head-on.

Lost in thought, she reached the Buddhist shrine. From inside came the steady clack of a wooden fish, unchanged from her very first visit.

Kuqiao knocked on the door for a long while before it swung open.

Matron Xiuzhen stood there again, though this time she did not shoo them away. "Ah, it's the Young Lady. What brings you here?"

Qianli Xun stepped forward. "I'm leaving Zhu Manor, perhaps for good. I wanted to see the Old Ancestor before I go."

"Wait a moment, Young Lady." Matron Xiuzhen shut the door again.

Moments later, the clacking of the wooden fish halted, then resumed after a brief pause.

The door opened again and Matron Xiuzhen stepped out. "The Old Ancestor says there's no need to meet, and wishes you a safe journey. She asked me to leave you with these words: life is only a journey from birth to death, and no road has just one waystation. At whichever station you reach, remember to pause and enjoy the scenery."

Both solace and timeless wisdom—gratitude swelled in Qianli Xun's chest. "Shuzhen takes her teaching to heart. My thanks to the Old Ancestor."

The shrine door closed once more. A deep certainty told Qianli Xun she might never see the Old Ancestor again. Remembering how the old woman had reached out to save her at her darkest hour, she dropped to her knees and kowtowed.

Both maids knelt and kowtowed alongside their mistress.

Outside Zhu Manor's side gate waited four carriages: two loaded with bundles and trunks, two for passengers.

Stepping outside, Qianli Xun saw neither her mother nor her brother and sister-in-law—only Matron Wei waiting there. No doubt Zhu Xiyan had forbidden them from seeing her off.

Matron Wei hurried up. "With Master inside, Madam dared not come out. Once you're settled in, Madam will slip over to Wangyue Pavilion to visit you."

Qianli Xun nodded and climbed into the carriage.

Both maids rode with her while Matron Wei took the carriage behind.

The wheels began to turn, carrying Qianli Xun step by step away from Zhu Manor—the place her heart had come to call home. The Old Ancestor was right: even home was just a waystation, and to reach the next, one had to leave the last behind.

As the carriage rolled through the streets, Qianli Xun paid no heed to the bustle outside; her thoughts clung to Wenyuan. With the Qiao family calling off the proposal, how was he holding up? Was he heartbroken, or pleading with his parents right now?

Master Jingguang had once foretold she would walk several paths of love. Were she and Wenyuan destined to meet without being meant to stay? The thought deepened her ache.

Through the grief, a quiet vow hardened in her chest: whatever the outcome, she would not give up without a fight. She would fight for her happiness so that even if it came to nothing, she would never look back in regret for failing to try.

The carriage came to a halt. Kuqiao lifted the curtain. "Why did we stop?"

Before the driver could reply, she spotted Zhu Jingyuan and Chen Wenxin outside and dropped the curtain. "Miss, Eldest Master and Young Madam are out there!"

Never expecting her brother and sister-in-law to come see her off, Qianli Xun hopped down at once. After greeting them, they drew her into a quiet side lane.

Chen Wenxin said, "Your brother and I didn't dare see you off at Zhu Manor's gate for fear Father would bring down the rod on us. So we made an excuse to slip out and intercept you halfway—to send you off and have a few words."

"Shuzhen is grateful beyond words for Brother and Sister-in-law's kindness."

Zhu Jingyuan said, "What's there to thank us for? We're family."

"You two still claim me? Aren't you afraid I've ruined the Zhu family's name?"

Chen Wenxin laughed. "Others might not know you, but don't I? Outsiders are talking rubbish. As for that line of ci you wrote, I love it! I memorized the whole lyric—want me to recite it for you?"

"Didn't Sister-in-law say you never liked my ci? Why take a shine to this one?"

"This one's different—it showed me a new side of you, bold and fiery! Your brother and I came to tell you: even if Father threw you out of Zhu Manor, we—and Mother too—are still your family. Anytime you need anything, come straight to us."

Deeply moved, Qianli Xun bowed again. "With the whole city spitting on my name, having Brother and Sister-in-law stand by me makes me..." Words failed her; before true family love, speech felt pale and thin.

Chen Wenxin caught her arms. "Your brother and I didn't come out here to make you bow. As for those filthy rumors, they make my blood boil! While waiting for you, we heard some idiot gossiping and I tore him a new one. Don't take any of it to heart—it's all idle chatter. You're famous in Lin'an, so the tiniest breeze gets blown up and twisted beyond recognition."

Hearing her sister-in-law's warmth made Qianli Xun feel guilty for ever suspecting Chen Haoyu.

"Don't worry, Sister-in-law, I won't let rumors get to me. An upright tree casts a straight shadow, and time reveals every heart. As long as I walk a straight path, people will see the truth one day."

Zhu Jingyuan signaled a servant standing nearby. "Bring it over."

The servant stepped up and presented a wooden box.

Taking it, Zhu Jingyuan handed it to his sister. "Take this. Father told me to give it to you."

"Father?" Qianli Xun accepted the box. "What's inside?"

Zhu Jingyuan explained, "With rumors flooding the city and the Qiao family calling off the proposal, tongues are bound to wag harder. Father had to throw you out to protect the family name, yet it broke his heart too. He told me to give you these land and property deeds."

Qianli Xun could not make heads or tails of this father. "He forbids you and Sister-in-law from seeing me off, then sends you after me with deeds? Doesn't he contradict himself?"

Zhu Jingyuan said, "Try to understand his bind. If we saw you off at the gate, he'd have to punish us—that's why we made an excuse to meet you here, and I bet Father counted on it."

"What a hypocrite!"

"Don't speak of Father like that."

"My old dowry is more than enough—why hand me these? I don't want them." Even knowing Zhu Xiyan's dilemma, stubborn pride flared in Qianli Xun, and she pushed the box back into her brother's arms.

Zhu Jingyuan pressed it right back into hers. "Why turn away Father's goodwill? Throwing you out already tore him up; if you refuse this, his conscience will weigh even heavier."

"So Father only sent these to ease his own guilt? Fine, I'll take them. Since I call him Father, putting his mind at ease counts as filial duty." Qianli Xun accepted the box and passed it to Kuqiao. "Keep it safe."

Zhu Jingyuan added, "Father also said he's sending men to hunt down whoever started these rumors. He suspects someone is targeting the Zhu family's reputation on purpose—once caught, he won't show mercy."

Just as she was about to dismiss her suspicion of Chen Haoyu, her brother's words stirred it right back up. Even her father suspected foul play.

Yet with zero proof and a sister-in-law who treated her like gold, she could never voice that suspicion aloud—better to let her father investigate. If it did trace back to Chen Haoyu, relations between the Zhu and Chen families would turn ugly, leaving her sister-in-law trapped in the middle.

Qianli Xun glanced at Chen Wenxin, whose warm smile melted every ounce of resentment inside her. Maybe she was overthinking things; with no proof, that troublemaker Chen Haoyu might have nothing to do with it at all.

"Tell Father I'm grateful for his trouble."

Bidding her brother and sister-in-law farewell, Qianli Xun boarded the carriage once more.

Kuqiao passed the wooden box to Matron Wei in the rear carriage, then climbed in beside her mistress.

Opening the box to find a thick stack of land and house deeds, Matron Wei sighed, "Master may look hard on the outside, yet he still cares for our Young Lady inside."

The carriages rolled onward through the bustling streets, yet to Qianli Xun all the clamor outside felt quieter than the storm in her chest.
