## Chapter 265: Calling on the Prefect and Trying Another Case

Zhu Xiyan received Prince Yi in person at the prefectural yamen, puzzled to see his former son-in-law trailing along yet daring not ask, standing in respectful attendance while Lin Jue hung behind him.

"Is Your Highness settling in comfortably at the Judicial Office?" Zhu Xiyan opened smoothly. "If the place feels too rundown, say the word and I'll have workmen renovate it."

*Pure hot air,* Shen Jianchen thought. *If you cared about renovating it, you wouldn't have waited till now.* "It *is* rundown, though making it too grand wouldn't fit a Judicial Investigator's station. Forget renovations—better put that silver where it's actually needed."

Zhu Xiyan cursed his loose tongue—one polite offer and silver was already flying out the door. Clearly the prince had come about money. "Please instruct me, Your Highness."

"I stopped by the Chen congee shed today, ran into trouble from Lazybones Village, and cleared it up. On the way here Commander Lin filled me in on the village, so I need two things from you."

"Anything within my power, Your Highness."

"First, send an expert in farming up to Lazybones Village to inspect their soil and figure out what crops actually grow there. Solve their livelihood, and they'll stop marching into Lin'an to mooch."

"Wise indeed, Your Highness! The County Magistrate of Linshui knows agriculture and soil inside out—I'll send him up tomorrow."

"I'll go with him."

"How could I trouble Your Highness with such a chore? Let me send Commander Lin with Magistrate Linshui, and I'll report straight back to you at the Judicial Office."

"Fine, leave that to you. Second, Commander Lin tells me Lazybones Village sits on plenty of mountain land—room for another hundred people easily. Have the yamen fund earthen cottages or cave dwellings up there and resettle a hundred or so of Lin'an's homeless vagrants into the village. Any difficulty with that?"

*So that's where the silver's going!* Sweat broke out on Zhu Xiyan's palms. "A brilliant plan, Your Highness—gives the village a livelihood and the homeless a roof! Only building cottages and digging cave dwellings costs real money; what we'd save on renovating the Judicial Office won't nearly cover it."

"Then the yamen covers the rest."

"Your Highness may not realize—Lin'an's yamen looks flashy outside, yet we barely make ends meet!"

Sick of watching a fat official cry poor, Shen Jianchen's tone hardened. "Cut a few banquets a year and you'll find plenty of silver. Better yet, His Majesty sends down annual welfare funds for the Juyang Yuan, Anji Fang, and Louze Yuan. Since Prefect Zhu has shelved all three, pour every copper of those welfare funds into this."

Cold sweat drenched Zhu Xiyan's forehead—had His Majesty sent Prince Yi to audit the welfare funds?! Thinking fast, he calmed down: if an audit were coming, his capital backers would have tipped him off already. Still, welfare funds got skimmed at every tier from the capital down—everyone in officialdom knew it. Better drop a subtle warning.

"Your Highness may not know, yet His Majesty has started realizing those three institutions bleed silver, so the funds coming down shrink every year—nowhere near enough."

*Think I don't know why they shrink?* Shen Jianchen sneered inwardly. *Rotten officials skimming all the way down!* "I want results, Prefect Zhu—how you scare up the silver is your headache. Once everything's built, I'll hike up to Lazybones Village to inspect it myself."

Testing whether he could talk the prince down, Zhu Xiyan ventured, "Has Your Highness considered that wandering paupers are like rainwater in a ditch? Drain one batch, and the next rain fills it right back up. Kind as Your Highness is, resettle one hundred and another hundred will pour in—can the yamen afford to house them forever?"

Shen Jianchen stood up and dropped one crisp line: "I fix what's in front of me today; tomorrow can look after itself. Make it happen, Prefect Zhu."

"We respectfully see Your Highness off!" Zhu Xiyan and Lin Jue bowed in unison.

Once the prince left, Lin Jue asked cautiously, "Where are we getting the silver, Your Honor?"

Zhu Xiyan glared at him. "If Commander Lin feels like donating, problem solved!"

"Don't joke, Your Honor—you know my meager salary and how tight the Lin household is."

Sinking wearily into his chair, Zhu Xiyan muttered, "If you won't cough up a copper, nobody else will either. Relax, I won't squeeze you boys."

"You've got a plan?"

"What else? Cancel this year's banquets, toss in the welfare funds—that covers half. For the other half, we'll have the Chen family shut down their congee shed and chip in the silver."

"We'll have to throw the Chens some favors in return."

"Obviously."

Returning to his rear courtyard at the Judicial Office, Shen Jianchen spotted Xilan waiting. "What brings you here?"

She rose and curtsied. "I arrived just after Your Highness stepped out. The Princess Consort sent me to tell you she started missing you the moment you left and hopes you'll come back to Youlin Retreat tonight."

Standing behind the prince, Bai Feng hid a grin—newlyweds glued together already!

Just as Shen Jianchen opened his mouth to agree, Han Gu hurried in. "Your Highness, a fresh case has arrived."

"See? Duty calls," Shen Jianchen told Xilan. "Tell the Princess Consort I'll come home once this wraps up, no matter how late."

"I'll repeat every word, Your Highness." Stepping out once the prince headed to the courtroom, Xilan swept a sharp eye over every nook of the yamen and exhaled in relief—not a single woman in sight! Her mistress could rest easy.

Inside the courtroom, Wen Xin and the constables stood at attention flanking a man and a woman with fresh scratches and bruises across their faces. Something about them looked oddly familiar, though Shen Jianchen couldn't place them until he and Han Gu sat down.

"Why, you're the Investigator!" the woman gasped. "You visited our house once!"

"You really did—you even hugged me!" the man chimed in.

"Drop to your knees before His Honor!" Wen Xin barked, startling both straight onto the stones.

Memory clicked. "Dongren Street?"

The woman bobbed her head like a rattle-drum. "Yes, yes, yes! What a memory, Your Honor—Dongren Street!"

"Your name is Qian Lixun?" Shen Jianchen asked the man.

"Your Honor even remembers my name!"

Han Gu froze with brush midair. "Hold on—*the* brilliant storyteller Qianli Xun?!"

Even stern Wen Xin broke into a huge grin. "No way! We caught the mysterious Master Qianli Xun right in our own courtroom! What's your next huaben about? Give us a sneak peek—I listen to your stories all the time!"

Qian Lixun flushed sheepishly. "Sorry to disappoint you, gentlemen—I'm *Qian Lixun*, written with *qian* (千, 'thousand' as in ten thousand troops), *li* (离, 'parted from home'), and *xun* (循, 'following the rules')."

Han Gu picked his brush back up in deflation. "Wrong guy. Makes sense—rumor says Master Qianli Xun is a striking young scholar, and you're way too homely."

Snapping back into his stone face, Wen Xin barked, "Kneel straight before His Honor!"

Suppressing a laugh, Shen Jianchen opened court. "State your names and why you're dragging a dispute into my court!"

"Commoner Qian Lixun."

"Commoner Madam Xue!" Jumping straight ahead of her husband, she rattled off, "Your Honor saw our miserable shack on Dongren Street! Women marry to eat and stay warm, yet I haven't seen one comfortable day since marrying him! For our child's sake I put up with scraping by, asking only that his heart stay true to me. Now he's defending some shameless hussy and calling everyone else liars! His heart's been hooked straight away from me—I can't live like this! I want a mutual divorce, yet he threatens to repudiate me! *Sob*..."

A domestic brawl—an imperial prince reduced to marriage counselor! Still, he'd declared no commoner's trouble was petty, so he heard them out.

"Don't listen to one side, Your Honor!" Qian Lixun protested. "I've tried to live decent with her. Knowing she had it hard with me, I never talked back, obeyed her every whim, even boxed up my exam books when she called studying useless and taught village kids for coppers to feed us! Yet after all these years she won't trust me—starts parroting street idiots cursing a woman as a shameless hussy! I lost my cool and tapped her lightly, and look how she clawed my face to ribbons! Of course I'm kicking her out!"

"Tapped lightly?! Look at my bruised cheek! Go ahead and repudiate me—you'll still never marry that shameless hussy, you'll die a lonely bachelor!"

"You—!"

"Hold it!" Shen Jianchen cut in. "Who is this 'shameless hussy' you're fighting over?"

"That female poet the whole town gossiped about last year—totally shameless!" Madam Xue blurted.

*CRACK!* Shen Jianchen slammed the magistrate's clapper onto the desk.

"Watch your tongue, Madam Xue! Youqi Jushi is the Poetess of Jiangnan, nothing like the filth you're repeating! Last year's gossip was malicious slander—stop parroting lies!"

Terrified, Madam Xue kowtowed frantically. "Forgive my loose mouth, Your Honor!"

"Sure act meek in court—why couldn't you listen to reason at home?!" Qian Lixun muttered.

"Youqi Jushi doesn't even know you two," Shen Jianchen pressed. "How did she spark a fistfight in your house?"

Qian Lixun pulled a thin booklet from his robe. "Because of this, Your Honor."

Bai Feng carried it up to the bench. Seeing *Youqi Jushi: A Fraud Without Substance* stamped across the cover, Shen Jianchen flipped open a page, scanned the vicious insults, and slammed it onto the desk in fury. "Where did this come from?"

"Someone was handing them out free in the west quarter this morning!" Madam Xue stammered. "Free paper is free paper—I barely read a word, tossed it on our table, and he saw it! Blew his top, threatened to march over and beat up the distributors, swearing the poetess wasn't like that! I asked what it said, he read me two lines, and seeing him get so worked up made me figure he had a thing with her—so I cursed her, we started shouting, hands flew, and here we are!"

Setting the couple aside for a moment, Shen Jianchen snapped, "Chief Constable Wen!"

"Here!"

"Take men to the west quarter right now! Confiscate every last copy of this slanderous booklet! Trace which printing house ran it and who paid for it—haul in a couple of distributors and the print manager for me to interrogate personally!"

"Yes, Your Highness!" Wen Xin swept out with the courtroom constables.

"Bai Feng!"

"Here!"

"Chief Constable Wen won't have enough hands—deliver my verbal order to Commander Lin to march soldiers straight to the west quarter as backup!"

"On it!" Bai Feng bolted out.

With that underway, Shen Jianchen's brow eased slightly as he turned back to the kneeling couple. "Madam Xue! Every word in this booklet is malicious slander. Is tearing your own marriage apart over somebody else's lies worth it?"

"How does Your Honor know it's slander?"

"Because I *know*! If you doubt me, I don't mind locking you in Lin'an's jail for a few days until you believe it!"

"Spare me, Your Honor! I believe you! Pure slander—fighting my husband over lies isn't worth a copper!"

Qian Lixun softened too. "You misjudged me, Wife. How would a guy like me ever meet Youqi Jushi? I just admire her poetry. Anyone with eyes can see that pamphlet twists her lines on purpose to trash her name—that's why I got mad. You didn't let me explain, just tore into me! A man has pride too!"

"My fault," Madam Xue sniffled. "I won't jump down your throat again."

Han Gu chuckled. "Made up just like that?"

"All good, all good!" Qian Lixun grinned.

"Not without a penalty," Shen Jianchen said, holding up the booklet. "Your punishment: go collect a hundred copies of this same pamphlet and bring them here to be burned."

Relieved, both kowtowed. "At once, Your Honor!"
