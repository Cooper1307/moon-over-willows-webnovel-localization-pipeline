## Chapter 329: The Imperial Decree at Prince Yi’s Manor

Inside Zichen Hall, Zhao Ji unfolded the sheet covered in circles and dots and frowned. “Not a single written character, so it meets the rule—yet what has she drawn here?”

He beckoned Liang Shicheng closer. Liang peered down and sneered. “Since Your Majesty forbade a single character, she scribbled nonsense to fool you. She ought to be charged with deceiving the Sovereign.”

Yet Zhao Ji felt a spark of meaning behind the marks. Summoning a junior attendant, he sent word back to the prison permitting her to write out the poetic meaning behind the drawing. Liang dared say no more about deception.

Soon the attendant returned with a second page. Setting both side by side, Zhao Ji admired the neat, tiny regular script—graceful, refined, revealing a mind as fair within as her appearance without. Reading the title, *Circle Ci*, he murmured in delight. “Splendid! No wonder she drew all those circles. Read the lines aloud, Imperial Attendant Liang, while I trace her circles and dots.”

Liang picked up the sheet and began reading:

“I wish to send my longing, yet find no way to send it, so I draw these circles in its stead.

The words lie outside the circle; my heart lies inside the circle.

A single circle is me; a double circle is you.

Within your heart is me; within my heart is you.

The moon wanes and will wax full; the moon waxes full and will wane.

Close and dense I add my circles; close and clear you must know my mind.

And for the endless love and longing past all count, I draw my circles all the way to the end.” [TN: Translator’s rendering.]

Finishing, Liang scoffed, “What sort of ci is this? Not even a tune title! Nor does it follow the rules of shi poetry—the lines are long and short, utterly uneven. A mongrel piece, if you ask me.”

“Your critique has merit,” Zhao Ji countered, “yet to my eyes, Zhu Shuzhen wrote something wondrous. When I bade her write a ci, it became one in her own mind. Our court’s literati always follow set tune patterns by custom; what harm if someone breaks custom with wit and skill? She is a rare talent indeed. Spread this piece abroad, then go to the Court of Judicial Review and announce that out of admiration for her gift, I spare her life.”

“Your servant goes at once,” Liang bowed, hiding his venom. “How long should she remain in prison, Your Majesty?”

“I first thought five or six years, yet seeing her talent—and Prince Yi’s devotion—hold her until the seventh year of Xuanhe. Release her on the Mid-Autumn Festival, the day of reunion.”

Counting nearly two years ahead, Liang felt satisfied. “Your servant obeys. Still, I will need an Imperial Decree in your hand so I can inform Prince Yi that Your Majesty bestows her upon him.”

Without hesitation, Zhao Ji wrote the decree and stamped it with the imperial seal.

After delivering orders at the Court of Judicial Review, Liang rode to Prince Yi’s Manor, where Shen Jianchen led the household onto their knees to receive the decree.

Liang unrolled the scroll and read aloud: “By Heaven’s mandate, His Majesty decrees: Though seated upon the imperial throne, Our devotion to rare talent never fades. Zhu Shuzhen stands guilty of attempting to murder a court official, a crime warranting beheading; yet treasuring her rare literary gift, We spare her life and sentence her to prison until her release on the Mid-Autumn Festival of the seventh year of Xuanhe. Observing her gentle grace and keen mind, a match well suited to Prince Yi, We hereby bestow her upon him. Chen Haoyu is ordered to return to Lin’an without delay and dissolve their marriage contract. Respect this decree!”

Delight at her pardon flashed across Haoyu’s face, only to turn to fury when he heard her given to Prince Yi. About to protest, he felt Shen Jianchen’s hand clamp down hard on his shoulder, whispering a warning not to get himself killed.

Swallowing rage, Haoyu bowed beside the prince. “This commoner receives the decree.”

“Your son receives the decree,” Shen Jianchen echoed.

Liang placed the scroll in the prince’s hands. “What blessed fortune, Your Highness! Hardly has Princess Consort Yi fallen ill than another beauty falls into your lap. Kneeling outside Zichen Hall paid off handsomely.”

Rising to his feet, Shen Jianchen held back his fury over what Liang had done to Mengrong, unwilling to ruin this fragile victory, and caught Bai Feng’s eye with a sharp glance. Understanding at once, Bai Feng slipped out of the crowd.

Liang turned to Haoyu with mock sympathy. “There are tens of thousands of women in the world, Young Master Chen. Give up competing with Prince Yi. You should have stepped aside when he crashed the wedding instead of letting things come to this. Go back to Lin’an, dissolve the marriage cleanly, marry another wife, and live a good life.”

Haoyu stayed stone silent, jaws clenched. Seeing Prince Yi ignore him too, Liang bowed out.

The instant Liang cleared the manor gate, Haoyu swung a fist straight at Shen Jianchen. “Now I see the truth! You only helped my wife so you could take her for yourself!”

Catching the punch in his palm, Shen Jianchen said, “If I told you that wasn’t true, would you believe me?”

“Never! You’re nothing but a hypocrite!”

Haoyu swung again. As servants moved to step in, Shen Jianchen blocked the blow and barked for everyone to stand back—this was between him and Haoyu.

Terrified his master would get hurt, Qinggua dropped to his knees begging Prince Yi for mercy, only for Haoyu to push the prince aside and shove Qinggua away, roaring at him to get lost and watch from afar with the others.

Haoyu lunged again, flinging punch after punch. Shen Jianchen refused to strike back, dodging with ease while clutching the decree in one hand, knowing one real blow from him would drop Haoyu flat.

Unable to touch him, yet forced to surrender his wife, Haoyu’s strength collapsed. He sank into a crouch and wept. “Why should I hand my wife over to you? I won’t! I’ll never give her up—even if it costs my life!”

Shen Jianchen stepped close. “With an Imperial Decree here, you have no choice.”

Haoyu glared up at the scroll through tears. “How did it come to this? You finally get her—aren’t you thrilled?”

“I want her, yes, though not like this. I want her to choose me of her own free will.”

“Liar! You’re thrilled!”

“Think whatever you like.” Shen Jianchen lowered his voice. “Regardless of the decree, I will respect her choice. Go back to Lin’an and dissolve the marriage on paper as ordered. When she walks out of prison, if she still chooses you, I will find a way to bring you together. If she chooses me, I’m sorry—I won’t step aside.”

Haoyu grabbed the prince’s collar. “Do you swear that’s the truth?”

“Every word.”

“Then never tell her about this decree. Let her believe our marriage bond still stands.”

“Agreed.”

“Words blow away in the wind. Put it in writing.”

After a brief pause, Shen Jianchen agreed. Inside, he wrote out the pledge, signed it, and pressed his thumbprint onto the paper. Reading it over, Haoyu pocketed it, satisfied, and asked for one private farewell visit to the prison before leaving for Lin’an. Shen Jianchen promised to arrange it.

Meanwhile, Bai Feng tied a black cloth across his face, tucked a black sack under his arm, bounded over the rooftops, and lay in wait along Liang Shicheng’s road. When the carriage rolled underneath, a dark shadow dropped from the trees, knocked both junior attendants unconscious with two swift kicks, dived inside the carriage, yanked the sack over Liang’s head, and rained down fists until Liang stopped stirring. Then Bai Feng slipped off, checked the street, and vanished.

When the two attendants came to and peeked inside, they thought their master dead until checking his faint breath beneath the bloodied sack, and whipped the horses straight for the palace.

With Prince Yi’s backing, Haoyu and Qinggua went to the prison, stuffing silver into the palm of every single guard without missing one, repeating that the poetess was his wife.

Silver worked wonders. The jailers treated him with deference and unlocked the cell door. Seeing his wife far thinner and more haggard than before broke Haoyu’s heart, though knowing she wore no chains eased his mind a touch.

Qianli Xun rose with misty eyes. “You came.”

Sitting beside her on the straw mat, she touched her plain hair and asked softly if she looked awful without makeup. Haoyu gazed at her tenderly. “You never look awful. In my eyes, you look lovely no matter what.”

“Smooth talker.”

“Young Madam, Young Master speaks the truth!” Qinggua chimed in earnestly. “You look lovely every way!”

She smiled through tears while Haoyu snapped at Qinggua to get out. Qinggua set his cloth bag on the low table and stepped outside.

“Your life is safe, so my heart can rest,” Haoyu said. “Tomorrow I leave for Lin’an to tell everyone you’re safe. Don’t worry—every six months I’ll come to Bianliang to see you, and on the day you step free, I’ll be right here to bring you home.”

Qianli Xun wrapped her arms around him. “All right. I’ll wait for your visits, and wait for you to take me home. Once we go back, I’ll consummate our marriage right away, give you plenty of children, and grow old with you.”

Holding her tight, Haoyu asked if she meant it.

“Every word.”

“Prince Yi gave so much for you. If he asked you to go with him… would you still choose me?”

“You are my husband. Of course I’d choose you. As for Prince Yi’s kindness, I can repay that in other ways.”

“What if the only thing he wants is you?”

She paused for a beat. “Then it will have to wait for another lifetime.”

Pulling back so he could read her face, Haoyu asked carefully, “What if—just suppose, not for real—what if His Majesty issued a decree canceling our marriage and giving you to Prince Yi? When you get out, would you still want to be with me?”

Qianli Xun stared in shock until he rushed to repeat it was only a wild “what if.”

“What’s going on in that head of yours, dreaming up something so unheard-of?”

“How would you choose?” he pressed.

“How do you want me to choose?”

“I want you to choose me, of course, yet I dread defying a decree brings ruin on everyone. I feel torn.”

“If even you feel torn, how would I feel? Stop imagining nonsense. Go back to Lin’an and tell our family I’m doing well here. You carry the whole Chen estate on your shoulders now—keep your mind on business instead of fretting over me. And… if you get too lonely while I’m in here, you may take a concubine. When I come home, I’ll get along fine with her.”

“I won’t take a concubine. I’ll wait for you. I want only you.”

Touched to the core, Qianli Xun leaned in and kissed him first. Caught by surprise after waiting so long for her tenderness, Haoyu returned the kiss eagerly until the jailer called time outside.

Haoyu pulled back reluctantly. “This one kiss is enough to keep me smiling for two whole years. Now I can wait for you with twice the patience.”

“Me too,” she whispered.

With the written pledge tucked inside his robe and her kiss warm on his memory, Haoyu set off for Lin’an with Qinggua and Kuqiao. Hearing her mistress was alive, Kuqiao felt easier, though she pitied her young master facing a sham annulment—and wondered what pledge he and Prince Yi had signed.

Once Haoyu left, Shen Jianchen never once visited Mengrong. Instead, he went to the prison every seven days to talk with Qianli Xun like an old friend, bringing her books and the Four Treasures of the Study—writing brush, inkstick, paper, and inkstone—so she wouldn’t grow bored. Seeing Prince Yi drop by regularly and Liang Shicheng stay clear, the guards moved her to a clean, comfortable cell and served far better meals.

With books and brush in hand, Qianli Xun read and wrote huaben tales in peace. Whenever Shen Jianchen visited, Bai Feng took her manuscripts out to the storytellers, and before long word spread across Bianliang that Scholar Qianli Xun himself was living in the capital.
