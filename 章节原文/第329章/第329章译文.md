## Chapter 329: An Imperial Edict Proclaimed at Prince Yi's Manor

Baffled by the sheet of circles and dots—which Liang Shicheng sneered was fraud—Emperor Zhao Ji sensed hidden genius and sent a eunuch back to let her write out the verse key in crisp miniature script under the title **"Quan'er Ci" (Circle Lyric)**, making Liang Shicheng read aloud while he traced every ring:

*"Longing to send my love with no way to send it, / I draw little circles instead. / Words stay outside the ring, / my heart sits inside the ring. / A single circle is me, / a double circle is you. / Inside your heart am I, / inside my heart are you. / The waning moon grows full again, / the full moon wanes once more. / Tightly I draw ring upon ring; / closely must you read my heart. / Still endless longing stays untold— / I circle on and on right to the very end."* [TN: Translator's rendering]

Brushing off Liang Shicheng's carping over breaking strict tune rules, Zhao Ji praised her unmatched ingenuity, ordered the lyric publicized, commuted her sentence to release on **Mid-Autumn Festival of the seventh year of Xuanhe** (nearly two years away), and stamped a formal imperial edict awarding Zhu Shuzhen to Prince Yi while ordering Chen Haoyu back to Lin'an to dissolve their marriage at once!

Kneeling to receive the edict inside Prince Yi's Manor, Haoyu nearly exploded until Shen Jianchen gripped his arm warning him not to get executed—while Shen Jianchen subtly signaled Bai Feng out the side door (where masked Bai Feng promptly ambushed Liang Shicheng's carriage down the road, stuffed a black sack over the eunuch's head, and beat him half to death before vanishing!).

The second Liang Shicheng stepped out the gate, Haoyu threw furious punches at Shen Jianchen—who dodged without striking back until Haoyu collapsed weeping on the dirt refusing to surrender his wife.

Moved by his raw devotion—and wanting Qianli Xun's free heart, never a forced edict—Shen Jianchen made an extraordinary pledge and signed it in ink with his thumbprint: *Go home and dissolve the marriage on paper; I will never tell her about the edict, and the day she walks out of prison, I will honor whomever she freely chooses!*

Granted a private farewell visit to her cell (after greasing every jailer's palm), Haoyu hugged his worn wife tight, testing her carefully with "hypothetical" questions about an imperial decree—and melted when she pledged to wait for him, refused to let him be lonely, and initiated her very first warm, tender kiss on his lips!

Carrying that kiss and the prince's signed pledge, Haoyu rode home with Qinggua and Kuqiao. Left behind in Bianliang, Shen Jianchen never once visited Mengrong—yet came to Qianli Xun's cell every seven days like a steady friend, securing her a clean private cell, decent meals, books, and writing brushes so her fresh *Qianli Xun* huaben soon took Bianliang's storytelling halls by storm.
