# Chapter 18: Willow Branches and Moonlight

The next day, Qianli Xun penned a note and sent Kuqiao back to Zhu Manor to slip it beneath the Buddhist Shrine's gate.

Mission complete, Kuqiao hurried back to Lin Manor and ran right into Huayu, Dongfang Chenze's page boy, outside the front gate.

One of Lin'an's premier young scholars—handsome, wealthy, and fond of hosting literary gatherings—Dongfang Chenze threw a salon almost every month, with Zhu Shuzhen always an honored guest.

Spotting Kuqiao, Huayu presented an invitation card at once. "Sister Kuqiao, my young master invites your mistress to a poetry salon today at Yongtai Teahouse by West Lake."

Taking the card, Kuqiao remarked, "Your master throws salons every single month—doesn't he ever get tired? My Miss is swamped these days, might not make it."

"My job's just delivering the card; whether she comes is up to her. Task done—farewell!"

After Huayu left, Kuqiao returned to Zhulan Residence, confirmed the note was delivered, and handed over the invitation.

Opening it for a quick glance, Qianli Xun asked, "Who's Dongfang Chenze?"

"A famed young scholar in Lin'an who loves gathering poets," Kuqiao explained. "Master always invites him to our annual banquets—that's how he met you. Whenever he hosts salons of his own, he always invites you too."

"And I usually like going?"

Kuqiao nodded. "Love it! Still, the divorce meeting is tomorrow—are you even in the mood?"

Cooped up between Zhu Manor and Lin Manor for days chasing elders, Qianli Xun felt rusty. Getting fresh air and meeting people sounded great.

"Plenty in the mood! If I always go, I'm going today. Help me fix up—can't show up looking messy."

Once dressed and groomed by Kuqiao, she boarded a carriage bound for West Lake.

Qianli Xun had always meant to vacation at West Lake back in her own era and never found the time—now transmigration handed her West Lake a thousand years in the past! She intended to soak in every view.

Stepping out by the shore, she found an ink-wash painting come to life: calm, crystal waters mirroring rolling hills and lush trees, little bridges over clear streams, ancient willows swaying in a cool breeze, wild ducks gliding across ripples.

Transfixed, Qianli Xun stopped in her tracks—until a woman's voice broke her reverie.

"Youqi Jushi, this is hardly your first trip to West Lake—why so captivated by the scenery today?"

Unfamiliar with the pen name Youqi Jushi, Qianli Xun didn't realize she was being addressed and kept gazing at the water.

Not until Kuqiao whispered in her ear—"Miss, Youqi Jushi is you! That's Miao Zhiyu, daughter of the Vice-Prefect of Lin'an"—did it click.

Right: Zhu Shuzhen, styled Youqi Jushi. Turning with a smile, Qianli Xun beheld a slender classical beauty radiating scholarly poise—even the maid trailing behind her looked refined by association.

Knowing Miao Zhiyu's name yet unsure how Shuzhen addressed her, Qianli Xun played it safe and skipped titles. "I come here often, yet scenery this fine never grows old. Today's view is extra lovely—couldn't help pausing a while."

"What happened to your forehead, Youqi Jushi?"

"Nothing much—just a clumsy bump."

Miao Zhiyu smirked inwardly. Everybody knows the Training Officer beats you—why bother covering it up?

"Heading to Young Master Dongfang's poetry salon?"

"Yes." Keeping answers short lowered the odds of giving herself away.

"Shall we walk together?"

Nodding, Qianli Xun fell into step beside her, holding her tongue. Miao Zhiyu kept quiet too. Soon a vast spread of lotus leaves carpeted the water ahead, dotted with lotus buds nodding in the light breeze. If only I had a phone, Qianli Xun thought—I'd snap fifty photos right here!

Noticing Miao Zhiyu didn't spare the view a glance, she mused how living next to beauty dulled the eye. To look like a local, she tore her eyes away and walked on.

Turning down a narrow path into a lakeside grove, they soon came upon a two-story teahouse. No trees blocked its front door—only flowerbeds opening straight onto tranquil blue ripples.

"After you, Youqi Jushi," Miao Zhiyu said with a graceful gesture worthy of a noble daughter.

Refusing to let Zhu Shuzhen lose on manners, Qianli Xun mirrored the bow. "After you."

Yielding back and forth, they stepped inside side by side.

The teahouse dripped with taste: flower arrangements on every table, famous calligraphy and paintings lining the walls while guests sipped tea and studied the brushwork. At some tables female tea performers displayed whisking art; at others crowds gathered to watch two tea performers face off in a tea competition.

A waiter hurried up, recognizing them at once. "Welcome, young ladies! Young Master Dongfang is waiting upstairs."

"Any new calligraphy or paintings hung?" Miao Zhiyu asked.

"Same as before, miss—new pieces go up next month."

Miao Zhiyu turned to Qianli Xun. "We've seen all of these already, Youqi Jushi. Let's head right upstairs—I heard Young Master Dongfang obtained a new poem by Yi'an Jushi and invited us to study it."

Yi'an Jushi? Li Qingzhao?! Qianli Xun grinned inside. Wonder which "new" poem he got—all classics to me, hehe!

"If it's Yi'an Jushi's poem, we definitely must examine it."

Following the waiter up to the second floor, they passed private booths each named after famous lines of verse: "Autumn Moonlight Bright on Cassia," "Yan Grass Like Green Silk," "Moistening Things Without a Sound," "Bright Moon Shines Between Pines," and more.

The waiter pushed open a booth labeled "Thick Dew, Slender Blossoms."

That phrase comes from a Li Qingzhao lyric, Qianli Xun thought, though the exact title slipped her mind.

Inside sat three poised, handsome young gentlemen sipping tea at separate low tables. Rising with warm smiles as Zhu Shuzhen and Miao Zhiyu entered, one called out, "Youqi Jushi, Lady Miao—at last! You kept us waiting!"

Aha—everyone called her "Lady Miao," and this host must be Dongfang Chenze. She'd ask Kuqiao about the other two in a second.

Qianli Xun bowed. "Apologies for keeping you three gentlemen waiting."

Miao Zhiyu bowed beside her.

"No need for ceremony, ladies—please sit," Dongfang Chenze urged.

All three gentlemen spotted the healing cut on Zhu Shuzhen's forehead, traded silent glances, and resumed their seats.

Just then a female tea performer entered with an assistant, took her place at the tea table, and began whisking tea. Everyone fell silent to watch.

Compared to Kuqiao's workmanlike moves, this performer's hands flowed with unhurried elegance—a feast for the eyes.

After watching a moment, Qianli Xun crooked a finger at Kuqiao. Kuqiao leaned in close as her mistress whispered, "Center one's Dongfang Chenze—who are the other two?"

A veteran of these salons, Kuqiao whispered back, "Left is Cao Ban, son of Military Commissioner Cao; right is An Mingxuan, son of Intendant An."

Locking both names into memory, Qianli Xun murmured, "How do I usually address them?"

"Always as Young Master." Answer given, Kuqiao stepped back into place.

Relaxed now, Qianli Xun watched the performer draw delicate lines across the foam with a slender bamboo slip. Once done, her assistant lifted the bowl and set it before Qianli Xun. "Please enjoy, Youqi Jushi."

Even the teahouse assistant knows her—Shuzhen really is a regular here, Qianli Xun mused, nodding thanks.

Glancing down, she caught her breath: delicate strokes on the tea foam formed willow branches curving around a bright moon. Too lovely to drink! People a thousand years ago lived with far more elegance than modern folks. Willows and moon brought back the earthquake—and Shen Jianchen. Where had the Divine Willow carried him?

The assistant set the next bowl before Miao Zhiyu. "Your tea, Lady Miao."

Bowing to the company, the tea performer withdrew with her assistant.

"Please drink, ladies," Dongfang Chenze invited.

Miao Zhiyu took a dainty sip, set her bowl down, and spoke: "I hear Young Master Dongfang obtained a new poem by Yi'an Jushi—please bring it out for us to admire!"

"Let Youqi Jushi taste her tea first before we turn to poetry," Dongfang Chenze suggested.

On cue, Qianli Xun lifted the bowl and gulped that poetic masterpiece straight down sip after big sip.

Setting down the empty bowl, she met four bewildered stares. Realizing she had drunk way too fast, she covered with a sheepish smile. "Parched from the ride over."

Dongfang Chenze chuckled. "I'll have the performer whisk you another bowl."

"No, no, plenty already!" Flushing slightly, she waved him off—no holding up the poetry session on her account.

Miao Zhiyu urged again, "Then let's see Yi'an Jushi's poem right away!"

Dongfang Chenze waved to his page boy Huayu. "Hang Yi'an Jushi's poem on the wall."

Huayu unrolled two scrolls and hung them side by side.

Every pair of eyes turned to the wall.

Qianli Xun cheered inwardly: Li Qingzhao's poem "Reflections"! With the prose preface on the right scroll! I know this one—no chance of embarrassing myself!

Dongfang Chenze introduced it: "Friends, Yi'an Jushi wrote this last year—not brand-new, yet hard to come by. Thanks to Brother Qiao Wenxian knowing a close friend of hers, he secured a copy and sent it to me by letter so we could all study it together."

At the name Qiao Wenxian, rosy color bloomed across Miao Zhiyu's cheeks. "Is Young Master Qiao in Lin'an, or still in Bianliang?"

"Were Brother Qiao in Lin'an, I'd certainly have invited him today," Dongfang Chenze replied. "Ever since Youqi Jushi married the Training Officer, Brother Qiao left for Bianliang and hasn't come back—word is he's staying at his nephew Prince Yi's Manor."

All three young gentlemen turned straight toward Zhu Shuzhen to gauge her reaction.

Flustered under three probing stares, Qianli Xun offered an awkward smile. Why did Dongfang Chenze go out of his way to link Qiao Wenxian leaving town with Zhu Shuzhen's wedding? What was between those two? Those looks felt awfully loaded.
