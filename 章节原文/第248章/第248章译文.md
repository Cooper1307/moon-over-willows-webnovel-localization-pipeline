## Chapter 248: Starting Out as a Shop Clerk

Steward Qiu did not rush to answer. He walked over, took the main chair behind the desk, and let his smile vanish into stone-cold gravity. "Since Eldest Master placed Second Young Master in my charge, you will follow my orders to the letter. Until you finish your apprenticeship, I won't bow to the King of Heaven himself. See that spot over there? That seat is yours."

Chen Haoyu glanced over. It was plainly a subordinate desk—smaller than Steward Qiu's, with a stiff, uncomfortable chair. He had assumed the steward's chair was meant for him, yet here was his setup. Fine, no point picking a fight over a chair. Pushing down his irritation, he started toward the desk to sit.

Steward Qiu halted him on the spot. "Not so fast, Second Young Master. Before you earn the right to sit at that desk, you have some seasoning to go through."

Qinggua, visiting the silk emporium for the first time, had tagged along behind his master taking everything in. He had assumed Haoyu would now kick back and rest in this nice room; instead, Steward Qiu had claimed the seat of honor and started throwing his weight around. *Young Master is going to blow his top and walk straight out,* Qinggua thought.

To his shock, Haoyu paused for a moment—annoyance flashed across his face, then vanished under a composed mask as he turned back to the steward. "What kind of seasoning?"

"First, you'll work two months up front as a shop clerk," Steward Qiu declared. "Then three months learning the books in the counting house. After that, you'll close three major deals on your own. Pass all of that, and you may sit at that desk and learn how to manage the shop under me."

"So I'm not even qualified to sit in that chair yet?"

"Second Young Master catches on fast."

"Then why bother bringing me in here at all?"

"If I didn't bring you in here, how else would you see my authority as steward?"

A flicker of regret pricked at Chen Haoyu. Had he known it would be this much of a headache, he never would have volunteered to join the family business. Then he thought of his feelings, and of the wife waiting at home, and hardened his resolve. "Did Brother Dong learn the same way?"

"Eldest Young Master started selling bolts up front like a common clerk when he was seven or eight. He's worked his way through just about every Chen shop in Lin'an."

Chen Haoyu set his jaw. "Then I'll learn the same way Brother Dong did. Since my father put me under your wing, Steward Qiu, assign me whatever you see fit."

Steward Qiu nodded in approval.

Qinggua stared in quiet awe. *Guess Young Master really has turned over a new leaf.*

Back at Youlin Retreat, Bai Feng returned from meeting Kuqiao to find Prince Yi sitting alone atop the raised wooden platform. Guessing his master was waiting for word of Youqi Jushi, Bai Feng climbed the steps.

Shen Jianchen spotted him. Every fiber of him yearned for news of Qianli Xun, yet the reality of their marriages forced him to keep his impatience leashed. "You saw Kuqiao? Is she well?"

Bai Feng knew full well the prince wasn't asking about Kuqiao. "Kuqiao told me it was Young Master Chen who spread those rumors about Youqi Jushi last year."

Shen Jianchen murmured as if to himself, "So she knows after all. I kept it from her because I didn't want to hurt her, yet she found out anyway. Discovering she married the very man who slandered her—how could she bear it?"

"Kuqiao says Youqi Jushi isn't holding the rumors against him anymore."

"Not holding it against him?" Shen Jianchen could not tell whether that should bring relief or sharper dread.

"Your Highness... there is one more thing, though I'm not sure I should say it."

"Speak."

"Kuqiao says Young Master Chen has reformed. He's fallen for Youqi Jushi, and he went straight to the Chen Silk Emporium first thing this morning to learn the business."

"Reformed? Fallen for her?"

Shen Jianchen could sit still no longer. He pushed to his feet and started down the steps, only for his stride to falter halfway down. What good would rushing off do? She was married. Chen Haoyu falling in love with her and mending his ways for her sake—nothing could be more natural. The strength drained out of his legs, and he sank down right onto the steps.

Seeing his master sit, Bai Feng dared not keep descending and sat on a step beside him.

They stayed there in silence until Matron Zou approached and bowed at the foot of the stairs. "Does Your Highness have a moment? I have something to discuss with you."

Shen Jianchen stood and walked down. "Say whatever you have to say right here, Matron."

Matron Zou glanced around. "This isn't the place for it. Better we speak in Your Highness's quarters."

Without a word, Shen Jianchen led her toward his courtyard. His brows stayed knitted in gloom the entire way, leaving Matron Zou reluctant to make small talk.

Once they were seated inside, Bai Feng had the sense to wait outside. He already guessed the matron was here to press the prince about Princess Jiahe—hardly a conversation for a guard to overhear. And if even Bai Feng could guess, Shen Jianchen knew it as well. Since the wedding, Matron Zou had held her tongue; today had to be about him and Mengrong.

When the prince stayed silent, Matron Zou rose from her seat to bow again. "Your Highness has likely guessed what I'm here to say. If I speak out of turn, please forgive an old servant."

"No need for ceremony, Matron. Sit and speak. Whatever you have to say, I will listen."

Matron Zou sat back down and spoke with bold candor. "Princess Jiahe is now Princess Consort Yi. Your Highness shouldn't leave her sleeping in an empty bed night after night. Why not spend tonight with her?"

Shen Jianchen said nothing.

"Back when she was still Princess Jiahe, I watched every bit of devotion she poured into caring for you, and it moved me to tears. Marrying a woman like her is Your Highness's blessing. You can't get your health back, come to Lin'an, catch sight of a few stray blossoms outside, and toss aside everything she sacrificed for you."

Shen Jianchen could not stop himself from replying. "I've told you before—I am not Zhao Pu..."

"Please stop saying such nonsense, Your Highness! If you aren't His Majesty's son Prince Yi, who else could you be? I know coming back from death's door wiped your old memories clean, but you're still a red-blooded young man. How can you stay cold to a wife as lovely as a flower? It must be because you've never tasted a woman's sweetness yet. Give it a try tonight, and by tomorrow you'll be stuck to the Princess Consort like glue! Hehe..."

Her chuckle died away when Shen Jianchen's face stayed utterly impassive. She pressed on in a more practical vein. "The Princess Consort told me you fell for a female poet after coming to Lin'an. You're a man, and a son of His Majesty at that—taking a fancy to a woman or two is nothing unusual. Bring her into the prince's household as a concubine if you must, but don't let some ordinary woman ruin things between you and your primary wife. Favor the Princess Consort as you ought, and enjoy your outside fancy too—wouldn't everyone be happy? Why make yourself miserable and break the Princess Consort's heart too? Besides, I hear that poetess is already married. All the more reason not to hurt your wife over her."

Shen Jianchen remained silent.

Finding she was talking to a brick wall, Matron Zou stood up. "I've said my piece. Your Highness can decide for yourself."

Once she left, Shen Jianchen walked out into the courtyard and gave Bai Feng a crisp order. "Come with me to the Chen Silk Emporium."

Bai Feng knew at once who his master was going to see.

Over at the silk emporium, Chen Haoyu had changed into a clerk's tunic and taken his post on the front sales floor. Watching for a few minutes, he noticed that every single clerk was a man, while almost all the shoppers streaming in and out were women. He crooked a finger, and Qinggua stepped right over.

"Tell me," Haoyu asked, "if most of the customers are women, why are all the clerks men?"

Qinggua blinked, stumped. "I'm just a house servant—what do I know about running a shop? Don't put me on the spot, Young Master."

"What good are you, then?"

"Running errands for you—I can handle that just fine!"

A clerk who had just wrapped up with a customer noticed an unfamiliar face in a clerk's tunic and walked over. "Hey, you new here?"

Qinggua opened his mouth to announce that this was the Chen family's Second Young Master, but Haoyu cut him off. "Yeah, I'm new."

"Then what are you standing around gawking for? Go wait on the customers!" The clerk barked, then eyed Qinggua. "You new too?"

"I'm his servant," Qinggua explained.

The clerk stared, baffled and irritated. "Since when does a shop clerk bring a personal servant to work?"

Chen Haoyu couldn't keep the act up any longer. "I'm the Chen family's Second Young Master. Why can't I bring a servant?"

The clerk deflated on the spot, plastering on an apologetic grin. "Oh! Second Young Master! Forgive my blind eyes—please carry on, I'll go see to the customers."

Now that the clerk was all bows and scrapes, Haoyu lost the thrill of it and wished he had kept his mouth shut. Still, Steward Qiu had paraded him around the workshops earlier; half the staff probably knew who he was already, and the rest would find out soon enough. Looking at Qinggua beside him, he decided working as a clerk while trailing a servant did look silly. "Qinggua, why don't you head back and leave me to it?"

"No way!" Qinggua dug in his heels. "I'm sticking by you, Young Master. Worst case, I'll put on a clerk's tunic and be a clerk too."

Haoyu laughed. "Fair point. Go get changed, then."

While Qinggua went to change, Haoyu studied how the other clerks handled buyers. The moment someone walked in, they stepped forward with a warm smile, led the shopper to the silk racks, and talked through each fabric one by one. Looked simple enough. He might as well try his hand.

Just then a young woman walked in. Haoyu hurried up with a bright smile. "What can I help you find today, Miss?"

She walked deeper into the shop as she answered, "I'm looking for cloth to make an everyday robe for my father."

"What a filial daughter!" Haoyu praised her, adding with a wave of his hand, "Our Chen emporium has every fabric under the sun. Take your pick!"

She paused, looking around with a knit brow. "I don't know which one to choose. Give me a recommendation."

Haoyu pointed to a nearby bolt that looked fine to him. "How about that one, Miss?"

She shook her head. "That's women's silk. Hardly suitable for my father."

Mixing up men's and women's cloth on his very first customer sent heat rushing to Haoyu's ears, though he had no time to kick himself. "Over there, Miss—those are for men."

She walked over, fingered several bolts, and stayed undecided. "Tell me about these fabrics so I can compare them and pick the right one for my father."

Haoyu froze. All his life he had only worn clothes—he had never paid attention to what they were woven from. "There's... not much to explain. If the texture feels nice and you like the color, just buy it."

The customer's face soured. "Whenever I've shopped at the Chen Silk Emporium before, the clerks always explained every bolt in detail. How do you not know a single thing?"

Another clerk hurried over with an apologetic smile. "Don't be upset, Miss. He's brand new and still learning the ropes. Let me walk you through them."

Forced to step aside, Haoyu stood back and listened closely.

"Everything in front of you is silk gauze," the clerk said with smooth ease. "Light in weight, fine in thread, tight and durable in the weave, yet airy with tiny open pores—ideal for summer robes. We carry weft-ribbed gauze, warp-ribbed gauze, and figured gauze in every pattern you could want. I overheard you say it's for your father's everyday wear—this plain-weave gauze without brocade patterns would suit him best."

The young woman beamed. "Wrap up the plain-weave gauze for me, please."

"Right away, Miss! Just a moment."

Working the counter was no joke after all, Haoyu realized. He had a lot of learning ahead of him.

Qinggua, already changed into a clerk's tunic, had watched his master get edged aside and sidled up to console him. "It's your first day, Young Master—nobody expects you to know it all. Give it some time and you'll outshine all of them."

"There," Haoyu grinned. "Finally said something I like hearing."

The clerk tied up the parcel and handed it over. "Turn right when you step outside, Miss, and you'll find the Chen tailor shop. Since you bought your fabric here, tailoring is half price. And if you ever want ready-made garments, our ready-to-wear shop is right next door."

Taking her bundle, the young woman walked past Haoyu and tossed him a sharp glance. "Take notes!"

Once she stepped outside, the clerk bowed to Haoyu. "Forgive me for taking your customer, Second Young Master."

Haoyu waved it off. "You did the right thing. What's your name?"

"Ergui, Second Young Master."

"From now on, you're in charge of training me. Teach me everything a shop clerk needs to know."

"I wouldn't dare—"

"What's there to dare or not dare? I'm ordering you to teach me, so teach me well—or I'll have Steward Qiu fire you."

"I'll teach you, I'll teach you! Please don't have Steward Qiu fire me, Second Young Master!"

"That's more like it."

In the reception parlor, Chen Haodong finished his negotiation and walked the visiting merchants out. Passing the sales floor, he caught sight of his cousin dressed like a common clerk, listening meekly while Ergui lectured him on silk weaves. A sneer curled Haodong's lip.

With shoppers everywhere, he held his tongue until the merchants departed, then walked over and tugged Haoyu's arm. "Stop letting these clerks feed you nonsense. Come walk around town with me—I'll teach you as we go."

Part of Haoyu itched to slip outside for a stroll, yet if he hadn't even mastered what a junior clerk knew, how could he follow his cousin's lessons? He shook his head. "You know way more advanced stuff than I can handle right now, Brother Dong. Once I get the basics down, I'll come learn from you."

Afraid pushing harder would make Haoyu suspicious of his motives, Haodong dropped the offer and addressed the nearby clerks instead. "Listen up, all of you. He's my younger brother—don't you dare neglect him."

"Rest assured, Eldest Young Master," the clerks chorused. "We'll look after Second Young Master."

Stepping out of the emporium in a foul mood, Chen Haodong spotted Scarface and his cronies loitering across the street. *Right on time,* he thought. *With them dangling temptation under his nose, Haoyu won't resist for long.* He shot Scarface a knowing glance, climbed into his carriage, and rolled away.
