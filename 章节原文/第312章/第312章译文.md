## Chapter 312: Time Flies Like a White Colt

Fresh tears welled in Qiao Mengrong’s eyes as she pivoted fast to shift the heat. “You can’t put Mount Tianmu all on me, Brother! Master Jiang’s wife—that ex-courtesan Chun Jinmo—hated Zhu Shuzhen for stealing Chen Haoyu and climbed Mount Tianmu herself to talk Black-Fiend Tiger into it! I only lost my head and got Chen Haodong’s map!”

The instant he heard Master Jiang’s wife was involved, Shen Jianchen dropped his lecture and strode straight for the door.

“Where are you going, Brother?!” Qiao Mengrong cried after him—receiving no answer except his shout summoning Bai Feng to ride to Master Jiang’s house.

Sinking onto the couch, Qiao Mengrong clenched her fists: smooth promises meant nothing when Zhu Shuzhen’s name came up!

Stepping back inside, Xilan urged softly, “Heard everything outside, Your Highness—please quit trying to kill the poetess.”

Wiping her dry-again cheeks—she had rehearsed that tearful confession days ago—Qiao Mengrong narrowed her eyes. “Quit?! Saw him bolt straight out the door for her again! When has he ever done that for me?! No more clumsy amateurs—next time I pick the right blade, stay spotless before Brother, and finish Zhu Shuzhen in one blow.”

“Picked someone already, Your Highness?”

“Picked him—just waiting for the opening. I’m writing Imperial Attendant Liang right now; send a courier straight to Bianliang.”

Over at the riverfront manor, a month locked indoors had finally broken Chun Jinmo’s illusions. Sold into a brothel by kin, robbed of Young Master Chen, stuck with a homely husband, sacrificing her body to Black-Fiend Tiger for nothing—where else could she go if Master Jiang tossed her out? Better make peace with reality and hold onto the one man who prized her.

Summoning Master Jiang, she dropped to her knees in tears, confessed everything, and swore to be his faithful wife from then on. Too in love to stay hard, Master Jiang forgave her—and had barely lifted her to her feet when a fist slammed into his back and floored him!

Scrambling up to face a furious Prince Yi, Master Jiang hit his knees with Chun Jinmo shaking beside him. “Calm your wrath, Your Highness—how did I offend you?!”

“Sending thugs to ambush Youqi Jushi, then colluding with Black-Fiend Tiger and wrecking Chen Manor—and you ask how you offended me?!”

Seeing Prince Yi knew the whole mess, Master Jiang hurried to explain how he’d sent bottom-tier scrubs on purpose so she’d walk away unscathed—and Chun Jinmo cut in owning up to Mount Tianmu entirely on her own, while Master Jiang explained he’d beaten her and locked her up a full month the day he found out.

Seeing their genuine remorse—and grateful Qianli Xun was unharmed—Shen Jianchen reined his anger in with one parting warning: “Outside real bouts, my fists don’t hit women. Catch your wife scheming once more, though, and I won’t guarantee she dodges them!”

Crumpled flat on the floor, Chun Jinmo trembled speechless while Master Jiang swore it would never happen again.

Once Prince Yi left, Master Jiang hauled his weeping wife up. “Told you! He’d kill for Youqi Jushi! Settle down with me and forget Young Master Chen—none of them treat you half as good as me.”

Throwing her arms around him sobbing, Chun Jinmo cried, “I was blind, Husband—nobody in this world loves me like you! I’ll never call you ugly again!”

Pulling her close, he chuckled warm. “Real men aren’t judged by pretty faces—you stay pretty, I’ll earn the silver, and in a year we’ll have a fat baby boy with your looks!” Smiling through her tears, Chun Jinmo finally let go of the past.

Riding home in his carriage, Shen Jianchen blamed himself again—since Mengrong repented, turning cold would only push her darker. Better wipe the slate clean and cherish her properly.

Sensing his master’s heavy mood, Bai Feng tried steering him to brighter news. “Our bamboo cottage is the prettiest in Zhuli Village—when’s Your Highness coming to see it? Clerk Han said you planned to invite Young Master Chen and Youqi Jushi over...”

“Thought about it—forget it now,” Shen Jianchen murmured.

“When are you moving in, then?”

“Leave it sitting for now.”

Baffled why his master dropped heaps of silver on an empty cottage, Bai Feng said he’d have Kujue keep watch over it.

Peace settled over Lin’an for the months that followed. As the heir’s wife, Qianli Xun learned household management beside Yao Qingyan each day and wrote huaben tales in her spare hours—stories now printed in bookshops and sung across every street under the name Scholar Qianli Xun, including a tale inspired by Ye Anya, who had eloped away from Lin’an with Cao Ban to start fresh. Chen Haoyu trained hard under his father and uncles, making rapid strides while never missing a single day whisking his wife’s morning and evening tea.

Meanwhile, Qiao Mengrong drank her warming tonic daily, basking in her brother’s attentive visits and West Lake outings—yet still feeling his heart remained out of reach, with zero reply yet from Imperial Attendant Liang in Bianliang.

Autumn chill arrived right as both Qiao Wenxian’s wife and concubine turned up pregnant. Following Black-Fiend Tiger’s autumn execution, Lazybones Village’s housing project wrapped up. Shen Jianchen relocated every vagrant in Lin’an there: wild greens sold fresh in warm months and dried for winter made the able-bodied self-sufficient, while elderly and infirm newcomers received monthly stipends funded by the yamen, wealthy donors, and confiscated illicit silver like Scarface’s hundred fifty taels. Chen Manor dismantled its congee shed, beggars vanished from Lin’an’s streets, and citizens sang Prince Yi’s praises.

Then an imperial edict dropped like a bolt out of a clear sky: Zhu Xiyan was stripped of Prefect of Lin’an and forced into retirement, replaced by a newcomer named Li Ke—known crony of Imperial Attendant Liang.

Too young to retire, Zhu Xiyan knew the score immediately: backing Prince Yi and quitting bribes had painted a bull’s-eye on him—killing the chicken to scare the monkey.

When Shen Jianchen hurried to Zhu Manor in guilt, Zhu Xiyan sighed calm. “In a rotten court, doing right is swimming upstream. I’m retiring clean ahead of you—feels lighter already! Watch your back, Your Highness: the new Prefect didn’t even pay respects to the Military Commissioner, clearly backed by Imperial Attendant Liang.”

“Let him ignore me—I’ll sit tight at the Judicial Office and see what Liang Shicheng pulls next.”

Once Prince Yi left, Zhu Xiyan urged his son Zhu Jingyuan to resign his yamen clerkship before Li Ke squeezed him out and run the family estates instead.

Arriving from Chen Manor to check on her father, Qianli Xun met her cheerful brother outside and walked in on Zhu Xiyan scolding tearful Madam Xiang—“Lost an office, not my head!”—ordering a full year’s severance paid to dismiss every singing and dancing girl in the house so he could write poetry in peace.

“Already famous, Father—saw two of your poems in a collection yesterday!” Qianli Xun laughed, cheering both parents at once—Madam Xiang thrilled both to see her daughter and to finally clear the back courtyard of entertainers.

Teasing him further, Qianli Xun let a modern slip fly: “Better out of office anyway—odds are the guy sitting on the throne ends up a prisoner in a few years!”

Zhu Xiyan and Zhu Jingyuan jumped in alarm—“Never say that outside or lose our heads! New Prefect Li Ke answers straight to Liang Shicheng and doesn’t even care about Prince Yi!” Chastened, Qianli Xun promised to bite her tongue.
