# Chapter 27: Pure Light in Darkness

From outside, both caves looked identical—except Master Jingguang's cave had its wooden window propped half open. Peering inside, Qianli Xun saw only pitch darkness.

Weird—why was the first cave bright with shut windows while the Abbess's cave was dark with an open one? Then she remembered: oil lamps had been burning inside the first cave; too busy with her three trials, she hadn't even noticed.

Just then a butterfly drifted straight out of the half-open window—bigger and lovelier than any butterfly Qianli Xun had ever seen, electric blue wings edged in velvet black. Fluttering in a circle around her, it landed right on the back of her outstretched hand instead of fleeing, softening her impulse to catch it into quiet awe.

Master Cixuan called toward the cave: "Abbess, the lady benefactor awaits outside."

The instant she spoke, the window pulled shut from within. The cave door opened to reveal the same nun who had whispered to Cixuan earlier: "Let her enter."

As if understanding human speech, the blue butterfly lifted off and vanished.

Watching it flutter away, Qianli Xun stepped toward the cave entrance.

Kuqiao started in behind her, only for Master Cixuan to block her: "Stay outside and wait."

Halting, Kuqiao waited until Cixuan walked off before taking in the quiet yard outside the caves.

Swept spotless and spare, it held only one unfamiliar tree blooming with vibrant rose-red flowers, petals scattered across a stone table and stools beneath its branches.

Blowing petals off a stone stool, Kuqiao sat down to wait.

The second Qianli Xun stepped inside, the nun shut the cave door behind her.

Pitch darkness swallowed everything. Stopping dead until her eyes adjusted, she made out the nun's dim outline beside her. "Master, where is the Abbess?"

Without speaking, the nun moved aside and struck a tinder tube to light an oil lamp, bathing the chamber in warm glow.

This cave ran much deeper than the other; she stood only in an outer chamber holding a table, two oil lamps, and a wooden box. Does the Abbess hate sunlight? Qianli Xun wondered.

The nun explained: "Benefactor, the Abbess is in ascetic retreat and sees no guests. Only because a butterfly braved the dark to fly inside and alight upon her did she sense a fated visitor and grant you this meeting."

Remembering the blue butterfly, gratitude warmed Qianli Xun's heart. Moths seek flame, yet that butterfly flew straight into darkness—no wonder the Abbess saw an omen. Either way, that butterfly had done her a massive favor; if she ever crossed paths with it again...

Not that she could think what favor you even do for a butterfly.

Peering toward the inner cave, she saw solid blackness—without the nun beside her, she'd have gotten chills.

Extending a hand, the nun said, "The Abbess sits within. Take my hand and follow me."

Gripping the nun's hand, Qianli Xun walked deeper into the dark. "With a name like Master Jingguang—Pure Light—I figured she'd love bright rooms, not sit in the dark."

"As a layperson, benefactor, you wouldn't know that ascetics also train in enduring the hardship of darkness."

Too deep for Qianli Xun—and off topic anyway. After walking several paces through ink-black air, the nun stopped, and she stopped too.

"Abbess, the lady benefactor is here," the nun announced.

An aged woman's voice floated out of the dark: "Cikong, have her sit."

Master Cikong guided her hand. "One step forward, benefactor." Qianli Xun stepped forward.

"Sit now." Feeling her way down in the dark, she landed square on a soft prayer cushion.

Listening to Cikong step aside without a stumble, Qianli Xun marveled at her dark-room navigation skills.

At last face to face with Master Jingguang—unseen yet unmistakably seated right across from her—Qianli Xun wasted no time.

"Abbess, I'm trapped in a life crisis with nowhere left to turn, which is why I climbed this mountain to beg your guidance—please show me a clear path forward."

"Unhappy in your marriage and desperate to break free of your husband?"

Spot on! Qianli Xun gasped inside—the Abbess really sees everything! "Yes!" she blurted out.

"Since ancient times, women have followed whatever husband fate dealt them, rarely escaping an unhappy match—and even when they do, gossip clings to their name. Have you thought what lies ahead once you leave him?"

"I haven't thought that far—I just want out! To me, leaving this marriage is the only way my real life can begin."

Silence descended, heavy and still in the dark.

As her eyes adjusted further, Qianli Xun made out a shadowy silhouette seated across from her, with Cikong standing beside. Tired of the hush, she spoke again:

"You guessed why I came on the first try—proving you truly see past and future! Tell me: do I actually get out of this marriage? And what does my future hold?"

At length the Abbess spoke, sidestepping her questions: "The instant you entered, I sensed your spirit does not belong to this era. Tell me first how you came here."

Stunned by the Abbess's insight, Qianli Xun held nothing back—telling of the earthquake, her death, and waking up inside Zhu Shuzhen's body. Finishing, she asked, "Above all, I want to know: am I Zhu Shuzhen or Qianli Xun? Will I end up living Zhu Shuzhen's tragic fate, or carve out Qianli Xun's own destiny?"

The Abbess countered, "Who do you believe yourself to be?"

Pausing a beat, Qianli Xun answered firm: "Everyone else sees Zhu Shuzhen—yet I still believe I am Qianli Xun."

"A sage of our dynasty wrote the Treatise on Response and Retribution, whose opening line states: 'Good fortune and calamity have no fixed gate; people summon them upon themselves.' Likewise Buddhism teaches the Five Precepts and Ten Virtues—all reminding mortals that the surest way to change one's fate is changing one's own conduct, not bowing to idols or chasing divination..."

Qianli Xun tuned out the preaching on treatises and precepts—until "change one's fate" perked her ears right up.

"Whether you live as Zhu Shuzhen or Qianli Xun depends on your actions—and actions follow mindset. Transform your mindset first, and you will live Qianli Xun's life in this era."

Relief warmed her—wearing Zhu Shuzhen's body or not, her mindset couldn't be more different! Clearly she was already on the right track.

"Still, this body belongs to Zhu Shuzhen, so her destiny will pull at yours—meaning you must strive harder. This marriage is one such tether. From what I perceive, Zhu Shuzhen's chart carries a rocky road in love; reborn through her, your path won't run smooth either, yet it differs from hers. Once you pass through a few romantic trials, your road opens clear."

Wait—how is she reading my face in pitch darkness?! Qianli Xun wondered. Can she see in the dark while I'm blind? Do Buddhist masters unlock night vision?!

Catching the hint about future romantic trials, she lit up: "Does that mean I'll get out of this marriage?!"

"Even reading your fate, Heaven's secrets must not be revealed—some things cannot be spoken plain. Remember only this: whatever strikes, hold to Right Thought and Right Mindfulness, and peril turns to blessing."

"Your words wake me like cold water, Abbess! I'll hold to Right Thought and Right Mindfulness without fail!"

"Since fate brought us together, let me offer one more boost." Pausing a breath, the Abbess went on: "You chose Wang Wei's 'The Bamboo Pavilion,' and I read your essay word for word. Original view—arguing the poem doesn't praise a hermit's loftiness so much as voice helplessness and critique the times. There's truth to that: talent like Wang Wei belonged in office serving the people, not hiding alone in mountain woods—a waste shaped by his era's politics. Thinking that independently proves you have a mind of your own, which is step one in mastering your destiny. Yet I noticed you never once mentioned the poem's key word: 'bamboo.' From that I sensed bamboo brings you good luck. Go home, find a place surrounded by a bamboo grove, and stay there one full month—you'll see its gift."

So the poem test wasn't an exam—it was a reading of her mind!

"Done! I'll do exactly that!"

"As for your tea—utterly dreadful, didn't touch a drop! Care to know why I still agreed to see you?"

"I have no idea—please enlighten me, Abbess."

"Because your awful tea looked identical to the very first bowl I ever made in my youth! Cixuan described every move—especially dumping the warming water straight onto the floor! Haha—I did the exact same thing my first time! Made me feel close to you right away!"

Qianli Xun grinned in the dark—dumb luck at its finest!

"On your way out, Cikong will hand you a red silk ribbon. Write your name on it—which name is your choice. Behind the mountain grows a Millennial Divine Willow; tie your ribbon to a western branch, and it will aid your path."

A Millennial Divine Willow?! Chills raced up her spine—a Divine Willow back in her old era, and another right here! Her destiny really was tangled up with that tree.

The Abbess fell silent, and Cikong's voice stepped in: "You may leave now, benefactor."

Stepping near, Cikong extended a hand. "Take my hand."

Rising, Qianli Xun took her fingers and followed her out.

Stepping from pitch darkness back into lamplight seared her eyes shut—yet Cikong didn't even blink!

Slowly squinting open, Qianli Xun asked, "Master, how come the light didn't blind you coming out of there?"

"You get used to it," Cikong replied brief, lifting a red silk ribbon from the wooden box on the table. "Take this. Ask the young nun at the wooden fish in the main hall—she'll point you toward the Divine Willow."

Accepting the ribbon with thanks, Qianli Xun pushed the door open and left.

Once she was gone, Cikong shut the door, lit the second oil lamp, and carried it into the inner cave, flooding the chamber with light.

On a stone platform spread with soft cushions sat Abbess Jingguang cross-legged, eyes closed, hands resting on her knees.

Setting the lamp down, Cikong teased, "Master never lights a lamp, so none of your devotees even know what you look like."

"All the better," Master Jingguang replied calm. "Saves me from getting mobbed every time I go down the mountain."

"Master, will the advice you gave that girl actually work?"

"Whether it works rests with her, not me. Blind mortals cling to fortune-telling; awaken to your true mind, and you see fate is crafted by your own hands."

"Master sees right through everything—that's why your words cut straight to the bone."
