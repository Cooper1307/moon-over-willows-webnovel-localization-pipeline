## Chapter 341: Sweet Words and Spiked Osmanthus Wine

After having Shen Jianchen dragged unconscious beyond Yanjing’s gates, Wanyan Chi strolled down into the Under-Manor and lied smoothly. “Time’s up. Your Song prince never showed. Stop dreaming he’ll buy you back.”

Ten million taels was no pocket change; raising it meant bankrupting every estate he owned, leaving Qianli Xun relieved instead. “Never dreamed of it once! Only dreamed of flattening your face under my fists!”

“Better than me with your hands, maybe—still powerless against me.”

Stepping closer, he revealed a fresh bruise on his cheek, drawing a cold laugh from her before she threw a punch straight at him. Trading two blows before dropping back, Wanyan Chi stomped the floor twice—and a heavy net snapped down from the ceiling, pinning her tight!

Furious over falling for his hidden traps again despite mimicking his exact steps in secret, she roared, “Only a spineless coward hides behind tricks and traps!”

Stepping right up to the net, he smiled. “Act sweet, and I wouldn’t need traps. Wait till my master arrives from Huining bringing shaman medicine—one dose turns you gentle and docile as a lamb.”

For the first time in two lifetimes, cold dread pricked Qianli Xun’s heart.

“Don’t be scared,” he coaxed. “I wouldn’t hurt you—just pluck off your thorns so I can keep you close and make you mine.”

“Bastard!”

“Curse all you want. Once you’re my woman, I’ll bring you out into the sun to tour the grand rivers and mountains of Great Jin.”

Hearing “grand mountains,” she seized the opening to goad him. “Jin armies are fighting Song right now, and you hide here in Yanjing enjoying soft living like a turtle pulling in its head! Call yourself a man?”

Unbothered, Wanyan Chi smirked. “Trying to bait me? Save your breath. Our twin columns will take Bianliang with ease without missing me—and guarding a captured city takes sharper hands than storming it. Nice try baiting me out so you can slip away! Better spend your time figuring out how to please me.”

Since goading failed, she tried appeal to gratitude. “Wanyan Chi, I saved your life once! Nobody treats a lifesaver like this!”

“And precisely because you saved my life, I’ll repay you with my own body and make you my woman.”

“A forcefully twisted melon is never sweet!”

“A forcefully twisted melon suits *my* taste best,” Wanyan Chi chuckled. “Crisp, fresh, hard-won—makes a man crave and treasure it twice as hard! Wait till a melon ripens mushy and drops in your lap, and you take two bites and toss it aside.”

Confronted with logic that shameless, she snapped she’d die before giving in and leave him empty-handed.

“I won’t let you die before I have you,” Wanyan Chi answered lightly, strolling back outside the door and tapping his heel twice so the net lifted back to the rafters.

Testing his exact spot and the threshold with frantic stomps that did nothing, Qianli Xun slumped onto her bed while the maid stood silently by. Could the traps only recognize Wanyan Chi’s own stride?

Staring up at the lamp-lit stone ceiling, she assumed Mengrong was healed back in Bianliang by now, prayed Haoyu stayed safe running the Chen business and found a new wife if she never returned, and hoped her elders stayed strong. Looking back over her life—cyberbullied before transmigration, battered by fate after—she remembered Master Jingguang’s teaching on “Right Thought and Right Mindfulness.” What good had clinging to right paths done her here? Out slipped a weary sigh.

Hearing her sigh, the maid spoke up gently. “So lucky as you are, Miss—why sigh?”

Qianli Xun sat up sharp. “Which eye of yours sees me lucky?!”

“Never once have I seen the young prince care over any woman like you. Doesn’t that make you lucky?”

“Luck like that I’d gladly throw away!”

“You don’t know, Miss—back in Huining, half the noble daughters begged to become his secondary consorts and he looked past every one, yet pours his whole heart into you!”

Realizing the maid might hold useful clues, Qianli Xun swallowed her scoff and softened her tone. “Well then, since he moves you so much, tell me about him. Who knows—maybe I’ll warm up to him once I know him better.”

“Ask anything, Miss!”

“How many women does he keep?”

“One primary consort and three secondary consorts.”

“Four wives already and still chasing after others—what a playboy!”

The maid frowned in confusion at the modern slang; once Qianli Xun explained, she rushed to defend him. “You wrong him! Elders forced every marriage on him—he dislikes all four. Outwardly grand, His Highness is actually pitiful.”

“*Him* pitiful?! Then everyone alive is a miserable ghost!”

Lowering her voice and glancing round, the maid whispered how His Majesty killed the girl Wanyan Chi truly loved in order to bind ministers through political marriages—even sneering at Wanyan Chi as a “Han-born bastard” unless he obeyed! What was more, Qianli Xun shared a striking resemblance round the eyes and brows to a portrait of that murdered girl!

“Serves him right,” Qianli Xun muttered.

“And his birth mother,” the maid whispered lower still, leaning close. “When His Majesty traveled south to Jiangnan years ago as a prince, he loved a Han maiden. Soon after our young prince was born, someone carried him straight to Huining and dropped him in a manor guard’s arms. Growing up, he rode south to Jiangnan again and again hunting his mother, swearing he’d trade his life for one look at her—and came home heartbroken every time. How can you reject a man that loyal?”

Annoyed that instead of finding leverage she had almost caught herself feeling sorry for him, Qianli Xun shot the maid a sideways glare. “Smooth tongue you’ve got—your master ought to reward you.”

“Please never repeat a word to His Highness—talking behind masters gets us beaten with bamboo!”

“Hmph! Beats his own servants over idle chatter—proves he’s rotten after all.”

“States have laws and households have rules,” the maid defended stubbornly, leaving Qianli Xun grumbling that no excuse washed him clean.

Five peaceful days passed without Wanyan Chi setting foot downstairs. With nothing to do in the dim stone rooms, thoughts of Shen Jianchen kept drifting back—especially strolling Yanjing’s autumn streets looking for the doctor. Just as a smile warmed her lips, heavy clicks echoed from the entrance.

Wanyan Chi strolled in followed by a maid carrying a tray with one pot of wine and two cups, dismissing her at once.

“What do you want now?” Qianli Xun snapped.

“Came to see you—with good news. Today I’m bringing you out of the Under-Manor. Underground damp isn’t good for you.”

“Since when did you turn generous?”

“When will you ever recognize my good intentions?” Wanyan Chi teased with half a smile.

“Drop the act. If you’re taking me out, let’s walk right now.”

“No rush. Share two cups with me first.”

“Sorry. I don’t drink.”

“Just Han osmanthus wine—barely counts as liquor.”

Remembering his threat days earlier, she narrowed her eyes. “Did you spike it with shaman medicine to control me?”

“The shaman medicine? Made it up to scare you—never thought you’d buy it.” Dropping his playful grin into quiet melancholy, Wanyan Chi spoke softly. “Truth is, today is my mother’s birthday. Never met her, never knew her birth day, so I celebrate hers on the day I was born—drinking alone each year to remember a mother I’ve never seen.”

Touched a fraction yet still wary, she scoffed, “Who knows if that’s true or another lie?”

“Why lie about my mother? She’s the sharpest ache in my chest. She comes from Lin’an just like you—younger sister to Shao Zhengye of the Shao Family Escort Agency. That’s why I went to Lin’an. Shao Zhengye told me she was dead, warned me off his gates, and refused to name her grave. I slipped into the Shao graveyard at night, combed every grave hill round Lin’an, and never found her stone. I’m certain she’s still alive—she just refuses to see me.”

Seeing genuine redness rim his eyes, Qianli Xun lowered her guard halfway. “You’re a Jin prince. No surprise she won’t see you.”

“Once Great Jin conquers Bianliang and things settle, I’m riding back to Lin’an and turning the Shao estate upside down till I find her.”

“Take my advice and drop it. Why disturb her peace if she doesn’t want to meet?”

“You could never know the ache of growing up without a mother.”

Thinking of her own childhood before transmigration—losing her mother young too, though at least she had tasted maternal love—Qianli Xun softened. “I *do* know.”

“Then sit and drink two cups with me—out of pity for a boy nobody wanted.”

Disarmed completely by his grief, she agreed: two cups only, then straight upstairs.

Pouring both cups full, Wanyan Chi toasted cleanly and drained his cup first without hesitation. Satisfied the wine held no poison, Qianli Xun drank hers—finding it sweeter and smoother than any osmanthus wine she had tasted.

Pouring herself a second cup, she smiled gently. “Nobody who remembers his mother like this can be all bad. Tell you what—let me go home to Lin’an. I know every alley there far better than you; I’ll help track down your mother for you—”

Halfway through her sentence, the room spun violently. Her cup clattered onto the table, spilling amber wine across the wood. Pitching sideways, she fell straight into Wanyan Chi’s waiting arms.

“You… tricked me…” she slurred before darkness claimed her.

“If I didn’t trick you,” Wanyan Chi murmured with a satisfied smile, “how would you ever behave?”
