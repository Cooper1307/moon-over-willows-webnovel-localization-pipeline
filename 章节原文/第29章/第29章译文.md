# Chapter 29: The Secret Landlady of the Bamboo Grove

Early next morning right after breakfast, Kuqiao had the carriage hitched and rounded up three or four maids to load last night's cloth bundles and a wooden chest aboard.

The bundles held clothes, cosmetics, and the Four Treasures of the Study; the chest held full whisked-tea gear—country villages lacked such tools, and her mistress drank tea every day.

Everything loaded, Kuqiao returned to Zhulan Residence. "All set to go, Miss."

"Packed enough silver?"

"Plenty, Miss!"

Stepping out of Zhulan Residence and leaving the West Courtyard, Qianli Xun lingered a beat over the Caesarweed bushes along the paths, last night's fragrant Peach Blossom Rice still fresh in her mind.

Right then a familiar voice piped up behind her: "My, Young Madam really means business—leaving even earlier than me this morning!"

Stopping to glance back, Qianli Xun saw Chun Jinqiao. "Heading back to Chunxiang Brothel?"

"Where else? A woman like me can't compare to Young Madam, free to come and go as you please."

Spotting a mosquito dive toward Chun Jinqiao, Qianli Xun raised a hand to swat it.

Chun Jinqiao clapped a handkerchief over her cheek in terror. "Going to hit me again, Young Madam?!"

Dropping her hand, Qianli Xun shrugged. "Swatting a mosquito—flew off already."

"Scared me half to death—thought you were slapping me again!" Lowering her cloth, Chun Jinqiao swayed past her down the path.

Watching her swaying hips—so that's Lin Jue's type—an idea struck Qianli Xun. "Hey! Hold up a second!"

Chun Jinqiao halted and turned. "Yes, Young Madam?"

Stepping close, Qianli Xun asked, "Every time Lin Jue brings a courtesan home, it's always you, right?"

Pride flashed on Chun Jinqiao's face. "Of course! Out of all Chunxiang Brothel, Officer Lin only cares for me."

"Then do him a favor and talk him into granting me a mutual divorce."

Studying Lin Manor's primary wife with sharp curiosity, Chun Jinqiao murmured, "You really are one of a kind, Young Madam—pity your heart isn't with Officer Lin. Don't worry, for his sake I'll try talking him round, though right now he still can't let you go."

Swaying on toward the front gate, Chun Jinqiao crossed paths with Lin Jue's two older brothers, neatly dressed on their way out. Ignoring them completely, she climbed straight into Chunxiang Brothel's carriage.

Eldest Brother fumed: "What a disgrace! Third Brother hauling brothel women home again—shaming the family name!"

Second Brother sighed: "Cut him some slack, Eldest Brother—his wife refuses his bed and has zero heart for him, and he's a healthy young man."

"Next chance I get, I'm telling Third Brother to take a proper concubine!"

Overhearing right behind them, Qianli Xun chimed in cheerful: "Morning, Eldest Brother, Second Brother! Brilliant idea—hurry up and get Lin Jue a concubine! Chun Jinqiao who just walked out looks like a great fit—why not her?"

Both brothers exchanged awkward glances.

Eldest Brother balked: "Even a concubine has to come from a respectable family—how could we pick a courtesan? Never!"

Dodging the topic, Second Brother asked, "Where are you headed so early, Sister-in-law?"

Making no secret of it—they were the ones who had blocked her divorce—Qianli Xun shot back, "Staying in the countryside for a spell to breathe fresh air! Stuck inside Lin Manor was suffocating me!"

Second Brother held back Eldest Brother before he could scold, leaving both watching helpless as Qianli Xun and Kuqiao stepped through the gate and boarded their carriage.

Eldest Brother shook his head: "What on earth is this?! Ever seen a wife act like that?!"

"If even Third Brother can't handle her, what can we do?" Second Brother sighed. "Might as well talk Third Brother into agreeing to mutual divorce and letting her go."

Eldest Brother nodded. "Let's invite him for drinks tonight—just us three brothers—and talk sense into him."

Rolling through bustling city streets, Qianli Xun spotted a jewelry shop out the window and ordered the driver to stop.

Inside, counters gleamed with gold and silver pieces in every style.

Spotting a handsome silver bracelet, Qianli Xun picked it up and smiled. "Hold out your wrist, Kuqiao."

Knowing her mistress meant to replace the bangle donated at the nunnery, Kuqiao didn't hesitate. "Thanks, Miss!"

Slipping the bracelet onto her wrist, Qianli Xun watched Kuqiao admire it—even prettier than the old one!

"One tael of silver," the shopkeeper said.

Without blinking, Qianli Xun ordered, "Wrap up a matching one!"

"One's plenty, Miss—no need to buy me two!" Kuqiao protested.

"Not for you—for your mother! I'm crashing at your family's place for a whole month; can't show up empty-handed."

"No need to be polite, Miss—my parents will be over the moon just hosting you! They'd never ask for gifts."

"Whether they ask is their business; bringing gifts is mine."

Wrapping the package, the shopkeeper said, "Two taels of silver."

Kuqiao accepted the bundle and counted out two taels from her pouch.

When Qianli Xun insisted on buying more, Kuqiao talked her down to picking up a box of Lin'an specialty pastries.

Once clear of the city gates, bamboo thickened along the roadside—taller and sturdier than the stalks in Zhulan Residence—until a vast emerald bamboo sea opened ahead.

Rolling deep into the dense grove, Qianli Xun marveled that anyone could live hidden in here—until the stalks thinned into a clearing dotted with bamboo houses!

Rough yet sturdy—human ingenuity never ceased to amaze her, lashing stalks into entire dwellings.

Where the track ended, the carriage rolled to a stop.

Peeking out, Qianli Xun saw villagers turning heads from every direction.

Carriages barely entered the village once a year, drawing instant crowds: faces poked out of doorways, hands paused mid-chore, folks headed out turned back for a look, and playing children ran right up.

"I'll hop down and grab some hands to haul our luggage, Miss," Kuqiao said.

The moment Kuqiao jumped out, a villager called, "Hey, it's Kuqiao! Working for rich folks in the city really upgrades you—rolling in on a fancy carriage! Thought the rent collector came early!"

Kids who knew Kuqiao swarmed around: "Sister Kuqiao, bring any treats from town?!"

Prepared, Kuqiao pulled five or six candies from her sleeve to hand out, then called to the crowd: "Couple of strong arms to carry luggage to my house!"

Three or four young men jumped forward at once: "I'll grab it! Me too!"

Climbing back up, Kuqiao passed down the bundles and wooden chest while the lads eagerly shouldered them.

Kuqiao stepped down first, followed by Qianli Xun.

Kuqiao instructed the driver: "Uncle, head on back to Lin Manor—come pick Miss and me up on this exact date next month!"

Nodding, the coachman turned the carriage back out of the village.

Turning around, Kuqiao caught every villager staring wide-eyed at her mistress—no surprise, country folk had never seen a refined noble lady like her up close.

Swelling with pride, Kuqiao announced: "This is Eldest Miss of Zhu Manor! She's staying at my home for a spell—and she's the owner of this bamboo grove, so mind your manners!"

"Owner of the bamboo grove?!" Qianli Xun blinked in surprise, shooting Kuqiao a sharp look—how many more secrets did this girl have?

So used to her lively mistress by now she'd clean forgotten her amnesia, Kuqiao scrambled to explain: "Didn't mean to hide it, Miss—forgot you don't remember!"

Holding further questions back in front of the crowd, Qianli Xun smiled at the villagers. "Good folks, apologies for imposing for a bit—hope you don't mind my stay."

Right then the village head hurried up; with the estate proprietor arriving in person, he had to pay respects. "Welcome, Proprietor! Forgive us for not greeting you sooner—had you sent word ahead, I would've readied proper quarters!"

"And you are?" Unfamiliar with him yet sensing status in his manner, Qianli Xun asked polite.

"Our village head!" Kuqiao introduced him fast. "Uncle Li, Miss is staying at my house—no need to trouble yourself."

"Don't stand on ceremony, Village Head," Qianli Xun added. "Kuqiao's place is fine."

"In that case, right this way, Proprietor."

Leading them forward—two steps in, a bold villager yelled: "Proprietor! Any chance you'll cut our rent?!"

Not having met Matron Wei yet and clueless about rent rates, Qianli Xun kept silent.

The village head barked at the man: "Shut your trap and get lost!"

Narrow lanes twisted past bamboo huts lined on every side by bamboo stalks. Rounding about ten homes, they arrived at Kuqiao's family house—decent-sized yet worn by years of weather, barely holding together.

Outside, the village head hollered: "Old Ku! Big guest at your door!"

Two men and a woman emerged: an older weathered man with frost at his temples; a sturdy lad around fourteen or fifteen, sun-darkened and muscled from toil; and a silver-templed woman with creased brow and skin weathered by years of work.

Spotting Kuqiao first, the woman rushed down the bamboo steps in joy. "Qiao, you're home! Mother missed you to pieces!"

"Mother, this is my Miss—she's staying with us for a while!" Kuqiao introduced her.

Turning to Qianli Xun, the mother beamed: "This beauty straight out of a painting is Miss Zhu? Such a kind face—knowing you serve her puts my heart at ease!" Taking Qianli Xun's hand, she urged, "Quick, come sit inside!"

Qianli Xun let herself be drawn into the bamboo house.

Having set the luggage inside, the young helpers lingered outside craning their necks through the doorway.

The village head bellowed: "What's there to gawk at? Get back to your own work!"

Scared of the village head, the lads scattered grinning.

Old Ku and his son invited the village head inside. "Anything the Proprietor needs, just say the word—don't be shy," the village head told them.

Grinning ear to ear, Old Ku replied, "With your backing, Village Head, you bet! Need anything, I'll send Kujue right over."

Lowering his voice, the village head voiced his worry: "Every year Matron Wei comes to collect rent—why'd the Proprietor show up in person this year? Something changing?"

"Always suspicious, Village Head!" Old Ku waved it off. "Didn't Kuqiao say she's just staying a spell? Doubt it's about rent."

"Either way, let me step in and greet the Proprietor before heading off."

They walked in together, Old Ku and Kujue trailing behind.

Every piece of furniture inside was crafted of bamboo—plain yet charmingly rustic. Seated on a low bamboo chair, Qianli Xun found steaming bamboo leaf tea waiting on the side table.

The village head bowed politely: "Settle in at Old Ku's first, Proprietor—need anything at all, send Old Ku for me."

"Thank you for looking out for me, Village Head. Go tend your duties—I'll call if anything comes up."

"Got village business waiting, so I won't intrude." With a bow, the village head departed.

Once Kuqiao introduced her family, Qianli Xun had her bring out the silver bracelet and pastries, delighting the entire Ku household.

Old Ku asked, "How long will you be staying, Proprietor?"

"One month."

"A noble lady like you can't stay in a rundown shack like ours," Old Ku said. "Lucky we built a brand-new bamboo house for Kujue last year—nobody's lived in it yet! Sits right beside the eastern bamboo grove with a fine view. If you don't mind, stay there!"

Sounded like heaven to Qianli Xun—she agreed on the spot.

Kuqiao blinked: "Father, Mother—is Kujue getting married? Why build his house so early?"

"About time! Match is already sealed—his bride comes home next year," Old Ku beamed.

Seated on a stool nearby, Kujue turned bright red at mention of his wedding.

Kuqiao's mother turned to Qianli Xun: "Thank you for looking after Kuqiao, Proprietor—when it comes to her marriage someday, we'll have to trouble you too."

Kuqiao cut in fast: "Mother, quit talking nonsense! I'm never marrying—I'm staying by Miss's side forever!"

Qianli Xun laughed: "Don't worry, Auntie—I'll make sure she lands a wonderful husband!"

After chatting a while longer, Kuqiao went to help her mother steam bamboo tube rice and stir-fry rehydrated dried bamboo shoots from last year; in honor of the Proprietor, Kujue netted a fresh fish from the stream behind the village for soup, and everyone gathered around one table.

Simple compared to Lin Manor's banquets, the meal carried a crisp mountain freshness Qianli Xun loved.

After lunch, the Ku family helped haul her luggage into the new bamboo house. Once they left, Kuqiao got busy arranging everything to her mistress's taste.

Furniture was sparse, yet a low bamboo table faced the open doorway—perfect for the Four Treasures of the Study so Miss could gaze straight into the bamboo grove while writing! Since it sat low for floor seating, Kuqiao fetched a square cotton cushion from home—just the right height.

A low bamboo cabinet doubled as a tea table; draping patterned homespun cloth across it, Kuqiao lined up every tea tool.

Inside the bedroom stood a fresh bamboo bed; Kuqiao spread Kujue's brand-new quilts over it and hung her mistress's garments along a bamboo rack.

Thrilled with the cozy setup, Qianli Xun eyed the fine silks hanging on the rack—and her own fancy dress—feeling way too flashy for a rustic village.

"Kuqiao, lend me a couple sets of your clothes."

"Why wear my clothes, Miss?"

"Wearing these silks around the village makes me stick out like a sore thumb—yours fit right in."

"If you don't mind rough cloth, Miss—happened to pack two spare sets. Try one on."

Changing into Kuqiao's simple dress, Qianli Xun still couldn't hide her poise beneath plain cotton.

She pulled off her hair ornaments too and had Kuqiao stow them away.

Stepping to the doorway, she faced a lush wall of swaying green bamboo; a cool breeze carried crisp bamboo scent straight to her lungs. Living a month in a paradise like this was bound to turn her luck around.
