## Chapter 206: The Second Night at Chen Manor

Absorbed in writing, Qianli Xun never heard Matron Wei step inside; unwilling to break her flow yet needing to report today's plans, Matron Wei signaled Kuqiao to keep quiet and watched her whisk tea.

"Your tea hand grows silkier by the day," Matron Wei whispered with a smile. "When you marry Bai Feng, who will make Miss's tea?"

"Already planned it, Matron! Even after marrying Bai Feng, I will come wait on Miss every day!"

"Silly girl—Bai Feng is Prince Yi's guard; sooner or later he heads back to Bianliang."

Adding hot water and whisking briskly, Kuqiao paused in sudden dismay. "Put like that, you make me hesitate..."

"Nothing to hesitate over. Hongdou is sharp—drilling servants all day is not enough; teach her tea-whisking in spare moments so Miss stays cared for once you wed."

"Smart thinking, Matron."

Setting the finished bowl gently beside her mistress's elbow, Kuqiao's fragrant brew drew Qianli Xun's brush to a halt. Sipping gladly, she spotted Matron Wei approaching and signaled Kuqiao to stash the huaben—Kuqiao hiding a grin since Matron Wei already knew Miss was Master Qianli Xun!

Rising warmly to seat Matron Wei, Qianli Xun praised the study's fresh layout.

"The rooms were easy; the yard takes three days," Matron Wei reported. "Spoke with Chen Manor's gardeners today—they start tomorrow: emerald bamboo outside Moxiang Courtyard's gate, keeping existing blooms inside while adding every chrysanthemum you love—daisies, zinnias, summer, autumn, ink, and Hangzhou mums—plus lotus roots in the pond, just like that line Miss always quotes about lotus rising clean out of mud..."

"Master Lianxi's line!" Kuqiao piped up. "'Rising from the mud yet remaining unstained, washed by clear ripples yet never coquettish!'" [TN: Translator's rendering]

"Yes, yes—sharp memory on Kuqiao!" Matron Wei laughed.

"Sounds perfect," Qianli Xun beamed. "Only the yard lacks a shade tree for summer—plant anything big, lush, and fast-growing."

"Beat you to it! Found several lush hehuan silk trees—whose name literally means 'harmonious union'—growing in the Chen nursery, and the gardener agreed to transplant one right in tomorrow!"

Catching the quiet hope woven into the tree's auspicious name—wishing harmony someday with Chen Haoyu—Qianli Xun felt a small tug: her heart would never bend that way. Curious for an elder's honest take, she asked Matron Wei the same question she had asked Kuqiao: "Taking over his yard, moving him out back, taking all his assets—did I go too far?"

Matron Wei chuckled warmly. "Only narrow minds would say so. To my eyes, every move you made benefits both Young Master and the Chen clan."

"You always take my side, Matron! Half the house is probably whispering against me right now—not that I care! Let the Chens see marrying me was a massive blunder!"

"If you truly did not care, why ask me?"

"True enough!" Shrugging it off, Qianli Xun remembered tomorrow's outing. "Dongfang Chenze invited me to a poetry gathering tomorrow and I accepted—should I notify Chen Haoyu's parents?"

"Definitely check with your elders."

"What if they say no?"

"Master and Madam are broad-minded; they will not block you."

"Great—I will go tell them right now."

With Kuqiao lighting the way with a lantern down Chen Manor's straight paths, a voice floated from behind in the dark: "Out after nightfall, Sister-in-Law—heading where?"

Turning, Qianli Xun recognized Chen Haodong, on his way to see his father. "Good evening, Eldest Brother. Going to tell Father-in-Law and Mother-in-Law something."

Stepping closer, Chen Haodong took in her striking poise—*no wonder Brother Yan fell hard!*—and asked smoothly, "Is Haoyu home?"

"No."

Putting on mild reproach, Chen Haodong advised, "Now that you are married to Haoyu, keep a tighter leash on him—letting him run wild outside invites mockery."

Annoyed—the whole Chen clan watched him run wild for twenty years without lifting a finger, and day two they expect the new bride to play nanny?!—Qianli Xun answered coolly, "Eldest Brother flatters me. How could one woman rein him in? If nothing else, excuse me."

Watching her lantern recede into the night, Chen Haodong sneered softly: "Half the credit for getting you into Chen Manor fast belongs to me—do not let me down!"

Reaching her in-laws' open courtyard with no maid at the door, Qianli Xun stepped inside—and stopped a beat at the doorway: Chen Jianglin sat beaming warmly at Yao Qingyan, making her laugh softly in easy devotion. Never in her modern childhood—losing her mother early, watching her father become a monk—nor in Zhu Manor watching Zhu Xiyan bark at Madam Xiang had she witnessed marital tenderness like this. A genuine pang of envy hit her for Chen Haoyu growing up under love so warm; wedded to him in name only, phrases like growing old together in harmony belonged to other worlds. She let out an involuntary sigh.

Hearing it, Yao Qingyan looked up warmly. "Oh, Shuzhen! What brings you over so late?"

Stepping in with a curtsy, Qianli Xun asked politely about attending Dongfang Chenze's poetry gathering tomorrow.

Unwilling to cage a famous poetess—and proud to keep Chen honor shining in Lin'an's literary circles—Chen Jianglin waved breezily. "Go freely to any poetry gathering from now on—no need to ask Mother-in-Law or me!"

"Thank you, Father-in-Law!" How sweet having in-laws this open—if only the husband had been Shen Jianchen...

"Shuzhen," Yao Qingyan's voice pulled her back, "is Haoyu back?"

"Vanished right after serving tea to Grandmother this morning."

"Know where he went?"

"No idea."

Rising to clasp Shuzhen's hands earnestly, Yao Qingyan said, "Father-in-Law and I already saw your prenuptial pact of non-interference—yet in our hearts, you are our true daughter-in-law. Tied by Grandmother's doting, we failed to discipline Haoyu and let him grow wild. Now that you are his wife, please whip him into shape for us! No matter how hard you discipline him, Father-in-Law and I will never step in your way."

*Did the Chens marry a bride or hire a drill sergeant?!* First Chen Haodong, now both in-laws! Having zero time, zero obligation, and zero interest—praying Chen Haoyu stayed permanently missing—Qianli Xun still kept polite face for her gracious mother-in-law. "Thank you both for your trust. If Chen—um... Haoyu pulls any mischief, I will be sure to teach him a lesson." Dropping his surname made her skin crawl, yet Yao Qingyan beamed in relief and sent her back early to rest after her busy day redecorating.

Meanwhile, Chen Haodong reported to his father Chen Jiangshu that Master Jiang had been paid cleanly and asked if Prince Yi's raid spelled fallout.

"Eldest Brother says Prefect Zhu sent word Prince Yi bears zero grudge against the Chens and told us not to bother apologizing," Chen Jiangshu smiled proudly at his son's scheme.

"Great—only I bumped into Miss Zhu on the way over and nudged her to rein Haoyu in, and she sounded totally indifferent! Spent silver and sweat rushing their wedding—what if she leaves Haoyu partying free without giving him grief?!"

"Day one and you want miracles? Patience—hot tofu burns an eager tongue! Heir selection is a full year off; mark my word, Miss Zhu will drag Haoyu down plenty."

Reassured, Chen Haodong remembered another wild card. "Father recall that friend Yan Chi I mentioned?"

"Of course—met him at our tea shop last year asking after some woman named Shao Yueting. By your description, sounded non-Han—Jurchen maybe?"

"No idea who he is, yet he knows Miss Zhu AND has his eye on her—even warned me to keep watch on her, sounding ready to snatch her away!"

"What?!" Chen Jiangshu marveled in glee. "First Prince Yi raiding her wedding, now this Master Yan sniffing around?! Quite a magnet—and pure poison for a wife! Old saying holds true: a woman's virtue lies in lack of talent! Parading around poetry salons with beauty and fame drags men swarming like bees—Haoyu will never see a peaceful day! His misery is my son's gain!"

"Oddly though, Yan Chi vanished thin into air lately."

"Forget him—a passing shadow. Keep your focus sharp on inheriting the Chen empire."

"Yes, Father."
