## Chapter 286: Clues Buried Under Fallen Petals

After Prince Yi had left the country house the night before, Wen Xin had his constables pull every servant out for individual questioning while he prowled the grounds outside the detention room by moonlight.

Lush flowers and shrubs filled the yard, anchored by a mature camphor tree in full bloom, drifting sweet fragrance through the air. Stepping up to inspect it, Wen Xin noted the trunk was thick enough to hide a person—and around its roots lay a neat ring of swept-up fallen petals.

Spotting a male servant passing by, Wen Xin hailed him. “Come here a second.”

“Yes, Chief Constable?”

Pointing at the heap around the trunk, Wen Xin asked, “Why pile dead petals under the tree?”

“Chen Manor’s gardener ordered it, sir. Says fallen petals rot into rich soil that feeds the roots so next year’s blooms grow brighter.”

“So last year’s petals stayed piled right here too?”

“Stands to reason, sir.”

Last year on the third day of the third month, the camphor wouldn’t have bloomed yet—still, even if someone hid behind the trunk and dropped something, somebody could have swept it up later. Dismissing the servant, Wen Xin smirked at himself for obsessing over a tree—right as a constable jogged up.

“Chief Constable Wen, we questioned every single servant. Turns out one maid wasn’t transferred away on the third day of the third month!”

Wen Xin’s eyes lit up. “Who? Bring her here!”

“Maid named Aye—she took leave that day to visit her family.”

“Leave?” Disappointment prickled, yet he pressed on. “Bring her anyway—and the caretaker.”

Moments later the caretaker walked up with Aye in tow.

“Ask whatever you like, Chief Constable,” the caretaker offered.

“You’re Aye? You took leave on the third day of the third month last year?”

Aye tensed up. “Yes, sir—my mother fell ill, so I had to go home. The caretaker can vouch for me.”

“True enough,” the caretaker confirmed. “I approved her leave myself.”

“When did you get back to the house?” Wen Xin asked.

Aye stammered, sweating. “I... I... I...”

“Timid girl—scared speechless in front of officials,” the caretaker jumped in. “I’ll tell you: I only gave her two shichen off, yet she didn’t show back up till the third day! Gave her a thorough chewing-out for it too.”

Still a dead end. Discouraged, Wen Xin waved them off.

With nothing else to go on, his gaze drifted back to the camphor tree’s roots. Walking around the trunk, he unhooked his sword and poked through the withered petals with the scabbard—nothing. Digging deeper into the dirt beneath, turning the soil over twice, he was about to quit when the scabbard tip struck something hard.

Crouching down, he picked it out and brushed off the dirt: a tiny, carved wooden peach-heart charm about the size of a thumb joint, knotted with a thin cord so soil-stained its original color was gone.

Rolling it in his palm, unsure if it mattered, he had the caretaker line up the entire staff and held the charm up. Too dark to see clearly, most shook their heads even after he described it.

One maid spoke up. “Too dark to tell, sir—mind bringing it closer?”

Stepping over, Wen Xin held it in the moonlight. The maid peered close. “I know that! It’s Aye’s! She lost it ages ago—can’t believe you found it, sir!”

Every eye turned to Aye. Parting the crowd, Wen Xin walked straight up and held the wooden heart right before her face. “Look close—yours?”

Aye folded at once. “Yes—yes, it’s mine.”

“How did it end up under the camphor tree?”

“I—I don’t know... must’ve dropped it by accident.”

Years of detective work told Wen Xin she was hiding something big. Time to bluff her wide open.

Pocketing the charm, he spoke dead-certain. “By accident? Don’t think so! Let me tell you what really happened. On the third day of the third month last year, you went home on leave and came back four shichen later after dark. Not a servant in sight. Looking around, you came right here, saw something you shouldn’t have, panicked, and ducked behind that camphor tree—dropping your wooden charm in the dark. Too tiny and dirt-colored to spot, and once the camphor blossomed, fallen petals buried it before you could find it. Am I right or am I right?”

Terrified, Aye hit her knees. “Spare me, Your Lordship! Nothing to do with me—I just saw it by accident!”

Dead on target! Wen Xin let out a triumphant breath, hauled Aye back to the Judicial Office that night, put her up in a room, and brought her before Prince Yi at dawn.

Kneeling in the Judicial Office hall, Aye spilled every word.

“Your Lordship, I got back right after dark. The main gate stood closed yet unlocked, so I pushed in. Not a soul around, and the caretaker was passed out dead drunk. Heading back to the maids’ quarters, I remembered the girl Young Master Chen had locked up and went to check if she was still there. And then...”

“And then what?” Shen Jianchen urged.

Hesitating one beat, Aye blurted, “I saw Scarface—the thug who always trails Young Master Chen—slip into her room and defile her while Young Master was away! Scared out of my wits, I hid behind the camphor tree, hoping to untie the girl once Scarface left. Next thing, the door opened and Scarface hauled out a bulging sack—plainly a person inside, had to be that girl! No way to save her, so I waited till Scarface cleared off—and just as I turned to go, someone else walked out of that room!”

Paydirt! Shen Jianchen leaned forward. “Who?”

“Couldn’t tell—they wore a long black cloak hiding head and face completely, though the build looked like a man’s. Petrified all over again, I stayed crouched behind the tree—must’ve dropped my wooden heart right then, searched for it later and never found it. Scared sick I’d get dragged in if anything blew up, I ran straight back home that same night—and somehow your officer caught me anyway.”

“Was the man in the black cloak Chen Haoyu?”

Aye shook her head hard. “No! Young Master Chen would never skulk around in disguise—he’s locked plenty of girls in that room, always bold as brass in broad daylight. Had to be someone else.”

“Why did you assume Scarface defiled her rather than the cloaked man?”

Aye looked torn. “I didn’t see the cloaked man at first—only saw Scarface go in and heard the girl screaming, so I assumed it was Scarface.”

“Aye, dare you testify in court on Chen Haoyu’s behalf?”

“Much as I despise Young Master Chen’s ways,” Aye said firm, “he didn’t defile that girl. I’ll testify for him.”
