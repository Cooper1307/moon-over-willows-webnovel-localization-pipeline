## Chapter 324: Bidding Farewell to Prince Yi

Arriving at Qiao Manor looking for Nan Shuang, Bai Feng learned Young Master Qiao had taken him straight out to Youlin Retreat—so back Bai Feng rode.

Reaching Youlin Retreat, he found Nan Shuang sitting at the foot of the wooden platform while Prince Yi and Young Master Qiao sat drinking and talking up top, facing toward the Divine Willow.

Plopping down beside Nan Shuang, Bai Feng complained, “Just rode all the way to Qiao Manor looking for you, and here you are sitting in my backyard!”

“Been waiting here a good while already.”

“You’re my only real friend in Lin’an—hate leaving you behind.”

“Why not ask Prince Yi to stay then?”

“No chance. His Majesty stripped his Military Commissioner post—what’s left keeping him here? Besides, with Youqi Jushi hauled to Bianliang in a prisoner cart, His Highness has zero heart left for Lin’an…”

“Prince Yi really is faithful to the bone—still can’t let go of Youqi Jushi after all this time.”

“Keep your voice down.” Tapping a finger against his temple, Bai Feng whispered, “Princess Consort Yi’s head isn’t right anymore. Local doctors can’t cure her, so His Highness has to take her to Bianliang for treatment.”

“Lost her marbles?”

“Out of her senses.”

Nan Shuang’s mouth fell open. “How on earth did that happen?!”

“Some big fright, plus grief—and she just snapped.”

“Whoa! Being scared too hard can wreck your head? Better watch myself from now on!” Shaking off the shock, Nan Shuang grinned shyly. “My master says I get to marry Youfan next spring—wonder if you’ll be back in Lin’an by then?”

“Congrats first off—though no telling if I’ll be back by spring.”

“What about you and Kuqiao? When’s the wedding?”

“Beats me. With Youqi Jushi in trouble, how could Kuqiao even think about marrying? And with Princess Consort Yi like this, I wouldn’t dare bother His Highness about my own plans—have to keep pushing it off.”

Nan Shuang sighed in relief. “Lucky my master didn’t marry Youqi Jushi after all—otherwise he’d be the one sweating bullets right now, and my wedding would be pushed off too!”

Irritated by Nan Shuang’s smug look, Bai Feng snapped, “Don’t think your master’s living some dream life—he only looks fine on the outside.”

“Still beats marrying someone hauled off to Bianliang in a prison cage!”

Bai Feng opened his mouth to curse Nan Shuang out, then bit it back knowing how long it might be before they met again—besides, who could blame Nan Shuang for thinking like an ordinary bloke? Youqi Jushi’s life really had been bumpy as cobblestones, dragging his master along for the ride.

“Knock it off—don’t talk about Youqi Jushi like that. She’s a good soul, just hit some rough luck.”

The two friends sat chatting over ordinary trifles, masking the sting of parting beneath casual banter.

...

Up on the platform, Shen Jianchen and Qiao Wenxian drank steadily, trading farewells of their own.

“Who could have guessed how fast things turn? Barely over a year in Lin’an, and you’re already heading back. Feels hard watching you go.”

Remembering those early months sharing Youlin Retreat with Wenyuan, Shen Jianchen sighed. “Same here—yet no feast lasts forever.”

“Is it because of Shuzhen?”

“Not *only* because of her—though she’s why my mind locked firm and why we’re leaving early.”

After a quiet pause, Qiao Wenxian spoke softly. “At last I see you love her deeper than I ever did.”

“With her, it goes well beyond a single word like *love*.”

“What is it then?”

Shen Jianchen shook his head. “Can’t even explain it. Only know wherever she goes, I have to go—otherwise I wouldn’t even know why I’m drawing breath.”

“She’s Chen Haoyu’s wife now.”

“Which is why I’m taking Chen Haoyu to Bianliang to help save her.”

“And once you rescue her, she’ll ride back to Lin’an with Chen Haoyu. Are you planning to come back too?”

Gazing toward the distant Divine Willow, Shen Jianchen murmured, “The Divine Willow stands in Lin’an. If she returns to Lin’an too, sooner or later I’ll be back.”

Too heavy a topic to linger on, Qiao Wenxian steered away. “How is Princess Consort Yi holding up?”

“Not well at all.”

“What do you mean?”

“Her mind’s slipped—keeps giggling blankly and muttering things nobody makes sense of.”

“How did it come to this?”

“Suffered a fright while out, finally conceived only to miscarry, got crushed by grief, then learned she can’t bear children again. All those blows shattered her—now she alternates between laughing and weeping or spouting gibberish.”

Qiao Wenxian shook his head in pity. “Who’d have thought trouble would hit you too in just a few days? Bianliang boasts master physicians—someone there might cure Her Highness.”

“That’s my hope too. Everything piled together leaves no choice but going back to Bianliang.”

...

Bright and early the next morning, Chen Haoyu rode up to Youlin Retreat with Kuqiao and Qinggua to meet Prince Yi’s party.

Pulling up outside the gate, he found a sizable crowd gathered to see Prince Yi off.

Prince Yi stepped through the gate trailed by Matron Zou and Princess Consort Yi. Even bystanders couldn’t miss how glazed and vacant the princess consort’s eyes looked, devoid of her old spark.

Once the women climbed inside their carriage, well-wishers stepped up one by one to bid Prince Yi farewell.

Zhu Xiyan spoke first. “To see Your Highness go to such lengths for my daughter’s sake moves me beyond words. Having nothing worthy to repay you with, let me kowtow in gratitude.”

As Zhu Xiyan dropped toward his knees, Shen Jianchen caught his arms. “I’m saving Youqi Jushi of my own free will—no need for you to kneel to me. If you kneel, it only sits wrong on my conscience.”

Everyone standing by understood perfectly what Prince Yi meant.

Watching from inside his carriage, Chen Haoyu’s jaw clenched tight: *Saying that right in front of everybody—doesn’t put me in his eyes at all!*

Seeing a line waiting to speak with the prince, Zhu Xiyan excused himself politely. “You’re busy, Your Highness. Let me climb into my son-in-law’s carriage for a word.”

With Prince Yi’s nod, Zhu Xiyan stepped inside Chen Haoyu’s carriage.

Next up stepped Master Jiang. “Your Highness, a word in private?”

Though Ferry Superintendent was only a minor post, it was still a government post. Seeing Master Jiang brave Liang Shicheng’s wrath to show up warmed Shen Jianchen’s heart; he nodded readily.

Stepping aside together, Shen Jianchen teased lightly, “Coming to send me off—aren’t you afraid the new Prefect will make life miserable for you?”

Master Jiang grinned. “Only virtue I’ve got is sticking by friends. You helped me out before—even if it costs me my post as Ferry Superintendent, I don’t turn my back on a debt.”

“Got a real conscience on you.”

“Came for two reasons, Your Highness: first, to wish you safe travels; second, to drop a hint—my wife suspects what happened to Youqi Jushi ties back to Princess Consort Yi. You know she and Her Highness pulled a dirty stunt against Youqi Jushi once before, so she reads Her Highness pretty well. Thinking it over, her hunch rings true—wanted to tell you straight before you left.”

Noticing Prince Yi’s expression stiffen instantly, Master Jiang wrapped up fast. “That’s all I had to say, Your Highness. Smooth roads north—farewell!”

After Master Jiang stepped away, Shao Zhengye and his son Shao Zhize walked up carrying two jars of wine.

“Two jars of fine vintage for Your Highness to drink along the road,” Shao Zhengye offered warmly.

Pushing down the dark churn stirred by Master Jiang’s warning, Shen Jianchen cupped his hands. “Thank you, Master Shao! Bai Feng, take Master Shao’s gift!”

Bai Feng stepped forward, accepted both jars from Shao Zhize, and stowed them neatly inside the hitched carriage.

An escort guard from the Shao Family Escort Agency followed with a tray holding three bowls of wine.

“Drink up, Your Highness,” Shao Zhengye beamed. “Everything goes unsaid in a bowl of wine!”

Clinking bowls together, the three men drained theirs dry in one gulp, Shao Zhengye and his son bursting into hearty laughter.

Before he knew it, Shen Jianchen laughed along with them, setting his heaviness aside for a moment. Grateful to father and son, he marveled how one hearty laugh of theirs could sweep away gloom and rekindle nerve to face whatever lay ahead.

Once the laughter settled, Shao Zhengye and his son cupped their hands. “Safe journey, Your Highness—farewell!”

Last in line stood the Judicial Office crew—Han Gu and Wen Xin leading several constables forward.

Before they could cross the grass, Shen Jianchen strode straight out to meet them.

“Thank you both for coming to see me off.”

The two officers dropped to their knees alongside every constable. “We pay our respects to Prince Yi!”

“We’re all brothers here—why kneel? Get up quickly!”

Rising, Han Gu sighed, “Only a few days gone and we already miss Your Highness running the Judicial Office. The new Judicial Investigator cares only for lining his pockets—wouldn’t lift a finger for common folk.”

Wen Xin spat, “Liang’s lapdog too! Can’t stomach staying Chief Constable under him anymore—soon as I finish today’s shift, I’m quitting the Judicial Office and heading home to farm. Anything beats serving a crooked boss.”

“I’m heading home to the countryside too,” Han Gu nodded. “The new guy knows how close we stood with Your Highness and squeezes us every turn. Zero point sticking around.”

“Hate seeing you both dragged down because of me.”

“Never crossed our minds, Your Highness,” Han Gu protested. “Blame a rotten court and treacherous ministers who can’t tolerate honest men speaking for the people. Not even an imperial prince could hold the line.”

Wen Xin added fiercely, “Why serve a court like that? Better go home and tend our own integrity.”

“Well said! ‘When successful, benefit all under Heaven; when thwarted, cultivate one’s own virtue in solitude.’ [TN: Translator’s rendering] In a fading age, keeping your own hands clean is no small thing.”

Both Han Gu and Wen Xin nodded firmly at the prince’s words.

...

Inside the carriage meanwhile, Zhu Xiyan pressed crucial instructions onto Chen Haoyu—most importantly a list of his own contacts in Bianliang to call on right after arriving, any one of whom might hold the key to freeing Shuzhen.

Memorizing every detail alongside the names and addresses Qiao Wenxian had sent via Nan Shuang last night, Chen Haoyu felt confident they stood a solid shot at rescuing his wife.

Suddenly remembering his sister Chen Wenxin, he asked anxiously, “How are my sister and Mother-in-law holding up?”

Zhu Xiyan let out a ragged sigh. “Coming home from Liu’an Villa yesterday, I told them what happened to Shuzhen. Your mother-in-law collapsed sick in bed from heartbreak. Between worrying over Shuzhen and nursing her mother-in-law, Wenxin’s barely holding together herself. Jingyuan and I have to stay strong as men. Every last hope in both households rests on your trip to Bianliang to bring Shuzhen home.”

“Liang took so much silver from our family—surely he won’t torment my wife too badly.”

“I know Liang Shicheng inside out: greedy for silver *and* hungry for women, wanting both ends at once. First time you brought silver, he turned it down because lust came first. When Shuzhen marched in, refused him, and even beat him up, he locked her up and slapped a crime on her head out of spite. Only when you came back the second time couldn’t he bear turning down five hundred thousand taels a second time. Now that the money’s in his pouch, no telling if he’ll lift a finger—don’t pin too much hope on *him*.”

“So five hundred thousand taels went straight down the drain?!”

“Straight down the drain—with nowhere on earth to appeal.”

“Liang Shicheng is pure scum! Why would His Majesty ever put a monster like him in power?!”

“An incompetent emperor naturally surrounds himself with treacherous officials.”

Chen Haoyu stared in surprise. “When you still served in office, Father-in-law, you sang His Majesty’s praises every chance you got—how’d your tune flip overnight?”

“In office, a man’s hands are tied—you say what the role demands, even when your heart sees clear as glass. Funny how what men think inside and what leaves their mouths are two completely different things.”

Chen Haoyu teased lightly, “So are your mouth and heart saying the same thing right now, Father-in-law?”

“Every single word.” Glancing out the window, Zhu Xiyan saw Prince Yi stepping toward his carriage. “Enough talk. One last reminder: every single contact I gave you—don’t miss a soul. Knock on every single door.”

“Got it.”

Just as Zhu Xiyan stepped down, the thunder of hooves and carriage wheels rolled up fast. All eyes turned as a carriage halted and Dongfang Chenze and An Mingxuan stepped down.

Hurrying straight up to Prince Yi, both men cupped their hands and bowed.

“Young Master Qiao sent word yesterday that Your Highness departs for Bianliang today,” Dongfang Chenze said warmly, “so we rushed out to bid you farewell.”

Pulling his foot back down from the carriage step, Shen Jianchen smiled. “Thank you both for coming all this way to see me off.”

An Mingxuan sighed, “Lin’an will feel much emptier with Your Highness gone.”

“You flatter me, Young Master An. To Lin’an, I was only ever a passing traveler—no loss at all.”

“No matter what anyone else says,” Dongfang Chenze replied earnestly, “in our eyes, what Your Highness did meant everything to the people of Lin’an.”

“I appreciate your high regard.”

With horses hitched and waiting, neither friend kept him longer, calling together, “Fair winds all the way, Your Highness!”

Together with everyone else, they watched Prince Yi’s and Chen Haoyu’s carriages roll away.

Three carriages moved out in convoy: Qiao Mengrong, Matron Zou, and Xilan in one; Chen Haoyu with Qinggua and Kuqiao in the second; Shen Jianchen and Bai Feng in the third.

As the wheels rolled down the highway, farewells faded behind them, and Master Jiang’s warning crept straight back into Shen Jianchen’s mind, darkening his mood toward Mengrong once more.

Over the forged scrolls and the Mount Tianmu kidnapping, because Qianli Xun had come out unscathed, he had forgiven Mengrong—even blamed himself. Yet if she had engineered *this* disaster too, he doubted he could ever forgive her again.

Some distance beyond Youlin Retreat, a cluster of figures flagged down the convoy—Qiao Shouren waiting alongside Madam Qian, Concubine Mi, and Wenyuan to say goodbye.

Though Wenyuan had already seen Prince Yi off yesterday, his parents insisted on coming, leaving him no choice as a dutiful son.

Climbing down from his carriage, Shen Jianchen received the Qiao elders’ bows.

Steadying Qiao Shouren’s arms, Shen Jianchen urged gently, “Once I leave, Grandfather, Grandmother, and Aunt Mi must take good care of yourselves. Soon as Wenyuan’s children arrive, you can enjoy bouncing your grandchildren on your knees.”

That warmed every Qiao face at once.

Still, Qiao Shouren couldn’t fully smile—knowing Prince Yi was only returning to Bianliang stripped of his Military Commissioner post, and having heard from Wenyuan yesterday that Princess Consort Yi had lost her wits, Qiao Shouren had lain awake hurting for the prince.

Mental ailments rarely healed; sooner or later, Prince Yi would likely wed again or take a concubine anyway. Better to get the heavy secret off his chest now.

Not wanting Wenyuan to hear, Qiao Shouren told Madam Qian, “Take those two over yonder for a moment—I need a private word with Prince Yi.”

Strolling aside, the three watched from afar as Qiao Shouren leaned close to murmur in the prince’s ear. Wenyuan frowned, wondering what his father could be whispering about, though Madam Qian guessed easily enough it concerned Princess Consort Yi.

Staying inside his carriage, Chen Haoyu stared sharply at the pair, too far to catch a single syllable.

Matron Zou had thought about climbing down to exchange pleasantries with the Qiaos, yet one glance at Princess Consort Yi’s vacant stare drained every ounce of heart from her. Seeing Qiao Shouren pull Prince Yi aside in private out the window sent a cold knot twisting in her gut—praying silently he wouldn’t spill Her Highness’s secret.

Once his family stepped out of earshot, Qiao Shouren lowered his voice. “I’ve swallowed something for months, afraid saying it might ruin things between Your Highness and Princess Consort Yi. Yet ever since Mount Tianmu bandits raided the Chens, my heart’s turned cold with dread. Now that the poetess’s been hauled off on some trumped-up charge, I can’t keep quiet anymore…”

Already guessing the rest, Shen Jianchen finished calmly, “Grandfather suspects Mengrong is behind all of it.”

Relief washed over Qiao Shouren’s shoulders. “So Your Highness already knew.”

“About the earlier plots, yes. This time I can’t be certain yet. Why did Grandfather suspect Mengrong?”

Qiao Shouren held nothing back. “Right after you and Princess Consort Yi returned from your wedding in Bianliang, Matron Zou came calling at Qiao Manor on Her Highness’s behalf—asking our house to find a way to make the poetess disappear. The Qiao family has never touched murder! How could I take that on? So I pointed Matron Zou toward Master Jiang instead. I spent months sweating in fear afterward—thank Heaven word never came that Master Jiang killed the poetess, or my conscience would never let me sleep!”

Now Shen Jianchen knew with absolute certainty: Liang Shicheng’s trap around Qianli Xun traced straight back to Mengrong. Worse, Matron Zou knew everything and had actively aided her—likely Xilan knew the whole story too! Fool that he had been to forgive Mengrong out of guilt last time, landing Qianli Xun straight in mortal peril!

Bidding the Qiaos goodbye and climbing back aboard, Shen Jianchen spoke not a single word the rest of the road—whatever thread of affection still lingered between him and Mengrong had snapped clean for good.
