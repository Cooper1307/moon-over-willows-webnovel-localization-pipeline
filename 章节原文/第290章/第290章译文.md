## Chapter 290: Ambush Beneath the Divine Willow

The moment Young Master left, Kuqiao couldn’t hold back. “Miss, isn’t two years way too long? Everybody else’s kids will be toddlers while you still have nothing!”

“Chen Haoyu spent way longer than two years pulling sleazy stunts—giving him two years is cutting him slack! In an era where women don’t pick their own husbands, at least I still control my own heart, and I’m not handing it to an unreliable guy on a whim. Time tests a person best; if he passes two years, then he’s earned me.”

“What if the Chen elders object?”

“After Chun Jinlan’s mess, even if they hate it inside, they’ll respect my call.”

Relieved, Kuqiao nodded. “So long as you know what you’re doing, Miss.”

With Chen Haoyu home safe, a weight lifted off Qianli Xun’s chest. Wanting fresh air, her mind jumped straight to the Divine Willow—high time she paid it a visit.

Reaching the foot of the hill, a swell of nostalgia hit her. Youlin Retreat sat right nearby; once she could stroll right up to see her friend without a care, yet now a stone’s throw felt like oceans away.

Walking up the path toward the willow, a full year’s worth of memories since crossing over washed through her—one year that felt like half a century. She had family now, had known close friendship, tasted love, even married. Some things were gone, others still in her hands; she no longer clung to what she’d lost, only feared losing what she still held.

Celebrated poetess in public, secret huaben author Scholar Qianli Xun on the sly—which one was the real her? She still couldn’t tell, just like the Divine Willow draped in red silk ribbons before her. She insisted it was the same willow from her modern world, yet back then nobody tied red ribbons onto its branches.

*Stop obsessing over riddles with no answers,* she told herself. *Just like this willow—hiding its mystic power behind plain bark, letting mortals tie ribbons all over its limbs without complaint. Accept what fate hands you, meet every twist with a clear head, and maybe that’s how you actually reshape your destiny.*

The Divine Willow really worked wonders—standing beneath it untangled the knots in her head.

Lifting her eyes to the red-draped crown, she spotted a single ribbon tied high up in the upper boughs. *Someone copied me? Could it be him?*

Curiosity tugging, she sprang up into the high branches with her light-foot technique and flipped the ribbon open—her name and Shen Jianchen’s stared back.

*Already consummated with Mengrong and still hanging our names together? What if Mengrong walks under here and it drops right in her lap? I’d never wash my name clean! Better take it down just in case.*

Untying the ribbon, she rolled it neat, slipped it into the pouch at her waist, retied the pouch, and floated back to the ground.

Her feet had barely touched dirt when a pack of black-clad, masked men brandishing heavy sabers swarmed out of nowhere, encircling her!

No time to wonder who sent them—Qianli Xun barked at Kuqiao, “Get behind the Divine Willow—now!”

Shaken out of her freeze, Kuqiao sprinted behind the huge trunk and poked half her head out to watch.

Unarmed yet confident in her fists, Qianli Xun didn’t flinch as gleaming blades slashed in from every angle. She ducked one sweep and drove a punch square into an attacker’s chest, sending him staggering back. Outnumbered, she pivoted and retreated in bursts, weaving between saber arcs with fists and kicks blurring through the dust.

Spotting an opening, she unleashed a thunderous kick that launched one masked man straight into the willow’s trunk. Kuqiao ducked back behind the bark, peeking a moment later to see the thug slumped on the grass coughing blood, done for the fight.

The rest surged back in. Slipping between them like a darting dragon, Qianli Xun struck with pinpoint precision—snatching one man’s wrist to drop his blade, using his own momentum to slam him down, then slamming a heel into another’s ribs until he doubled over.

Even so, a blade nicked her arm, slicing a shallow red line. Ignoring the sting, she kicked into higher gear, whipping through the ring like a gale as heavy blows landed thud after thud. After she dropped several more, the surviving thugs saw the tide turn and bolted.

Letting them run, Qianli Xun stood catching her breath and eyed the gash on her arm.

Kuqiao dashed out from behind the tree and bound the cut with her handkerchief. “Does it hurt, Miss?”

“Just a scratch.”

“Who were those men? Clearly they came straight for you!”

Noting the downed thug groaning beneath the willow, Qianli Xun walked over. Kuqiao darted ahead and kicked his rear. “Dare jump my miss?! Get back up and try again if you’ve got the guts!”

Groaning unable to rise, he let Kuqiao rip off his black mask. “Ambushing my miss in broad daylight behind a mask—coward!”

Staring at the plain, unfamiliar face, Qianli Xun asked, “Who are you people, and who sent you to kill me?”

Kuqiao scooped up his dropped saber and leveled the point at his nose. “Spill it, or I’ll carve lines across your mug!”

Terrified, the man begged, “Mercy, heroine! Promise you’ll let me go if I talk!”

Qianli Xun sneered. “Your life isn’t worth a single copper to me.”

“Talk!” Kuqiao jabbed the blade closer.

“We’re Master Jiang’s men from the Qiantang Gang! Somebody paid for your head, so Master Jiang sent us!”

Mengrong—straightaway Qianli Xun knew. A hollow ache washed through her chest. Saying nothing more, she turned to leave; Kuqiao dropped the blade, gave the thug one parting kick, and hurried after her.

Three steps down the path, Qiao Mengrong stepped out to block their way, Matron Zou and Xilan right behind her.

Seeing only a bandaged cut on Zhu Shuzhen’s arm, Qiao Mengrong clicked her tongue. “Useless fools! Still alive and kicking, eh?”

Any plea would bounce off a wall of hatred. Qianli Xun met her gaze cold and flat. “Killing me won’t be that easy.”

Stepping closer, Qiao Mengrong stared icy-eyed. “Not easy, no—yet I’ll find ways to make it easier. Consider today a warm-up.”

Unable to bite her tongue, Kuqiao piped up bold. “My miss is married and just wants a quiet life with Young Master—she’s no threat to Princess Consort Yi! Why won’t Your Highness leave her in peace?!”

“Who let a servant run her mouth?!” Qiao Mengrong snapped. “Xilan—slap her!”

Xilan stepped up to strike, only for Qianli Xun to block her arm hard. “Nobody touches my maid—not even royalty!” Shoving Xilan aside, she sent the maid stumbling back to catch her balance.

Knowing rank meant nothing against those fists out here, Qiao Mengrong sneered. “Strong fists—I’ll let your maid slide for now. Still, she’s talking garbage! No threat to me? Then what are you doing sneaking up to the Divine Willow today?!”

Refusing to swallow false accusations, Qianli Xun shot back, “Wrong, Princess Consort Yi. I visited the Divine Willow long before you and Prince Yi ever set foot in Lin’an—what scheme is there in that?”

“Times changed! Since moving into Youlin Retreat, Brother comes here all the time—dare swear you didn’t come hoping to ‘accidentally’ bump into him? Nobody knows you better than me: preaching virtue while playing the harlot behind closed doors! Laughable!”

Anyone else spitting that filth would have caught a fist to the jaw; against Mengrong, Qianli Xun’s hands stayed heavy at her sides.

“Think whatever you like—my conscience is clear.” Reining in her hurt, she curtsied. “Enjoy the view, Your Highness. I have duties at home—excuse me.”

“Hit a nerve and running away?”

Ignoring the jab, Qianli Xun stepped past with Kuqiao—only for Matron Zou to block her path. “How should I address you?”

First time meeting her—around Matron Wei’s age. “Call me whatever you please, Matron.”

“Everyone calls you Youqi Jushi, so I’ll use that. Allow me to introduce myself: I’m Prince Yi’s wet nurse. I raised him from infancy and watched Princess Consort Yi tend his sickbed day and night with her own hands. Back then His Highness had eyes for her alone—until he recovered, came to Lin’an, and put someone else in his heart: Youqi Jushi. Since we crossed paths, let an old woman offer some advice.”

Every word looked like fact from the outside—arguing was pointless. Qianli Xun kept silent.

“Imperial sons follow strict rules when taking wives or concubines,” Matron Zou went on. “You’ve already had one mutual divorce, your reputation in Lin’an is tattered, and you’re another man’s wife now. Aside from decent birth and a pretty face, you don’t fit a single mark. Drop any wild dreams. Keep your head down, and I’ll persuade Her Highness to spare your life.”

Grating as the lecture was, Qianli Xun wanted out fast—and decided to aim her parting plea at the matron instead of deaf Mengrong. “Sound advice, Matron. I know my place and never harbored wild dreams. All I want is a peaceful life—neither bothering anyone nor being bothered. Please urge Princess Consort Yi to stop treating me as an imaginary rival. I’m not afraid to die; I just hate being dragged into someone else’s drama for nothing. And don’t worry—I won’t come back to the Divine Willow again.”

She brushed past Matron Zou with Kuqiao.

Qiao Mengrong laughed bitter at her back. “For nothing! Talking like you’re pure as snow—when you admitted right at the poetry salon that you fell for Brother!”

Etiquette forbade ignoring a royal’s words outright. Halting a few paces on, Qianli Xun turned back, curtsied silent with Kuqiao beside her, then turned clean away and strode off.

A pang of loss pricked Qiao Mengrong—no more nights sharing a bed, talking till midnight and sleeping till noon.

Matron Zou sighed. “She looks like zero threat to you now, Your Highness. Why not let her go—and give yourself peace too?”

“She talked you round in three sentences—doesn’t that prove how dangerous she is?!” Qiao Mengrong snapped. “Slick tongue, and unmatched at hooking men without lifting a finger! Hooked Young Master Qiao till he went on a hunger strike, got Brother risking disgrace to raid a wedding, and even turned a playboy rake like Chen Haoyu into a reformed saint! Tell me she isn’t dangerous!”

“Dangerous or not, she’s married and clearly settling down.”

“No—you don’t know her! Anywhere, any time, she doesn’t even have to speak a word to steal Brother’s soul! How could I ever sleep easy? She has to die!”

Matron Zou sighed in defeat. “Stubborn as you are, nothing I say gets through—I’ll quit preaching. Still, you saw her martial skills today; taking her down won’t be easy.”

Glancing at the groaning thug on the grass, Qiao Mengrong spat, “Hmph! Master Jiang’s men are useless trash—a whole mob couldn’t kill one woman! We need tougher killers. Go see Chen Haodong again, Matron.”

“I’ll head to Yongtai Teahouse tomorrow.”
