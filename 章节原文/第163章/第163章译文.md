## Chapter 163: A Dull Match, Yet Someone Watches

After her laugh faded, Madam Qian turned back to the field and marveled again. "Miss Zhu's chuiwan skill is remarkable, neck and neck with Prince Yi. Looking at how this is going, the Chen siblings are bound to lose."

"Too soon to call it, Sister," Concubine Mi disagreed. "I have kept my eyes on them the whole time. Both sides have three tallies each; there is no telling who will win yet."

A seasoned player herself, Madam Qian had already read the match. "You seldom play, Sister, so you miss the fine points. They tied the first three tallies, yet in both technique and power, the Chen siblings fall short of Prince Yi and Miss Zhu. The Chens took the Middle Tally earlier only because their rivals played poorly. Against real opponents today, they will fall apart by the Grand Tally."

Hearing her young mistress praised warmed Matron Wei's heart. Watching Shuzhen knock yet another ball cleanly into the opposing hole, she beamed. "I never saw my mistress play chuiwan before. I wonder when she picked up such skill."

Madam Qin searched her memory. "Now that you mention it, Matron, I believe Miss Zhu played once before, and her form was nothing special then. She has improved by leaps and bounds."

Madam Qian recalled what Chen Wenxin had remarked upon arriving. "Have you forgotten, Matron Wei? Young Madam Zhu said earlier that she taught her. Truly, the student has surpassed the teacher."

Matron Wei chuckled. "Look at my failing memory."

Out on the field, the match moved at an easy pace, the four players chatting as they went. Paired together, Qianli Xun and Shen Jianchen naturally talked the most. Shen Jianchen thought partnering up for chuiwan had been a stroke of genius: he could walk openly beside her and talk without restraint. Winning or losing mattered little; he only hoped the match would stretch on as long as possible.

From her pavilion, Qiao Mengrong kept her eyes fixed on her brother. Watching his strong, nimble swings, that old sense of strangeness she had not felt in months crept back into her chest.

When they were children, a year after her brother had brought her into Prince Yi's Manor and his health had briefly improved, they had wandered outside the gates and come upon a group of boys playing chuiwan on packed dirt. Her brother had joined in for the first time, yet the ball refused to obey him and never went near a hole. He had quit on the spot and sworn never to touch chuiwan again—and he had kept his word.

When he had sought her out in the gardens earlier to play, she had grown so used to the brother who had come back from the brink of death that she had given it little thought. Now, sitting still and watching close, sorrow tugged at her. He truly was not the brother she used to know.

Reading her mistress's mood, Xilan offered gentle comfort. "His Highness excels at everything now. You ought to be glad, and hold the prince he is today in your heart."

"Xilan, tell me—do I love the brother from before, or the brother now?"

"Before or now, they are both His Highness himself."

"You are right. Why am I letting my mind wander again? I have not had thoughts like that in ages." Worried Bai Feng might have overheard, Qiao Mengrong scanned the pavilion, saw no sign of him, and asked in relief, "Where is Bai Feng?"

"Kuqiao called him away."

Half joking and half earnest, Qiao Mengrong sighed. "Bai Feng is a good lad. I had half a mind to match the two of you up, yet Kuqiao beat me to him."

"I never want to marry," Xilan replied. "I mean to stay by Your Highness's side for life."

Touched beyond words, Qiao Mengrong answered with warm silence and looked back at the field. Her brother stood beside Shuzhen, chatting as they watched Chen Haoyu line up a shot. They looked so cheerful. It was the first time she had ever seen her brother talk with another woman in such bright spirits. Were it anyone other than Shuzhen, she would have sworn he fancied the girl.

Why had her brother never looked that carefree when talking to her?

An answer soon came to her: the spring sun was bright, the field was lively, and dozens of eyes rested on him. Staying stiff would ruin the mood and make him seem aloof. And why did he keep drifting straight to Shuzhen's side, leaving her no chance to chat with her sister-in-law? Because they were teammates, of course—they had to discuss strategy.

Satisfied with her own reasoning, Qiao Mengrong went back to watching him play. Truth be told, chuiwan bored her stiff; if her brother were not on the field, she would not even bother looking.

After leaving the pavilion, Miao Zhiyu strolled alone through the gardens. Recalling how Qiao Wenxian had placed the peach blossom in her hair, she reached up to touch the petals nestled neatly by her chignon. A soft smile curved her lips as she imagined him trailing after her.

She glanced over her shoulder, only to see Youfan behind her and no trace of Qiao Wenxian. Disappointment dulled her mood as she walked on at a slow pace. Her marriage was settled, and to the man she had longed for—Heaven had been kind to her. Yet the thought of that concubine soured her joy all over again.

Just then, a voice called from behind: "Lady Miao!"

Miao Zhiyu turned and saw Xiaguo, Youqi Jushi's maid. Really, what nerve Youqi Jushi had—unable to marry Young Master Qiao herself, she was planting her own maid beside him. What scheme could she be cooking up?

"What do you want?" Miao Zhiyu asked in a cool tone.

Stepping close, Xiaguo dropped into a proper maid's curtsy, as though greeting her mistress. "Lady Miao must already know that on the day you wed Young Master Qiao, I am to be carried into Qiao Manor too. Since we will live under the same roof, I hope Lady Miao will find room to tolerate me."

Youfan flared up on her mistress's behalf. "Hey! What kind of talk is that?"

"I mean no disrespect," Xiaguo replied. "I only wished to learn Lady Miao's true thoughts, and to hope we can live in harmony once we are in the Qiao household."

Miao Zhiyu looked Xiaguo up and down. The girl had decent looks, and a figure no worse than Youqi Jushi's. "To speak plain, this came out of nowhere and I have not made full peace with it yet. What baffles me is why Youqi Jushi would give you to Wenyuan."

"Do not misunderstand, Lady Miao. My mistress had nothing to do with it. It was... it was me. I fell in love with Young Master Qiao and begged him to take me as a concubine. At first he refused, yet I pleaded over and over until he took pity on me and agreed. Today he asked my mistress in person, and she nodded yes with Master Qiao standing right there."

Xiaguo's earnest manner sounded genuine enough, and Miao Zhiyu's tone softened. "When did you fall for Wenyuan?"

"The very first time my mistress met Young Master Qiao."

"The first time?" Miao Zhiyu remembered well that when she and Youqi Jushi had first met Wenyuan, only Kuqiao had been at Shuzhen's side—and Shuzhen's heart had still belonged to Young Master Xu back then. "I do not recall Youqi Jushi having a maid like you at that time."

Catching her own slip, Xiaguo hurried to correct herself. "Not that first time! I meant the first time my mistress saw him after he came back from Bianliang. That was when I fell for him. I meant to wait until my mistress married him and then beg her to let him take me in, yet then everything went sideways..."

"Since Youqi Jushi did not plant you beside Wenyuan on purpose, I need not fuss over it," Miao Zhiyu said. "Besides, what good would fussing do when Master Qiao has already agreed? Once we are at Qiao Manor, know your place, and I will allow you yours."

"Thank you, Lady Miao!"

"Run along now. Do not bother me again."

Xiaguo curtsied and skipped back the way she had come in high spirits. The Qiao chuiwan tournament had paid off beyond her wildest dreams: her future was settled in a single afternoon, leaving only the wedding sedan to arrive.

Watching Xiaguo's retreating back, Youfan scowled. "Miss, anyone can tell Xiaguo is a scheming little minx. You should show a primary wife's authority right now and put her in her place, or she will walk all over you once you are in Qiao Manor."

Miao Zhiyu scoffed. "I am a lady from an official household. Why stoop to sparring with a country maid? What sort of family are the Qiaos? Do you think they will ever take a servant girl seriously?"

Saying no more, Youfan resumed walking beside her mistress.

Meanwhile, after leaving the pavilion, Qiao Wenxian had hidden in a quiet corner watching Shuzhen on the field, replaying sweet memories of their old outings together. Then his mind drifted to the day she broke things off—her face and her words colder than winter snow. Looking back at the green, he saw her chatting and laughing with Prince Yi, nothing like himself, still drowning in heartbreak.

"Heartless woman!" Qiao Wenxian sighed.

Staying close beside his master, Nan Shuang could guess nine-tenths of his thoughts. "Stop dwelling on Youqi Jushi, Young Master. You are about to take a bride—and a... a concubine too. What a grand occasion."

Catching the sour edge in his tone, Qiao Wenxian knew his impulsive promise to take Xiaguo had stung Nan Shuang. "Do you hold it against me, Nan Shuang?"

"Of course I do! Young Master knew very well that I liked Xiaguo."

"Ah, you and I are two pitiful souls, both in love with women we cannot have."

"I am not like you, Young Master. At least Youqi Jushi loved you back once. Xiaguo never spared me a single glance."

"With things as they are, you and Xiaguo are out of the question. Later, I will find you someone far better."

Nan Shuang perked up at once. "Young Master, may I fancy Youfan?"

"Youfan?" Having paid zero notice to Miao Zhiyu's maid, Qiao Wenxian drew a total blank.

"Lady Miao's maid. She was very pleasant to me today and even agreed to be my friend."

Seeing Nan Shuang bounce back with such cheer filled Qiao Wenxian with envy. If only he could let go of a love half as fast.

Just then, Cao Ban and An Mingxuan strolled past and spotted Qiao Wenxian tucked away in the shadows with Nan Shuang. Puzzled, they walked over. "Brother Qiao, why are you hiding back here?" Cao Ban asked.

Qiao Wenxian turned around. "When did you two get here?"

"Ages ago," An Mingxuan said. "You were either with Lady Miao or sitting with your elders, so we dared not interrupt. If you are free now, come join us. Brother Dongfang is here too."

"Where is he hiding out? I did not see hide nor hair of him at the field." Preoccupied with Shuzhen as he was, Qiao Wenxian had not forgotten his friends; Prince Yi had been easy to spot, yet the three scholars had vanished without a trace.

"Your mind was all on Lady Miao, so how could you notice us?" Cao Ban teased. "Chuiwan is a bore. We found a quiet stretch outside the field for a picnic and kite-flying. Care to come along?"

Before his master could answer, Nan Shuang jumped in excitement. "Yes, yes! I love flying kites! Say yes, Young Master!"

Qiao Wenxian glanced back toward the field. Shuzhen had just sunk another shot, beaming in triumph without a clue that he stood watching from the sidelines. Better to clear his head elsewhere. "Chuiwan is dull enough. I will come chat with you."

Beyond the chuiwan grounds stretched a broad meadow bathed in warm spring sunlight. Young people with no taste for chuiwan or social climbing had gathered here to enjoy the spring breeze and send kites soaring high into the clouds.

Marvelling that the Chen estate boasted such a fine meadow, Qiao Wenxian spotted Dongfang Chenze sitting with several young gentlemen on a rough cloth spread over the grass, a low wooden table loaded with food and drink set before them. His eldest sister, Qiao Wenhui, sat among them too—natural enough, since she and Dongfang Chenze were already betrothed.
