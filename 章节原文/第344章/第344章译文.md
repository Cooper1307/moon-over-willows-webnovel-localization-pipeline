## Chapter 344: An Age in Decline, a Heart in Sorrow

At the gate of the prince's mansion, a troop of guards blocked her way.

Qianli Xun didn't waste a word. She waded into them, every guard she saw becoming Wanyan Chi in her eyes, every ounce of her rage poured out.

By the time the last of them lay flat on the ground, Wanyan Chi came hurrying up. He had taken his medicine and looked much better.

"So you mean to leave me?"

Qianli Xun kept her back to him. "Since I can't bring myself to kill you, I'll just stay out of your sight. Do yourself a favor and don't block my way."

"Have you no attachment to me at all?"

"Hah! Don't make me laugh. What would I have to cling to you for?"

Wanyan Chi's heart twisted. "Fine. If I can't keep you, I'll let you go. But remember this: I'm letting you go because I love you."

Qianli Xun spun around. "Pah! Spare me your talk of love. I don't buy it. You bully me in love's name, and don't flatter yourself that I'll turn soft and clingy like the other women, cooing 'my little prince' and hanging on your sleeve. Dream on! I choose only the man I judge to be right. You're not in my range of choices. Even if you beat me black and blue, I will never bend."

The scolding jolted Wanyan Chi awake, though his mouth still refused to yield. "Wherever you go, one thing won't change: I was your first man."

He had found the soft spot again. She itched to fight him, but to get out cleanly she swallowed it, turned without a sound, and walked toward the gate.

Regret washed over him. A sudden fit of kindness seized him. "Hey! The Prince Yi of Great Song came here once with ten million taels. I told him you were dead and took him for another ten million. The banknotes are in my room. Don't believe me? I'll take you to see."

Qianli Xun stopped short. Tears burst from her. He really had come to save her. The mansion in Bianliang and all his holdings would likely be forfeit now. He had done so much for her, and she was no longer the woman she had been—she was worth none of it.

Since Wanyan Chi believed her dead, she would let him go on believing it. Only then could he live his life in peace. Besides, the woman she had been truly was dead.

Wanyan Chi went on. "Because I love you, I want the best for you once you're gone. Go to him. I can see his feelings for you are real. If you're with him, I can rest easy."

Qianli Xun didn't answer. She walked straight out of the mansion.

Wanyan Chi stood staring at the empty gate, unwilling to move. He had finally cared for someone, and now he had no choice but to let her go. However much he resented it, there was nothing he could do.

Lu Yuanfeng came in from outside and found the young prince still staring at the gate. "Why are you standing here, my lord?"

"Your master has gone. Follow her quietly, don't let her notice, and report back when she's settled."

"She's likely heading for Lin'an."

"Wherever she goes, follow her until she's settled."

"Yes, sir."

…

When word reached Old Madam Xia that Shuzhen was dead, she could hold on no longer and breathed her last. The Chen family wept, then everyone busied themselves with the funeral.

Bereft of his wife and then his grandmother, Chen Haoyu took the double blow hard. He was worn to the bone, yet he kept his vigil at her coffin.

His father Chen Jianglin and two uncles handled the arrangements, which lifted a weight off him. All he had to do was kneel with his cousin Chen Haofei on either side of the mourning hall and receive those who came to pay their respects.

Two years earlier, Chen Haofei had sat the palace examination and placed in the third rank. Because of Old Madam Xia's connection to Liang Shihui, Liang Shicheng had not dragged the Chen family into the woman poet's case.

But Chen Haofei was a straight-backed man. He could not stomach the way courtiers curried favor and formed cliques, still less His Majesty's muddle-headedness. He made enemies at court and, after much thought, resigned and went home.

When he first returned, Chen Jiangmu and Mrs. Cui never stopped grumbling. All those years of study, they said, just to pass the exam—and now that he had, he threw it away?

Chen Haofei didn't care to explain. He threw himself into learning the trade from his elder uncle and his cousin Haoyu, often sighing that he should have given up the idea of an official career sooner.

Under Lin'an's new prefect, the family business wasn't what it had been, but it beat day after day of court intrigue. Chen Haofei grew content with the busy work.

His daughter had spent nearly two years in prison, then been seized by the Jin, and now she was gone. No one grieved more than Zhu Xiyan. He shut himself in his room and refused to see anyone.

Madam Xiang had been improving. The news of Shuzhen's death laid her low again.

Chen Wenxin had to tend her mother-in-law and worry over Old Madam Xia's funeral besides. Only after she had settled the maids did she leave Zhu Manor with Zhu Jingyuan for the Chen house.

Two days after Old Madam Xia was buried, the Zhu family's old ancestor passed away as well.

It was Matron Xiuzhen who came with the news. Only then did Zhu Xiyan come out of his room, looking utterly haggard.

Matron Xiuzhen said, "The old ancestor went very peacefully. She knew her hour had come. She told me to tell the master of Zhu Manor not to make a great show of it—cremate the body and bury the ashes in the Zhu family cemetery, that's all."

Zhu Xiyan did as she asked. After the old ancestor was buried, he thought of his daughter lying in a strange land, and tears ran down his old face.

After two days of quiet, Zhu Xiyan called at the Chen residence in person. He told Chen Jianglin and Yao Qingyan that his daughter had been denied a good fate, and asked them to persuade Haoyu to let Shuzhen go and take another wife.

Chen Jianglin and Yao Qingyan nodded and agreed, but once he was gone neither could find the words. They settled on waiting until after the New Year.

Once Old Madam Xia's funeral was over, Chen Haoyu shut himself in the Suxin Study in Moxiang Courtyard with only Qinggua for company.

He lay on the bed his wife had slept in, going over their past again and again—the way she had kissed him in prison. He hugged a pillow and cried.

Qinggua said nothing. His master's heart was aching; a good cry would help.

For days on end, Chen Haoyu did nothing but lie on his wife's bed, aside from eating and using the privy. He couldn't even be bothered with the family business.

His family understood and left him alone.

Matron Wei had begun to worry the day the young mistress was imprisoned. In nearly two years, white hairs had crept plainly into her black. When the word came that the Jin had killed her, half her hair turned white in two days, and she looked years older.

The young mistress's marriage had long been annulled. She and Kuqiao and the ten servants were still at the Chen house only because everyone knew the annulment was a blind to fool the Jin.

Now that she was gone, Matron Wei saw no reason to stay. She asked the master and madam first, hoping they would understand and let her take the young mistress's servants away.

Yao Qingyan looked at her drawn face, her nose stung, and tears came before she could stop them. "This needs Haoyu's consent."

Chen Jianglin added, "What Haoyu says goes. Ask him. If he agrees, take your people and go. If not, you're welcome to stay—Qingyan and I would be glad of it."

Yao Qingyan dried her eyes. "The old madam is gone; the house is so quiet. If you stay, I'll have someone to talk to."

Matron Wei thanked the master and madam and went back to Moxiang Courtyard. She stood outside the Suxin Study and called in, "Is the young master still inside?"

Qinggua opened the door and asked softly, "Matron, what is it?"

"I need to ask the young master something."

"He's lying inside. He sees no one."

"I'll speak louder out here. He's bound to hear me."

Qinggua had no choice but to let her, silently accepting the idea. He opened the door wider so his master could hear clearly.

Matron Wei raised her voice. "Young master—'young master' no longer fits, I know, but I can't change my habit overnight, so forgive me. The mistress is gone, the marriage is gone. It wouldn't be right for me and her servants to stay on. I've come to ask permission: in a few days, I'd like to take Kuqiao and the ten servants back to Zhu Manor."

Kuqiao heard her from Yuening's room and came out, her eyes red and swollen from crying.

She went to Matron Wei's side. "Are we really going back to Zhu Manor? I don't believe the young mistress is gone. She has to be alive."

"Silly girl. I wish she were alive too. The word came from Prince Yi, and no one wanted her alive more than he did. Would he lie about it?"

Chen Haoyu stepped out of the inner room and stood in the doorway. He looked worse than Matron Wei or Kuqiao.

"Stay in Moxiang Courtyard. Not one of you leaves. She is my wife forever—gone or not, she is my wife."

Matron Wei coaxed him. "You're wrong, young master. Moxiang Courtyard will have a new mistress sooner or later. It isn't right for us to go on here."

"No one is coming. No one, ever…" He mumbled it and went back inside to lie down.

Matron Wei said to Kuqiao, "I thought if we left of our own accord, it might help him climb out of his grief and start a new life. But he… ah, well. Let it be. We'll stay a while longer."

Kuqiao cried as she spoke. "Matron, Hongdou and the others are hurting enough. Seeing me like this will only hurt them more. Could I sit in your room for a while?"

"Come, then. Keep me company. I keep thinking I hear the mistress calling me."

Her words cut Kuqiao deeper. She wiped her eyes and followed Matron Wei to Jingyi Hall.

…

The Jin poured south in force, driving straight at Bianliang. The Song court was in chaos, and His Majesty hurriedly sent envoys to their camp to sue for peace. Wanyan Zongwang would hear none of it; he meant to take Bianliang and press south without pause.

Zhao Ji, at his wits' end, followed his ministers' advice and passed the throne to the crown prince, Zhao Huan.

Zhao Huan took the throne at the worst of moments and could hardly rejoice. His father was plainly washing his hands of it. He refused outright, then several times pretended to faint dead away, or broke into great tears—none of it worked. Under his father's pressure he finally mounted the throne, and Great Song entered the first year of Jingkang.

In the first month of Jingkang, Wanyan Zonghan led the Jin eastern army to the walls of Bianliang.

Li Gang, vice minister of the Court of Sacrificial Worship, directed the fighting in person and beat back the besiegers several times. Volunteer armies in Hebei and Shandong rose up as well. The tide turned against the Jin, who took heavy losses and had to withdraw.

Wanyan Zongwang would not give up. He tried to lure the Song into surrender. The court split at once: one faction wanted to cede territory for peace, the other to fight. Li Gang opposed the cession tooth and nail, and Zhao Huan stripped him of office. The soldiers and people protested, and Zhao Huan reinstated him.

Li Gang commanded the defense of Bianliang, and Wanyan Zongwang could make no headway. In the end Song offered the three towns of Zhongshan, Hejian, and Taiyuan, and the Jin withdrew. The defense of Bianliang was won—but the appeasers gained the upper hand, and Li Gang was forced to leave the city.

The teetering court caught its breath, and the common people found a few more ordinary days.

The New Year came and went without a spark of celebration; luckily, it passed in a daze. At last spring arrived, the world woke again, and hearts lifted a little.

All winter Chen Haoyu had ignored the business. He dragged about, low and listless—sitting alone with cup after cup of bitter wine, or taking out his wife's poems to read.

Every poem she had written seemed to put her beside him again. To keep that feeling each day, he read her poems daily, until most of them were fixed in his memory.

He no longer needed paper or a collection. The lines came at a word's notice.

As he drank, he recited one of her *Busuanzi: Ode to the Plum Blossom*: "One bough leans sideways deep in the bamboo grove, / lending the quiet woods a stillness deeper still. / After rain, pure beauty no brush could ever paint— / sparse shadows stretched across the shallow water. / A flute plays the Little Chanyu tune clean through, / stirring old sorrows back to mind once more. / Faint fragrance drifts across the gentle breeze, / while chill moonlight wraps the blossoms cold." [TN: Translator's rendering]

Qinggua worried about his master. Ever since the young madam's death, he had not set foot outside Moxiang Courtyard. This couldn't go on. He had to find a way to get him out, if only for a change of air.

"Master, why don't we go to the tiles and listen to a storyteller?"

"What's to hear? What could beat my wife's poems?" He drained another cup. Then he remembered: she had once said there was another way to know her—to hear the tales the scholar Qianli Xun wrote. He set down his cup and stood to go.

Qinggua saw him heading out and was delighted. He hurried after him.

They reached Qiaotou Tile and found the storytelling house, only to learn the performer had been ill and hadn't come for days.

Chen Haoyu was disappointed and turned to go home.

Qinggua suggested, "Since we're out, why not wander a bit, master? Take your mind off things."

"What's to see? What lane or street of Lin'an haven't I been down?" He had no heart for it, and drifted listlessly out of the tile-house.

Qinggua followed, turning over ways to get him to stroll willingly. He couldn't help marveling: once his master could hardly stay home; now he had to be coaxed out of the door. All of it, because of the young madam.

Thinking of her gave him an idea at last. "There's one place I bet you've never been, master."

Chen Haoyu didn't answer. He went to where the carriage waited and stepped aboard.

Qinggua climbed in after him and went on as they sat down. "Have you ever been to Huayun Pavilion, master?"

There was no house of pleasure in Lin'an Chen Haoyu had missed; of course he had been to Huayun Pavilion, though never a regular. The girls there were all accomplished, and the men they kept company with were scholars and poets—the old Chen Haoyu had looked down on the whole place. He had gone a few times and never returned.

His master said nothing, but the look on his face said yes. Qinggua had expected as much, and he was ready for it.

"You may have been, master, but you don't know Huayun Pavilion has a girl who sings my young madam's poems better than anyone. The tunes, they say, were written by Master Dongfang. Rather than recite her verses here alone, why not hear how the girl at Huayun Pavilion sings them?"

Chen Haoyu remembered Xiangrong at Huayun Pavilion singing his wife's words at the Dongfang poetry gathering—and singing them beautifully. This must be the girl Qinggua meant.

"How do you know all this? Have you been to Huayun Pavilion?"

"You didn't know, master. When I was busy with you at the Yongtea Teahouse, a few girls from Huayun Pavilion came in for tea. I overheard them talking."

If she sang his wife's poems, he would go. "To Huayun Pavilion."

He had finally talked him around. Qinggua beamed and called to the driver outside, "To Huayun Pavilion!"
