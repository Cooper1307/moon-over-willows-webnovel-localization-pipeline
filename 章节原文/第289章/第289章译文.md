## Chapter 289: Two More Years Before Consummation

Back at Chen Manor, Chen Jianglin didn’t summon the elders to air Chen Haodong’s meddling—family shame stayed behind closed doors. Keeping only Chen Haodong in the reception hall, he had servants shut the doors.

Chen Haodong dropped right back to his knees. “I was wrong, Uncle—give me whatever punishment I deserve.”

Sitting back with a sigh, Chen Jianglin asked, “Was this over the family business inheritance?”

No point hiding it now. “Yes. I feared the elders might pick Haoyu, throwing away everything I’ve worked for since childhood.”

“Use your head, Haodong,” Chen Jianglin reasoned. “Where does Haoyu even measure up to you? Improved as he is, he doesn’t have a fraction of your business savvy—why treat him as a rival? One day Chen Manor passes to your generation. You three brothers have to pull together, not tear each other apart over scraps and wreck the house.”

Hanging his head in mock shame, Chen Haodong swore, “Never again, Uncle.”

“Enough. Go back—and don’t breathe a word to your parents. Consider it forgotten.”

Covering it up meant Uncle wanted his record spotless—which meant Uncle still backed him for heir! Thrilled inside, Chen Haodong bowed out to hurry straight home and brief his parents.

Sitting alone in the hall a while to think, Chen Jianglin finally headed back to his own courtyard, where Yao Qingyan rushed out to meet him. “How did the hearing go?”

“Haoyu’s cleared and released.”

Relief flooded her face. “Then why didn’t he come greet his own mother?”

“Chun Jinlan died, and guilt’s eating him alive. He went straight to Moxiang Courtyard—he’ll pay respects once he collects himself.”

“Died?! How?”

“Come sit inside and I’ll tell you everything.”

Seated on the couch, Chen Jianglin walked her through the entire trial. Yao Qingyan’s smile faded. “Still Haoyu’s own fault—if he hadn’t snatched her, none of this happens. He ought to feel guilty. Least we can do is give her a decent burial.”

“Already seeing to it. Now there’s another matter I want your take on: choosing the business heir.”

“That’s huge—shouldn’t you ask Mother and your brothers?”

“I will, yet I want your thoughts first.”

“Since you ask: as a mother, of course I’d wish for Haoyu, yet he’s miles behind Haodong, and Haofei has zero interest. Haodong is the only real fit. If Haoyu just pitches in to help him, I’ll be content.”

Chen Jianglin nodded. “Glad you see it that way. The selection wasn’t due till next year—I want to move it up to this year.”

“Move it up? Is there any precedent for changing the schedule?”

“None, yet I set the date the day I took over, so I can change it too.”

“Fine by me if the elders agree. Only why the sudden rush?”

He rarely kept secrets from his wife, yet airing Haodong’s scheme would only upset her and sour relations with her sister-in-law. “Just as His Majesty names a Crown Prince early to steady the realm, naming our heir early steadies the house. A steady house keeps the juniors at peace—and peace makes the family prosper.”

“You’ve got my full backing.”

With her blessing, Chen Jianglin escorted Yao Qingyan to report the trial outcome to Old Madam Xia and raise moving up the selection.

Meanwhile, Chen Jiangshu had just returned from his shops and taken a sip of tea when Chen Haodong rushed in and waved the servants out.

“What’s the fire? How’d the trial go?”

Shutting the door once the help cleared out, Chen Haodong lifted the teacup right out of his father’s hand and set it on the table.

“Hey—I was drinking that! What’s the idea?”

“Eldest Uncle knows I put Chun Jinlan up to it.”

Chen Jiangshu’s hand twitched—now he saw why his son took the cup away. Stepping from the inner room, Madam Bao asked in panic, “If Eldest Brother knows, will it sink your inheritance?!”

Chen Jiangshu sat grim, fearing years of groundwork had blown up.

“Uncle doesn’t know you two were in on it,” Chen Haodong added. “Even warned me not to tell you.”

Chen Jiangshu’s shoulders dropped in relief. “That means he’s keeping it under wraps—you’re still in the driver’s seat! Makes sense: out of the three boys, you tower over the rest. Eldest Brother has no other choice, so he won’t tarnish your name.”

“If it was already in the bag,” Madam Bao sighed, sinking into a chair, “why pull all those stunts behind the scenes?”

“Needed them!” Chen Jiangshu argued. “Sunk Haoyu for good and nudged Eldest Brother into deciding early—letting him know Haodong pulled the strings wasn’t even bad. Wait, how did the hearing end? Did Haoyu take the courtesan as concubine?”

“Prince Yi’s sharp—dug up a witness proving Haoyu wasn’t there that night and forced Scarface to take the fall. Out of moves, Chun Jinlan threatened suicide and killed herself right in court.”

Madam Bao clicked her tongue. “Scarface did it?! Haoyu’s got devil’s luck—picked every flower in sight and dodged the one with thorns.”

“Never mind who did it—good that a Chen lad came home safe,” Chen Jiangshu said firm. “When the yamen stepped in I broke a sweat too—if he’d been guilty, I’d have urged him to take her as concubine just to save his head. Remember this, Haodong: scheme all you want, never gamble with a kinsman’s life.”

“Understood, Father.”

“Worked out best for us anyway—Haoyu’s home safe, yet his reputation with the clan is mud. Nobody stands in your way now, so quit while we’re ahead.”

*Time to let Master Jiang make his move,* Chen Haodong thought silently.

Over at Moxiang Courtyard, Chen Haoyu arrived with Qinggua, everything looking the same while his heart sat heavy as lead. Days ago he felt sure he’d win his wife over; now he felt unworthy to stand in her shadow.

Without waiting to be scolded, Chen Haoyu dropped to both knees right outside Suxin Thatched Hall. Understanding his mood, Qinggua knelt beside him.

Touched after Qinggua had already kept him company at the Judicial Office, Chen Haoyu scolded, “You didn’t do anything wrong—why kneel?”

“I’m your man, Young Master. You kneel, I kneel.”

“Don’t bother—go check on Chouya and let her know you’re safe.”

Mention of Chouya popped Qinggua straight up. “I’ll check on Chouya and come right back to kneel with you!”

Watching him dash off toward the west wing, Chen Haoyu smiled wry. “Lucky guy—walks straight back to the girl he likes with a clear conscience, while your master...”

Early summer sun beat down fierce even as afternoon slanted west, beading sweat across his brow in minutes.

Indoors, Qianli Xun sat at her desk writing her huaben—the last few days had sparked fresh plot twists, and writing beat stewing over Chen Haoyu.

Aware of the scene outside, Kuqiao stepped up and whispered, “Young Master’s back, Miss—kneeling out in the yard.”

Qianli Xun paused her brush, relieved—home meant cleared. Still, none of this would’ve happened if he hadn’t snatched girls in the first place. Let him kneel and pay a little penance. She dipped her brush and kept writing. Seeing her temper still up, Kuqiao left her be.

Soon after, Old Madam Xia arrived with Chen Jianglin and Yao Qingyan, unable to stay away once she heard her grandson was cleared. Spotting him kneeling in the sun, Old Madam Xia raised a hand to halt everyone. “Leave them be—the young couple has scores to settle.”

Yao Qingyan winced. “Kneeling in this blazing sun...”

“Better kneeling in the sun than rotting in jail awaiting the axe!” Old Madam Xia turned right back out, Yao Qingyan trailing with a backward glance.

After half an hour of writing, Qianli Xun remembered his stabbed arm—sweating in the heat would infect the wound. Softening, she set down her brush and opened the door.

Head bowed and brow drenched in sweat, Chen Haoyu wore a fresh robe over his bandaged arm.

“Get up and come inside.”

Her voice hit like a cool summer breeze. “Coming!” Scrambling up on numb legs, he hobbled toward the door.

“Kuqiao! Help Young Master in!”

Kuqiao hurried out to steady him—and Chen Haoyu beamed inside: the first time his wife had called him “Young Master” in front of a servant, owning him as her husband!

Once Kuqiao seated him and went to whisk tea, Qianli Xun returned to her desk and asked flat, “Arm any better?”

“Much better with Qinggua looking after me.”

“Tell me how the trial went.”

“Scarface confessed. Chun Jinlan hid a dagger and killed herself in court.”

Qianli Xun almost mentioned Chen Haodong’s hand in sending Chun Jinlan over, then bit it back. Trouble had just blown over, and Haoyu looked beat up enough without learning his own cousin had knifed him. “Quit pulling reckless stunts.”

“Never again, I swear! Can you forgive me?”

“Forgive or not doesn’t matter—I married you, so I’m stuck with my bed.”

A relieved grin broke over his face. “Long as you don’t ask for a mutual divorce, I’ll do anything you say!”

Her temper flared. “Is that what you think of me?! I only went through a mutual divorce once because I had zero choice! What woman treats marriage like a joke, tossing divorce around every five minutes? Even if I didn’t mind leaving you, I’d never bear leaving Grandmother and my in-laws—don’t bring up those words around me again!”

“Never! Swear I won’t breathe them!”

Kuqiao brought over the freshly whisked tea. Parched from the sun, Chen Haoyu downed it in one gulp and handed back the bowl. “Soon I’ll be making all my wife’s tea.”

Kuqiao teased, “Young Master’s busy learning business—where will you find time to whisk tea?”

“Give me a few more days with the tea master—once I’m sharp, whenever I’m home, don’t you dare fight me for tea duty.”

“Deal—all yours!” Setting the bowl down, Kuqiao tucked away her miss’s huaben manuscript discreetly.

“Chun Jinlan was a pitiable soul,” Qianli Xun said. “At the root, you dragged her into this—see to her burial and look after her family.”

“Don’t worry—Father already arranged everything.”

A brief hush fell before Qianli Xun spoke up. “Chen Haoyu, since you said you’ll do anything I ask, I’ve got one condition...”

“Name it, Wife.”

“I’d been trying to warm up to you and consummate our marriage—if none of this had hit, maybe we already would have. Now... who knows when I’ll feel ready.”

His shoulders sagged.

“I’ll give you two years,” she went on. “Keep away from other women and stay chaste for me for two full years, and at the end of two years, I’ll consummate our marriage.”

Hope flared like sunrise in the dark. “Deal! Think of it as two years to scrub every stain off myself—I’ll work my tail off to become a man worthy of you!”

“Shaken and sealed!”

Standing behind her miss with the stacked manuscript, Kuqiao frowned in worry.

Chen Haoyu rose and bowed solemn as a scholar. “From this day on I swear off all women and treat my wife with utmost respect, never stepping a single inch out of line until the day you’re satisfied.”

His dead-serious formality threw Qianli Xun right off balance, leaving her blinking dumbly as he marched out of Suxin Thatched Hall.
