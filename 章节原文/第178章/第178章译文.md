## Chapter 178: Scheming to Make Master Jiang a Matchmaker

Having caroused late at Chunxiang Brothel the night before, Scarface still rose at dawn while Chen Haoyu slept off his night in Chun Jinmo's arms, slipping over to feed Chen Haodong fresh intel and pocket extra silver—playing both sides like a fish in water.

Hiding behind a thick tree outside Chen Manor, Scarface kept watch on the front gate. Eldest Young Master Chen juggled business and pleasure with equal ease, sneaking trysts with Chun Jinchan in spare moments yet always first out the door at dawn for family trade.

Sure enough, Chen Haodong stepped out first. Scarface poked his head from behind the trunk. "Eldest Young Master! Over here! Over here!"

Glancing around before walking over, Chen Haodong snapped, "You scruffy rat—making me walk extra steps? Show some respect next time."

"No offense, Eldest Young Master," Scarface grinned. "If anyone catches me talking to you, my cover as your eyes and ears is blown."

"More like you are just chasing silver."

"Whatever the motive, long as it helps Eldest Young Master, it is all good!"

Chen Haodong tossed him a silver ingot. "Your pay. People will be coming out soon—make it snappy."

Pocketing the ingot with a broad grin, Scarface reported, "Somebody tried to jump Young Master Chen at Chunxiang Brothel last night."

"Who?"

"Master Jiang."

"Master Jiang runs the underworld—how did my cousin cross him?"

"Over Chun Jinmo, who else? Master Jiang wants her too, yet she only takes Young Master Chen and shuts everyone else out."

"How did it end?"

"Young Master Chen hired a tough bodyguard who stays glued to his hip, so Master Jiang struck out. Then Division Commander Lin stormed in with troops and broke it up."

"Ah, that cousin of mine is good for nothing, yet he has the devil's own luck. Anything else?"

Scarface shook his head. "That is all."

"From now on, do not lurk outside Chen Manor," Chen Haodong warned. "Bring news to Yongtai Teahouse by West Lake—I stop by every few days."

"Who knew Eldest Young Master had such refined taste?" Scarface flattered. "I hear scholars and poets pack that place!"

"Save the lip and beat it before someone sees you."

Once Scarface slipped away, Chen Haodong lost all mind to visit the shops and turned straight back into the manor. His father had returned late from the Qiao wedding the night before, so he had not disturbed him with news of Miss Zhu's rampage; now, with Scarface's report sparking a bright scheme, he went straight to consult him.

Reaching his father's quarters, he found Chen Jiangshu already up and eating breakfast. "Eaten yet?" his father asked.

"I have. Meant to head to the shops, then something came up I need to talk over with Father."

"Sit down and talk," Chen Jiangshu said between spoonfuls of congee.

Taking a chair, Chen Haodong stayed silent and glanced at the maids. Catching the hint, Chen Jiangshu dismissed them. "Leave us—I need words with Eldest Young Master."

Once the maids curtsied out, Chen Jiangshu picked up his spoon again. "Speak freely. Nobody here but the two of us—your mother is still asleep inside."

"Father and Mother went to the Qiao wedding yesterday and missed quite a show."

"Miss Zhu storming the gates?"

"Father already knows?"

"A ruckus that loud, every servant in Chen Manor knows. Your Third Uncle dropped by last night and filled me in too."

"Saves me explaining, then. Did Third Uncle mention Grandmother wants Haoyu to inherit the family business?"

Chen Jiangshu froze, laying his spoon down inside the bowl. "He mentioned nothing of the sort. Where did you hear that?"

"I hid nearby during Miss Zhu's rampage yesterday and heard every word. Grandmother felt sorry for Haoyu and agreed to cancel the match. When Eldest Uncle and Aunt pleaded with her to reconsider, Grandmother snapped that Haoyu has heavy responsibilities ahead and cannot marry a bride with a bad name. Sounds clear as day her vote is going to Haoyu."

Pushing his bowl aside, Chen Jiangshu leaned back and mulled it over. "So Old Madam really wants Haoyu as heir—I misread her before. Looking back, she agreed to the Zhu match at first thinking Prefect Zhu would give Haoyu official backing; now that she sees Miss Zhu's tarnished name might hurt her precious grandson, she wants out."

"My thoughts exactly."

"So—do you want the match broken off, or kept?"

Chen Haodong weighed it a beat. "Since marrying Miss Zhu gives Haoyu zero edge for inheriting the business—and will even drag him down—letting him marry her is best. Besides, whatever Haoyu hates doing most is exactly what I want him stuck doing. I refuse to let him sail through life easy."

Chen Jiangshu nodded. "Then this marriage must not fall through. I wonder where your Eldest Uncle stands—is he still preparing the wedding?"

"I asked servants in Eldest Uncle's wing yesterday. Word is he claims he values the match so much he wants a grander feast than the Qiaos' and postponed the date to prepare."

Chen Jiangshu let out a dry laugh. "Pure pretext! From what I know, the wedding your Eldest Uncle already laid out beats the Qiaos' hands down. Looks like the match is stalling out."

"Father, why not give Haoyu a helping hand and make sure he marries that she-demon?" Chen Haodong leaned forward eagerly.

"What scheme do you have in mind?"

Rising, Chen Haodong leaned close and whispered his plan into his father's ear.

A wide grin spread across Chen Jiangshu's face. "Splendid! Set it in motion. Take whatever silver you need—only make certain you do not lose our Chen family fortune for real."

"Leave it to me, Father."

Taking his leave, Chen Haodong skipped the shops again and rode his carriage to the quietest edge of the eastern quarter, pulling up before Master Jiang's lair. Above the gate hung a plaque reading "Qiantang Gang"—named simply because Master Jiang liked the Qiantang River.

Two brawny guards stood outside. Alighting, Chen Haodong stated his business, and one guard led him in past yards full of men drilling weapons—quite a sprawling outfit, Chen Haodong marveled. After waiting briefly outside the main hall, he was shown inside.

Seated at a table gnawing a chicken leg with two mean-looking lieutenants flanking him, Master Jiang wiped his grease-stained fingers on a rag. "Chen Haoyu's cousin, eh? What brings you here?"

Cupping his hands, Chen Haodong bowed smoothly. "Long have I admired Master Jiang's fame. Family trade kept me tied up, or I would have called to make your acquaintance sooner. Today I finally carved out time—hope Master Jiang will not turn me from your door."

"I love making friends! You walk right into my hall—why would I turn you away? Bring a seat!"

A lieutenant set a chair down. Settling in, Chen Haodong smiled. "I brought a modest gift in my carriage outside. Master Jiang may have your men fetch it."

Eyes brightening, Master Jiang waved at his lieutenants. "Go haul it in for a look!"

Moments later, the two carried in a wooden chest and flipped the lid to reveal gleaming stacks of white silver.

Master Jiang grinned from ear to ear. "Shut the lid before it blinds me!" As the guards moved the chest aside, he eyed his guest shrewdly. "Spending this heavy, Eldest Young Master Chen—surely you are not here begging mercy for Chen Haoyu?"

"Far from it. I came to ask Master Jiang to help seal my cousin's marriage to Miss Zhu."

Master Jiang blinked. "When did I turn into the Old Man Under the Moon, playing matchmaker?"

Before explaining, Chen Haodong poured on smooth flattery. "Folks say Master Jiang is a stand-up hero who built the Qiantang Gang so his brothers could make a living. They say you aid the poor and commoners give you thumbs-up on the street. Above all, your word is solid gold—you never go soft on silver that is owed you, and never touch a single coin that is not."

Master Jiang blinked in mild amusement. "Am I really that saintly? One thing you got right, though: what is mine I take to the last coin, and what is not mine I leave untouched. Even our line of work has its code. Quit buttering me up and spit out the job—I do not take silver for free."

Reassured, Chen Haodong stood, stepped beside Master Jiang, and whispered his scheme into his ear.

Understanding lit up Master Jiang's face. "So THAT is how I play matchmaker! Aren't you scared I walk off with the entire Chen fortune for real?"

"I came to you precisely because I trust Master Jiang," Chen Haodong replied. "Once it is done, two more chests of silver come straight to your door—only I will need Master Jiang to put it down on a signed pledge."

Master Jiang scowled. "Sweet talk on your lips, distrust in your bones! Fine—seeing your plan lets me mess up Chen Haoyu, I will write your pledge. Bring brush and paper!"

One guard cleared the chicken bones while the other laid out writing gear. Master Jiang wrote out the pledge and pressed his thumbprint. "One lesson I learned walking the rivers and lakes: never get greedy for wealth not meant for you, lest you choke on it and lose your neck."

Tucking the pledge safely inside his robe, Chen Haodong praised him. "Master Jiang sees life clear as crystal. Respect!"

Master Jiang let out a sigh. "Clever plan—only a crying shame for that heroine. Pretty, packs a wallop, writes fine poetry, and stuck marrying Chen Haoyu. What a waste."

It took Chen Haodong a beat to catch who "that heroine" referred to. "Who knew Master Jiang had a tender streak for fair ladies? No need to pity Miss Zhu—fierce as she is, she will never take the losing end at Chen Manor. Best of all, my cousin will live in daily misery under a shrew's thumb, venting your grudge for you too."

Master Jiang brightened at once. "Good point! With a tigress wife watching him like a hawk, Chen Haoyu will never dare set foot in Chunxiang Brothel again—and Chun Jinmo falls straight into my lap! Heh, the more I chew on this plan, the sweeter it gets!"

With the conspiracy sealed, Chen Haodong exited the Qiantang Gang and headed off to manage Chen trade, waiting with quiet relish for good news.
