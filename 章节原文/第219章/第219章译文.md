## Chapter 219: Eldest Sister-in-Law's Secret Romance

Once the storyteller finished and left, the younger clan members drifted away as well. Chen Haofei had skipped today's session, so Madam Cui excused herself to check on him. Reluctant to let Yao Qingyan hog Old Madam Xia's ear and outshine her, Madam Bao stayed behind. Seeing Grandmother in good company—and guessing Yao Qingyan had heart-to-heart words to say—Qianli Xun bowed and left.

Together, the two daughters-in-law helped Old Madam Xia indoors. The old lady glowed inside; her courtyard hummed with life, her eldest daughter-in-law had caught on at last, her eldest son would soon follow, and she would never lack company again.

The moment Old Madam Xia sat down, Yao Qingyan dropped to her knees. "Mother, I was wrong all these years to misread your heart. Beat me or scold me however you wish."

Before Old Madam Xia could speak, Madam Bao cut in tartly, "Sister kneeling like that leaves me nowhere to sit."

Ignoring Madam Bao, Old Madam Xia told Yao Qingyan, "Get up. From now on, drop by whenever you have spare time to chat and keep me from getting bored. Don't leave me stranded in this yard forcing me to pick fights just to get noticed."

"Yes, Mother. I'll come keep you company every day." Helped to her feet by Yingxue, Yao Qingyan took a chair in peace.

Madam Bao pouted. "Honestly, Mother, if you wanted company you only had to say so. I'll have Haodong and his father visit every day, and I'll come too."

"Don't stand there—sit," Old Madam Xia said. "So long as you keep me in your hearts, I'm happy."

Madam Bao sat down. Surrounded by two daughters-in-law chatting away, Old Madam Xia felt a rare surge of joy and beamed nonstop.

Having won Old Madam Xia over for good, Qianli Xun felt light on her feet. On paper, everything in Chen Manor was falling into place: she held her own courtyard and assets, had neutralized the manor's biggest boss, enjoyed warm in-laws, and had a friendly sister-in-law in Ye Anya. Life was running smooth. So why did an ache still sit hollow in her chest?

In a subdued mood, she jotted an address on a slip of paper and handed it to Hongdou. "This is Xiaguo's hometown. Go find the old blind ballad singer and ask him to come to Chen Manor whenever he's free—tell him Old Madam Xia would love to hear him sing."

Hongdou took the note and set off, leaving the room hushed. Kuqiao was off somewhere, and Qianli Xun did not care to wonder where. Sitting alone with her own thoughts, the source of her gloom surfaced: she had nobody beside her whom she loved.

Her thoughts drifted to Shen Jianchen, who must be nearing Bianliang by now. Remembering everything between them, she sighed at how crooked her path of love had turned, cursing this era where no one got to choose their own heart.

Kuqiao walked in and spotted her mistress propping her chin on her hand at the desk, staring off in low spirits. "What's on your mind, Miss?"

Skipping the question, Qianli Xun asked, "Where were you?"

"With the storyteller here, I didn't dare show my face, so I went to the West Courtyard to watch Qinggua practice martial arts."

Qianli Xun lowered her hand. "Hardly martial arts—just one self-defense trick I taught him out of pity so Chen Haoyu would quit kicking him. How's he doing?"

"With Asheng sparring against him, he's looking pretty sharp—much cleaner and faster than when you first showed him."

Qianli Xun had no interest in Qinggua's drills. "I want to go see the Divine Willow."

Recognizing her mistress's dark mood, Kuqiao asked no questions, accompanying her out of Moxiang Courtyard and into a carriage bound for the hills. Kuqiao had never quite grasped why her mistress and Prince Yi stayed so attached to that tree. Bai Feng had told her that since Youlin Retreat sat close by, Prince Yi went there all the time, sometimes even talking to himself as if the willow could hear him.

Lost in thought, Kuqiao glanced out the carriage window and spotted Huayu walking alongside Nan Shuang, both headed toward Chen Manor.

"Miss, I just saw Huayu and Nan Shuang! Must be on their way to Chen Manor to ask for your poems."

Qianli Xun told the driver to stop. "Call them over."

Kuqiao leaned out the window. "Nan Shuang! Huayu! Come over here!"

Hearing her call, both servants hurried up to the carriage.

"How did you two bump into each other?" Kuqiao asked.

"My master ordered me to visit Chen Manor every few days from now on to ask for Youqi Jushi's new verses," Nan Shuang explained. "Ran right into Huayu on the road, and it turns out he's on the same errand."

"Come to think of it, you haven't come asking once since Young Master Qiao got back from Bianliang," Kuqiao noted. "Miss is heading out right now, so come back tomorrow. Don't worry—she already wrote plenty, all set for you."

Huayu jumped in at once. "Those are for my master, right? Don't cut our share just because Nan Shuang showed up!"

"My mistress writes fast and well," Kuqiao laughed. "Seven or eight new sheets are already sitting on her desk. You won't lose a single one, and there'll still be extra for Nan Shuang."

Thrilled, both servants watched the Chen carriage roll on.

Having listened in silence the whole time, Qianli Xun now asked, "Nan Shuang used to come asking for my poems all the time?"

"Hardly asking—he used to scavenge the drafts you threw away and try his luck."

"Then why did he stop after Wenyuan returned from Bianliang?"

Kuqiao studied her mistress with care. "Think about it, Miss, and you'll know."

The hint made it clear in an instant. After returning from Bianliang, Wenyuan and she had fallen hard for each other; with love in hand, why cling to scrap poems? Then their romance hit rocks, leaving him in no mood for verses, and after their breakup beneath the Divine Willow, even less so. Now he had sent Nan Shuang back for poetry—proof that Wenyuan had healed at last. That final talk at Zuiyun Tower had given their story a clean, proper closing period.

A smile touched Qianli Xun's lips. Funny how love worked: stepping onto the road together felt ecstatic, yet walking off it with peace could feel light and freeing too.

Then her smile faded. She had wandered onto a secret road with Shen Jianchen and never once felt happy—or rather, never dared feel happy, because even a flicker of joy felt like betrayal. When would this chapter close with a clean period everyone could smile at? She kept trying to draw that period inside her heart, yet stalled halfway every time. Why torture herself? Was she not even allowed to keep the man she loved locked away inside her own heart? Torn between guilt and longing, she left Shen Jianchen right where he was.

Beyond the city gates, the carriage halted at the foot of the hills. Qianli Xun stepped down and walked toward the Divine Willow. A warm spring breeze brushed her cheeks with drifting willow catkins, while more lay scattered underfoot like white down feathers.

Drawing near the great tree, she took in the fresh contrast: if the crimson silk ribbons dressed the Divine Willow in bridal red, the swirling white catkins draped a wedding veil over the bride. The mashup of ancient and modern imagery amused her; a tree a thousand years old straddled both eras anyway and would hardly mind.

Across the pond, young lotus pads floated on the surface, dusted in a thin white coat of catkins, just like the open water between them. Such a scene begged for Zhu Shuzhen's voice, and Qianli Xun recited aloud:

"Whirling through the bright sky like drifting snow, / Floating aloft, refusing to touch the mud and dust. / Soft and clinging by the flowers, snagging a bee's wing, / Wild and reckless on the lane, chasing a horse's hooves. / Pressed to water, turning to duckweed drifting far on waves, / Riding the breeze, shadowless, slipping over low walls. / Gathering in clusters, grieving spring's departure, / Trying to confuse the Lord of Spring on his homeward road." —"Willow Catkins" [TN: Translator's rendering]

Kuqiao clapped the instant she finished. "What a poem, Miss! Especially 'Soft and clinging by the flowers, snagging a bee's wing'—so true to life! I literally just watched a honeybee dive into a wildflower, get tangled in catkins on the petals, and buzz like crazy before shaking free!"

Qianli Xun smiled. Kuqiao's praise only proved how keenly Zhu Shuzhen observed real life. A sudden insight struck her: whether composing Song poetry or writing modern web fiction, staying rooted in real life made all the difference. Back in her old world, the cyberbullying had snowballed because her books drifted further and further from reality. Adapting huaben in this era, she would weave the living texture of Song daily life into every line—even tales of gods and fox spirits should make listeners hear their own lives echoing back.

Still, she had not come here for writing advice; she had come for her heart. Spotting the boulder beneath the willow, she walked over and blew a breath across its top, scattering the coat of catkins clean away. Worried the stone would chill her mistress, Kuqiao spread a handkerchief over it and stood quiet beside her.

Qianli Xun sat with her back against the trunk. Though she had never seen him here, she could picture Shen Jianchen sitting on this exact stone, leaning against this exact bark, wrestling with his own feelings. Remembering Bai Feng trying to train a piglet to climb the tree beneath these very branches brought a lump to her throat—half moved by his refusal to accept the impossible, half amused.

After a pause, she opened her eyes. "Kuqiao, did you know Bai Feng was out here trying to train a piglet to climb the Divine Willow?"

Kuqiao had never seen it firsthand, yet knew the tale. "Bai Feng told me Prince Yi had him buy a piglet and teach it to climb trees. Heaven knows what Prince Yi was thinking—pigs climbing trees! Sounds hilarious."

"It is hilarious. He just wanted to turn the impossible into the possible. Doesn't get that impossible means impossible, yet throws himself into hopeless effort anyway." Qianli Xun closed her eyes again, heavy-hearted.

Knowing everything between her mistress and Prince Yi, Kuqiao caught every layer beneath that sigh.

Qianli Xun sat quiet, daring not pray to the Divine Willow for any romantic miracle—any prayer of hers meant hurting Mengrong. Better to lock her feelings deep down and live out her days as a dutiful Chen daughter-in-law.

At length, stiff from sitting, she stood with Kuqiao to head back.

Barely a few steps down the path, a man and woman walked around from the back hill. She recognized the man first—Cao Ban. About to step out and say hello, she froze at the sight of the woman beside him: Eldest Sister-in-law Ye Anya, clutching a red silk ribbon with a face radiant with joy.

Shocked, Qianli Xun pulled Kuqiao behind a tree trunk and peered out. Cao Ban held Ye Anya's hand as they walked up to the Divine Willow, knotted their red ribbon together onto a branch, and embraced—looking every inch a devoted pair.

"Miss," Kuqiao breathed, "if Eldest Young Master Chen finds out, Eldest Young Madam is doomed!"

"There's bound to be a story behind this," Qianli Xun warned in a whisper. "Remember: take what we saw today to your grave. Never breathe a word to a soul."

"Yes, Miss. My lips are sealed."

Before the couple noticed them, Qianli Xun and Kuqiao slipped back down the path.

Her own romantic woes faded behind a wave of sorrow for Ye Anya. She was far from the only woman in this era barred from being with the man she loved; countless others like Zhu Shuzhen and Ye Anya lived trapped in the same cage. Never had Qianli Xun missed her modern world more—taken for granted when free, priceless once lost. Returning was impossible, so she would treasure whatever was truly hers to hold here.

First came family: loving parents, a brother and sister-in-law, a hundred-and-twenty-year-old Old Ancestor (another year older now), plus a Chen grandmother and kind in-laws. Second came friendship: the three scholars of the poetry salon, Wenyuan as a confidant, Xiangrong, and her bosom friend Mengrong.

Thinking of Mengrong brought back the slap on her wedding day and those words of hatred. Even so, Qianli Xun held no grudge. No matter how Mengrong treated her from here on out, she would remain her one true confidante in this era.
