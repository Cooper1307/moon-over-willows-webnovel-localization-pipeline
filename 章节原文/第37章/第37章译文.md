# Chapter 37: The Banquet Ends, Returning to the Prince's Residence

The palace banquet at last drew to a close. By the time Shen Jianchen and Qiao Wenxian rode back to Prince Yi's residence in their carriage, dusk was settling in.

Shen Jianchen had no interest in reliving the day's palace experience. His thoughts kept returning to one thing—his new post as Military Commissioner of Lin'an.

Lin'an—he was going there anyway, and now he had legitimate reason. Wasn't this fate working in his favor? It proved he was destined for Lin'an. *Could Qianli Xun really be there?* Those thoughts only deepened his longing.

As soon as he and Wenxian stepped into the residence, Qiao Mengrong was the first to greet them. "You're finally back! I've been worried about you all day."

Qiao Wenxian smiled. "Your Highness needn't worry. Not only is Prince Yi perfectly fine, but the Emperor himself has appointed him Military Commissioner of Lin'an."

Joy lit Qiao Mengrong's face. "Now that the Emperor recognizes you, no one will look down on Prince Yi's residence anymore."

Qiao Mengrong was glowing—eyes bright, cheeks flushed, a joy she could not quite contain. And for a reason Shen Jianchen couldn't name, it made him feel like a fraud. "Wenxian and I are thirsty. Let's not stand here—come inside for some tea."

"It's my fault for worrying so much," Qiao Mengrong admitted. "I blocked you in the courtyard. Please, come inside. I'll have the maid brew some loose-leaf tea. Xilan, go prepare it."

"Yes, Your Highness." Xilan left with a smile.

The three went inside. They hadn't been seated long when Xilan entered with a teapot and two tea bowls. She poured tea for Prince Yi and Young Master Qiao, then stood beside the princess, listening to their conversation.

"Brother, the tea is just brewed and still hot," Qiao Mengrong cautioned. "Wait a bit before drinking."

Shen Jianchen nodded.

Watching the loving gaze Qiao Mengrong directed at her brother, Qiao Wenxian teased, "I think I should leave. If I stay any longer, I'll feel like a third wheel."

Qiao Mengrong caught herself—she'd been staring. Her cheeks warmed. She pulled her gaze away from Prince Yi. "Young Master Qiao always likes to joke. How could you be a third wheel? You must have helped my brother at the palace. Please accept my thanks on his behalf." She stood and bowed.

Qiao Wenxian sprang up and returned the bow. "Your Highness flatters me. How could Prince Yi need my help? He excels in both literary and martial arts, winning the Emperor's praise entirely on his own merit. I had nothing to do with it."

"Can you two stop being so formal?" Shen Jianchen cut in.

"You're right, Brother. I'll listen to you."

Qiao Mengrong sat back down, no longer standing on ceremony. She pondered what Qiao Wenxian had just said. He claimed her brother excelled in both literary and martial arts. She had already witnessed his martial prowess, but as for literary talent—her brother had once said that writing poetry and composing ci required too much mental effort. With his weak constitution, he couldn't afford to expend energy on such things, so he had never dabbled in them.

She couldn't help asking, "Young Master Qiao, could you tell me about my brother's performance today? I'd love to hear about it."

"There's nothing worth mentioning," Shen Jianchen interrupted. "Just a lot of flattery and sycophancy."

Qiao Wenxian, now seated again, objected. "Your Highness is wrong. You did say quite a bit of flattery, but you also displayed genuine talent. Take the ci you composed, for instance—it was truly remarkable. Let me recite it for Her Highness..."

The ci wasn't composed by Shen Jianchen at all. He had to stop this. "I didn't write that ci. Someone else did."

Qiao Wenxian challenged. "If not you, then who? It's the first time I've ever heard such words from your mouth."

Shen Jianchen could only say, "I learned it in another world after I died. It was written by a great man."

Qiao Wenxian didn't believe a word. "I knew you'd say that. I've never seen this 'other world' of yours. Since I first heard these words from you, I can only assume you wrote them."

Qiao Mengrong leaned forward, hands clasped. "Young Master Qiao—please. Recite it. I want to write it down and memorize it."

Shen Jianchen couldn't stop Wenxian, so he let him be. *Grandpa Mao's words, hitching a ride through Wenxian all the way to the Song Dynasty—not a bad way to spend an afternoon.*

Judging the tea had cooled enough, Qiao Wenxian took a few sips, set down his bowl, and stood. He gathered his emotions and recited:

> *Wind and rain escort Spring's return,*  
> *Flying snow welcomes Spring's arrival.*  
> *Though a hundred-foot cliff is clad in ice,*  
> *There remains a sprig of blossoms, elegant and nice.*  
> *Elegant, yet she vies not for Spring's grace,*  
> *But only heralds Spring's embrace.*  
> *When mountain flowers bloom in full array,*  
> *She will smile amongst them, hidden away.*

Qiao Mengrong savored the words and found herself drifting into memory...

One winter, the cold was biting, and her brother's condition had worsened. The thick quilts and single brazier weren't helping at all. Qiao Mengrong ordered the guards to bring another brazier into the room, and she carried over an extra quilt herself to cover him. Only then did he feel better and slip into sleep at last.

Outside, heavy, feathery snowflakes were falling. Qiao Mengrong draped herself in a red cloak and led Xilan out of the room. Stepping onto the fresh layer of winter snow, they made their way to the garden, where a plum tree stood in full bloom, defying the snow.

Her brother never came out in winter, so the winter plums in bloom were a rare sight for him. Qiao Mengrong told Xilan to pick a few branches.

Back inside, she filled a vase with water and arranged the branches herself. Then she sat by the bed, admiring her sleeping brother.

When her brother opened his eyes, he caught a faint, delicate fragrance. "Why does it smell like plum blossoms?"

"I had Xilan pick a few branches and arrange them in the vase."

"Help me up so I can see."

Qiao Mengrong helped her brother sit up, then had Xilan bring a stool and place it beside the bed. She set the vase on it so her brother could admire the flowers to his heart's content.

Miraculously, when the plum blossoms "saw" her brother, one of them burst into bloom. Qiao Mengrong took it as an auspicious sign, a harbinger that her brother was sure to recover.

But her brother said, "Mengrong, in my eyes, you are that blooming plum blossom—the plum blossom in my heart that gives me the courage to live. If it weren't for you, I would have given up long ago..."

Thinking of this, tears welled up in Qiao Mengrong's eyes. Through her tear-blurred vision, she gazed at her brother, now free from illness. Had he written this ci because he was remembering the past?

Shen Jianchen noticed Qiao Mengrong's reddened eyes and asked, "Mengrong, what's wrong?"

Qiao Wenxian also glanced at Her Highness and realized he couldn't stay any longer. "I have other matters to attend to. Please excuse me." With that, he hurried off.

Only then did Qiao Mengrong speak. "Brother, I do love the ci you wrote. No matter what you were thinking of when you wrote it, to me, it's a poem you wrote for me."

Shen Jianchen fell silent. He could neither explain too much nor bear to hurt her, so he lifted his tea bowl and drank, using the gesture to mask his expression.

He took the chance to steady himself. Setting the bowl down, he smiled. "Mengrong, I'm a bit tired from the banquet today. I want to go back and rest early. Aren't you sleepy?"

Qiao Mengrong's smile dimmed. In the past, her brother would have agreed without hesitation. But now, he only wanted to avoid her feelings.

"Let me walk you back to your room."

Shen Jianchen stood. "No need. I can manage on my own."

Qiao Mengrong rose as well, watching his retreating figure. She felt even more dejected. When he was ill, she had hoped he would recover. But now that he was well, he no longer depended on her.

"Brother!"

Shen Jianchen stopped and turned. "Something else?"

"When are you leaving for Lin'an to take up your post?"

"Tomorrow I need to go to the palace to collect the appointment documents and official seal. It'll take a day. I plan to set off the day after."

"Would you be willing to take me with you?"

"If you want to go to Lin'an, I have no reason to refuse. Just come along."

Qiao Mengrong beamed. "Thank you, Brother!"

"We're family. No need to be polite with me." With that, Shen Jianchen went back to his room.

Hearing the word "family" from her brother's mouth filled Qiao Mengrong with warmth. It meant she was still important in his heart.

Shen Jianchen and Qiao Wenxian had already made plans: after finishing business at the palace, they would pack their bags and head south.

At daybreak, he went to the palace. Liang Shicheng handed him the documents bearing the Emperor's seal and the official seal of the Military Commissioner of Lin'an.

"Lin'an is a fine place. If Your Highness needs to know anything once there, just seek out Prefect Zhu Xiyan. Last year, although the court sent forces to defeat Fang La and offered amnesty to the Huainan bandits, the roads south are still plagued by roaming bandits. Your Highness must be cautious on the journey."

"I wonder what happened to the Huainan bandits after the amnesty?"

"After the amnesty, the court resettled them." Liang Shicheng's tone cooled. "But these thieves never changed their ways. They gathered again and caused trouble—their surrender was never sincere. A week ago, General Zhe Kecun put them down. Song Jiang was killed. The other ringleaders, executed one by one."

"Dead!" Shen Jianchen had hoped for a chance to meet the man he'd read about in books. *So this was how it ended—no chance to meet him after all.* Fate had shut the door for good.

Liang Shicheng couldn't understand Prince Yi's disappointment. "Of course he's dead. There's no good ending for those who oppose the court."

Leaving the palace, Shen Jianchen sat in the carriage, immersed in the disappointment of never meeting Song Jiang.

The carriage passed through the bustling market, and the noisy clamor of Bianliang's streets roused him from his melancholy. He looked out the window. The streets were lively and prosperous, with people bustling about—noblewomen, traveling monks, storytellers, sedan chairs passing by, and camels plodding along, hauling people or goods across the wide streets...

As he watched, a shop came into view with the sign "Zhao Taicheng's House"—it was a medical clinic. Bai Feng came to mind. The rascal still hadn't come to find him at the residence. Might as well stop by and ask around.

Just as the thought crossed his mind, a wail rang out from the clinic. "Waaah! You killed my mother with your treatment! Give her back! Give me back my five taels of silver! Waaah!"

Shen Jianchen told the driver to stop. He stepped down and entered Zhao Taicheng's clinic.

Bai Feng was on the floor, wailing and thrashing, making a real scene. Around him stood the clinic's attendants and a few patients who'd come for treatment.

Shen Jianchen stepped forward and asked, "Bai Feng, why are you causing trouble here instead of coming to find me?"

Bai Feng, seeing a savior, scrambled up at once and clutched Shen Jianchen's sleeve. "Waaah! Brother, you're the head guard at Prince Yi's residence. Help me! The doctor here killed my mother with his treatment and won't refund my money! Waaah!"

Hearing that he was from Prince Yi's residence, the doctor came out from inside and bowed. "May I ask what your position is at Prince Yi's residence?"

Shen Jianchen offered, "What if I told you I am Prince Yi himself? Would you believe it?"

The doctor bowed low at once. "Your Highness Prince Yi, when this boy brought his mother here, she was already beyond help. There was no cure..."

Bai Feng argued through his tears. "If you couldn't cure her, why did you take five taels of silver from me?"

The doctor pressed on. "Seeing your filial piety, I thought I might be able to save her, so I treated your mother. The herbs for her illness aren't cheap—five taels was already a bargain. I didn't even charge you the consultation fee.

I took her pulse and prescribed the medicine. But by the time you got home, she passed before taking even a single dose. How can you blame me?"

"I'm not blaming you for my mother's death, but since she never took the medicine you prescribed, why won't you refund my silver?" Bai Feng was relentless.

Shen Jianchen understood at last—Bai Feng just wanted his five taels back. "Doctor, can the herbs this boy bought be returned?"

The doctor looked troubled. "The herbs were prepared according to the prescription, with different ingredients mixed in specific amounts. They can't be returned. But for His Highness's sake, whether the herbs are returned or not doesn't matter. I'll refund this boy two taels of silver so he can give his mother a proper burial. How does that sound?"

Bai Feng was still dissatisfied and kept arguing, but Shen Jianchen stopped him. "That's fine."

After the clinic refunded two taels to Bai Feng, Shen Jianchen pulled the boy up and led him out of Zhao Taicheng's clinic and into the carriage.

Bai Feng tucked away the two taels and asked, "Are you really Prince Yi?"

"Of course."

Bai Feng leaned in, looking him over and over. "So this is what a prince looks like. There's no difference from me, except you dress better."

"Where do you live? I'll take you home."

"With my mother gone, where's home? No point going back."

"Aren't you going back to bury your mother?"

"I buried her yesterday."

"Do you have any other family?"

"My father died when I was born. It was just my mother and me. Now she's gone too. I have no home. I planned to get five taels today and go find you at the residence, but I didn't expect to run into you again. Seems we have quite the connection, Your Highness."

"In that case, let's go to Prince Yi's residence now. From now on, you'll follow me."

"Yes, Your Highness." The tears hadn't even dried, but Bai Feng was already grinning. He had a new home again.
