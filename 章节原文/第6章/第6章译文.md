# Chapter 6: Awakening as Prince Yi

In the outskirts of Bianliang, the Eastern Capital, stood Prince Yi's Manor—home to Zhao Pu, thirteenth son of the reigning sovereign Zhao Ji. Zhao Pu's mother, Consort Qiao, had died right after giving birth to him. Raised by a wet nurse, sickly since infancy and unfit for state duties, he was the least regarded of all the imperial princes.

On this day, Zhao Pu's strength gave out at last. He closed his eyes and drew his final breath.

Qiao Mengrong, wet nurse Matron Zou, and Zhao Pu's personal maids wept in bitter grief.

The moment Shen Jianchen caught faint echoes of weeping, the Divine Willow's force vanished. Floating in midair, still shaken, he stared down at the people in period dress. I'm dead—why am I on a film set? Where's Qianli Xun?

A strange pull tugged at him from the body lying on the bed, weak enough that he held himself back from drifting into it.

Then a glowing golden shade peeled free from the corpse and floated up to him. "You share a bond with my flesh. I am leaving—you may dwell in it. Remember: love Mengrong well for me."

Infuriated at being saddled with a stranger's dying wish, Shen Jianchen snapped, "Who's Mengrong? Why should I love her for you? I already love someone—Qianli Xun!"

The shade couldn't hear a word he said.

A beam of golden light broke open higher overhead, accompanied by ethereal celestial music that filled the air with bliss. The shade drifted up toward the radiance.

Drawn by the breathtaking glow and music, Shen Jianchen tried to float upward too—only for a shaft of golden light to strike him down, dropping him dead-center into the corpse on the bed, plunging him as if down a bottomless abyss.

When the falling stopped, the weeping returned—no longer distant and hazy as it had been in midair, but crisp and loud right beside his ears, grating on his nerves. He shouted, "Stop crying!"

Silence fell at once. The weightless float was gone, replaced by a solid, grounded heaviness throughout his limbs. Did I really drop into that shade's body? Better open my eyes and see.

He forced his eyes open to a bright room—and three female heads hovering right over him. One wore a maid's garb; another, a woman around forty, had a kindly face; the third was pretty, poised, and refined, every inch a sheltered young lady.

Seeing Prince Yi wake up by some miracle, Qiao Mengrong threw herself onto him in joy. "Brother! I thought I'd never see you again! You came back to life! Sob...!"

Matron Zou cried in haste, "Fetch His Highness's medicinal pills right now! Give him one before anything else goes wrong!"

A maid dashed to the side table, grabbed a brocade box, popped it open, pinched a pill with a silk handkerchief, and handed it to Qiao Mengrong.

Drying her tears, Qiao Mengrong let go of Prince Yi, held the pill between slender fingers, and brought it to his lips. "Brother, swallow this pill—it's good for your health."

Before he had even figured out what was going on, someone was shoving medicine down his throat! Worse, he hated bitter pills—just smelling them turned his stomach, let alone swallowing one.

The foul medicinal reek hit his nose so hard he slapped the pill away and sat straight up. "I'm not sick! Why would I eat that junk?"

Rubbing the stinging back of her hand, Qiao Mengrong stared in shock—her brother had never once been rough with her. His voice rang like a bronze bell, and he sat upright without a trace of his old frailty. Forgetting her sting and surprise, she gasped, "Matron Zou! Did Brother walk to the Gates of Hell and come back cured of every illness?"

Knowing a bit of medicine, Matron Zou seized Prince Yi's wrist, checked his pulse, and burst out in excitement, "His Highness is cured! Cured! Really cured!"

The maids standing around cheered in delight.

Overjoyed, Qiao Mengrong threw her arms around him again. "Brother, Heaven must have been moved by my prayers—not only did you come back from the dead, your whole body is healthy!"

Held tight by a beauty, Shen Jianchen felt not a flicker of joy. He knew he had landed inside a stranger's body—this wasn't a movie set, it was another era. Right now, he needed answers.

He eased Qiao Mengrong off him and asked, "Who are you? Who am I? Where is this?"

Everyone froze at his questions—especially Qiao Mengrong, whose face fell at the realization he didn't know her.

Seasoned Matron Zou stepped in at once. "Don't worry, Princess. Coming back from the Gates of Hell, His Highness must have drunk Mengpo's Soup before being sent back. The downside is forgetting one's past life; the upside is that it cures all illness!"

Qiao Mengrong exhaled in relief. "Hearing you say that puts my heart at ease, Matron Zou. Brother's health matters most. As for his memories, there's no rush—we can help him remember bit by bit, and if not, his memories can start fresh from today..."

Tired of their chatter without answering him, Shen Jianchen cut in, "You still haven't answered my questions."

"Don't fret, Brother, I'll tell you everything," Qiao Mengrong said. "You are Prince Yi, Zhao Pu, and this is Prince Yi's Manor..."

Before she could finish, Shen Jianchen blurted out, "I'm a prince? The emperor's son? What's my father's name?"

"Yes, Brother, you are His Majesty's thirteenth son. People of our station dare not speak His Majesty's personal name aloud, though I can tell you it is the fourth year of the Xuanhe reign of the Great Song." Watching his expression as she spoke, she saw only blank bewilderment.

Dying and waking up as an imperial prince wasn't a bad deal at all. "You call me Brother—are you a princess?"

Matron Zou explained, "Your Highness, she is Princess Jiahe—a sister you found abandoned by the roadside when you were eight. Later His Majesty granted her the title Princess Jiahe. You two grew up together as childhood sweethearts, and His Majesty even issued an imperial betrothal..."

"What? An imperial betrothal?" Shen Jianchen took a closer look at this adopted sister—softer and gentler than Qianli Xun. Remembering the shade's parting plea, he asked, "Your name is Mengrong?"

Qiao Mengrong lit up. "Brother remembers my name!"

Her excitement did nothing for him. Pointing at Matron Zou, he asked, "And who is she?"

"That's Matron Zou, the wet nurse who raised you," Qiao Mengrong replied. "She and I are your closest family in this world."

"Wet nurse? What about my birth mother?" Surely a birth mother came first.

Matron Zou let out a sigh. "Ah, I served Consort Qiao as a maid in her maiden home and married after she entered the palace. Who knew she would pass away in her twenties right after giving birth to you? I had borne a child two months earlier—he only nursed from me for two months before a grave illness took him, and his father followed him out of grief. The Qiao family sent me into the palace to care for you in Consort Qiao's stead. Raising you helped heal the pain of losing my own son."

Moved by the sad tale, Shen Jianchen held back further questions lest he dig up more grief. He had the gist of who these people were; the rest could wait. Since he had borrowed the original owner's body to live again, he needed to figure out how to live this new life.

Done sitting in bed, he threw off the brocade quilt and swung his feet to the floor. Two maids rushed forward at once, kneeling to put on his shoes, while Mengrong and Matron Zou stepped aside in quiet deference.

Shen Jianchen relished the feeling. Even as a big-company CEO, he had never enjoyed royal treatment like this. Being a Song prince had its perks.

Mengrong had called it the fourth year of Xuanhe. Never big on history, Shen Jianchen hadn't a clue what Xuanhe meant.

Thoughts of Qianli Xun surged back. If she were here, she'd unpack "the fourth year of Xuanhe" in a heartbeat. They had died together in the quake and been swept off together by the Divine Willow. He had landed in the Song dynasty—had she landed here too?

Once his shoes were on, Shen Jianchen stepped outside, gazed up at the sky above Prince Yi's Manor, and asked in silence: Qianli Xun, where are you?

A servant hurried in from the front gate, spotted the prince standing right there, and went weak in the knees. "A ghost...!"

A maid snapped, "What ghost? That's Prince Yi!"

The servant dropped to his knees. "I just went to the palace to report His Highness's death! Now I walk back through the gate and His Highness is alive again—haven't I committed the Crime of Deceiving the Sovereign?"

Qiao Mengrong and Matron Zou stepped out behind him.

Qiao Mengrong instructed, "Go back to the palace and explain that His Highness walked to the Gates of Hell and returned—cured of every illness."

The servant kowtowed. "Congratulations on your new lease on life, Your Highness! I'll run back at once!" Jumping up, he bolted out the gate.

Qiao Mengrong stepped beside Prince Yi. "Brother, are you hungry? I'll tell the kitchen to prepare food."

Checking in with his new body, Shen Jianchen felt no hunger. "Not yet. I want to walk around the manor. I woke up remembering nothing, and the place feels foreign. Since I'll be living here, I ought to learn my way around."

"I'll walk with you, Brother."

Shen Jianchen didn't refuse, nor did he wait for her; he set off along the stone paths alone, taking in everything around him with curiosity.

Qiao Mengrong trailed behind with her maids, watching his back with an eager yet unfamiliar gaze.

Matron Zou turned to a maid beside her. "Tell the kitchen to get His Highness's meal ready so it's on the table once he's tired of walking. For so long His Highness stayed bedridden without ever setting foot in the grounds. What a blessing—Princess Jiahe has finally made it through the dark days."
