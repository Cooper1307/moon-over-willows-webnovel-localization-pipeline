## Chapter 320: Choosing to Face It Bravely

After stepping down from the carriage with her son and grandson, Old Madam Xia knocked on the main gate of Liu’an Villa and told the servant who answered to report one name alone: “Liang Shihui.”

Inside a garden pavilion, Liang Shicheng sat engrossed in a musical performance, tapping the beat against his thigh and humming low along with the singer. The servant approached, wary of disturbing him aloud, leaned in, and murmured the name into his ear.

Liang Shicheng’s eyes snapped open. He waved the singers away and asked, “Are you certain you heard right?”

“Your humble servant heard every word clear as day. An elderly woman is at the gate, and that is the exact name she gave.”

“An elderly woman?” Moisture welled in Liang Shicheng’s eyes. He rose and hurried out to meet her at the gate.

Chen Haoyu and Chen Jianglin stared in astonishment as Liang Shicheng came out in person, his face brimming with emotion, as though greeting long-lost kin.

Before him stood a silver-haired old woman leaning on a walking cane. Liang Shicheng searched her features for some trace of a familiar face, yet found none. Even so, he called out in a tender voice, “Older Sister, is that you?”

That name worked like a charm, Old Madam Xia thought. She hastened to reply, “I hate to disappoint you, Imperial Attendant Liang. I am not your older sister, Liang Shihui. I am from the Chen family. However, Sister Shihui and I were close confidantes, and she left a few things with me to place into her younger brother’s own hands.”

Only then did father and son understand what trump card the old madam had been holding.

Disappointment washed over Liang Shicheng, and he knew at once they had come on account of the Chen family and the poetess. Still, since she held his sister’s belongings, she had to know what had become of her. He would have to invite them inside.

“I see. Please come inside so we may talk.”

Meanwhile, Qianli Xun had told Kuqiao to wait in their carriage while she stayed out of sight and peered toward the villa’s front gate. She spotted Chen Haoyu and her father-in-law standing there beside Grandmother. Moments later, a man came out, exchanged a few words with them, and invited all three inside. Qianli Xun guessed the man who had come to greet them must be Liang Shicheng.

“What are they doing here?” she wondered. “Does the Chen family’s business have ties to Liang Shicheng?”

Determined to slip inside and find out, she glanced up at the courtyard wall of Liu’an Villa. Not too high—an easy hop with her light-foot technique. Gathering her breath, she launched herself up and landed inside without a hitch.

A row of trees provided good cover, keeping her out of view. Peering through the gaps between the trunks, she had a clear sight of her family trailing Liang Shicheng deeper into the estate. Taking a roundabout route, she shadowed them on silent feet, her eyes scanning every corner in constant alert.

The group rounded a rockery of stacked boulders. She crept up to the stones, about to follow, when a squad of patrolling guards marched past. She tucked herself out of sight with care.

The last guard in the file caught a flicker of movement, halted, drew his sword, and edged toward the back of the rockery. Fearing discovery, Qianli Xun held her breath and pressed her body flat against the rock. The instant the sword-bearing hand came into view, she snatched his wrist, yanked him behind the boulders, and knocked him out cold before he could react. Then she sprang onto the roof with her light-foot technique.

From her perch above, she watched the three of them enter a building below. With guards patrolling the yard, tailing them on the ground was too risky, so she chose to eavesdrop from the rooftops. Skimming across the roof tiles as light as a dragonfly touching water, she glided from eave to eave.

Her path carried her right over the building where Shen Jianchen was held captive. Catching the faint sound, Bai Feng alerted him, “Your Highness, someone is on the roof.”

Shen Jianchen had heard it already. Eyeballing the sturdy iron cage enclosing him, he muttered, “Here’s hoping whoever it is will do me the favor of killing Liang Shicheng.”

On second thought, history recorded that Liang Shicheng met his end at the hands of Emperor Qinzong, so whoever had just crossed the roof could never be the one to kill him. Shen Jianchen consoled himself. “He has three years of life left. I have the patience to wait for that day.”

Reaching the roof above the room where the others sat, Qianli Xun eased onto her stomach, shifted a tile aside, and pressed her ear to the gap. She could make out most of what was being said below.

Once everyone had taken their seats inside, Liang Shicheng pressed without delay, “Elder Madam, do you know where my sister is?”

Old Madam Xia let out a sigh. “Alas, she passed from this world over fifty years ago. Before she went, she asked me to place this into her younger brother’s own hands, telling me he had entered the palace to serve the nobles within.”

As she spoke, she held out a large purple silk pouch. A servant received it and carried it to Imperial Attendant Liang.

With trembling hands, he undid the pouch and drew out the letter. He unfolded it and read every line with full attention, his eyes swimming with tears by the end. When he pulled out the jade piece carved with his sister’s name, all doubt vanished—these were indeed his sister’s keepsakes.

“No wonder all my years of searching turned up nothing,” he said. “She has been gone all this time. Yet why did you not bring these things to me sooner?”

Old Madam Xia explained, “It is not that we never tried to find you. We went to the capital several times, only for the guards at the palace gates to tell us no such person existed. Later, once you won His Majesty’s favor and your name began to spread, I worried that seeking you out would look as though the Chen family were trying to curry favor with the mighty, so I kept putting it off. Had it not been for our family’s current trouble, I doubt I would have brought this pouch out in my lifetime.”

“Where is my sister buried?”

“In the Xia family cemetery.”

“How did you come to know my sister?”

“I was not yet married back then. Out on the street one day, I saw a young woman kneeling by the roadside offering to sell herself. My heart went out to her, so I bought her and brought her home. That young woman was your sister, Liang Shihui. Shihui and I hit it off from the start, swore sisterhood, and kept her right there in the Xia household. After I wed, my parents adopted her as their own daughter, hoping to find her a good husband in time. Who could have known she would fall prey to a strange, incurable illness and pass away not long after? Before she died, she gave me that pouch, told me she had a younger brother who had been forced into the palace, and begged me to find him and deliver it into his own hands. Today, at long last, I have fulfilled her dying wish.”

Grief tore at Liang Shicheng, fat tears plopping down one after another. “My sister was eight years older than me. We lost our parents as children and scraped by in misery. She raised me as both father and mother. I could not bear to watch her toil, so I had myself castrated and entered the palace of my own accord, hoping to give her a comfortable life. Yet by the time I had the means to give her that life, I could not find her anywhere...”

Imperial Attendant Liang began to sob. The appeal to his emotions had reached its peak. Seizing the moment, Old Madam Xia dropped to her knees, and Chen Haoyu and Chen Jianglin knelt right behind her.

“For the sake of the bond I once shared with Sister Shihui, I beg you, Imperial Attendant Liang, to show mercy to the Chen family and spare Shuzhen.”

Listening from above, Qianli Xun grasped at last that the trouble weighing down her father-in-law and Chen Haoyu was all about her. She had never crossed paths with Imperial Attendant Liang—why on earth was he targeting her? Her thoughts jumped straight to Mengrong. Did Mengrong still refuse to let her go, going so far as to enlist someone of such immense power? To be sure of her hunch, Qianli Xun kept listening with rapt attention.

Imperial Attendant Liang checked his sobs, lifted his gaze, and dabbed his tears with a handkerchief. “Please rise at once, Elder Madam. You showed great kindness to my sister, so of course I will not lay a finger on the Chen family. On your account, I will even help the Chens expand their business in Bianliang. From now on, with me standing behind you, no one will dare touch the Chen family.”

Overjoyed at his words, the three of them gave thanks in unison. “Thank you, Imperial Attendant Liang!”

Father and son helped Old Madam Xia back to her seat, all three convinced that Shuzhen was safe now.

Yet Liang Shicheng went on, “As for the poetess, I would still like to meet her once. Rest assured, it is just a meeting—nothing more.”

The smiles vanished from all three faces in an instant. If all he wanted was a simple meeting, Chen Jianglin thought, why go to such drastic lengths?

Unable to hold back, Chen Haoyu spoke up. “If Imperial Attendant Liang wishes to see my wife, I will accompany her to pay a formal call tomorrow.”

The smile paired with Liang Shicheng’s red-rimmed eyes looked unsettling and out of place. “There is no need for Young Master Chen to escort her in person. Since I will no longer make trouble for the Chen family, the two-day deadline is off. Today during the hour of wei, between one and three in the afternoon, Commander Lin will call at Chen Manor to invite the poetess to Liu’an Villa as my guest.”

Hearing that, Qianli Xun felt even more certain Mengrong was behind this. A trip to Liu’an Villa would bode far more peril than good. Part of her itched to drop straight down and beat Liang Shicheng to a pulp. Still, the Chen elders had treated her with loyalty and warmth; storming in now would only drag the Chen family into disaster. Besides, the villa teemed with guards, so the odds were not in her favor. Using her light-foot technique, she bounded across the eaves and walls and sped away.

Inside, Chen Jianglin opened his mouth to protest, but his mother stopped him. Old Madam Xia spoke up instead. “Since Imperial Attendant Liang insists on meeting my granddaughter-in-law, we shall await his envoy at the hour of wei.”

After leaving Liu’an Villa, the three boarded their carriage and rode back the way they had come.

Inside the carriage, Chen Haoyu asked in alarm, “Grandmother, do you really intend to let Imperial Attendant Liang’s people come to Chen Manor and haul my wife away?”

The old madam explained, “What sort of person do you take your grandmother for? Imperial Attendant Liang has his heart set on seeing Shuzhen. Nothing you or your father said would have swayed him—better to hurry home and devise a plan. We no longer face an impossible choice between the family and her; now our sole challenge is protecting Shuzhen, which is far easier to handle. We still have a full shichen—two hours—before the hour of wei. I have thought it through. Once we get back, we will send Shuzhen into hiding in the countryside. When Lin Jue arrives at Chen Manor and finds no one there, he will have no choice but to leave empty-handed.”

Chen Jianglin frowned in worry. “I fear that if he cannot find Shuzhen, Imperial Attendant Liang will take it out on the Chen family all over again.”

“I was his sister’s benefactress. He will never bring harm to the Chens. Thank goodness I did not hand that pouch over years ago, or how else could I have saved this family today?” Old Madam Xia felt a measure of relief over her past choice.

Knowing his mother well, Chen Jianglin hazarded a guess. “I suspect Mother had another reason for holding back the pouch all those years—or perhaps you were waiting for the right moment to bring it out.”

Old Madam Xia nodded in approval. “You are half right. There was indeed another reason. At first I could not track him down, so I set it aside. By the time word of him reached me, he had risen in His Majesty’s favor and become a treacherous official reviled by the common people. Wanting no ties whatsoever between the Chen family and a man like that, I hid the pouch away. I thought I would never deliver it into his hands, yet when he backed the Chens into a corner, I had no choice.”

Chen Haoyu voiced a fresh worry. “Now that the Chen family has a connection to Liang Shicheng, people will find out sooner or later. I am afraid our family will be condemned for it down the line.”

The old madam gave them a strict warning. “Both of you remember this well. Today we leaned on that tie only to save the Chen family and Shuzhen. Going forward, keep your heads down and guard the foundation the Chens already have. Do not dream of expanding into Bianliang, and never go begging at Imperial Attendant Liang’s door. Only then will our family stay safe.”

The two answered together, one saying, “Your son will remember,” and the other, “Your grandson will remember.”

By the time the three returned to the manor, Qianli Xun had been home for a while. Her mind was made up: she would face whatever was coming on her own. Even death did not scare her. She had died once already, so every extra day she had lived was a stroke of luck. Let her see what Liang Shicheng thought he could do to her.

To avoid arousing Shuzhen’s suspicions, Old Madam Xia and Chen Jianglin stayed away from Moxiang Courtyard, leaving the crucial task to Chen Haoyu.

Putting on an air of casual ease, Chen Haoyu stepped into the courtyard and called out at the top of his lungs, “Wife! I’m back! There’s something I have to tell you!”

Before he even reached the doorway, his wife stepped out in front of him. “What is it?”

Chen Haoyu did his best to sound breezy and carefree. “Business requires me to make a trip out to the countryside. You have nothing keeping you at home anyway—why not come along with me?”

“Are we going to stay at the country house?”

“Not that place. This one is farther out—two full days and nights on the road.”

So Chen Haoyu meant to spirit her away. That had to be the elders’ plan.

“Too far. I’m not going.”

Chen Haoyu dropped the act. “You’re going whether you want to or not. That’s an order.”

“What a joke. Since when can you give me orders?”

“On this matter, I call the shots, and you don’t get a say.” Casting all caution aside for his wife’s sake, Chen Haoyu shouted, “Kuqiao!”

Kuqiao emerged from the room. “Young Master, what is it?”

Panic crept into Chen Haoyu’s voice despite himself. “Pack some clothes for your mistress! Right now! This instant! I’m taking her to the countryside!”

Clueless as to what was going on, Kuqiao went back inside to pack all the same.

Next, Chen Haoyu bellowed, “Hongdou!”

Hearing him shout, Hongdou ran over from the west side of the yard. “What are your orders, Young Master?”

“Gather every last servant! You’re all escorting me and your mistress to the countryside!”

Even more bewildered, Hongdou glanced at her mistress standing off to the side in silence, then went off to obey.

Chen Haoyu muttered to himself, “Right—almost forgot Matron Wei.”

He was about to bolt toward the back courtyard when his wife wrapped her arms around him from behind.

Held like this by his wife for the first time, Chen Haoyu felt an inexplicable rush of happiness flood his chest. Yet the thought of Lin Jue arriving with his men any minute snapped him out of the spell.

“Wife, I need to go find Matron Wei. Let go for a moment.”

Qianli Xun kept her arms right where they were. “Matron Wei isn’t in the back courtyard. She took two servants and went out first thing this morning.”

“Then we’ll have to leave without her. Let’s go first.”

“What’s the big rush?”

“No choice—some urgent business came up.”

“I already know it has nothing to do with business. Imperial Attendant Liang wants to see me, and because you’re worried sick, you want to spirit me off to the countryside to hide.”

Chen Haoyu could not fathom how his wife knew the truth, yet he kept trying to cover it up. “Where did you hear such nonsense? Who do you think Imperial Attendant Liang is? Why would a man like him ask to see you? Besides, even if he did, what would be the harm in meeting him? Stop letting your imagination run wild—it really is just business.”

“I tailed you, Grandmother, and Father-in-law all the way to Liu’an Villa. I was up on the roof and heard every word you said inside.” Qianli Xun let go of him. “You said it yourself—what’s the harm in a meeting? Fine, then. I’ll go meet him.”

Realizing the charade was over, Chen Haoyu spun around, urgency pouring from his voice. “Since you already know the truth, there’s no point hiding it. Imperial Attendant Liang’s demand to see you is nowhere near that simple. To force your hand, he sent men to stir up trouble at our tea shop, and he has both Prince Yi and my father-in-law locked up. Going to him puts you in grave danger. Father told me your dad said Liang Shicheng is a lecher—with a special taste for married women. How could I ever let you walk into that trap?”

Qianli Xun let out a cold laugh. “Him? A treacherous toad like that thinks he can get his hands on me? Not a chance! One punch from me and I’ll crack his skull open like a melon!”

Seeing his wife’s fearless expression eased his heart a fraction, yet he still tried to reason with her. “Rather than clashing with him head-on, why not go into hiding where he can’t find you? Give it time, and maybe he’ll lose interest and forget.”

Qianli Xun thought Chen Haoyu was being far too naive. Liang Shicheng had locked up both Prince Yi and her father, Zhu Xiyan. Until he laid eyes on her, he would never back down. What good would hiding do?

“Hiding solves nothing. A man like Liang Shicheng has plenty of ways to hunt me down. I’ve made up my mind. I’ll walk right in and face him in the open—and see what he thinks he can do to me.”
