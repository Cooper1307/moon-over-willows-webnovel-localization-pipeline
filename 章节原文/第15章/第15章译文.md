# Chapter 15: The One Person Father Can't Ignore

Outside Zhu Manor's side gate waited Zhu Jingyuan beside a hitched carriage.

The second Qianli Xun stepped out, she threw her arms around him in glee. "Having a brother is the best!"

Still adjusting to his bold new sister, Zhu Jingyuan couldn't help grinning anyway. "Took leave from office today just to make the rounds of the clan elders with you. Let's talk inside the carriage."

Climbing aboard, Kuqiao took the seat opposite and watched her mistress cozy up to Eldest Young Master, thinking to herself: The old Miss never acted this close with Eldest Young Master—now they're thick as thieves!

The carriage rolled down quiet lanes into bustling Lin'an streets, lively chatter spilling past the curtains.

With his sister tucked against his arm, Zhu Jingyuan looked right content. "Mother told me what happened last night. Once Father says no, nobody under his roof dares step out for you. Don't blame Mother, or Wenxin and me."

"Why would I blame you? Father's the heartless one!" Leaning her head against his shoulder, Qianli Xun asked, "Heard if you cross him, he brings out family discipline—what does that even look like?"

"Twenty blows of the cane for the men, palm strikes for the women, kneeling in the ancestral hall for two days on water alone—and confined inside Zhu Manor for a full year."

Sitting bolt upright, Qianli Xun fumed, "Total Fascist!"

"Fascist? What's that?" Zhu Jingyuan blinked.

"Means Father is a ruthless tyrant!"

Recalling Madam Xiang's tale of how Shuzhen had smashed a dining table clean in half with one fist—something the old Shuzhen lacked both nerve and muscle to do—Zhu Jingyuan knew his sister had changed for good.

"Shuzhen, gaining all that strength after your rebirth... I know you aren't the same as before, yet in my heart you'll always be my little sister."

Warmed to the core, Qianli Xun leaned back on his shoulder. "And you'll always be my big brother."

"At least with you this strong, I won't worry about anyone pushing you around," Zhu Jingyuan went on. "You probably don't remember, yet His Majesty prizes scholars above all, lifting literary figures to the skies across the empire. You're a celebrated poet here in Lin'an—tell me you didn't lose your gift for poetry and ci?"

Unwilling to disappoint him—with Zhu Shuzhen's works locked in her head anyway—Qianli Xun sat straight and met his eyes. "Relax, Brother! Your sister's poetic genius is right where it belongs—didn't lose a scrap!"

"I can vouch for that, Young Master!" Kuqiao chimed in, remembering how her mistress had recited "Chrysanthemum" just yesterday—a poem the old Miss had written one tipsy evening after rare cups of wine. If she remembered her own verse, her talent lived on—a blessing amid disaster.

"Good, good! As long as your talent remains, the title Poetess of Jiangnan won't slip away."

The carriage halted outside the first clan uncle's gate. Zhu Jingyuan left his sister and Kuqiao inside while he went in alone.

Minutes later he stepped back into the carriage and shook his head—no luck.

Five houses in a row, exact same result.

"Why won't any of our uncles back me, Brother?" Qianli Xun asked.

Zhu Jingyuan sighed. "Because our father is Prefect of Lin'an—none of them dare cross him! First question out of every mouth was, 'Does the Prefect approve?' The instant they heard Father said no, every last one pulled his head into his shell like a turtle."

"Brother, you're too honest! Why didn't you lie and tell them Father sent you to invite them?"

"Can't lie about something this big! Mutual divorce decides your whole future. Drag them there under false pretenses, they spot Father missing and walk out—you'd lose face and ruin your reputation!"

"Good thinking, Brother. One uncle left—let me go in with you so he sees I'm sincere."

The last elder, an older cousin-uncle senior to Zhu Xiyan, received the pair politely—only to ask the Prefect's stance first, refuse outright, and launch into a lecture about preserving a woman's good name, warning that even mutual divorce scarred a lady's reputation and made remarriage hard.

Squeezing out tears on command, Qianli Xun wailed, "Uncle! If your own daughter married a husband who beat her and dragged courtesans home, would you leave her trapped in that fiery pit? Sob...!"

Moved by her logic and tear-streaked beauty, the elder softened. "Were you my own daughter, I'd step forward in a heartbeat. Yet your father is Prefect of Lin'an—who dares cross him? Still, I'll give you a hint: there is one person in all of Lin'an whom your father dares not say a single word against."

Perking up through half-fake tears, Qianli Xun asked, "Who?!"

Before the uncle could answer, light dawned on Zhu Jingyuan. Jumping up with cupped hands, he bowed. "Thank you for pointing the way, Uncle! I'll take Shuzhen to see the Old Ancestor right now!"

The elder chuckled. "Go on! If you two can move her, she alone outweighs every elder in the Zhu clan."

Still fuzzy on who this Old Ancestor was, Qianli Xun beamed at having a way out, bade the uncle farewell, and climbed back into the carriage behind her brother.

Inside, bursting with curiosity, she asked, "Brother, who on earth is this Old Ancestor that even the Prefect of Lin'an can't defy?"

Calling out "Home!" to the driver, Zhu Jingyuan explained, "She lives inside Zhu Manor's Buddhist Shrine—a hundred and twenty years old! She's our grandfather's grandfather's mother. Grandparents, great-grandparents, even great-great-grandparents have all passed on, yet she's still alive and well—amazing, right? With her age and seniority, she doesn't give Father a second glance."

The tongue-twister family tree made Qianli Xun's head spin; forget tracking generations, all that mattered was she outranked Zhu Xiyan!

"Brother, if you and Mother had thought of her sooner, we wouldn't have wasted all day running around Lin'an!"

"Can't blame Mother or me—she stays shut inside the Buddhist Shrine year after year, forbids visitors, even Father rarely lays eyes on her. Over time everybody forgets she's there."

"A hundred and twenty years old... can she even walk?"

"No idea—heard of her all my life, never met her once."

Fresh worry crept in. At one hundred and twenty, could she even leave the shrine, let alone trek out of Zhu Manor for a divorce? Still, dead horse or not, Qianli Xun had to try doctoring it—no other play left.
