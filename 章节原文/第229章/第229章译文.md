## Chapter 229: Putting the Ugly Truth Up Front

Ye Anya drained her tea bowl in one gulp, dabbed her mouth with a silk handkerchief, and spoke steady and firm. "Don't worry for me, Sister. If you truly want what's best for me, tell me how to end this marriage."

Reflecting on her own tangled path since arriving in the Song, Qianli Xun felt a deep pang. Compared with Ye Anya, she had been lucky—neither Lin Jue nor Chen Haoyu stood a chance against her fists, whereas her sister-in-law endured Chen Haodong's violence helplessly. She owed her the honest truth.

"In this era, mutual divorce for a woman is uphill through thorns. I moved heaven and earth and still only pulled it off because Prince Yi stepped in to pressure my father and Lin Jue into signing."

"Prince Yi stepped in?" Anxiety clouded Ye Anya's face. "So it really is that hard..."

Qianli Xun took a slow sip of tea. "Yes. Sometimes I wonder if I ever would have escaped Lin Manor if Prince Yi hadn't come to Lin'an."

Her mind drifted to Shen Jianchen—helping her divorce Lin Jue the instant they crossed paths, helping win over Qiao Manor, helping stamp out last year's rumors...

Seeing Shuzhen fall quiet, Ye Anya asked in a low voice, "Did Prince Yi already have feelings for you back then?"

After the wedding raid, the question was natural enough. Wanting to stay honest without sparking fresh gossip, Qianli Xun replied, "You must have heard about the wedding raid. Whatever Prince Yi felt back then I cannot say, yet everything between us has always been clean. Princess Jiahe was my closest friend—how could I ever betray her? What happened on my wedding day blindsided me completely."

Ye Anya sighed. "We're two miserable souls in the same boat."

Steering off Prince Yi, Qianli Xun returned to the point. "If you truly want a mutual divorce, Eldest Sister-in-law, you don't just have to win over Eldest Cousin-in-law—you need both families' elders to agree. If a single side refuses, mutual divorce hits a wall."

Twisting her silk handkerchief between pale fingers, Ye Anya fell silent in distress. Looks like she's backing down, Qianli Xun thought.

Instead, Ye Anya lifted her chin. "I'll find a way to persuade them one by one. And if all else fails, I'll push Chen Haodong to repudiate me."

Remembering Kuqiao's warning from her earliest days in the Song, Qianli Xun asked sharp and fast, "Do you have sisters back at your maiden home?"

"Two younger sisters."

"Then under no circumstances let him repudiate you!"

"I know being cast out ruins my sisters' marriage prospects," Ye Anya whispered, "yet I cannot bear one more day of this. Worst comes to worst, let the Ye family disown me as a daughter."

Moved by the steel beneath her fragile exterior, Qianli Xun clasped her hands. "You're rarer than most women in this era, Eldest Sister-in-law. Just remember: whatever path you choose, plan every step in advance—and keep yourself safe."

Ye Anya squeezed her fingers back. "Thank you, Shuzhen. Watching you gave me the nerve to try."

Qianli Xun had no words, nagged by quiet dread that her influence might push Ye Anya into a fire she could not survive.

With everything said, Ye Anya took her leave. Seeing her out beyond Moxiang Courtyard, Qianli Xun sent a silent wish after her: May Eldest Sister-in-law find happiness.

Why not wish her a successful divorce? she asked herself—and saw the answer at once. Succeed, and she won happiness with the man she loved; fail, and she still had to live on, needing every blessing she could get.

Setting Ye Anya's troubles aside for her own pressing business, Qianli Xun headed straight for Old Madam Xia's courtyard. Knowing her father-in-law and mother-in-law gathered there every morning after breakfast, one trip would let her face the whole clan at once instead of repeating herself room by room.

Approaching the gate, lively family chatter drifted from indoors—a full house. When a yard maid started forward to announce her, Qianli Xun raised a hand. "No need. I'll walk straight in."

She wanted to catch the elders' unfiltered reactions before they had time to put on polite masks.

Recognizing Second Uncle's voice rising inside, Qianli Xun stepped boldly across the threshold. Conversation snapped off mid-sentence.

Old Madam Xia, Yao Qingyan, and Chen Jianglin all greeted her with warm smiles—relieving her at once, though she had come braced for a showdown over the rumors either way. Both aunts wore sour expressions laced with gleeful anticipation of drama, exposing how paper-thin their polite harmony was. Both uncles masked their feelings better, yet one quick glance and lowered eyes proved they already knew Chen Haoyu was under lock and key.

"Shuzhen, come sit!" Old Madam Xia called warmly.

Instead of sitting, Qianli Xun dropped straight to her knees, with Kuqiao kneeling beside her.

"What are you kneeling for?" Old Madam Xia asked.

"Grandmother, yesterday I marched Chen Haoyu home from a brothel in ropes and locked him in the back courtyard. In my own conscience I did nothing wrong, yet I feared the Chen elders might disagree, so I kneel first to beg pardon."

Before Old Madam Xia could speak, Chen Jiangmu burst out, "If you know you crossed the line, hurry up and let Haoyu out instead of kneeling here for show—"

Old Madam Xia raised one hand, silencing her third son on the spot. "What pardon do you need? Get up at once."

Rising alongside a relieved Kuqiao, Qianli Xun tested the waters. "Grandmother, Father-in-law, Mother-in-law—you truly aren't angry with me?"

"When were we angry?" Chen Jianglin asked. "We put Haoyu's discipline in your hands; handle him however you see fit."

"We heard yesterday and didn't lift a finger to stop you," Yao Qingyan added. "That shows we back you completely. Discipline him whichever way works."

Old Madam Xia chuckled. "You have my word: while you're straightening Haoyu out, none of the three of us will get in your way."

Chen Jianglin backing her came as no surprise; Old Madam Xia and Yao Qingyan not even slipping in one plea for mercy baffled her.

Finding the elders' blind faith laughable, Chen Jiangshu traded a look with Madam Bao, who caught his cue at once. "Funny—yesterday Mother and Eldest Sister-in-law looked panicked when they heard Haoyu was tied up, yet today they don't mind a bit! Oh, wait—as I left yesterday, I spotted Matron Wei heading into Mother's courtyard. Must have put in a good word for Shuzhen! That matron of yours is truly something else."

Whatever poison Madam Bao meant to stir, she just solved Qianli Xun's mystery. Good old Matron Wei had already paved the whole road for her last night!

"Shuzhen isn't your daughter-in-law—keep your nose out of it!" Old Madam Xia snapped at Madam Bao.

Madam Bao pressed her lips together, while Madam Cui swallowed the comment she had been about to offer.

Old Madam Xia beamed at Qianli Xun again. "Sit down already! Why stand?"

Qianli Xun stayed on her feet. With the lockup approved, one thorn still sat raw in her chest—useless to vent or not, she needed it off her tongue. "Let me stay standing, Grandmother. What I have to say next might displease you."

Guessing at once, Old Madam Xia beat her to it. "About Haoyu spreading those rumors?"

"Yes."

"All water under the bridge. You're married in now—we're one family. No sense dredging up the past."

Those three words—"one family"—nearly dulled Qianli Xun's edge with unexpected warmth, until Chen Haoyu's smug grin flashed back in her mind. "Granddaughter-in-law wants to know one thing: knowing full well Chen Haoyu spread slander that ruined my happiness, why did the elders still agree to this match? Tying two bitter enemies together as man and wife—if that isn't hurting me, what is?"

The blunt blast landed like a cannonball—half the elders squirming, the other half secretly licking their lips.

Chen Jianglin let out a heavy sigh. "We understand your resentment, Shuzhen. Yet when Prefect Zhu marched Haoyu in trussed up and laid out what he'd done, I was frantic and furious. When Prefect Zhu proposed this match, it seemed the right way to make amends, so I didn't think deeper. Haoyu caused the mess; he had to take full responsibility."

"Plenty of ways to take responsibility—why shove me into a marriage trap and dress it up as noble?"

Yao Qingyan rose and clasped Shuzhen's hands. "How is Chen Manor a trap? Have you suffered one ounce of mistreatment since arriving?"

Honestly, no—even Old Madam Xia's "trouble" had been an act—yet the knot in her chest refused to loosen. "The Chen family is wonderful and the elders treat me well, except Chen Haoyu... we're oil and water. He is nowhere near the husband I ever wanted."

Yao Qingyan laughed. "Then whip him into the exact husband you want! Wouldn't that be perfection?"

Old Madam Xia and Chen Jianglin joined in chuckling.

Madam Bao sneered inside: Can't discipline their own son and expect a bride to do miracles—laughable!

Madam Cui thought: Never heard of a wife reforming a rake! If the Zhu girl whips Haoyu into shape, I'll write my surname Cui upside down!

Chen Jiangmu found it absurd too, yet kept quiet seeing his eldest brother smiling.

After holding back, Chen Jiangshu finally spoke up. "Mother, Eldest Brother—if you ask me, send Haoyu to the Chen shops under my wing to learn trade while I keep an eye on him. Sounds far better to outsiders than letting a new bride discipline her husband! Shuzhen's fists are impressive, sure, yet raising a man takes more than fists."

Madam Bao knew her husband had zero interest in actually mentoring Haoyu—just taking a clean dig at Eldest Brother's failed parenting and mocking his choice of enforcer.

"Shuzhen is doing splendidly," Old Madam Xia shut it down. "Case closed. End of discussion."

Not wanting the job anyway, Chen Jiangshu bowed his head. "Whatever Mother thinks best."

Old Madam Xia turned back to Shuzhen. "I know you married in reluctantly, and learning he started those rumors makes your blood boil. Still, nobody held a knife to your throat on your wedding day. Call it tangled fate—looks like an ill-starred bond right now, yet Grandmother trusts you to turn ill fate into good fortune."

Still holding her hands, Yao Qingyan added, "Mother's right—ill fate can turn sweet. Put the rumors behind you, Shuzhen; bright days lie ahead. If anger still burns, treat disciplining him as venting your spleen—"

Finding their optimism wildly naive—expecting revenge to blossom into romance—Qianli Xun seized Yao Qingyan's slip to set firm ground rules upfront. "Since Mother-in-law put it that way, I'll take you at your word: I'll treat disciplining Chen Haoyu purely as venting my grudge. If he doesn't turn into the paragon you elders hope for, don't come regretting today's choice."

Chen Jiangshu grinned inside: Vent all you want—makes my son's victory easier!

Madam Bao gloated silently: Smooth talk, Eldest Sister-in-law—dug your own pit and jumped right in!

Third Uncle and Aunt exchanged scandalized looks at such brazen arrogance, yet held their tongues.

Dropping Shuzhen's hands in alarm, Yao Qingyan fluttered, "I only joked about venting—don't take it literally!" Retreating to her chair, she shot an anxious glance at Chen Jianglin.

Qianli Xun knew her warning sounded blunt, yet ugly truths belonged upfront: she was out for payback, not miracle-working, and had zero intention of taking blame if Chen Haoyu stayed hopeless.
