## Chapter 255: Bosom Friends Turned Mortal Enemies

Bai Meiruo curled her lip and snatched up her brush. "Call yourself a refined lady? I'm writing down *vulgar and ill-mannered*! Humph!"

Ignoring her, Qianli Xun turned back to Miao Zhiyu. "Don't tell me Madam Miao has never even read the *Book of Songs*?"

Miao Zhiyu lifted her chin with pride. "Of course I've read it. I could recite it front to back by age thirteen."

"And what good is reciting it front to back if you can't grasp a shred of its beauty? Cramming lines into your skull without understanding them just turns your brain into a trash bin of dead words!" Watching fury darken Miao Zhiyu's cheeks felt downright delicious. "Surely Miss Miao has also heard of the *Nineteen Old Poems*. The very opening line of the first poem—*On and on, always on and on* [TN: Translated by Arthur Waley]—also uses repetition! Five characters in that one line, and the character *xing* (行, meaning 'to go on') appears four times! Are you going to lecture that poet for running dry of words too?"

Miao Zhiyu sniffed with disdain. "The *Nineteen Old Poems* are the crown jewel of five-character verse—unmatched in prestige. How could Youqi Jushi's lines possibly be mentioned in the same breath?"

"Who's comparing my lines to the ancestor of five-character poetry? I'm talking about the *technique* of repetition! If even a 'literary talent' like Madam Miao can't wrap her head around it, the rest of you must be even more clueless. Of course, Princess Consort Yi certainly understands, only her exalted rank makes it beneath her to lecture—so I'll gladly shoulder the heavy burden of giving you all a basic literacy lesson!"

At the words *basic literacy lesson*, disgusted scowls broke out across the room.

Qianli Xun faced them all squarely. She knew perfectly well these women understood the craft and were twisting it out of pure malice—all the more reason to set the record straight for Zhu Shuzhen's verse.

"In *On and on, always on and on*, the first *on and on* and the second mean the same thing on the surface, yet their emotional weight is worlds apart—the second cuts far deeper with grief. My lines work the exact same way: *I walk alone, sit alone, sing alone, drink alone, and sleep alone.* Walking alone outdoors, at least you still have the scenery for company—a quiet, graceful solitude. Sitting alone indoors with no one to talk to, your thoughts trapped inside—a bleak, hollow solitude. Trying to lift your spirits by singing, only for no one to listen—the aching solitude of lacking a kindred soul. Pouring wine and toasting yourself—the bitter solitude of sorrow that won't dissolve. And at last, lying down alone at night on a freezing bed—the sharp, tear-soaked solitude of pure heartbreak."

She swung back to Miao Zhiyu. "After hearing that, Madam Miao, do you still claim I've run dry of talent and words?"

Miao Zhiyu kept her mouth shut and sneered inwardly. *Of course I know your lyric is brilliant—blame yourself for crossing Princess Consort Yi! Talk as pretty as you like, I'm still writing down "run dry of talent and words."* Dipping her brush, she wrote those exact characters onto her sheet.

Now it was Bai Meiruo's turn. Qianli Xun pivoted straight toward her. "Bai Meiruo!"

Hearing her full name barked like that made Bai Meiruo bristle. "What do you want?"

"You are the dumbest woman I have ever met in my life."

The blunt bolt struck Bai Meiruo speechless for two seconds before she sputtered, "Slander! Pure slander!"

"Ever heard the proverb *don't air dirty laundry outside the family gate*? Yet you love blabbing every ugly mess inside your house to half the city—if that isn't sheer stupidity, what is? Now everyone knows your domestic circus, and you even dragged *me* into it! Let me spell it out for you: I couldn't stand Lin Jue! If I'd wanted him, you wouldn't even be in the picture! Instead of staying home figuring out how to win your husband's heart, you strut over here running wild and picking apart *my* poetry—who gave you the nerve?"

"What business is it of yours? Princess Consort Yi invited me—she hasn't scolded me, so where do you get off lecturing me?"

Watching the shouting match flare up, Qiao Mengrong smiled inwardly. *Inviting you here wasn't just to vent over your poem—it was to goad you into snapping so everyone has fresh ammunition to smear you! Go right ahead—put on a show for me. Your meltdown today will strip you of that "Poetess of Jiangnan" crown for good.*

"I couldn't care less about your mess—until you walk all over my name, spout filth, and trash my reputation! That I *will* answer for!" In one swift move, Qianli Xun grabbed Bai Meiruo's wrist and put just enough squeeze into her fingers.

Bai Meiruo shrieked, tears bursting down her cheeks. "Ow! Princess Consort Yi, save me!"

Panic rippled through the other three young women—if Youqi Jushi squeezed *their* wrists next, their bones would snap! One after another, they dropped their brushes and tucked their wrists under their sleeves.

"Youqi Jushi," Qiao Mengrong said coolly, "this is a poetry salon, and every lady here is fragile and unarmed. Why resort to brute force?"

Qianli Xun let go and turned toward Mengrong. She knew full well every stunt today had Mengrong's blessing, yet she argued back anyway. "The deadliest weapon in this world isn't a fist—it's character assassination. Bai Meiruo just smeared me with the word *seduction*. Pinching her wrist is the mildest slap on the wrist she could ask for."

"As I recall, I used the exact same word about you a moment ago," Qiao Mengrong said. "Are you planning to grab *my* wrist too?"

Qianli Xun held silent for a beat. "This commoner would not dare."

Rubbing her red wrist with tears still spilling, Bai Meiruo whined, "Princess Consort Yi, you must uphold justice for me!"

"Enough, bear with it. Carry on scolding them, Youqi Jushi—I'm quite enjoying the show."

Mengrong's frigid sarcasm hit like a bucket of ice water, quenching all of Qianli Xun's fire. What was the point of tearing into these lackeys? The real knot sat right in front of her—Mengrong.

"I need to speak with Princess Consort Yi alone."

"I have nothing to say to you."

"The Mengrong I knew was pure and kind. She would never herd a pack of people together to sling filth like this."

"You still have the nerve to bring up the past?" Qiao Mengrong rose and stepped right up to Zhu Shuzhen. "You destroyed the old Mengrong with your own two hands!"

"I treasure our friendship," Qianli Xun said, her voice softening. "I miss lying on the same bed talking half the night, sleeping in late together, sitting side by side by the brazier—and I miss how you rushed over to comfort me when I was hurt."

Every memory flashed across Qiao Mengrong's eyes, stinging them with tears. Unwilling to let the room see her crack, she waved a hand in dismissal. Taking the cue, the guests rose and filed out through the rear door with their maids.

Outside, Bai Meiruo rubbed her bruised wrist with a nervous frown. "Madam Miao, do you think Princess Consort Yi might patch things up with Youqi Jushi?"

"Rest easy," Miao Zhiyu scoffed. "Talk as slick as she wants, they'll never go back to how they were. Stealing the man a woman loves leaves blood-deep hatred. Ask yourself: if another woman stole Commander Lin's heart, would you ever stay friends with her?"

"Not in a million years!" Bai Meiruo snapped. "The whole reason I can't stand her is that my husband still pines for her and measures me against her!"

Behind her back, the other women hid their snickers—this Madam Bai really couldn't shut up about her own marriage; five minutes with her and you knew every scandal under her roof.

"Let's stroll around Youlin Retreat and enjoy the gardens for a bit," Miao Zhiyu suggested. "Once Youqi Jushi leaves, we'll check back in with Princess Consort Yi for further orders."

Everyone agreed and wandered off down the paths with their maids.

Inside the hall, only Qianli Xun, Qiao Mengrong, and their two personal maids remained.

Qiao Mengrong's lips trembled. "What's the point of bringing all that up? Hoping to touch my heart so I'll forgive you?"

Qianli Xun stepped closer. "You're crying."

A single tear slipped down Mengrong's cheek; she dabbed it away with a silk handkerchief. "Yes, I'm crying—crying for the sincere friendship I gave you, only to watch you stomp it into the mud!"

"I couldn't cherish it enough—how could I ever stomp on it? Fate played a cruel joke on all of us."

"Humph! *Fate played a joke*—what a convenient excuse!"

"Today I'll tell you everything about Prince Yi and me, holding nothing back."

"Not only do you know how to steal Brother's heart, you've got excuses ready too? Go on—let's hear how you talk your way out of this."

"I'm not making excuses. I only want you to know the truth so you won't misunderstand me so deeply."

"Cut the preamble and talk."

A distant look entered Qianli Xun's eyes. "Do you remember when I lost my memory?"

"What if I do?"

"I come from another era. I died in an earthquake, and some force pulled me into this world. When I woke up, I was Zhu Shuzhen, without a single shred of her memories."

Remembering Brother saying the exact same thing after coming back from the dead, Qiao Mengrong gave a cold laugh. "Even the lies you two spin match word for word!"

So Shen Jianchen had told Mengrong too. "It's the truth. I know it sounds impossible to believe, yet every word is real."

"Then explain how you write poetry and ci like that!"

"Zhu Shuzhen's poems survived into my era. I memorized heaps of them back then, so writing them down from memory isn't hard."

"Your skill at spinning tall tales rivals your skill at verse."

"Prince Yi came back from death—and the man who woke up in his body came from the exact same era as me. When the earthquake struck, we were sparring beneath the Divine Willow. The ground split, the willow started toppling, and he threw himself over to save me—so we died together. Only last winter did I discover Prince Yi was him, and that's when my feelings began to change. Perhaps stumbling across a familiar face in a strange world does that to a person."

Qiao Mengrong squeezed her eyes shut, forcing out two fresh tears. At last Zhu Shuzhen had admitted loving her brother! Thinking back to the chuiwan field, she had treated this woman with wide-open trust while she was already stealing Brother's heart behind her back. Opening her eyes, she drilled a glacial stare into Zhu Shuzhen.

"You trampled our friendship and stole Brother's heart—that is the sole fact that matters. Why bury it under fancy stories? Don't imagine a pack of excuses will buy my pity or understanding! Hear me well: from this day on, we are strangers! We will never be friends again!"

Qianli Xun's own eyes reddened. "No matter what you think of me, you'll always be my best friend. For your sake, even when I had a chance to escape my parents' arranged marriage, I walked away from it and willingly married into the Chen family—married the very man who slandered me—just to force myself to give up a love that didn't belong to me."

Not a flicker of pity touched Qiao Mengrong's face; she only sneered. "Listen to you painting yourself as the martyr! *I* am the victim here! The night before your wedding, Brother abandoned me to run off with you. On your wedding day, he stormed in to steal you away, turning me into the laughingstock of all Lin'an! Until that humiliation is paid back, my heart will never rest."

Only now did Qianli Xun grasp the horrifying truth: Mengrong's pain had curdled into pure hatred. A chill rippled through her chest. "Mengrong... what do I have to do for you to let go of this hatred?"

"Disappear—or crawl back to that imaginary era you invented!" Murderous frost glinted in Qiao Mengrong's eyes. "Until you do, I'll know no peace—and you won't have a single comfortable day either."

The threat didn't frighten Qianli Xun; what chilled her bone-deep was seeing such hatred in Mengrong's eyes.

"You and Prince Yi are already married," Qianli Xun pleaded softly, "and I'm settled down with Chen Haoyu. Can't we each just live our own lives in peace?"

"No! So long as you draw breath, Brother will never forget you—and so long as he remembers you, I can never breathe easy! If you know what's good for you, pick your own end: hang yourself, swallow poison, or jump into the Qiantang River. Choose whichever method you prefer. If you haven't got the guts to finish yourself, sit back and watch me drive you into a dead end, step by step!"

A shiver crawled down Qianli Xun's spine under that venom-soaked glare. Had someone put a curse on Mengrong? How had such a gentle girl turned this terrifying? "Mengrong, how did you become so ruthless?"

"Blame yourself—you murdered the girl I used to be." Qiao Mengrong returned to the main seat, settling back into regal coldness. "Well? How do you plan to die?"

Forgiveness was dead and buried; their friendship had reached the end of the road. Fine. A person paid for her own mistakes. "How I'll die? Never thought about it, and I'm not choosing. I'll leave that to Heaven. All I'll do is stay true to myself and walk each step straight."

"In that case, go home and wait. I'll gladly play Heaven and choose your death for you."

Mengrong's voice fell flat and arctic, stretching the gulf between them until no step could ever cross it.

"Farewell... Princess Consort Yi."

The front doors swung wide, letting daylight flood across the threshold. After soaking so long in Mengrong's freezing hatred, the warm sun outside felt neither harsh nor stifling—just a welcome relief.

She stepped across the sill, crossed the courtyard, and boarded her carriage out of Youlin Retreat. She had come brimming with hope and left empty-handed. *How on earth did it come to this?* the question echoed in her chest all the way home.

Beside her, Kuqiao fretted aloud. "Princess Consort Yi looked terrifying just now, Miss! I'm scared to death!"

Qianli Xun patted her hand. "What's there to be scared of? If she had the power to kill me, she wouldn't have let me walk out that door. Means she can't touch me yet. Fragile as she is, one punch from me would knock her flat—not that I'd ever lay a finger on her. Besides, a princess consort can't commandeer Lin'an's garrison; knocking me off isn't that easy. She's just venting and trying to rattle me—don't lose sleep over it."

"Hearing you say that makes me feel better," Kuqiao sighed, then muttered darkly, "If you ask me, it's all Prince Yi's fault! He's the one who kept coming after you until everything tangled into this mess."

"What's done is done. Pointing fingers won't fix anything. We'll take it one step at a time—meet every blow as it comes. So long as I stay away from him and live my own life right, things will settle down." Maybe it really was time she tried learning how to accept Chen Haoyu.

Back inside Youlin Retreat, Miao Zhiyu and the others finished their stroll and returned to pay their respects.

"Take everything you wrote down," Qiao Mengrong ordered, "polish it up, spice it up even sharper, and take it to a printing house to run off booklets. Hire street urchins to hand them out everywhere. Within a month, we'll rip that 'Poetess of Jiangnan' crown straight off her head."

"Yes, Your Highness!" they chorused.
