## Chapter 335: Princess Yi Dies in Yanjing

“That man in armor looks like a general. I admire his spine,” Shen Jianchen said, then worry crept into his voice. “So many Jin soldiers. He's throwing an egg against a rock.”

“Why don't we help him?”

“Of course we will. Let's watch a moment, then move when there's an opening.”

The two stayed crouched on the roof, watching how things unfolded.

Wanyan Zongwang's face twisted in contempt. He waved to the men behind him. “Take him down!”

The surrendered Song officials and the other generals stepped aside.

Jin soldiers closed in, surrounding the Song general who still had his backbone. He drew the sword at his waist and fought back.

The Jin soldiers' faces were ugly with malice. They swung sabers and spears, pressing in step by step. The Song general did not flinch. He let out a roar and charged like a cornered tiger. Sword flashes met their weapons in showers of sparks. He moved lightly, slashing left and right, and for a while not one Jin soldier could get near him.

But there were too many of them. Bit by bit, his strength began to fail. One Jin soldier stole behind him and drove a spear hard into his back. The general grunted, blood spilling from the corner of his mouth. Still he did not fall. He whirled and cut the attacker down with a backhand stroke.

He swallowed the pain and kept fighting. Every swing carried his fury and his refusal to yield, as if he could drive every invader back with the blade alone.

More Jin soldiers poured in. His chances grew thinner by the moment...

Qianli Xun sprang down from the roof with light-footed skill. Shen Jianchen saw him go and followed at once.

The two dropped from the sky beside the general, drove off the surrounding Jin with a few punches and kicks, then caught him one on each side and carried him clear of the siege.

Wanyan Zongwang was about to send men in pursuit when a voice rang out. “I'll go!”

Wanyan Chi rode up from another direction. He had seen someone carry the Song general away after the fight, but could not make out their faces. Lu Yuanfeng rode at his side, a band of Jin soldiers trailing behind.

“Since you've entered the city, Uncle will send you after the rebels,” Wanyan Zongwang told the mounted general behind him. “Follow the young prince! Catch all three and execute them on the spot!”

“Yes!” the men answered.

Qianli Xun and Shen Jianchen carried the Song general onto the roofs and dropped quickly into the street they had watched from before, reaching the abandoned house where Qiao Mengrong and the others were hiding.

Shen Jianchen told Bai Feng to take Mengrong and Xilan back to the inn by a side alley and wait for them there.

Bai Feng led the two out of the abandoned house. They had just turned into the alley when hoofbeats and running feet came after them.

Mengrong crouched down, clutching her head, her face full of terror. “I'm scared, I'm scared, I'm scared...”

“Don't be afraid, Princess. The alley is safe. We'll be at the inn in no time,” Xilan comforted her.

“Follow me,” Bai Feng said.

Xilan helped Princess Yi to her feet and hurried after him.

They made their way along the alley. It ran on and on, and there was no telling where it led. Bai Feng tried to ask, but there was no one in sight but the three of them, and every door along both sides was shut tight.

Bai Feng knocked at one house himself. No one answered. He led Princess Yi and Xilan farther down the alley.

They had gone some way when seven or eight Jin soldiers appeared ahead, blocking the path.

Xilan grabbed Princess Yi in fear.

“I'm here. Don't be afraid. Take the Princess and hide,” Bai Feng said over his shoulder.

Xilan helped Princess Yi back the way they had come while Bai Feng charged into the Jin.

The sounds of fighting made Qiao Mengrong more frightened still. She crouched with her head in her hands, mumbling, “Brother, save me. Brother, save me...”

Princess Yi stayed crouched and refused to move. Xilan bent over her. “Princess, we have to go. If we stay, we'll be killed.”

Qiao Mengrong shook her head hard. “No. No. I'll wait for my brother to save me.”

Xilan thought: behind them were Jin soldiers too, so running was no use. Better to wait until Bai Feng drove them off, then slip out of the alley. She crouched down with Princess Yi and watched the fight.

Bai Feng handled himself well. Seven or eight men were nothing he couldn't manage. Seeing that, Xilan's fear eased. She watched him with hope in her eyes, praying he would win quickly.

Bai Feng's fists and feet were fierce. The seven or eight Jin were no match for him, yet he could not finish them in a few moves. It still took time and effort.

After several exchanges, Bai Feng knocked a few down bare-handed, then tore the sabers and swords from their hands. With steel in his grip he struck at the rest, aiming at vital spots, until every one of them lay in the alley.

Bai Feng dropped the weapons. “Hold the Princess steady. Come with me, quickly,” he told Xilan.

“Princess, listen to me. Come quickly,” Xilan urged.

With the Jin dead, Qiao Mengrong was no longer afraid. She got to her feet obediently. The two followed Bai Feng through the alley.

They twisted and turned until the mouth of the alley came in sight. Before Bai Feng could feel relief, the bright opening darkened. More Jin soldiers, blades in hand, were closing in on the three of them.

Cold sweat broke out over Bai Feng. He could not hold off so many alone. “I'll stop them! Take the Princess and run!” he said urgently to Xilan.

There was no time left for Xilan to be afraid. She pulled Princess Yi up and ran.

After a few steps, Qiao Mengrong stopped and crouched again, curling into a ball. “I'll wait for my brother to save me. I'll wait for my brother...”

“The Princess's brother is just ahead,” Xilan coaxed. “Instead of waiting for him, why don't we go find him?”

Qiao Mengrong believed her and stood, running on with Xilan.

There were too many Jin. Bai Feng could not hold them all. Even as he fought, many broke past him and chased after Xilan and Qiao Mengrong.

Bai Feng tried to turn back, but the moment his attention wavered a saber cut into his back and a kick sent him sprawling. Jin soldiers poured into the alley, some even stepping over his body.

When they had passed, Bai Feng struggled up and steadied himself. He leapt ahead with light-footed skill and landed in front of the Jin. His wounds and his exhausted state nearly made him stumble. He swallowed the pain and planted his feet. “If you want to go forward... you go through me first.”

The Jin barely spared him a glance. Several raised their blades and cut at him. Bai Feng knocked one down with a punch, took his sword, and went after the others.

But he could not beat them all. Two more cuts landed. His strength gave out and he fell, forced to watch as the Jin chased Princess Yi and Xilan.

Qiao Mengrong and Xilan were women and unused to running. They were soon caught. One Jin thrust a saber through Xilan's back. She had no time even to cry out. She fell and got out four words. “Princess... run...”

Mengrong crouched beside her, stroking her face. “What's wrong? What's wrong?”

Xilan did not answer. Mengrong hugged her knees and curled up tight. “Brother, save me! Brother, save me!”

One of the Jin soldiers said, “Not a bad looking girl. Let's have her before we kill her.”

The others grinned. “Heh, heh, heh...”

“I want my brother. I want my brother...” Qiao Mengrong mumbled, getting to her feet to run.

A Jin soldier stepped in front of her. “Where do you think you're going?”

She darted to one side and ran straight into another soldier, who grabbed her. “You won't get away that easy!”

She pushed at him with all her might, but she was too weak. She kicked him in the groin. He let go with a yelp of pain, and she broke free.

The Jin laughed at her scrambling escape. “Ha, ha, ha...!”

She could not get away. Another soldier blocked her. “Where to, beauty?”

With nowhere left to run, she stared at him in terror. “My brother will kill you.”

“Your brother?” the soldier laughed. “Ha, ha, ha! I'm not afraid of him. Be a good girl and play with us. If we enjoy ourselves, maybe we'll spare your life.”

At the word “kill,” Qiao Mengrong was terrified. She crouched, clutched her head, and shook all over. “Don't come near. Don't come near. Don't come near...”

The soldier laughed, picked her up, and kicked open the door of a house amid the others' jeering. The place had clearly been abandoned. Only a few plain pieces of furniture and an oil lamp remained.

Qiao Mengrong beat at him and screamed. “Let me go! Let me go!”

He carried her inside and threw her onto a wooden couch, a filthy grin spreading over his face as he moved to pounce. Another man came in and held him back. “Why should you have all the fun? Let me taste the beauty too.”

“There's an order to this,” the first soldier said.

“I'll go first. You can wait,” said the second.

“Why should you?” the first demanded. “I carried her in here.”

“Because I outrank you,” said the second.

The first threw a punch. The two began to fight.

The first was losing. He pretended to give in. “All right, all right. You outrank me, so you go first.”

“Smart choice.” The second let go and walked toward Qiao Mengrong, leering.

She was too frightened to move, shrinking back on the wooden couch.

Before he could reach her, he fell flat at her feet. The first soldier had crept up behind him and run him through with a sword.

A man dead at her feet made her more afraid still. She stared at the bloody blade in his hand. The memory of Liang Shicheng's needle striking her flashed through her mind. Shame and regret broke her into loud sobs. “Wuwu...”

“Don't be scared. I won't kill you,” the soldier soothed her, laying the sword on a nearby table. “Once I've moved his body, we'll have our fun.”

She paid no attention to him dragging the corpse. She wept, her eyes fixed on the bloody blade. The day of Shuzhen's wedding, when her brother had stormed in to take her back and she had rushed toward the sharp steel—it all came back to her...

Qiao Mengrong stopped crying. She stepped down from the couch, walked to the blade, and picked it up. “Brother, I miss you...”

She drove the sword into her own belly, a smile on her face. “Brother, Mengrong has come for you...”

The soldier moved the body aside and turned to find the beauty on the floor, the sword buried in her belly and blood spreading out. He knelt, put fingers to her nose, and felt no breath. “What rotten luck! I hadn't even had my fun and she's dead!”

He pulled the blade from her body, wiped it clean on her clothes, and walked off muttering.

Bai Feng had taken many cuts, but none in a vital spot. He lay on the ground a while, and when he had recovered enough, he got up.

There were no Jin left nearby. He staggered back. He had no idea how long he walked before he found Xilan's body. His heart clenched. He hurried forward and fell to the ground. He crawled to her and shook her. “Xilan! Wake up! Wake up!”

Xilan did not move. Bai Feng was close to tears. “Princess Yi? Where is Princess Yi?”

He looked all around. There was no sign of her. He forced himself up again and staggered on, searching. Then he saw a house with its door wide open, and on the floor of the main room lay a woman in garments much like the Princess's.

His fear grew. He wanted to rush straight in, but his wounds slowed him even in his haste. Step by step he went.

When he saw the woman on the floor was Princess Yi, his legs gave way. He fell and wept. “Wuwu... If something has happened to the Princess, how can I face the Prince?”

While Bai Feng led Qiao Mengrong and Xilan into the alley, Wanyan Chi came riding at the head of Jin officers and soldiers. He scanned the street as he went. “As I see it, they can't have gone far. They must be hiding nearby. Seal this street tight, all of you. Don't let them grow wings and fly away!”

Jin officers led their men around the whole street until it was hemmed in, and they ringed the alleys off as well.

Wanyan Chi did not dismount. He looked up at the roofs on either side. “The ones who got away know light-footed skill. They may have taken the rooftops. Keep your eyes open!”

Wanyan Chi swung down from his horse. Lu Yuanfeng dismounted too and went with the young prince on his inspection.

Outside the abandoned house, Wanyan Chi stopped. “Come in with me.”

Qianli Xun, Shen Jianchen, and the rescued Song general had not had time to leave. The Jin arrived, and they hid inside.

The Song general swallowed the pain in his back. “Thank you, heroes, for saving me. You should go. Don't get dragged into this because of me.”

“If we dared step in to save you, we weren't afraid of being dragged in,” Shen Jianchen said.

“There aren't many men with the courage to talk back to the Jin as you did,” Qianli Xun said. “Since we happened on you, we'll see you through, however hard it gets.”

The door was pushed open with a creak. The three went on alert at once.

“Out the back door, then over the wall,” Shen Jianchen whispered.

Qianli Xun nodded. The three slipped out the back. Each of the two took an arm of the general and leapt over the rear wall. They landed in the alley behind the house—and found themselves surrounded by a dozen Jin soldiers.

The two fought the dozen. The Song general, badly wounded as he was, did not idle either. A mere ten-odd men were nothing they could not handle.

As Wanyan Chi entered the courtyard, he heard movement from the back. He turned at once with Lu Yuanfeng, left the house, and went into the street. He snatched a bow and arrows from his horse and raced around to the rear.

Lu Yuanfeng and the other officers hurried after him.

The three had beaten the dozen Jin to the ground when they heard quick footsteps closing in. From the sound, many men were coming.

Qianli Xun and Shen Jianchen exchanged a glance and nodded. They grabbed the Song general and sprang up. They were nearly at the roof when an arrow whistled—and with the sound gone, the general cried out and sank downward...
