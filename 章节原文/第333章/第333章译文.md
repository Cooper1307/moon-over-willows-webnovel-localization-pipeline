## Chapter 333: Seeking the Miracle Physician in Yanjing

When their party reached Yanjing, peace still hung over the Song realm on the surface. Shen Jianchen had Bai Feng secure rooms at an inn, and once everyone settled in, invited Qianli Xun out to ask after Liu Miaoshou—mostly wanting an excuse to stroll the streets alone with her.

Watching them leave the inn, Xilan felt a pang for her mistress, though she bore Youqi Jushi no ill will. All the way from Bianliang to Yanjing, Qianli Xun had kept proper distance, declining every open or subtle offer from Prince Yi to share his carriage so she could stay beside Mengrong, while Shen Jianchen never masked his feelings. Still, remembering the Imperial Decree, Xilan sighed and told herself that once cured, Mengrong would just have to learn to live alongside her.

Turning toward the guest room, Xilan bumped straight into Bai Feng and scolded him for ditching his duty as personal guard.

“His Highness likes having Youqi Jushi to himself and told me to stay behind,” Bai Feng answered candidly.

“If His Highness ends up with Youqi Jushi, what happens to the Princess Consort?”

Putting on a knowing air, Bai Feng replied, “My guess? They’re never getting together.”

“Why?” Xilan lowered her voice. “There’s an Imperial Decree, and Young Master Chen dissolved his marriage!”

“You know half the story and miss the rest. Buy me tea downstairs and I’ll tell you.”

“Fine—the Princess Consort is fast asleep from the road anyway.”

Downstairs over tea, Bai Feng filled her cup and launched in. “The second half is Young Master Chen. He loves Youqi Jushi just as deep as His Highness—and even with that annulment on paper, His Highness gave him a written pledge!”

“Saying what?”

“I wasn’t in the room, so I don’t know the exact words. Probably some promise to respect her choice. Didn’t you see Young Master Chen still rode to Bianliang every six months calling her ‘my wife’ like nothing changed?”

“True enough,” Xilan nodded. “No wonder His Highness banned the manor from breathing a word about the decree. What a tangled mess!”

“Messy indeed. Youqi Jushi just has rotten luck.”

“I think her luck is enviable! Prince Yi loves her heart and soul, Young Master Chen turned over a new leaf for her, and Young Master Qiao even starved himself for her. If I ever met one man willing to go half that far for me, I’d die content.”

“Try your next life,” Bai Feng teased, bursting her daydream.

Unfazed, Xilan asked pointedly, “How do you treat Kuqiao?”

“Like His Highness—one heart, one love,” Bai Feng boasted. “Soon as the Princess Consort is cured, I’m asking His Highness to let me marry Kuqiao.”

Touched with envy—recalling how Mengrong had once offered to pair her with Bai Feng before Kuqiao beat her to it—Xilan asked boldly, “Bai Feng, what do you think of me?”

Taking a sip of tea, he answered without thinking, “You’re all right.”

“If there were no Kuqiao, could you have liked me?”

Caught off guard by such bluntness from a maid, Bai Feng looked closely at Xilan for the first time. Pretty as Kuqiao, yet his heart didn’t stir an inch—strange how seeing someone daily bred nothing while distance only sharpened his longing for Kuqiao.

Seeing him stare without answering, Xilan blushed. “Why stare instead of answering?”

Snapping his eyes away, Bai Feng replied plainly, “If I were going to like you, I’d have liked you long ago instead of waiting for Kuqiao to come along.”

Rejected flat out, Xilan huffed, shot to her feet, and stormed upstairs, leaving Bai Feng grinning smugly into his teacup over how jealous Kuqiao would get if she knew.

Meanwhile out on Yanjing’s streets—far quieter than Bianliang or Lin’an, crisp with autumn chill—Qianli Xun asked several passersby about Liu Miaoshou without luck.

Shen Jianchen couldn’t hide his delight; the longer nobody knew, the longer he got to walk beside her. “Stop asking random strangers. Wait until we spot the right person.”

“Oh, and you can tell by looking who knows him?”

“Naturally. Stroll with me a bit, and watch me find him on the very first person I ask.”

“Don’t lie to me.”

“I’d lie to anyone before I’d lie to you.”

“Fine. I’ll trust you once.”

Pleased his little trick worked, Shen Jianchen kept conversation flowing so she wouldn’t rush the search. “Know why Yanjing feels so empty?”

“Of course. When the Jin seized the Sixteen Prefectures of Yanyun, they only handed back seven, Yanjing included. When Tong Guan marched in back in the fifth year of Xuanhe, he got a hollow shell—mostly poor elderly locals left behind after the Jin carried off all the young people.”

Watching bent, weathered elders shuffle past, she guessed others here shared similar errands to theirs, or came out of pride like Lady Wei to see the recovered borderland His Majesty boasted over. Unwilling to dwell on politics, she glanced round. “Well? Spot anyone who knows Liu Miaoshou yet?”

Having already spied a raw-herb apothecary ahead, Shen Jianchen told her to keep walking. Walking shoulder to shoulder in a strange city where nobody knew their names, he longed to slip his hand into hers—just once.

Right as his fingers hovered near hers, Qianli Xun pointed straight ahead. “Look! An apothecary shop! Someone inside is bound to know Liu Miaoshou. Always playing mysterious—what’s so clever about asking a clerk at a medicine shop?”

Foiled, Shen Jianchen laughed it off, praising her quick mind while slipping out that in his heart, she would always come first. Caught off guard, she hurried alone into the shop to avoid blushing, leaving him gazing fondly after her—regretting more than ever that signed pledge he gave Haoyu.

Soon she skipped back out radiant. “Found him! Straight ahead, turn west at the corner, a few steps down is Liu Miaoshou’s Clinic!” Seeing Shen Jianchen gawking moon-eyed at her, she shook a fist. “Stare like a fool one more second and you get a punch.”

“Can I help it if my heart runs away with me? Why so fierce?”

Rolling her eyes, she marched ahead while he hurried after, asking if she were angry, then throwing out his question once more: if given the choice between Chen Haoyu and him, whom would she pick?

Qianli Xun stopped. “You asked that the day I left prison.”

“And you never answered.”

“Because no such choice exists—that’s my answer.”

“What if it did?”

Turning squarely to face him, she spoke evenly. “If such a choice existed, I’d follow my heart and choose the man I love.”

“Who do you love right now?”

“Big grown man nagging like an old auntie! Who I love is none of your business!”

“I know it’s me,” he said straight out.

“Tch! Shameless!” She walked faster to hide the smile tugging at her lips—knowing fantasy changed nothing about reality.

Shen Jianchen stepped ahead to block her path, only for her calm, sober eyes to dash his hope. “Don’t you love me?”

Qianli Xun poked his chest with one finger with every word. “You’re married. I’m married. And here you stand in the middle of the street talking love? Trying to drag me into an affair? Let me tell you straight: I despise cheaters. Do you want me to turn into the kind of person I hate most?”

Stepping back under her finger, Shen Jianchen answered softly once she stopped. “One day, I will make you mine freely, with nothing standing in our way.”

Remembering broken Mengrong, she nearly blurted out her old line—“Only when pigs can climb trees”—then bit her tongue mid-sentence, terrified of tempting fate with another jinx, and sidestepped past him.

Smiling to himself that she couldn’t bring herself to say the refusal aloud, Shen Jianchen caught up without pressing further.

Rounding the corner westward, they spotted the signboard for Liu Miaoshou’s Clinic—and stopped short as apprentices hauled bundles out onto waiting carts.

“Is Physician Liu in?” Qianli Xun asked an apprentice.

“Master is in, though we aren’t seeing patients today—we’re moving to Yangzhou.”

Hurrying inside past nearly emptied shelves, they found a lean, bright-eyed old man at a round table sipping tea straight from the spout of a small purple clay teapot while watching the move.

Pulling the spout from his lips, the elder told them to take their ailments elsewhere. Shen Jianchen stepped up courteously to confirm his name, and Qianli Xun bowed deep.

“Great Physician, please accept this maiden’s bow. A friend of mine suffers from a disorder of the mind that physicians in both Lin’an and Bianliang proved unable to cure. Hearing you once healed such a case, we traveled all the way from Bianliang. Please do not turn us away.”

Flattered by her respectful manner and far-flung fame, Liu Miaoshou brightened. “One day later and I’d have been gone—call it fate. Still, my rule stands: if you want me to treat her myself, present a poem or lyric that satisfies me.”

As Qianli Xun searched her memory for the right Zhu Shuzhen verse, an apprentice carried in a pot of thick, jade-white chrysanthemums in full bloom—a thank-you gift from Xiao Liuzi, whose mother Liu Miaoshou had cured.
