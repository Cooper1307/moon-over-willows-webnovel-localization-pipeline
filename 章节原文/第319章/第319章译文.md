## Chapter 319: Family and Prince Scrambling in Desperation

Swords glinting in the lamplight, the imperial guards closed in on Prince Yi from every side.

Undaunted, Shen Jianchen unleashed the Shen Fist counters designed specifically against armed blades, plunging straight into the fray.

Every one of His Majesty’s palace guards had come up through brutal drills—each man could handle several Shen Jianchens on raw skill. Swarmed from all angles, Shen Jianchen held his own smoothly at first, yet as the barrage kept hammering in, keeping up grew tougher by the breath.

Seeing his master starting to slip into disadvantage, Bai Feng tried to jump in—only every twitch sent blood gushing fresh from the arrow wound, leaving him half-paralyzed with pain.

Even pinned down, Shen Jianchen gritted his teeth and fought hard, knocking several guards flat to the floor.

Watching Prince Yi battle like a cornered lion terrified Liang Shicheng. Desperate to end it fast, he waited for an opening and pressed a hidden catch beside his couch.

With a metallic clatter, an iron cage plummeted straight down from the ceiling rafters.

While Shen Jianchen focused hard on parrying incoming blades, every guard suddenly leaped wide clear.

Spotting it first, Bai Feng shouted, “Your Highness! Look out!”

Too late—before Shen Jianchen could dodge, the heavy iron bars slammed around him, trapping him tight with zero room to swing a fist.

Sauntering over to the cage, Liang Shicheng offered a mock bow. “Forgive the offense, Your Highness. Please stay as my guest at Liu’an Villa for a few days. Once I wrap up an important piece of business, I’ll see you safely back to Youlin Retreat.”

“What are you planning?!”

“Best not ask, Your Highness—and stay out of the poetess’s affairs.”

“If you lay a single hand on her, the second I get out I swear I’ll never let you live!”

Ignoring him, Liang Shicheng walked over to a cabinet, pulled out a drawer, grabbed a vial of wound medicine, and tossed it into Bai Feng’s lap.

“Tend your scratch, Guard Bai, and wait nicely on His Highness.”

Leading his guards out the front door, Liang Shicheng ordered the women hiding in the back out through the rear, then had every door and window boarded solid and even the roof tiles patched—leaving only one narrow slit in a window to slide through bowls of rice and water.

Bai Feng groaned, “Don’t panic, Your Highness. Once I stop the bleeding, the two of us together can tip this iron cage over.”

Yanking the arrow straight out, Bai Feng hissed as blood spurted, peeled open his robe, and dumped Liang’s medicine powder straight onto the gash.

Testing the bars himself, Shen Jianchen threw every ounce of strength against the cage—it didn’t budge a hair. “This thing weighs at least a thousand catties. Even both of us together couldn’t shift it.”

Once the bleeding stopped cleanly, Bai Feng buttoned his shirt and limped up to the bars. “What do we do now?”

Shen Jianchen sank onto the floorboards. “Get some sleep tonight. We’ll figure something out at daybreak.”

Bai Feng could tell just looking at him how frantic—and helpless—his master felt underneath.

In another wing of the villa, Liang Shicheng pushed the whole clash clean out of his head and went straight back to his twisted amusement. Chasing status and vengeance, Chun Jinqiao grit her teeth through every needle prick, screaming loud on cue.

Satisfied at last, Liang Shicheng sent Chun Jinqiao home to Lin Manor in his own carriage.

Gifting Chun Jinqiao to Imperial Attendant Liang was easily the lowest stunt Lin Jue had ever pulled, yet for his own career he had swallowed his pride and done it. After dropping her outside Liu’an Villa, he had returned to Lin Manor and tossed sleeplessly all night in Zhulan Residence.

Bai Meiruo knew about it too, of course. At first she cursed Lin Jue as a spineless beast for pimping out his own woman—then thought twice and brightened right up: he’d handed over a *concubine*, not his primary wife! That proved in her husband’s eyes a primary wife held sacred status no outsider could touch. Thrilled, she slept like a log, never even noticing what hour Chun Jinqiao crept home.

Paying no mind to the raw punctures stinging across her back, Chun Jinqiao marched straight into Zhulan Residence upon returning. A lamp still burned inside, proving Lin Jue hadn’t slept. She pushed straight through the door.

Startled half out of his skin, Lin Jue tumbled straight off the couch and scrambled up. “Y-you’re back already? Don’t tell me Imperial Attendant Liang didn’t like you!”

Chun Jinqiao sat down by the table. “Lift my shirt and look at my back—you’ll understand everything.”

Stepping over, Lin Jue peeled back her collar and stared. “What on earth poked you like that?! How could he be so vicious?!”

Only then did the throbbing ache really hit Chun Jinqiao. “Stop gawking, get me some medicine, and I’ll tell you while you rub it on.”

Fetching ointment, Lin Jue smeared salve gently across her bare back while listening to her account. Finished, he sighed guiltily. “You suffered terribly tonight.”

Pulling her clothes back on, Chun Jinqiao smirked. “Still, he agreed to everything I asked. As long as he pulls through for me, a few pokes were worth it.”

Lin Jue’s sliver of guilt vanished instantly. “I’m relieved you’re back safe. Go to your room and rest—time I finally caught some sleep too.”

Remembering Prince Yi barging into Liu’an Villa, Chun Jinqiao added, “Wait—one more thing you ought to hear.”

Drowsy already, Lin Jue waved it off. “Too sleepy. Tell me tomorrow.”

“Even if it’s about Youqi Jushi?”

Lin Jue snapped right awake. “What about her?”

“Prince Yi stormed Liu’an Villa tonight and kicked up a huge brawl—only to get locked up by Imperial Attendant Liang instead! Guess who he came charging in for?”

“Zhu Shuzhen?!”

“Well, I heard Prince Yi roaring at Liang never to touch Youqi Jushi. Chances are Imperial Attendant Liang wants the poetess dancing stark naked too so he can poke a few needles into her.”

“Dirty old lecher!” Lin Jue slammed a fist onto the table. “No wonder he sent me to squeeze the Chen family—so *this* was his real game!”

“Oh? Planning to march back and murder Imperial Attendant Liang now? If you’re that righteous, why deliver me to his doorstep tonight?”

Pulling his fist back, Lin Jue muttered, “If my career weren’t on the line, I’d knock every tooth out of his mouth!”

“Save it! You only bark tough behind closed doors. Put Imperial Attendant Liang right in front of your nose and you wouldn’t dare let out a fart—you’d hold it in till your belly burst! Why talk big?”

Lin Jue shut his mouth. If not even Prince Yi could stop the eunuch, what could *he* do? Besides, Zhu Shuzhen wasn’t his wife anymore—none of his concern. His own advancement came first.

Early the next morning, skipping breakfast altogether, Zhu Xiyan rode straight to Liu’an Villa.

Guessing at once why Zhu Xiyan had shown up, Liang Shicheng refused to waste breath arguing and had servants usher him straight into a side room.

The instant Zhu Xiyan stepped inside, guards pressed blades to his throat, trussed him tight with rope, and stuffed a gag into his mouth.

One guard announced flatly, “Imperial Attendant Liang invites you to stay a few days. Once his business is done, he’ll send you back to Zhu Manor.”

Locking the door from outside, the guards left him stranded.

Helpless, Zhu Xiyan sank down thinking, *I fear my daughter won’t escape this time.*

...

When Prince Yi failed to return by morning after slipping out the night before, Matron Zou panicked—yet dared not breathe a word to Princess Consort Yi for fear of triggering another collapse.

Thankfully Her Highness barely stirred from bed anymore. Ordering Xilan to stick to a rehearsed lie—that His Highness had been summoned to the yamen on urgent matters and would stay there several days—kept the peace.

Qiao Mengrong half-doubted the excuse, yet felt secretly glad her brother was away; it bought time for the scabbing needle marks on her skin to fade cleanly before he came back.

After waiting a whole day with zero word, Chen Jianglin and Chen Haoyu checked Zhu Manor and Youlin Retreat separately—only to learn neither Zhu Xiyan nor Prince Yi had ever come home!

The Zhu household panicked like ants on a hot skillet, while Matron Zou at Youlin Retreat grew desperate enough to beg Chen Haoyu to scour the city for Prince Yi.

Out of choices, father and son returned to Liu’an Villa for answers.

Liang Shicheng sent one guard out to the gate to tell them, “Both Prince Yi and Zhu Xiyan are staying here as guests for a few days. The instant Imperial Attendant Liang lays eyes on the poetess and feels pleased, both men walk free unharmed.”

Then the guard added a cold warning: “Our Lord says you have one day left. Fail to deliver her on time, and you can kiss every Chen shop across the empire goodbye.”

Staggering back to Chen Manor beaten and hollow, father and son kept the women in the dark and slipped straight into Old Madam Xia’s room, dismissed the maids, shut the door, and laid everything bare.

After the initial shock, Old Madam Xia turned icy calm. “So Imperial Attendant Liang means to back the Chens into a wall with no way out. Fine—then I’ll make a trip to Liu’an Villa myself.”

Chen Jianglin stepped in front of her. “Mother, not even Prince Yi or my in-law could budge him—what can you do? I’m terrified he’ll lock you up right alongside them!”

“If I’m going, I’ve got something that will put him right in his place.” Old Madam Xia raised her voice toward the door. “Come in!”

A maid pushed the door open. “Your orders, Old Madam?”

“Go to the inner chamber and fetch the large purple pouch buried at the bottom of my pearwood chest.”

Father and son exchanged bewildered stares as the maid slipped inside.

Moments later she carried out a bulky purple brocade pouch and placed it in Old Madam Xia’s hands.

Opening the drawstrings, Old Madam Xia pulled out a folded letter and a jade pendant carved with a name. Studying the characters carefully, she tucked both back inside.

“You two—come with me to call on Imperial Attendant Liang.”

Ever since two nights ago, Qianli Xun had noticed Chen Haoyu acting off. Two mornings straight, when he came to whisk tea for her, his usual chatter had vanished.

Taking the tea bowl and asking why he looked so glum, she only got brushed off with vague excuses about “too many tangled business headaches” before he bolted out the door.

Puzzled, Qianli Xun spotted Qinggua passing outside the window, hurried out, and flagged him down. “Qinggua, where are you rushing off to?”

“To Yongtai Teahouse, Young Madam. Young Master told me to meet a merchant alone today and close the spice deal.”

“Chen Haoyu isn’t going?”

“He told me to handle it myself—said he’s tied up with other urgent business these past couple of days.”

“What business?”

“He didn’t say, so I’m really not sure.”

Getting nowhere, Qianli Xun sighed, “Go on then, don’t miss your meeting.”

Qinggua bowed and hurried off on business.

Suspicion itching inside her, Qianli Xun decided to ask her mother-in-law instead.

Walking into Yao Qingyan’s room, she found her mother-in-law sitting there looking equally baffled.

Yao Qingyan had noticed her husband acting strange for two straight days—distracted, spaced out, barely speaking a word over breakfast this morning with gloom written across his face. Pressed on what was wrong, Chen Jianglin had only mumbled about work pressures.

Out at dawn, back late at night, out at dawn again today with brows knotted tight—even during previous trade crises, her husband had never looked like *this*. Had disaster struck?

Just then Shuzhen walked in. Greeting her warmly, Yao Qingyan asked at once, “Did Haoyu mention anything troubling about business?”

Qianli Xun shook her head. “Not a word—though he’s looked miserable ever since coming home yesterday. Could something major have happened with the shops? Did Father-in-law say anything to you?”

“Your father-in-law’s looked heavy-hearted since last night too, and won’t tell me details either.”

“It’s got to be business—otherwise why would both of them wear the exact same face? Should we ask Grandmother?”

“We sat with Old Madam yesterday and she seemed completely in the dark—asking won’t tell us much. Still, it’s time for our morning visit with her anyway. Let’s go together.”

Walking arm in arm toward Old Madam Xia’s courtyard, the two women rounded a bend—and ran straight into father and son escorting Old Madam Xia toward the gate!

Intercepting them, Yao Qingyan asked first, “Why are you two back—and where are you taking Mother?”

Old Madam Xia spun a smooth lie. “Just business. An old client insisted on seeing me in person, so father and son came to drag this old bone out of retirement. You two run along back to your rooms—skip sitting with me today, and tell Second and Third Daughters-in-law too.”

Testing the waters, Qianli Xun smiled brightly. “Why not take me along, Grandmother? Let me broaden my horizons!”

Old Madam Xia shot it down fast. “You’re a poetess—stay home writing verse and lyrics. Showing your face in merchant squabbles doesn’t suit you. Stay put indoors.”

One look at the grim tightness on both men’s faces told the two women something smelled fishy—only neither could pin down what.

Once the trio rolled away, Qianli Xun escorted her mother-in-law back to her rooms, grabbed Kuqiao, had a carriage hitched, and ordered her coachman to tail the Chen carriage that had just pulled out.

Mystified, Kuqiao asked, “Why are we sneaking behind them like spies, Miss?”

“Don’t ask me why—I just know they’re hiding something big from Mother-in-law and me. I won’t rest easy until I see what it is.”

“I haven’t seen Bai Feng for two whole days either, and my chest feels tight with dread for no reason. Do you think something happened to him?”

“Probably not. He’s Prince Yi’s bodyguard—who’d touch him? Prince Yi’s been in a rotten mood these past two days, so Bai Feng’s probably stuck cheering him up and couldn’t slip over.”

“Why’s Prince Yi in a rotten mood?”

“Word is he lost both the Military Commissioner post and his spot at the Judicial Office—anyone would be moody.”

“Sigh… to think someone as noble as Prince Yi still gets pushed around like a pawn.”

“Even His Majesty will get pushed around sooner or later, let alone a prince.”

Peeking out past the curtain, Qianli Xun judged by the street signs they were heading north. Dropping the curtain, she slid the front panel slightly and murmured to the driver, “Don’t tail too close or they’ll spot us.”

The driver eased off the whip and kept a cautious gap.

Shutting the panel, Qianli Xun stopped chatting with Kuqiao and closed her eyes in thought.

She was beginning to see that affection really could grow over time. Six or seven months married to Chen Haoyu, and things between them had warmed nicely. At this rate, someday when Shen Jianchen crossed her mind, it might feel like nothing more than old friendship.

Since the Black-Fiend Tiger ordeal, life had turned remarkably peaceful—surely Mengrong had finally found her conscience and decided to leave her in peace.

She even pictured a few years down the road: she’d have children with Chen Haoyu, Mengrong and Prince Yi would have theirs, and one day both families might run into each other with kids in tow, laugh off old scars, and maybe even become close again.

Right in the middle of that sweet daydream, the carriage rolled to a stop. From outside came the coachman’s hushed voice: “Young Madam, Old Madam and Master’s carriage just pulled up outside Liu’an Villa. I’ve parked around the corner out of sight—you’re safe to step down.”
