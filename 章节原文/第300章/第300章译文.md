## Chapter 300: Picking the Right Go-Between

Shao Zhengye shot it down on the spot. “Shao Escort Agency runs an honest trade—never murder or arson. Wrong door, Eldest Young Master.”

Stung by the blunt brush-off, Chen Haodong pulled out his trump card. “Know who sent me?”

“Wouldn’t budge if it were anyone.”

“The highest-ranking lady in all of Lin’an—Princess Consort Yi!”

To his shock, father and son traded a glance and turned the rebuke right back on him. “Want somebody dead, don’t drag Princess Consort Yi’s name through the mud! Prince Yi visited our hall—peerless fighter, upright to the bone. His wife would never hire assassins! Watch your tongue before you smear royalty. I’ll pretend I heard nothing—Zhize, show our guest out!”

Shao Zhize rose. “After you, Eldest Young Master.”

Humiliated, Chen Haodong stood to leave—then flashed a nasty smirk and turned back. “Quit playing saint, Master Shao! You’ve got blood on your hands too—think I don’t know?”

A jolt froze Shao Zhengye, masked in half a second. “Watch your mouth before walking out of here gets tough.”

“What, gonna kill me too—after that noble speech? Let’s see if one name makes you sweat: Yan Chi!”

In a flash, Shao Zhize clamped a steel grip around Chen Haodong’s throat. “What else do you know?!”

Choking yet cocky, Chen Haodong gasped, “Dare kill Yan Chi’s father, yet too righteous to help Princess Consort Yi?!”

Shao Zhize squeezed harder. “One more crazy word and you’ll be the first corpse ever carried out of Shao Escort Agency! Talk—what else?!”

Terrified of losing his neck over Princess Consort Yi’s errands, Chen Haodong caved. “Mercy! That’s all! Just overheard Yan Chi grumbling—maybe I misheard!”

Shao Zhize loosened his grip. “Keep your mouth shut and we stay friends.”

Shao Zhengye pressed sharp, “What’s your tie to this Yan Chi?”

“Barely knew him! Passing acquaintance—stayed a few days at our chuiwan grounds and vanished.”

“Friendly warning: stay clear of that man, or you’ll bring ruin down on the Chens. Out!”

Brushed out like dirt, Chen Haodong slunk off cursing his luck—no hired blade meant risking Princess Consort Yi skipping next month’s vote, and he was dead set on flaunting her backing before the clan! He’d have to dig up another killer fast. As for Yan Chi’s parting warning back then—just hot air from a drifter, nothing to lose sleep over.

Once Chen Haodong cleared out, Shao Zhengye asked his son, “When did Prince Yi get back from Bianliang?”

“A month ago—meant to tell you right before Chen Haodong barged in.”

“Promised to pay respects once he returned—can’t break my word. I’ll visit Youlin Retreat tomorrow.”

“He’s serving as Judicial Investigator now, Father—mostly at the Judicial Office. Plus while you were on the road, he sent Lazybones Village back to the hills with a trade and got the yamen building earthen homes and cave dwellings for every vagrant in town!”

“Far better than His Majesty’s Juyang Yuan, Anji Fang, and Louze Yuan combined!” Shao Zhengye beamed. “Bring two jugs of fine wine—tomorrow we call on Prince Yi at the Judicial Office!”

Meanwhile, back from the salon, Qianli Xun remembered her promise to Master Jiang. She hadn’t dared even glance at Prince Yi at the salon with Mengrong glaring, yet she never broke her word. Sneaking into the Judicial Office at night? He slept at Youlin Retreat. Visiting by day? Risk bumping into Mengrong. How absurdly complicated seeing one friend was in the Song Dynasty—unlike the modern world where Shen Jianchen came running at one phone call.

Opting for the clean route, she penned a quick note, folded it, and summoned Hongdou. “Run this to the silk shop for Young Master—ask him to drop it off at the Judicial Office for Prince Yi when he has a moment. And don’t breathe a word about why I went to the Qiantang Gang!”

“Not a syllable, Miss!”

Guided to the silk shop’s accounting room by a clerk, Hongdou heard brisk abacus clacking—and burst out laughing at the doorway seeing Young Master perched behind the head ledger like a grizzled old bookkeeper.

Setting down his brush, Chen Haoyu frowned. “What’s funny?”

“Never pictured Young Master looking this dead serious—it’s great!”

“Better! Miss send you?”

Handing over the folded note, Hongdou relayed her instructions. Opening it, Chen Haoyu muttered, “My wife says yes to everybody! Funny—why’s Master Jiang asking her to play go-between?”

“Ask Miss tonight—above a maid’s pay grade!”

Folding it into his robe, he nodded. “Tell her I’ll finish this ledger and head straight over.”

Stepping out, Hongdou spotted Qinggua smoothly pitching bolts of silk to a customer and grinning bright once he saw her off. “Come to buy silk?”

“Can’t afford this stuff! Dropped a note for Young Master and checked how you’re doing so I can brag to Chouya.”

“Don’t bother—I tell her every night! Clerking pays triple being a valet, plus Young Master says once he joins the family business he’ll bring me along! Regretting picking Asheng over me yet, Sister Hongdou?”

“Oh, tearing my hair out with regret!” she joked back.

“Too late! I’m loyal to Chouya just like Young Master is to Young Madam!”

“Guess I’ll go bash my head on a block of tofu—keep clerking, I’m off!”

Watching her go, Qinggua muttered smugly, “Pretty as Hongdou is, Chouya’s way sweeter.”

Finishing his accounts, Chen Haoyu rode to the Judicial Office—feeling a sheepish twinge at the gate where he’d once been locked up, vowing again to walk the straight and narrow.

Surprised by his visit yet receiving him for Qianli Xun’s sake, Shen Jianchen invited him to sit in his modest courtyard—leaving Chen Haoyu baffled why a prince traded Youlin Retreat for this bare little office.

Handing over the note with both hands, Chen Haoyu said, “From my wife, Your Highness.”

Reading the request for Master Jiang, Shen Jianchen nodded. “Tell Youqi Jushi not to worry—easy fix. I’ll visit Master Jiang myself.”

Watching Prince Yi say yes without blinking made jealousy prick Chen Haoyu’s gut. Rising quick, he bowed. “Errand done—I won’t intrude.”

“Stay a minute—I’ve been wanting a private word with you anyway.”

Unable to refuse a royal, Chen Haoyu sat stiffly back down.

“She asks you to run notes to me, and you don’t mind?” Shen Jianchen asked.

“Whatever my wife asks, I’m glad to do.”

A bitter smile tugged Shen Jianchen’s lips. “Funny how life works—you didn’t lift a finger and won the woman I wanted, while I fought tooth and nail and still watched her marry you. Chen Haoyu... I’d trade places with you in a heartbeat.”

“Even if we could trade, I wouldn’t.”

“True—nobody who holds her would ever give her up. So I won’t ask for anything else—just treat her right. If she’s happy, I’m at peace. If you ever hurt her, I won’t hesitate to take your place.”

First Qiao Wenxian, now Prince Yi! Dropping deference, Chen Haoyu spoke firm. “I’ll treat her ten times better and never give Your Highness an opening! Please devote yourself to Princess Consort Yi and let my wife go—the wedding raid still stings fresh. Would you really break Princess Consort Yi’s heart twice?”

Catching his slip, Shen Jianchen eased back. “Relax—just talking, didn’t mean to rattle you. Past is past; forward is the only road.” Chatting neutrally a few minutes more, he saw Chen Haoyu off—then saddled a horse and rode straight to the Qiantang Gang alone, determined to show Master Jiang he’d bet on the exact right person.

Minutes after Chun Jinmo stepped out, a runner burst in reporting Prince Yi waiting alone on horseback at the gate. Overjoyed, Master Jiang didn’t even jam on shoes—he sprinted out barefoot and dropped to his knees. “Your Highness honors our humble gang!”

Eyeing his bare feet, Shen Jianchen teased, “No shoes—that how you welcome guests?”

“So thrilled to hear Your Highness arrived I forgot shoes existed—bare feet prove how eager I am to greet you!”

Slick talker for a rough customer. “You asked someone whose face I prize very much to bridge us—so here I am. Going to invite me inside?”

*Betting on the poetess was the smartest move of my life!* Master Jiang sprang up, ordered his men to tend the horse, and ushered Prince Yi inside.
