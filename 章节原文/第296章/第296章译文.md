## Chapter 296: A Fragile Truce?

At last Chen Haoyu spotted Qiao Wenxian among the scholars—gazing straight toward their table.

*Bet he hasn’t walked over only because I’m sitting here!* Smug he’d tagged along, Chen Haoyu raised a cup of rice wine to test him. Qiao Wenxian didn’t even blink—eyes locked dead on his wife! Scowling, Chen Haoyu tossed the drink back himself.

One scholar stood first. “Gentlemen, I nominate ‘Bo Xi’ from the Odes of Wei in the Book of Songs: ‘O for rain! O for rain! But brightly the sun comes forth. Longingly I think of my husband, till my heart is weary, and my head aches. How shall I get the plant of forgetfulness? I would plant it on the north of my house. Longingly I think of my husband, and my heart is made to ache.’ [TN: Translated by James Legge] That plant of forgetfulness—daylily shoots—is a wild green! Why not name the dish ‘Forget-Sorrow Herb,’ wishing every diner forgets their troubles?”

Murmurs of approval rose.

Another scholar rose. “I nominate Tang poet Xing Qun’s couplet: ‘Wild greens through winter keep their verdant green; before twelfth-month cold, mountain plums bloom everywhere.’ [TN: Translator’s rendering] Let’s call it ‘Verdant Green’!”

Several more followed before Qiao Wenxian stood. “I nominate Tang poet Li Qunyu’s ‘Fisherman on the Yuan River’: ‘Leaning on my oar by the sandy islet as evening falls: river delicacies, wild greens, and peach-blossom rice. One long song into the deepening mist, then homeward across the far green waves of the blue river.’ [TN: Translator’s rendering] Blending two phrases, I propose ‘River-Fresh Green Waves’!”

Warm applause met his suggestion.

Then Ye Mengde stood and bowed. “Greetings, Your Highness Prince Yi and Princess Consort Yi—forgive me for paying respects only now.”

Neither Shen Jianchen nor Qiao Mengrong recognized him until Zhu Xiyan rose to introduce him. “Ye Mengde, Prefect of Yingchang.”

Recognition hit Shen Jianchen—a giant of Song letters! “No formality needed, Prefect Ye.”

Zhu Xiyan teased him half-serious, “Sneaking into Lin’an without paying respects to Prince Yi or letting me play host, hiding in a poetry salon to ambush us—you owe a forfeit!”

Ye Mengde parried smooth as silk. “Left Yingchang on private errands and came here on private errands—how dare I march up and advertise playing hooky from my post? Better lie low! Only Prince Yi’s fame drew me into this salon hoping to blend into the crowd, until your noble relief project moved me so deep I couldn’t stay hidden anymore!”

Laughter swept the hall. Zhu Xiyan pointed at him. “That silver tongue of yours makes a man love and hate you at once!”

Impressed by a man who didn’t fawn over rank and kept his dignity sharp with wit, Shen Jianchen invited, “Why not propose a poem and dish name too, Prefect Ye?”

“Gladly, Your Highness—I’ll nominate a poem of my own! The title’s too long to bother reciting, yet four lines read: ‘Late spring sweeps away the lingering blossoms; thick leaves hold no cicadas yet. From the high terrace the gaze sweeps all four quarters, where green plains float in lush expanse.’ [TN: Translator’s rendering] How about ‘Lush Floating Greens’?”

Cao Ban jumped in grinning. “I know that poem! Longest title in history—let me show off my memory: ‘The Day After Tomorrow, Returning Once More with General Manager Dunli, Transport Commissioner Wu Desu, and Zhang Sitai Past Tianxi Temple to Ascend Yuhua Terrace, Using the Former Rhymes Again’!”

Laughter and nods confirmed the record.

Even if the name wasn’t his literal favorite, Shen Jianchen admired Ye Mengde’s backbone and called it. “‘Lush Floating Greens’ wins! That’s our dish name.”

Ye Mengde bowed his thanks, and Zhu Xiyan urged everyone to promote “Lush Floating Greens” across taverns and household kitchens before handing the stage back to Dongfang Chenze.

“Next up: Classic Appreciation!” Dongfang Chenze announced as Huayu unfurled a massive scroll of Su Shi’s lyric “Dingfengbo: Pay No Heed to the Rain Beating Through the Forest Leaves.”

While scholars dove into spirited critique, Qiao Mengrong cared nothing for poetry—watching her brother like a hawk, pleased he hadn’t shot a single glance toward Zhu Shuzhen. Still, she wasn’t leaving without issuing a warning. Rising from the head table, she strode straight toward Youqi Jushi.

Assuming she was just saying hello to her old friend, Shen Jianchen paid little mind.

*Not a single glance at Prince Yi all morning—what’s she marching over here for?* Qianli Xun wondered, rising with Chen Haoyu, Xiangrong, and Kuqiao to bow.

Qiao Mengrong aimed a barb straight at Chen Haoyu. “No head for poetry, yet Young Master Chen tagged along—don’t trust your wife? Fair enough, surrounded by handsome scholars! Friendly tip: keep a tight leash on her; those eyes hook men on sight.”

Swallowing his anger in public before royalty, Chen Haoyu replied stiff, “I trust my wife completely. She invited me along herself.”

“Mind stepping aside with this lady a minute?” Qiao Mengrong dismissed him. “I’d like a private word with Youqi Jushi.”

Hesitating, Chen Haoyu caught his wife’s quiet murmur—“I’ll be fine”—and squeezed over to a nearby vacated seat with Xiangrong, eyes glued to his wife the whole time.

Sitting down unruffled, Qianli Xun spoke flat. “If you came looking for a fight, Princess Consort Yi, keep your cool—don’t embarrass your rank in public.”

Smirking as she took the chair across, Qiao Mengrong shot back, “Save your lectures. Just wanted you to know: I wasn’t even invited today—Brother insisted on bringing me. Know why?”

“Royals’ private lives don’t interest me.” Qianli Xun turned her ear toward the scholars, yet Qiao Mengrong’s voice cut straight through.

“Interest you or not, hear this: since consummating our marriage, Brother’s heart belongs to me alone, with zero room for anyone else—even insists on taking me everywhere he goes!”

Sadness welled in Qianli Xun—not for herself, for Mengrong. Boasting like this only laid bare how terrified she was inside; high rank couldn’t cure an empty heart.

“Reminds me of a story,” Qianli Xun said softly. “A little girl bragged nonstop to other kids that she had a mother. The other kids laughed—every kid has a mother, why brag? Turns out she had a stepmother who treated her rotten. People always flaunt loudest what they’re starving for most. Follow me, Princess Consort Yi?”

Stung raw, unable to explode in front of Lin’an’s officials, Qiao Mengrong hissed, “Then that stepmother must’ve had other children stealing her love! If I were that girl, I’d wipe out every other child until I was the only one left—then the mother’s love would all be mine!”

“She was a good kid—she wouldn’t do that. Besides, once a mother knows who killed her children, could she ever love the killer?”

“Good?! Being good just gets you stepped on, no matter your rank—even by your own best friend!”

Quiet words, yet heavy as stones—making Qianli Xun’s chest ache. “If I were that girl, I’d use my head to heal the family instead of burning it down. Mengrong, put your energy into your life with Prince Yi and leave me be. Look over there—my husband loves me and changed his whole life for me. How many women get that lucky? I treasure what I have.”

A tremor softened Qiao Mengrong’s eyes. “You really mean that? You didn’t even love Chen Haoyu!”

“Give me your hand,” Qianli Xun asked gently.

Hesitating a beat, Qiao Mengrong laid her hand on the table, and Qianli Xun covered it with hers. Tears welled in Qiao Mengrong’s eyes—Shuzhen’s hand felt just as warm as before.

Across the room, Chen Haoyu exhaled, Shen Jianchen assumed they had patched things up, and Zhu Xiyan—tense since Her Highness walked over—relaxed at last. Standing behind her miss, Kuqiao prayed silently their friendship was healed for good.

Holding Mengrong’s hand, Qianli Xun felt real hope. “Time changes everything. Before I didn’t love Chen Haoyu—now I’m changing, and tomorrow I’ll change more. I’ve made up my mind to grow old with him, and I truly wish you and Prince Yi a lifetime of harmony.”

Meeting Shuzhen’s earnest gaze, Qiao Mengrong forced herself to believe. “All right—I’ll trust you once.” Pulling her hand back, she returned to her seat beside Prince Yi, who wisely asked nothing lest he stir fresh doubts.

The second Qiao Mengrong walked away, Qiao Wenxian strolled over and sat. “Long time no see, Shuzhen.”

Before she could reply, Chen Haoyu shot back to the table. “Not critiquing Dongpo Jushi’s lyric, Qiao Wenxian? What’re you doing over here?”

“Just finished—dropped by to share a drink with Shuzhen. Mind?” Filling Shuzhen’s cup, he poured one for Chen Haoyu too.

“Who gave you permission to call her ‘Shuzhen’?” Chen Haoyu bristled. “Careful your wife and concubine don’t throw a fit and bother my wife!”

“You call her ‘Wife,’ so what’s wrong with ‘Shuzhen’? Always called her that! Come on, drink with me.”

“Fair enough.” Lifting his cup, Chen Haoyu declared, “To the three of us!”

Qianli Xun clinked her cup with his first. “Not bad today.”

Glowing at her praise, he boasted, “I’ll shine every single day till you run out of compliments!”

Envy stabbed Qiao Wenxian—that seat belonged to him in another life. Hearing “Cheers, Wenyuan,” he raised his cup and drained it with them.

“Not critiquing Dongpo Jushi’s piece, Shuzhen?”

“Haven’t had a spare second—Princess Consort Yi barely stood up before you sat down! Plenty of scholars eager to talk anyway, so I’m slacking off.”

Itching to cut Qiao Wenxian short, Chen Haoyu cut in. “Hey, Qiao Wenxian—got a question my wife shouldn’t hear. Lean in.”

Catching his ploy, Qianli Xun played along. “I’ll go sit with Xiangrong a minute—boys’ talk.” Kuqiao trailed her over.

“Petty as ever,” Qiao Wenxian grumbled. “Can’t even let me trade two sentences with Shuzhen?”

“I’m generous on everything except my wife—don’t like other men getting cozy, you most of all!”

“Excuse me, then.” Rising to leave, Qiao Wenxian felt Chen Haoyu tug his sleeve.

“Hold up—I really do have something to ask, and now that she’s gone I don’t even have to whisper!” Chen Haoyu started chuckling before getting a word out.

“Spit it out or I’m walking.”

“Wait, wait, it’s just too funny!” Composing himself, Chen Haoyu stuck up a thumb. “Never guessed it—you, my friend, are a real man!”

Qiao Wenxian blinked in total confusion. “What on earth are you talking about?”
