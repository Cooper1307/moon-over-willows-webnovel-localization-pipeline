## Chapter 314: Delivering Silver Notes at Liu’an Villa

A gust of autumn wind swept over him, sending a chill straight into Shen Jianchen’s dream. Opening his eyes, he saw the moon had already cleared the branches of the Divine Willow.

Time to head back, or Mengrong would fret.

He pushed himself up off the cold boulder, cast one last look at the willow bathed in moonlight, and walked past the pond into the dense woods.

With her brother still missing so late, Qiao Mengrong had grown frantic with worry.

Earlier, before dusk, Bai Feng had returned all by himself.

“Why are you back alone?” Qiao Mengrong had demanded. “Where is my brother?”

“His Highness wasn’t at the Judicial Office, so I figured he’d come back to Youlin Retreat.”

Qiao Mengrong’s irritation with Bai Feng had been mounting. After vanishing for a stretch and refusing to say what he’d been doing beyond “important business for His Highness,” now he’d gone off playing around and left her brother unattended.

“You’re his personal bodyguard—you’re supposed to stay right by his side, not wander off on your own! Pull this again and I’ll have my brother dismiss you and hire someone else!”

Never having seen Princess Consort Yi flare up like that, Bai Feng hurried to say, “I’ll go check under the Divine Willow.”

When Bai Feng went to the willow, daylight hadn’t faded and Prince Yi wasn’t there yet. Returning empty-handed earned him a sharp scolding. To make up for it, he planted himself on a stone stool right outside the main gate to wait.

After a long stretch, he spotted Prince Yi emerging from the woods.

Bai Feng scrambled to meet him. “Your Highness! Did you go to see the Divine Willow?”

Shen Jianchen let out a low grunt and kept walking.

Bai Feng trotted at his heels. “Weird—I checked under the willow for you and didn’t see a soul!” Before the prince answered, Bai Feng supplied his own explanation. “You must not have reached the willow yet when I looked. Silly me—I should’ve gone back for a second look.” Remembering the princess consort, he added, “At least Princess Consort Yi can stop worrying now that you’re back safe.”

Shen Jianchen let him chatter on without replying to a word. Reaching his quarters, he found Mengrong still fully dressed, having waited up for him.

The instant he stepped inside, Qiao Mengrong threw her arms around him. “Where did you go, Brother? You scared me half to death!”

Shen Jianchen answered honestly, “I had a few too many drinks with Wenyuan at a wine shop, then sat under the Divine Willow for a bit, which made me late.”

Relief washed over Qiao Mengrong—as long as he hadn’t been sneaking off to meet Zhu Shuzhen, she could breathe easy. Letting go of him, she asked, “Isn’t the Judicial Office keeping you busy? Why did you have time to drink with Young Master Qiao—and in a tavern at that?”

Shen Jianchen forced an easy smile onto his face. “From today on, I’m no longer Military Commissioner of Lin’an, and I won’t be going back to the Judicial Office either. Now I’ll have all the time in the world to spend with you. Doesn’t that make you happy?”

Instead of looking pleased, Qiao Mengrong frowned in puzzlement. “What happened?”

Shen Jianchen stepped aside, turning his back to her. “Zhu Xiyan’s been replaced as Prefect of Lin’an by someone named Li Ke. His Majesty wants me to stay a leisurely prince, and honestly, I’m glad to be free of it.”

Qiao Mengrong cared nothing for her brother’s lost title. “Who did His Majesty send to Lin’an to handle this?”

“Who else? His Majesty’s personal favorite—Liang Shicheng.”

Had Shen Jianchen turned around, he would have caught an unexpected smile curve across Mengrong’s lips. Since he didn’t, he had no clue what wheels were turning inside her head.

“Will Imperial Attendant Liang be staying in Lin’an for a while?”

“His Majesty sent him to set Lin’an’s prefecture office back in line, so yes, he’ll be here a stretch.”

“Do you happen to know where he’s staying, Brother?”

That made Shen Jianchen turn around. “Why ask where he lives?”

Qiao Mengrong covered fast. “Just for your sake, Brother. I thought I might pay him a visit, slip him some silver, and get him to put in good words with His Majesty—maybe restore your post as Military Commissioner of Lin’an.”

Thinking Mengrong far too naive to grasp official politics or His Majesty’s folly, he shook his head. “Don’t waste your time buttering him up. Right now I just want to live as a leisurely prince.”

Unable to pry Liang’s address out of her brother, Qiao Mengrong decided she’d send Matron Zou out to investigate first thing tomorrow.

...

The next morning, after breakfast with her brother, Qiao Mengrong saw him drilling Bai Feng in fist forms in the courtyard. Afraid her brother might overhear if she summoned Matron Zou here, she brought Xilan along and walked straight to Matron Zou’s quarters.

Matron Zou had just finished eating and was getting ready to pay her morning respects and chat with Princess Consort Yi.

Since Black-Fiend Tiger’s arrest, the princess consort hadn’t breathed another word about killing the poetess. Matron Zou felt deeply relieved, believing her mistress had finally let it go.

Stepping out her door, she ran straight into Princess Consort Yi entering the yard. Matron Zou greeted her warmly. “I was just heading over to see you, and here you are.”

Qiao Mengrong took Matron Zou’s hand. “Let’s talk inside. I need your help with something.”

Worried she might bring up assassinating the poetess again, Matron Zou sat down inside and asked cautiously, “What important matter does Your Highness have in mind?”

To avoid arousing suspicion, Qiao Mengrong kept the truth even from Matron Zou. “Last night Brother told me His Majesty stripped him of the Lin’an Military Commissioner post and told him to be an idle prince. He acted unbothered when he told me, yet I can tell it hurts him inside. I want to help him.”

“You’re a delicate lady, Your Highness—how could you help Prince Yi?”

“Brother said His Majesty sent Imperial Attendant Liang to handle things and that he’ll be staying in Lin’an for a spell. I want to call on him myself. He’s greedy for money—if I grease his palms with enough silver, he’ll speak well of Brother before His Majesty, and the Military Commissioner post will come right back to him.”

Matron Zou thought it sounded plausible enough; after all, Imperial Attendant Liang had helped move the royal wedding forward too.

“Does His Highness know what you’re planning?”

“He mustn’t find out. He’s too proud to stoop to it. Once I pull it off, I’ll surprise him.”

“It moves me to see how devoted you are to Prince Yi. Tell me whatever you need done.”

“To call on Imperial Attendant Liang, I need to know where he’s staying. I can’t go snooping myself, so I’ll have to trouble you.”

“Nothing to it—leave it to me. I’ll find out within one shichen.”

...

Matron Zou returned from the city just before noon. Waiting until lunch was over, she suggested a stroll to help Princess Consort Yi digest, giving them clean cover away from Prince Yi’s ears.

Walking side by side along a path inside Youlin Retreat until sure Prince Yi wasn’t trailing them, Matron Zou spoke. “Imperial Attendant Liang is staying at Liu’an Villa in the north of the city.”

Qiao Mengrong sighed. “To think the grandest villa in northern Lin’an belongs to Imperial Attendant Liang.”

“Care to guess who told me where he’s staying, Your Highness?”

“Who?”

“Commander Lin’s wife, Bai Meiruo.”

Qiao Mengrong scowled. “Hmph! Lin Jue’s nothing but a weathervane! Barely one day in town and he’s already sucking up to Imperial Attendant Liang—knows his exact address and everything.”

“That’s how men climb in officialdom, nothing surprising about it. His Highness never stood high in His Majesty’s favor growing up. Getting made Military Commissioner of Lin’an looked like a sign His Majesty had started valuing him, which is why everyone lined up to flatter him. Now the title’s gone, and Youlin Retreat will turn colder and emptier than ever.”

“No matter what, Brother is still His Majesty’s son.”

“True, he’s His Majesty’s son, yet His Majesty has so many sons—some favored, some neglected. Officials have sharp eyes; they read the wind and follow profit. If His Highness can’t bring them advancement, why would they waste time on him?”

“For Brother’s sake, I have to visit Imperial Attendant Liang right away. I’m going now.”

Losing all interest in walking, Qiao Mengrong turned straight back with Xilan.

Returning to their bedchamber, she found her brother stretched out listlessly on the bed.

Sitting on the mattress edge, Qiao Mengrong said, “Brother, I have to step out for a bit. A few noblewomen in Lin’an invited me to a gathering, and it wouldn’t be polite to refuse.”

Shen Jianchen sat up. “I’ll escort you there.”

The last thing Qiao Mengrong wanted was her brother coming along. She refused quickly. “Stay and rest, Brother. Having Xilan with me is plenty—besides, it’s all married women, it wouldn’t look right for you to come.”

“I’ll only see you to the gate. I won’t go in.”

“You’ve been running yourself ragged lately. Stay and catch your breath. Next time an invitation comes up, I’ll let you take me.”

Sensing Qiao Mengrong really didn’t want him tagging along, Shen Jianchen gave up and lay back down. “Go and come back soon then.”

Qiao Mengrong nodded and boarded the carriage with Xilan.

Once the wheels rolled, Qiao Mengrong asked, “Did you bring enough silver notes?”

“Yes, Your Highness—twenty thousand taels of silver in all.”

“That’s practically every ounce of savings I have. Still, as long as it works, no price is too high.”

The carriage passed through the city gate, threaded through bustling markets, and halted outside Liu’an Villa in the northern quarter.

Heart fluttering with nerves, Qiao Mengrong walked up to the grand gate and had Xilan knock.

A servant opened the door. “Who are you looking for?”

Xilan answered, “Kindly announce that Princess Consort Yi is here to call on Imperial Attendant Liang.”

Hearing “Princess Consort Yi,” the servant dared not stall. “Wait a moment, noble lady. I’ll announce you at once.”

The gate clicked shut again.

Moments later, the servant swung the doors wide and bowed. “The Imperial Attendant says to invite Princess Consort Yi inside.”

A spark of irritation pricked Qiao Mengrong. Like it or not, she was His Majesty’s daughter-in-law, part of the imperial clan; trusted or not, Liang was still a palace servant—yet he hadn’t bothered coming out to greet her. Clearly he held her brother in zero regard.

She could already feel her brother’s standing slipping straight back to his sickly days, though she reminded herself not to pick fights when she had come asking a favor.

Liu’an Villa dwarfed Youlin Retreat—it rivaled Prince Yi’s own manor back in the capital. Bitter resentment tightened her chest: an emperor’s attendant lived better in Lin’an than an imperial prince, proving he commanded more favor and far deeper pockets than her brother.

Would twenty thousand taels of silver even catch his eye? Remembering how eagerly Liang had pocketed every small bribe she slipped him during her brother’s sick days whenever he brought messages, Qiao Mengrong felt a little steadier.

Led into an inner chamber, Qiao Mengrong held her chin high; if he wouldn’t greet her at the door, she saw no reason to curtsy to him either.

She stood straight inside the hall, waiting for Imperial Attendant Liang to pay his respects.

Instead, she saw him lounging sideways on a couch in loose indoor robes, hair hanging unbound. One maid sat beside the couch massaging his legs; another knelt on the floor holding a fruit platter, feeding him grapes one by one.

Anger flared hot in Qiao Mengrong’s chest. Receiving her dressed and sprawled like that screamed disrespect. Still, having come this far, she forced herself to swallow it.

At her nod, Xilan spoke up. “Imperial Attendant Liang, Princess Consort Yi has come expressly to call on you.”

Liang Shicheng had spotted her from the corner of his eye the second she walked in, pretending not to notice until Xilan spoke. Only then did he sit up and wave the two maids out.

“Princess Consort Yi graces us—forgive me for failing to greet you at the gate. Please, take a seat.”

Even seated upright, he didn’t bother standing to welcome her. Cursing his insolence under her breath, Qiao Mengrong sat down anyway—what choice did a petitioner have?

“Should Imperial Attendant Liang go change into proper clothes?”

Liang Shicheng chuckled. “Back in Bianliang, when I chat privately with His Majesty, I dress just like this. If His Majesty doesn’t mind, why should Princess Consort Yi?”

Qiao Mengrong choked on her reply. An outsider with no royal blood sat around casually dressed chatting with His Majesty, while her brother—His Majesty’s own flesh and blood—had never known such closeness. No wonder this eunuch dared treat her like dirt.

Seeing her bite her tongue, Liang Shicheng shifted to business. “I received the letter Princess Consort Yi sent to Bianliang. Since I was coming down to Lin’an myself, I didn’t write back—trust Your Highness forgives the oversight.”

Hearing what mattered most, Qiao Mengrong swept aside every slight. “No need for apology, Imperial Attendant Liang. Can you handle the matter I wrote about in that letter?”

Liang Shicheng smiled with oily certainty. “There isn’t a thing under Heaven I can’t pull off.”

Glad she’d picked the right man at last, she called behind her. “Xilan.”

Understanding, Xilan stepped up and presented the exquisite brocade box in her hands.

Liang Shicheng eyed the box, knowing well enough she meant to buy him. Opening the lid for a glance, he saw the stack inside was hefty indeed, snapped it shut, and set it beside him on the couch.

“Surely Princess Consort Yi didn’t come for just one matter?”

“Nothing slips past you, Lord Attendant. Aside from what was in my letter, I also came for my brother. I hope you’ll speak kindly of him before His Majesty and restore his post as Military Commissioner of Lin’an.”

Liang Shicheng looked her up and down from head to toe. “Did Prince Yi send you to me?”

“No. My brother knows nothing about this.”

Greedy eyes crawling over Qiao Mengrong’s figure, Liang Shicheng said softly, “Tell your maid to wait outside. I want to speak with Princess Consort Yi alone.”

Having delivered the box, Xilan had stepped back behind her mistress. Hearing his command, she glanced down. “Your Highness?”

Qiao Mengrong turned her head. “Step outside for a little while.”

Full of misgiving, Xilan had no choice but to walk out.

The instant the maid left, Liang Shicheng finally stood—barefoot—walked to the door, and shut it tight.

Dimness swallowed the room. Cold dread crept up Qiao Mengrong’s spine, and she rose to her feet. “Why are you shutting the door, Imperial Attendant Liang?”

Sliding the heavy bolt home, Liang Shicheng turned back. “Shut the door, and nobody overhears what you and I are plotting.”

She let out a tense breath, though unease still gnawed at her as she watched him walk back to the couch, pick up the brocade box of silver notes, and step right up to her.

“Take your brocade box back, Princess Consort Yi. You brought plenty of silver, true—only right now, silver is the very last thing I lack.”

Afraid to touch the box, Qiao Mengrong asked carefully, “Are you refusing to help me, Lord Attendant?”

Liang Shicheng shoved the box straight into her arms. “Help? Oh, I’ll help—depends on whether Princess Consort Yi is willing to give me what I want.”

“What… what does the Lord Attendant want?”

“I may not be a real man, yet I still enjoy women—married women most of all. If Princess Consort Yi keeps me happy for one afternoon, I’ll do whatever you ask.”
