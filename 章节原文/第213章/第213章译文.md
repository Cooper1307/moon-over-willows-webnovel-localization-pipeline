## Chapter 213: He Rides to Bianliang, She Visits Her Maiden Home

Beneath the Divine Willow, Shen Jianchen sat still, replaying his meeting with Qianli Xun at Zuiyun Tower. He had laid his heart bare to her: even married to Mengrong, he would stay chaste for the woman he loved.

Right or wrong in other people's eyes did not matter; what mattered was his own conscience. He had always seen Mengrong as a sister, and what kind of older brother could bring himself to lay hands on his own sister?

Dusk settled and the moon climbed into the sky. Seated on the boulder with his back against the willow's trunk, Shen Jianchen listened to the truest voice inside him, and it said the same thing: he could not let Qianli Xun go.

In silence, he spoke to the tree. "Divine Willow, the nun up at the nunnery told me to follow fate instead of clinging to it. I'll follow this fate and set off for Bianliang tomorrow to marry Mengrong, yet I still can't let Qianli Xun go. Even with both of us married, I still want her. Tell me what I'm supposed to do."

A gust of wind shook loose a red silk ribbon from the upper branches. It drifted through the air and brushed his cheek before landing at his feet. Opening his eyes, he picked it up and smoothed it out. Two names stood inked across the top: Chu Linfeng and Yun Qingwan—refined names that spoke of a cultured young couple.

Below the names ran a couplet: "No water's wide enough when you have crossed the sea; / No cloud is beautiful but that which crowns the peak." [TN: Translated by Xu Yuanchong]

Shen Jianchen knew those famous lines by Tang poet Yuan Zhen from "Thinking of My Dear Departed." They cut straight to his own heart. Was the Divine Willow urging him not to give up?

Further down the ribbon sat a quatrain titled "Longings in Sorrow":

"Gulls and mandarin ducks swim in the same pond, / Everyone knows they don't belong together. / The Lord of the East has failed as master of flowers, / Why must we be intertwined branches all our lives?" [TN: Translated by Ronald Egan]

Those four lines carried Shen Jianchen straight back to a memory before his transmigration.

One day, he had brought his disciples to Qianli Fist Manor for a scheduled spar, only to walk in on Sun Heng proposing to Qianli Xun. Sun Heng was a textbook rich second-generation heir—wealthier than Shen Jianchen, young, handsome, and rumored to have dated several actresses.

Outside the manor gates, Sun Heng had arranged roses into a giant red heart with a crimson velvet box at the center holding a diamond ring whose size screamed astronomical price.

Sun Heng bellowed toward the doors, "Qianli Xun! If you don't agree to marry me today, I'm not budging!"

His entourage took up the chant: "Marry him! Marry him!"

Passersby crowded in to gawk. Shen Jianchen had no idea how Qianli Xun had crossed paths with a tycoon like this, and watching the spectacle drained every ounce of confidence from his chest.

A disciple fretted beside him. "Master, with competition like that, you don't stand a chance."

Nervous inside, Shen Jianchen kept his chin up. "Aside from money, how does Sun Heng beat me at anything? Qianli Xun doesn't even look twice at me. Why would she want him?"

Qianli Xun strode out of Qianli Fist Manor with her students at her heels, unmoved by the roses and wearing a look of pure disdain. "Sun Heng, did you forget your meds? I've met you twice in my whole life and you show up proposing? You think I'm some cheap pushover who'll say yes just because you threw down a bunch of flowers?"

"It's precisely because you aren't a pushover that I'm proposing," Sun Heng declared.

Qianli Xun did not buy a word of it. Wanting him gone fast, she set her terms. "You want my yes? Either fight me a round, or appreciate a poem. Pick one."

"With your skills, I'd never beat you in a fight," Sun Heng admitted. "I'll take the poem."

Her disciple Er'gang pulled out a sheet of paper and recited Zhu Shuzhen's "Longings in Sorrow": "Gulls and mandarin ducks swim in the same pond, / Everyone knows they don't belong together. / The Lord of the East has failed as master of flowers, / Why must we be intertwined branches all our lives?"

While Er'gang read, Sun Heng looked it up on his phone. The moment Er'gang finished, Sun Heng announced, "That's by the Song poetess Zhu Shuzhen—a lament about marrying the wrong man. Am I right?"

Qianli Xun grinned. "Spot on. Now that you know what it means, sweep up your roses, pack up your ring, and give my dojo back some peace and quiet."

Sun Heng blinked in confusion. "Wait—didn't you say if I picked one condition, you'd say yes?"

Unable to hold back a second longer, Shen Jianchen stepped out of the crowd and addressed him loud and clear. "Still don't get it? Qianli Xun is saying you're the gull and she's the mandarin duck—stuck in the same pond, completely mismatched. Even the Lord of the East, the god of spring, wants nothing to do with your roses, so why would he play matchmaker for you two? She just turned you down with a poem. Couldn't be clearer. Pack it up and leave."

Sun Heng fumed, yet dared not throw a punch. This was Qianli Fist Manor, and the man lecturing him was the heir to Shen Fist; he stood no chance in a brawl. He ordered his men to grab the ring, stomp the roses to pulp, and stormed off.

After Sun Heng left, Qianli Xun gave Shen Jianchen an approving nod. "Your explanation was a bit stretched, yet you got the gist right. Thanks for chasing him off."

It was the first time Qianli Xun had ever thanked him, and he had never forgotten it. That day had also sealed his respect for her; how many women in the world could shrug off a billionaire heir like Sun Heng without blinking?

Back beneath the Divine Willow, Shen Jianchen held the ribbon and murmured, "Looks like this couple shares our fate—hearts set on someone they can't be with, pinning their last hope on a strip of silk. Let me tie it back up for them. Maybe the Divine Willow will lend them a hand."

He stood, knotted the ribbon onto a low willow branch, and, judging that Miao Zhiyu and Bai Meiruo must have left by now, walked back along the pond toward Youlin Retreat.

Bai Feng trailed behind, debating what fun trinkets to buy for Kuqiao in Bianliang.

At daybreak, two carriages rolled out of Youlin Retreat and left Lin'an on the road to Bianliang.

Shen Jianchen had told none of the Lin'an officials his departure hour. He wanted to ride back to the capital without fanfare and meet his fate in peace. His mood was bleak enough without an audience; slipping out spared everyone the bother.

Qiao Mengrong's wound had not healed all the way, yet joy put fresh color in her cheeks, leaving her radiant compared with the day before.

That same morning, a carriage departed Chen Manor with Qinggua and Kuqiao walking alongside.

Riding in the same carriage with Chen Haoyu for the first time left Qianli Xun stiff and out of sorts. Chen Haoyu, by contrast, slumped back and started snoring the instant he climbed aboard.

Listening to his snores filled Qianli Xun with self-pity and sharper sympathy for Zhu Shuzhen. Four lines popped into her head and slipped off her tongue: "Gulls and mandarin ducks swim in the same pond, / Everyone knows they don't belong together. / The Lord of the East has failed as master of flowers, / Why must we be intertwined branches all our lives?"

That roused Chen Haoyu, who had only been faking sleep to avoid talking to her. He cracked his eyes open with a scowl. "Can't you act normal? We're just making a return visit and you're reciting poetry? You ruined my nap!"

Shaking off her gloom, Qianli Xun decided to chat instead. "Hey, Chen Haoyu, what sort of woman do you actually like?"

He shot her a mocking grin. "What, fishing for my tastes so you can mold yourself to fit and make me fall for you?"

"Mold my foot! Can't you talk like a normal person?"

Chen Haoyu sat up straight and grinned. "No harm telling you. I like untouched maidens, so divorced women like you can step to the back of the line. She has to be pretty—you pass on that count. Most important, she needs a sweet, melting voice and a gentle temper. Someone coarse like you who starts swinging fists at the drop of a hat doesn't even make my list."

Qianli Xun did not take offense; she liked him no better. "As your primary wife, I'm willing to let you take a concubine or two. Want me to scout around for someone matching your tastes?"

Her real aim was practical: tie Chen Haoyu down at home with women he liked so he would quit gallivanting outside, and maybe the Chen elders would stop nagging her to whip their wayward boy into shape.

Chen Haoyu squinted at her. "You're being this generous? What's the catch?"

"No catch," Qianli Xun said with a straight face. "Just looking out for all of us. As your primary wife, it's my duty to think of your comfort. Why not buy Chun Jinmo's freedom and bring her home? Beats running to Chunxiang Brothel and giving folks something to gossip about."

He smirked again. "You really aren't like other women. Did watching Lin Jue haul courtesans home lower your standards so much that I look like a prize by comparison, making you want a real marriage with me? Guess I owe Lin Jue a big thank-you."

Qianli Xun flared up. "Talk more trash and see if I don't deck you!"

"A couple of sentences and the mask slips right off," Chen Haoyu gloated. "Let me tell you, I'm not done playing around. I'll think about concubines once I've had my fun. Besides, I don't like Chun Jinmo enough to take her as a concubine—she's just someone to pass time with when I'm bored. Mind your own business and quit fretting over mine."

Qianli Xun itched to thrash him on the spot, yet Zhu Manor was almost in sight, so she held back. "You're pure scum!"

"So what if I am? Look at you, the celebrated Poetess of Jiangnan, and you still ended up married to scum like me! Hmph. Seems to me you're fated to marry brothel-hoppers for life. Accept your fate, Zhu Shuzhen!" Chen Haoyu leaned back against the carriage wall, grinning ear to ear.

That push did it. Qianli Xun threw a punch, yet a split second of hesitation slowed her arm, letting him duck clear.

"Hit me and I'll tattle to my father-in-law the second we arrive," he crowed. "Let's see him bring out the Zhu family rules on you."

Qianli Xun pulled back her fist. "Don't think I have no way to handle you. Today's the return visit, and I won't upset my mother. You get a pass for now. Once we're back at Chen Manor, I've got plenty of ways to deal with you."

Chen Haoyu let out a huff and held his tongue as the carriage pulled up outside the side gate of Zhu Manor.

Kuqiao called from outside, "Miss, Young Master, we're here. Please step down."

Qianli Xun hopped out first, meeting her brother and sister-in-law waiting at the gate. "Brother, Sister-in-law."

"Married life in the Chen household agrees with you, Shuzhen," Chen Wenxin praised. "Your complexion looks even brighter."

Climbing down behind her, Chen Haoyu overheard his sister and chimed in, "Of course she looks great—she spent the whole ride driving me up the wall."

Chen Wenxin marched right up and pinched her brother's ear. "Spout more nonsense and I'll twist this ear right off! Shuzhen is educated and refined; why would she give you grief? You must have been provoking her nonstop, am I right?"

"Ow, ow, ow!" Chen Haoyu yelped. "What a miserable life—married to a tigress and stuck with a fierce older sister! Brother-in-law, aren't you going to save me?"

Watching her sister-in-law handle Chen Haoyu gave Qianli Xun pure delight.

"All right, Wenxin," Zhu Jingyuan interceded. "Haoyu came along for Shuzhen's return visit today. Let go of his ear."

Chen Wenxin released him. "For your brother-in-law's sake, I'll let you off this once. Behave yourself today, understand?"

"Understood," Chen Haoyu answered like a scolded puppy.

Seeing him turn meek amazed Qianli Xun. Her sister-in-law's ear-pinch worked better than her own fists.

The return visit ran its usual dull course: paying respects to the elders, trading small talk, and sitting through a banquet where Chen Haoyu met every senior member of the Zhu clan. To his credit, he did not embarrass the Chen family, bowing properly to every last one.

After the feast dispersed, Chen Haoyu stayed in the front hall chatting with his father-in-law and brother-in-law, while Qianli Xun talked domestic matters with Chen Wenxin and Madam Xiang.

Midway through, a prefectural official called on Prefect Zhu. They spoke in the outer hall for a few moments before the visitor excused himself.

Returning to the inner room, Zhu Xiyan remarked, "Prince Yi set off for Bianliang first thing this morning without telling a soul. I had planned to lead the city officials to see him off, yet we never got the chance."

Remembering his promise to hand Zhu Shuzhen over to Prince Yi, Chen Haoyu worried the prince might not come back. "Father-in-law, will Prince Yi return to Lin'an?"

"Of course," Zhu Xiyan said. "He only went back to Bianliang to hold his wedding."

Zhu Jingyuan filled in the rest. "His Majesty's decree arrived on your wedding day, moving up the marriage between Prince Yi and Princess Jiahe and ordering them back to Bianliang at once."

Chen Haoyu sighed to himself. Even an imperial prince could not choose his own bride with an emperor for a father. Compared with that, his own lot was not bad—especially since he had the foresight to sign a contract with his tigress.

Glancing at Zhu Xiyan's beaming face, Chen Haoyu grumbled inside: You played matchmaker blind, ruining my life and your own daughter's—what's there to grin about?

"Haoyu," Zhu Xiyan said, "tell your father when you get home that once Prince Yi returns from Bianliang, I will host a wedding banquet with all the officials of Lin'an. Have him prepare a generous gift and attend."

Choking back his private complaints, Chen Haoyu answered dutifully, "Yes, sir."
