## Chapter 345: Longing Turned Song, A New Bond

The fighting in the north around the New Year had touched Jiangnan somewhat, and Huayun Pavilion felt the ripple too. Its regular crowd had all but halved.

The moment Chen Haoyu walked in, he named the singer he wanted: Xiangrong. An attendant showed him into a private room.

"Young Master Chen, wait a moment. Xiangrong will be with you shortly."

Once the man left, Qinggua filled his master's cup. "Sir, they say Xiangrong is a fine-looking girl."

Chen Haoyu said nothing. He lifted the cup and drained it.

Qinggua was pouring a second when Xiangrong came in. "What would you like me to sing, Young Master Chen?"

"Anything written by my wife."

Xiangrong had heard what had happened to Youqi Jushi in Yanjing and had wept over it in private. One look at the sorrow and emptiness on his face told her how much he was hurting.

She sat down at the zither. Slim fingers touched the strings, and she sang: *"Slanting winds and fine rain bring a spring chill. / Before the wine cup, / I recall old joys. / Once beside the pear blossoms, / Lonely tears streamed down..."* [TN: Translator's rendering]

Her voice was lovely to hear, and the music let him feel the words all the more. He drank as he listened, and the past came back to him...

She sang five songs in a row. He showed no sign of wanting her to stop, so she lifted her fingers from the strings. "If you enjoy them, Young Master Chen, come again tomorrow."

Chen Haoyu stared at her, at a loss. "That's it? My wife wrote so many poems. Why stop at five? You can't tell me those are the only ones you know."

Xiangrong told him the truth. "I can sing every poem Youqi Jushi ever wrote. Isn't it dull to finish all the good things at once? Besides, Huayun Pavilion has a rule. I can't sing more than five songs in a single day."

He could not keep feeling that his wife was still beside him, and disappointment carried a thread of grief. "All right. I'll come tomorrow."

It touched Xiangrong. "Young Master Chen, your love for Youqi Jushi moves me and makes me envy her too. I'll break the rule and sing one more for you."

Chen Haoyu had half risen; he settled back down, his grief tinged with sudden light. "My thanks."

Xiangrong said, "This next one is a new ci by Youqi Jushi, the *Circle Ci*..."

"New?"Hope showed on Chen Haoyu's face.

Xiangrong guessed he could not bear to admit Youqi Jushi was dead. That was why he asked.

"She wrote it in Bianliang. They say His Majesty spared her life because of that ci."

Chen Haoyu's face fell. "It's been over two years. Why tell me she wrote it recently, just to humor me?"

"I said that because I won't admit she's gone from us. To my mind, she wrote that ci for you."

"For me?"

Xiangrong nodded. "Yes. Let me sing it for you now."

Her fingers moved over the strings again. "*I wish to send my longing, yet find no way to send it, so I draw these circles in its stead. / The words lie outside the circle; my heart lies inside the circle. / A single circle is me; a double circle is you...*" [TN: Translator's rendering]

Chen Haoyu knew only that Prince Yi's kneeling had saved his wife's life. He had no idea she had written this too. He could feel her longing in every line. His eyes blurred with tears, and he berated himself for never caring about literary matters. Two years had passed before he learned what was in her heart.

When the song ended, he asked, "Could you write the words down for me? I'd like to take them home and savor them properly."

"Wait here, Young Master Chen." Xiangrong got up and went to a table with paper and brush, then set to work.

When she had finished, she had a maid carry the sheet over to him.

He took it. Apart from the three characters of the title, *Circle Ci*, the page held nothing but circles and dots.

"You were singing words just now, Xiangrong. Why fob me off with circles and dots?"

"You see, Young Master Chen, this is the ci as it was written. What I sang was only Youqi Jushi's explanation of the circles and dots."

He looked again at the marks on the page, and understanding slowly dawned. A smile broke through. "Thank you, Xiangrong. I'll take my leave."

In the carriage, Chen Haoyu held the *Circle Ci* in both hands. *Wife,* he thought, *you must have known I wouldn't understand the words, so you drew circles and dots to let me feel what you meant. I feel it now. The Circle Ci is the last thing you left me. I'll keep it safe.*

Qinggua had no heart for his master's thoughts. He lifted a corner of the thick carriage curtain and watched the street. By the roadside stood Amei, a child of a year and more in her arms, worry written all over her face.

He dropped the curtain and asked, "Sir, Amei's on the street with a child. She looks like she's in trouble. Mind if I go down and ask?"

Chen Haoyu was still lost among his circles and dots. He nodded without thinking.

Qinggua told the driver to pull over, jumped down, and called out to her. "What are you doing here? Whose child is that?"

"I don't know whose, sir," Amei said. "He'd only just learned to walk. He wandered into the street with no grownup watching. I picked him up and waited here. Maybe his family will come looking."

Qinggua studied the boy. "I've seen him somewhere before."

"Think hard," Amei urged.

He couldn't place him, then spotted a longevity lock hanging on the boy's chest. He turned it over without thinking. On the back was the character *Jiang*. It came to him. "That's it. He's Master Jiang's boy. Sir brought me to the full-month banquet last year and I played with him. I remember now. No wonder he looked familiar."

"So he's Master Jiang's. Do you know which way his house is?"

Qinggua pointed. "Straight down this street. Take the second turning north, and when you see the words Jiang Manor, you're there."

Amei did not even stop to thank him. She carried the boy off at once.

Qinggua climbed back into the carriage. The paper had gone from his master's hands, folded away into his robe, he guessed. He settled in his seat and the carriage moved on down the street.

His master had retreated into his own world and Qinggua did not like to disturb him, so he shut his eyes and rested.

After a while, a noisy crowd came from outside. Qinggua opened his eyes and peered through the curtain. A sign hung outside a steamed-bun shop: *Qianli buns, Qianli congee.* Beggars had gathered to collect their buns and congee.

He let the curtain fall and remarked idly, "Ever since Prince Yi gave up his military post, there are more beggars in Lin'an every day. He's a good man. Back in Lin'an, he started handing out free buns and congee again. I can't see why he calls them 'Qianli buns' and 'Qianli congee,' though."

At the mention of Prince Yi, Chen Haoyu roused a little. "How do you know it's Prince Yi doing this?"

"You shut yourself up in Moxiang Courtyard all day, sir, so you miss things. I hear he gives the bun shop enough silver each month to hand out a set number of buns and congee to beggars every day. Same idea as the Chen family's old congee stalls."

"Do you know where he lives?"

"Kuqiao says Zhuli Village. I can't work out why a prince would live in the same village as common farmers. What's he after?"

Qinggua did not know, but Chen Haoyu did. The bamboo grove was his wife's property. Prince Yi lived there because he could not forget her.

The jealousy and resentment he had felt toward Prince Yi were gone. His wife was gone too; one more person missing her was a good thing.

After Chun Jinmo died in childbirth, Master Jiang, thinking of the child, had bought a house in Lin'an and hired a wet nurse to look after him.

One day the nurse took the boy out. She carried him at first, but he was restless and wanted to walk, so she took his little hand. They stopped at a pouch stall. She liked the look of the pouches and led him closer. She let go for a moment to look, and the boy walked off into the crowd on his own.

When she put the pouch down and found the boy gone, she searched the whole street frantically and could not find him. She had to go home and tell Master Jiang.

Master Jiang had no time to punish her. He sent men out to search the streets at once.

Before they returned, a servant came to report that a young woman had brought the little master back. She was at the gate.

Master Jiang's heart leapt. He went out and saw the girl holding his son, no doubt about it.

The nurse hurried forward and took the child from Amei's arms. "Thank you, miss."

Master Jiang looked the girl over carefully. Her face was like a peach blossom, and her maid's clothes could not hide her lively beauty.

"Thank you for saving my boy. May I ask your name?"

"Amei."

"A name to fit you. I feel I've seen your face before. Where was it?"

"You have seen me. I once beat up some of Master Jiang's men."

It dawned on him. The only woman who had ever fought his men was a servant of Youqi Jushi's. "You're Youqi Jushi's maid?"

"I am."

"No wonder you seem different."

He told a servant to bring a bag of silver and pressed it into her hands himself. "A small token. Please accept it."

"It was nothing. Why give me silver, Master Jiang? If I took it, it would look as if I came for the money." She refused, smiled, and walked away.

Master Jiang stood with the purse in his hand, watching her go. "Beautiful face, kind heart, and skilled with her fists," he murmured. "This is a marriage sent from heaven."

The next day he went to Chen Manor to ask for her hand. Chen Jianglin and Yao Qingyan would not decide lightly and asked their son. Chen Haoyu dared not nod either. Amei had come with his wife, and her wishes had to be respected.

Amei thought it over. As a maid, marrying Master Jiang as his proper wife was no bad thing. She consented.

With that settled, Master Jiang hurried home, planning the wedding for the following month.

For half a month Chen Haoyu spent every day at Huayun Pavilion listening to Xiangrong sing. Chen Jianglin and Yao Qingyan grew worried. After dark, they sent for Qinggua.

Chen Jianglin's face was stern. "I hear you've been taking the boy to Huayun Pavilion a lot lately. Has he slipped back into his old ways?"

"No, sir, you've got it wrong. Xiangrong sings Youqi Jushi's poems. That's why he goes to hear her every day."

Yao Qingyan asked, "That's the only reason?"

"That's the only reason," Qinggua said firmly.

The two of them breathed easier and told him he could go.

Yao Qingyan said, "They say the girls at Huayun Pavilion only meet scholars and men of letters. All they do is exchange verses and show their talents. Her background is clean enough..."

Chen Jianglin did not follow. "What are you getting at?"

"Ever since Shuzhen died, Haoyu has had no life in him. Now he's willing to leave the house and be around another woman. That's progress, and it proves Xiangrong can reach him. Why don't you go to Huayun Pavilion and buy out her contract? Put her beside Haoyu. With any luck he'll come out of this grief and start living again."

"No, no. Even if she only sells her songs and not her body, plenty of men have admired her. And Haoyu only goes because she can sing Shuzhen's poems, not for her own sake."

"Tell me this: which matters more, your son or your old-fashioned ideas?"

"My son, of course."

"Then there's nothing to decide. We can't let him go on like this. He's the heir to the Chen family business. He has duties. He keeps going to Xiangrong because of Shuzhen, and I keep thinking this girl is the one who can bring him back."

Chen Jianglin stopped arguing. "Have it your way, wife. I'll go buy out Xiangrong's contract tomorrow. But you must grant me one condition."

"What condition?"

"Put her in Haoyu's house as a concubine first. Later, when a girl from a good family comes along, we'll talk about taking a proper wife again."

Yao Qingyan nodded. "That's what I had in mind."

Chen Jianglin sighed. "The men of our family have never taken concubines. Haoyu will be the first."

"Plans can't keep up with changes. We have no choice. Finish your tea and get some sleep. Tomorrow will be busy."

The next day, Chen Haoyu had finished lunch and was about to go back to Huayun Pavilion when Xiangrong appeared as he stepped out of Moxiang Courtyard.

He noticed the maid carrying a zither behind her. "I didn't send for you yesterday. Why come to the house today?"

Xiangrong curtsied. "Old Master Chen has bought out my contract. He has asked me to stay and serve you, Young Master Chen."

"Nonsense!"The word burst out. He walked past her, meaning to ask his parents what was going on.

Xiangrong called after him. "There's no need to question your elders. What's done is done. I came to Chen Manor of my own free will, to look after you for Shuzhen. It settles the debt I owed her friendship."

He stopped. "My heart holds only her. I'm afraid there's no room for anyone else."

Xiangrong came up to him. "I know. I don't ask you to forget Shuzhen. I only hope that when I sing each day, you'll remember her with me."

He was moved. "Moxiang Courtyard was her place. It isn't right for you. I'll find you a courtyard of your own."

"Anything you say," Xiangrong said, untroubled.

Xiangrong moved into her courtyard. It was small but wonderfully warm. She thanked Shuzhen from the bottom of her heart. Because of Shuzhen's poems, she had quietly fallen in love with Chen Haoyu. Because of Shuzhen, someone had bought out her contract, and she had a normal life now.

She looked around the room and said softly, "Don't blame me, Shuzhen. I'll look after him for you, and love him well for you."

Every day Chen Haoyu went to her courtyard to hear her sing. Music filled Chen Manor again, and the house that had been so quiet came back to life.

One night he woke from a dream of his wife and could not sleep again. He dressed, drank alone until the wine took hold, and the drink only sharpened his longing. He decided to go to Xiangrong for a song. He had heard her that day, but now he wanted it more than he had ever wanted anything.

He pushed open the door of Suxin Thatched Hall, unsteady on his feet, left Moxiang Courtyard alone, and reached Xiangrong's courtyard. He hammered on the door with all the strength the wine gave him.

The maid opened up and saw who it was. "Sir, why come at this hour?"

"I've come... to hear a song."

"You heard one today, sir."

"Less talk. Get Xiangrong... up... to sing."

From inside came her voice. "Let him in."

The maid opened the inner door and Chen Haoyu staggered into the room. Xiangrong was already at the zither, in her sleeping clothes, her long hair loose, her face bare of powder and paint. She looked like a lotus bud about to open.

He sat down at the table he always used, still warm with drink. "Sing *Jianzi Mulanhua: Spring Resentment*."

Xiangrong plucked the strings and sang: *"I walk alone, sit alone, sing alone, drink alone, and sleep alone..."* [TN: Translated by Tony Barnstone and Chou Ping]

The lines carried him back to the Eastern Poetry Society. He saw his wife's every smile and every small movement there...

When the song was over, he said to himself, "Wife, I'm here. You'll never be lonely again..."

He raised his eyes. Xiangrong had come to stand beside him, looking at him with all her heart. "You won't be lonely either. I'm here with you. I want to be your wife."

"My wife?"

"You can think of me as your wife."

"My wife?"

The wine made it seem that his wife stood right before him. He remembered kissing her in the prison cell, and he leaned in and kissed Xiangrong...
