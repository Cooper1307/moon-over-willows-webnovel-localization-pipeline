## Chapter 316: Unsettled Mind After Miscarriage

Xilan sprinted straight to Prince Yi’s courtyard, burst into the room, and dropped to her knees sobbing. “Wuu! Your Highness! Princess Consort Yi is bleeding heavily down below! Hurry and look at her!”

Without saying a word, Shen Jianchen dashed out the door, colliding with Bai Feng on his way back. “Ride into town and fetch a physician—now!”

Seeing the prince so frantic, Bai Feng asked no questions, sprinted straight out of Youlin Retreat, hitched a carriage, and raced for town.

Reaching Mengrong’s courtyard gate, Shen Jianchen remembered Matron Zou knew a bit of medicine and called over his shoulder to Xilan, “Go fetch Matron Zou right away!”

Xilan gasped out a “Yes!” and bolted off.

Stepping into the bedchamber, Shen Jianchen saw Mengrong lying pale as paper with tears rolling down her face.

Sitting down on the edge of the bed, he gripped her hand warmly. “A physician will be here any moment. I’m right here—don’t be afraid.”

The abdominal cramps had eased, yet all that blood terrified Qiao Mengrong. She clung to him. “Brother… am I going to die?”

“No,” Shen Jianchen soothed her. “With me here, nothing’s going to happen to you.”

Matron Zou rushed in, lifted the quilt, and saw the dark puddle soaking the mattress pad—her heart sank. Gently taking the princess consort’s wrist from Prince Yi, she pressed her fingers to the pulse for a long moment before laying her hand back down with a grim expression.

Shen Jianchen asked, “Can you tell what’s wrong, Matron?”

Matron Zou sighed. “I only know the basics. We’ll have to wait for the physician to be certain.”

Sensing Matron Zou was holding back to spare her feelings, Qiao Mengrong said weakly, “Speak plainly, Matron. Don’t worry about upsetting me—once the physician gets here, I’ll hear the truth anyway. Whatever it is, I can take it.”

Only then did Matron Zou speak out. “It’s my fault for getting careless lately and not checking your pulse regularly. I had no idea you were with child…”

“With child?” A spark of joy flickered through Qiao Mengrong’s tear-rimmed eyes. “I’m pregnant? That’s wonderful news—why couldn’t you bring yourself to say it, Matron?”

“Let me finish, Your Highness.” Matron Zou kept her voice gentle. “After months of taking medicine, getting pregnant was indeed a blessing. Yet somehow you’ve miscarried—that’s why you’re bleeding so heavily. When the physician arrives, let him examine you properly.”

The fragile smile vanished instantly. Qiao Mengrong clapped both hands around her head, murmuring as if to herself, “How could this happen? How could this happen? How could this happen…”

Shen Jianchen pulled Mengrong into his arms. “It’s going to be alright. Once the physician prescribes tonics to nurse your body back to health, we can still have children.”

His gentle words lit a flicker of hope again. Lowering her hands, she asked, “Really?”

Shen Jianchen nodded with certainty. “Really. When has Brother ever lied to you?”

Tears spilled fresh as Qiao Mengrong threw her arms around him and wept aloud. “Wuu…!”

Kneeling on the floor, Xilan broke down sobbing too. “Wuu! Your Highness, it’s all my fault for not taking proper care of her! Wuu…”

Matron Zou dabbed her eyes beside them. “Enough kneeling and crying—hurry and fetch clean cloths to slip under Her Highness!”

Scrambling up, Xilan grabbed fresh cloths and had just tucked them beneath her mistress when Bai Feng ushered in the physician carrying his medicine chest.

Shen Jianchen yielded his spot beside the bed while Xilan set down a stool. Taking a seat, the physician felt her pulse—and confirmed every word Matron Zou had guessed.

Hearing the physician’s verdict made Qiao Mengrong’s heart ache unbearably. To finally carry her brother’s child only to lose it like this—how could Heaven be so cruel?

Pulling a vial of pills from his chest, the physician handed it to Xilan. “Give Her Highness one pill with warm water; it will stop the bleeding.”

Xilan took the vial, poured warm water, and helped her mistress swallow the medicine.

Moving to the table to write a prescription, the physician explained, “This formula clears any lingering residue inside the womb. Take it for three days, and I’ll return to check her pulse again.”

Once Mengrong swallowed the pill, Shen Jianchen sat back down by the bed and asked softly, “Feeling any better?”

Qiao Mengrong nodded. “Much better.”

Picking up a handkerchief from the bedside, Shen Jianchen gently wiped away her tears.

Blaming herself for being too foolish even to notice she was expecting, Qiao Mengrong couldn’t understand why the baby had slipped away so easily.

Turning toward the physician writing at the table, she asked, “Doctor, can you tell how far along I was?”

Finishing the prescription, the physician handed it to Matron Zou. “Have this filled—twice daily, same as before.”

Matron Zou took the slip and hurried out to send someone for the herbs.

Only then did the physician reply, “To answer Princess Consort Yi: judging from your pulse, you were a little over one month along.”

“Was something wrong with my body that made me lose the child?”

“No, Your Highness. After taking my earlier prescriptions, your internal cold had cleared; by rights, once conceived, a pregnancy should hold steady. My guess is that, unaware you were expecting, you engaged in overly violent exertion, which dislodged the fetus.”

Yesterday’s nightmare crashed straight back over Qiao Mengrong: dancing naked at Liu’an Villa before Imperial Attendant Liang, then screaming in agony and thrashing under his needle one stab after another—no wonder she had lost the baby!

Guilt, terror, and torment snapped something inside her. Clasping her head again, she slid down off her propped pillows, turned her back to her brother, and wept uncontrollably, chanting over and over, “I hate myself! I hate myself! I hate myself…!”

Alarmed, Shen Jianchen asked, “Mengrong, what’s wrong?”

She ignored him completely, lost in her loop: “I hate myself! I hate myself…!”

When the physician stepped forward to feel her pulse again, Qiao Mengrong kept her arms locked tight around her head.

Coaxing her gently, Shen Jianchen pried her hands away and held her wrist firm so she couldn’t yank it back.

The physician rested his fingers on her pulse for a moment, then drew back. “Her Highness’s mind is severely rattled. Keep any shocks or agitation away from her for the foreseeable future, or she risks losing her wits completely.”

Shen Jianchen stared in shock. “How could it be that serious?”

Distracted by surprise, his grip loosened—and Mengrong wrenched her wrist free, clutching her head and muttering once more.

Unable to say for certain, the physician offered his best guess. “Her Highness must have suffered a severe fright, followed right away by losing the child—it’s more grief than her heart can bear. I can mend women’s physical ailments well enough, though disorders of the mind lie outside my skill.”

“A fright?” Shen Jianchen couldn’t fathom how—Mengrong had left yesterday morning in fine spirits. Could something have terrified her at that ladies’ banquet?

“Xilan!”

Xilan dropped straight to her knees, guessing he was about to question her about yesterday and too terrified even to keep crying.

“Tell me—what frightened Princess Consort Yi while she was out yesterday?”

Dare she breathe the truth and risk His Highness casting her mistress aside? Xilan stammered, “N-nothing frightened her out there! Maybe… maybe it was a nightmare last night! Yes, yes—Her Highness woke up drenched in cold sweat from a terrible nightmare!”

Shen Jianchen couldn’t quite buy it, yet saw no point pressing further right now. “Get up and look after your mistress properly.”

Noticing the physician lingering as if holding something back, Shen Jianchen asked, “Is there something else, Doctor?”

The physician bowed. “A word outside, Your Highness.”

Stepping outside the door, the physician spoke low. “I dared not say it in front of Her Highness for fear of shocking her further, yet you deserve the full truth. Conceiving this child was already hard-won; now that she’s miscarried her first pregnancy, it will be near impossible for her to conceive again.”

A heavy ache settled in Shen Jianchen’s chest—purely out of pity for Mengrong. She had nursed Zhao Pu hand and foot, only for Zhao Pu to vanish from the world; she married his reborn body, yet his heart belonged to another; she finally conceived, only to lose the baby and crumble under some sudden trauma.

Right then Shen Jianchen resolved that, no matter what became of Mengrong, he would care for her and never abandon her.

“You were thoughtful to spare her. Thank you. Tell me, which physician in Lin’an excels at treating ailments of the mind?”

“A Physician Guo in the southern quarter specializes in such cases. Once Her Highness regains her strength, you can send for him.”

“Much obliged.” Calling toward the doorway, Shen Jianchen ordered, “Xilan, bring out the doctor’s chest.”

Xilan carried the chest outside, tear tracks still glistening on her cheeks.

Stepping back inside, Shen Jianchen found Mengrong still hugging her head, whispering, “I hate myself, I hate myself…”

Sitting beside her, he leaned over and wrapped both arms around her from behind. “Stop blaming yourself. You’ve done nothing wrong—every bit of this is on me.”

At his comforting voice, her muttering stopped. Lowering her hands, she turned and buried her face against his chest. “Promise me, Brother—stop loving her. Love only me.”

Desperate not to set her off again, Shen Jianchen hesitated a heartbeat, then slipped into the role of the real Zhao Pu. “I am Zhao Pu, and Zhao Pu has only ever loved Mengrong.”

She squeezed him tighter. “You aren’t lying to me, Brother?”

“Zhao Pu has never lied to Mengrong.” Seeing the mattress pad beneath her stained dark with blood, he added softly, “Let me carry you back to our room while Xilan changes the bedding here.”

Still soaking in his words, Qiao Mengrong nodded yes.

Drawing back the quilt, Shen Jianchen scooped her into his arms. Within three steps, memories of the needle punctures and sharp stabs flared across her skin; curling tight inside his embrace, she trembled slightly.

“N-no… no… I’m not going to Brother’s room… I want… I want to stay right here.”

Her fingers clutched at his tunic as if trying to anchor his feet in place.

Xilan had already walked back in to strip the blood-soaked bedding and lay fresh sheets. Hearing her mistress’s voice, she stopped mid-task and stepped forward. “Your Highness, why not let Her Highness stay here? She needs quiet to rest alone right now. Whenever you’re worried about her, you can come sit with her every day.”

Seeing the sense in that, Shen Jianchen set her gently back down. “Finish making up the bed quickly, clean Her Highness up, and change her into fresh clothes.”

Before long, Xilan had everything tidied and spotless.

Matron Zou carried in a tray bearing a steaming bowl of decoction. “Guard Bai doesn’t mess around—he rode full gallop to the nearest pharmacy in town and straight back, so I got the maids brewing this in no time.”

Xilan lifted the bowl from the tray and brought it to the bedside.

Wanting a private word with Matron Zou anyway, Shen Jianchen stepped aside to yield his spot.

While Xilan spoon-fed the medicine to Mengrong, Shen Jianchen caught Matron Zou’s eye and gestured toward the yard. Setting down the tray, Matron Zou followed him outside.

“What orders does Your Highness have that must be kept from Her Highness?”

Shen Jianchen let out a sigh. “The physician says Mengrong suffered a fright outside, and coupled with losing the child, her spirit is dangerously shaken. Any further shock could unhinge her mind.”

“What? How did it get this grave?”

“Yesterday she went out perfectly fine, then came home and shut herself in here refusing to come out. Something must have terrified her. Xilan won’t tell me the truth—find a moment in private to get the real story out of her.”

Matron Zou’s heart skipped a beat. Yesterday Princess Consort Yi had gone to see Imperial Attendant Liang on Prince Yi’s behalf—had something happened at Liu’an Villa?

“Understood. I’ll ask her for Your Highness.”

“Mengrong looks a bit calmer now. I’ll step out for a while so you have an opening to question Xilan.”

Leaving the small courtyard, Shen Jianchen left Matron Zou standing in thought, struggling to fathom how the princess consort could unravel overnight.

Walking back inside, she saw Xilan still spooning broth-dark medicine to Her Highness. Pale though she looked, she seemed no different from normal.

Feeding the final spoonful, Xilan dabbed the corners of her mistress’s mouth with a cloth, set the bowl aside, and eased her back onto the pillows.

Matron Zou stepped closer and tested gently, “How are you feeling now, Your Highness?”

Qiao Mengrong stared blankly, offering no answer at all.

Matron Zou tried again. “Your Highness, feeling any better?”

Only then did Qiao Mengrong turn her eyes toward her. “Did you say something, Matron?”

Fearing Her Highness would catch the tears brimming in her eyes, Matron Zou turned away fast. “N-nothing. Get some sleep and build back your strength, Your Highness.”

By the time she wiped her face dry and turned back, Princess Consort Yi had shut her eyes.

Matron Zou tugged Xilan’s sleeve and motioned her outside.

Stepping out, they shut the door behind them.

Matron Zou fixed Xilan with a stern glare. “Confess every word! What happened at Liu’an Villa when you went with Her Highness?”

Dropping straight to her knees, Xilan burst into tears. “Wuu… Matron, Princess Consort Yi forbade me to breathe a word! Wuu…”

Matron Zou hauled Xilan up off the dirt. “Quit wailing before you wake Her Highness! I already know all her business—telling me won’t hurt, and two heads are better than one.”

Carrying her mistress’s secret alone had crushed Xilan’s chest; sharing it felt like dropping half a mountain.

Choking back her sobs, she whispered, “Imperial Attendant Liang is an animal! He refused the silver notes and demanded Her Highness instead—even stabbed needles all over her skin! Standing outside, all I could hear were her bloodcurdling screams, and there wasn’t a thing I could do! She hid in this courtyard afterward terrified His Highness would find out. Who knew she’d miscarry today—yesterday’s ordeal must have caused it!”

Bitter regret washed over Matron Zou. “Blame me! Why did I ever track down Liang’s address for her, and back her going there in person?”

Xilan pleaded urgently, “I’ve told you everything, Matron—please, whatever you do, don’t tell His Highness! Leave Her Highness a shred of dignity.”

Torn between self-reproach and heartache for her mistress, Matron Zou resolved to keep the secret. “Go back in and watch over Her Highness attentively. I’ll go give His Highness a story.”

Xilan caught her arm. “You aren’t going to tell him the truth, are you?”

“Relax—not a single syllable. He asked me to look into it, so I owe him a believable explanation.”

“Oh—one more thing! When Her Highness moved into this courtyard yesterday, I lied to His Highness that she had a feminine discharge ailment. Don’t give me away.”

“Set your heart at ease. For His Highness’s sake and hers, I know what to say.” Matron Zou sighed, wiped her cheeks, and walked out of the courtyard.
