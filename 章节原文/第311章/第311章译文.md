## Chapter 311: The Heir Is Decided

Shock and disbelief froze every face in the hall.

Chen Haodong made one last desperate grab. “Your Highness must be mistaken—how could I ever do such a thing?!”

“Scarface already confessed everything, so drop the act.” Shen Jianchen held the silver note up before the room. “Fresh out of jail, Scarface collected a hundred and fifty taels from Chen Haodong. Admitting he covered it up for silver, he spilled every detail of the night of the third day of the third month last year. Clerk Han, read out the full record.”

“Yes, Your Highness.” Han Gu addressed the hall. “By Scarface’s confession, he fed Chen Haodong regular reports on Chen Haoyu’s doings for silver. On the third day of the third month last year, once Chen Haoyu left the Chens’ country residence, Scarface ran straight to Chen Haodong’s private tryst house nearby—the place he used to meet brothel courtesans. Hearing about the girl, Chen Haodong cooked up a foul plot: first he went back to town and had Butler Wang pull every servant away on the pretext of cleaning city properties, while Scarface sent the rest of his crew off and drugged the caretaker’s wine. After dark, Chen Haodong slipped in shrouded head to face in a black cloak, violated Chun Jinlan, and ordered Scarface to sell her to a Yangzhou brothel—then spread rumors that Chen Haoyu made a habit of selling abducted girls to brothels to smear his cousin’s name.”

Every last defense stripped away, Chen Haodong felt his legs go boneless beneath him.

Before he could sink, Chen Jiangshu lashed across his son’s face with a resounding crack. “Wretched beast! How dare you pull something this vile after all the years your uncle and I poured into raising you?!”

Sent sprawling across the flagstones, Chen Haodong watched what should have been his crowning day crash into the darkest hour of his life.

Chen Jiangshu dropped into a crouch clutching his head and sobbed aloud. “It’s all my fault! All my fault!”

Madam Bao threw herself to her knees before Prince Yi weeping. “Spare Haodong, Your Highness! I beg you!”

“This hundred and fifty taels will be donated to the poor in the Judicial Office’s name.” Tucking the bank note away, Shen Jianchen told Madam Bao, “The realm has laws, and every clan has rules. Chen Haodong broke Song law and must pay—only then can Chun Jinlan’s spirit rest. Chief Constable Wen, arrest him and bring him back to the Judicial Office.”

Wen Xin signaled constables to bind Chen Haodong in ropes and haul him upright.

Shen Jianchen cupped his hands to the hall. “Farewell, everyone!”

Even after Prince Yi marched out, Chen Haoyu stared stunned. “How could it be Brother Dong?”

“Never expected him either,” Qianli Xun sighed. “Took Prince Yi to turn the whole board upside down.”

“What do you mean?” Chen Haoyu blinked.

Too tired to explain, Qianli Xun watched Madam Bao crawl on her knees toward Old Madam Xia sobbing uncontrollably. “Mother! Save Haodong—please!”

Everything stood crystal clear to Old Madam Xia now. Narrowly dodging handing the clan empire to her eldest grandson was a blessing, yet seeing any Chen blood hauled off in ropes tore at her heart.

Eyes brimming, she announced, “What’s done can’t be undone. Once the Judicial Office sentences him, I’ll have Jianglin appeal to Prince Yi—no matter how much silver it takes, we’ll buy his life.”

Hearing that, Madam Bao fainted dead away. Qianli Xun darted forward and pinched the philtrum under her nose until she gasped awake shrieking “Haodong, my son!” Yao Qingyan signaled two maids to help her back to her quarters.

Bracing herself upright, Old Madam Xia addressed the room. “Unforeseen as today’s shock is, we started this selection and can’t leave it hanging. Settle the heir first, deal with the rest after. Two grandsons remain—who do we choose?”

Rising from the floor where he had wept, Chen Jiangshu spoke first. “Mother, pick Haoyu. Wild as he was, his heart has changed. Business skills can be taught—between Eldest Brother, Third Brother, and me, he’ll master it. And his wife Shuzhen has proven herself right before our eyes—nothing like those street rumors.”

Utter disillusionment drove Chen Jiangshu’s turnaround. He had backed petty maneuvering, never a capital crime pinned onto a cousin’s neck; remorse burned him for feeding his son’s ruthless greed since childhood.

Chen Jiangmu stood next. “Haofei is bent on court office and heads to Bianliang next spring for the palace exam—no time or head for trade, plus he’s still an unmarried lad. Pick Haoyu! Steward Qiu says he already outperforms veteran clerks and picks up bookkeeping fast—born for business.”

Old Madam Xia nodded and turned to the elders. “Uncle Lu, what say the clan?”

“Give us a minute to confer.” Chen Lu huddled with the other elders.

Qianli Xun nudged Chen Haoyu with a smirk. “No escaping your fate now.”

Still heavy-hearted over Brother Dong—still blaming himself for abducting Chun Jinlan in the first place—Chen Haoyu muttered, “The family heir falling onto my lap? Still can’t believe it.”

“Like they say: life loves plot twists.”

“True,” he sighed, then added, “Back then, if Brother Dong wanted a girl, one word to me and I’d have helped him—why sneak around and sell her off?”

Catching his wife’s razor glare, he backpedaled fast. “I meant *back then*, not now! With you, I’d never touch dirty business!”

“Think everyone sins out in the open like you? Plenty of folks wear saint masks by day and crawl through gutters by night.”

Finishing their huddle, Chen Lu spoke for the clan. “We’re unanimous: on paper Haofei fits best, yet his heart is set on civil service. Someone has to step into Jianglin’s shoes, so Haoyu it is. Let both brothers put the Chen clan first, whether in office or in trade.”

“Then Jianglin, draft a fresh appointment scroll,” Old Madam Xia decreed.

Never dreaming the mantle would land on his own son—and too weighed down by Haodong’s fall to celebrate—Chen Jianglin unrolled a blank scroll and brushed out the decree anew.

One by one, everyone pressed red thumbprints, silently praying no fresh thunderbolt would strike. When the final print dried without incident, a collective breath escaped the hall.

Scanning the document and rolling it tight, Chen Jianglin summoned his son. “Step forward, Haoyu.”

With no way out, Chen Haoyu stepped up ready to shoulder the weight.

“Take this,” Chen Jianglin said, holding out the scroll. “Once I pass on the Chen trade secrets, the entire house rests in your hands.”

Feather-light scroll, mountain-heavy burden. Chen Haoyu bowed low. “For our clan’s future, I accept this duty. I’ll hold myself to every rule of an heir, better myself every day, and give my all to the Chen name.”

Since two of those six rules spoke directly to the heir’s wife, Qianli Xun rose and curtsied to the elders. “As Chen Haoyu’s wife, I owe my pledge too. My natal family’s standing is clear; never mind who I was before, from this day on I’ll guard my words and deeds and run the inner household well so my husband can devote himself to the business without worry.”

Warm nods of approval swept the elders.

“Settled and sealed,” Old Madam Xia concluded. “Jiangmu, report the outcome to Prefect Zhu and the Lin’an Chamber of Commerce. Jianglin, handle Haodong—spend whatever silver it takes to spare his neck.”

“Yes, Mother!” both answered in unison.

Under Song law, Chen Haodong faced hanging, yet Chen wealth—working within legal redemption rules—bought his life at the price of permanent imprisonment. Devastated, Chen Jiangshu and Madam Bao both fell ill and wept anew every time they visited their broken son in jail.

Sunk to rock bottom, refusing any self-reflection, Chen Haodong blamed every ounce of his ruin on Prince Yi—and determined to drag the prince down into misery with him, sent word through a jailer demanding to see him.

Hearing the jailer’s message at the Judicial Office, Shen Jianchen figured it couldn’t hurt to hear him out. Just back from Zhuli Village after finishing the bamboo cottage, Bai Feng followed close behind as personal guard.

Pinching his nose against the prison stench, Bai Feng griped, “Your Highness shouldn’t set foot in a dump like this.” Rounding a corner into a noticeably cleaner block, he blinked. “How come these cells look so much nicer?”

“Got enough silver, you can live like this too,” Shen Jianchen joked.

“Hey—sounds like you’re wishing me behind bars!”

A jailer unlocked a cell revealing a disheveled figure sitting motionless on the bunk. Stepping in, Shen Jianchen asked, “What did you want to tell me, Chen Haodong?”

Remaining seated without a shred of deference—what was left to lose?—Chen Haodong sneered. “You wrecked my life, Your Highness. Can’t sleep easy in here unless I share a few little secrets about Princess Consort Yi.”

Shen Jianchen tensed. “What secrets?”

“Princess Consort Yi wants Haoyu’s wife dead...”

“Lies!” Bai Feng barked. “How dare you slander Her Highness?!”

Cold dread tightening his chest, Shen Jianchen raised a hand. “Let him talk.”

“I was Her Highness’s errand boy,” Chen Haodong smirked. “Hired Master Jiang for her—his thugs blew it. Approached Shao Escort Agency next—they turned me down. Then somehow she bought off Black-Fiend Tiger and had me hand over Chen Manor’s floor plan for that raid. You know the rest.” Watching Prince Yi’s face turn stony dark, he chuckled nasty. “Quick to lock me up, Your Highness—why not throw your own Princess Consort in a cell too? She’s got plenty of dirt on her hands!”

“Say another word and I’ll bust your jaw!” Bai Feng snapped.

Ignoring him, Chen Haodong pressed the knife in. “Failed three times, yet her malice runs deep—she won’t quit. The day she finally succeeds, will Your Highness sentence *her* to autumn beheading too?”

Unable to stomach another syllable, Shen Jianchen wheeled out of the cell as Chen Haodong’s raw laughter echoed behind him. Skipping the Judicial Office, he rode straight for Youlin Retreat.

Meanwhile, after months of sharing a bed with no sign of pregnancy, Qiao Mengrong had Matron Zou summon a skilled physician, who diagnosed a chill womb and wrote a three-month warming tonic guaranteed to bring a little prince.

Just as the physician departed, her brother strode in—thrilling her until she saw the thundercloud across his brow. “Home so early, Brother! Just saw a physician who says three months of tonic will give us a baby—wait, what’s wrong?”

Studying her sweet, gentle face, Shen Jianchen struggled to picture her plotting murder. “Xilan, wait outside. I need a private word with Her Highness.”

Sensing danger as Xilan shut the door, Qiao Mengrong asked timidly, “What is it, Brother?”

Locking eyes with her to read every flicker, he began, “Chen Haodong told me a few things about you in prison today...”

Knowing the game was up, Qiao Mengrong dropped straight to her knees in a flood of tears. “I know what you’re going to say, Brother, and knew you’d find out sooner or later! Hatred for Zhu Shuzhen blinded me into doing awful things—I regret it to death and swear I’ll never do it again! Thank goodness she’s safe; if anything had happened to her, I’d never have known peace!”

Watching her weep so piteously melted every harsh rebuke on Shen Jianchen’s tongue—turning the blame right back on himself for driving her to desperation. Stepping forward, he lifted her up. “Knowing you were wrong is enough. Never harbor thoughts like that again.”

“I’ll listen to you, Brother,” she sobbed, dabbing her cheeks.

Still, he laid down one steel warning. “If not for Youqi Jushi’s martial arts, she’d have died on Mount Tianmu. Had she died, as Judicial Investigator I would have dug straight to you—and enforced Song law without sparing kin.”
