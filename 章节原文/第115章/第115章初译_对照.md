第115章 茶肆喝茶听流言
## Chapter 115: Drinking Tea and Overhearing Gossip

经过一晚上休息，千里寻的心情并不比昨日好多少。
A full night's rest left Qianli Xun in no better spirits than yesterday.

大家如何议论她并不重要，唯一担心今后如何才能与文渊在一起。
How folk gossiped about her didn't matter; all that weighed on her was how she and Wenyuan could ever be together now.

穿越而来的时代，不是相爱之人靠自由恋爱就能走到一起的时代，父母若不同意，一切只会徒劳。
This era she had crossed into was not an age of free love—without parents' consent, romance ran straight into a brick wall.

流言不早不晚，偏偏在提亲之日被两家知晓，难道老天根本不想让她与文渊在一起？想到这些，千里寻更加痛恨流言。越痛恨，越想去亲耳听听，再亲眼目睹大家议论的样子。
Why had the rumors hit both families on the exact day of the proposal, not a day earlier or later? Did Heaven really mean to keep her and Wenyuan apart? The more she brooded, the fiercer her hatred for those rumors burned—and the stronger her urge grew to go hear and see the gossipers with her own eyes.

用过早饭，还不到与文渊约定的时间，她打算去街上走走。
After breakfast, with hours to spare before meeting Wenyuan, she decided to take a turn down the street.

“苦荞，给我找身男装。”
"Kuqiao, go find me a set of men's clothes."

“小姐要男装做什么？”
"What does Miss want men's clothes for?"

“别问那么多，找来便是。”
"Quit asking questions and just fetch some."

苦荞感到为难：“小姐的衣柜里一身男装都没有。”
Kuqiao hesitated: "There isn't a single set of men's clothes in your wardrobe, Miss."

千里寻想到一个好办法：“去问外面的下人要一身。”
Qianli Xun thought of a quick fix: "Go ask one of the male servants outside for a set."

“小姐岂能穿下人的衣裳？”
"How could Miss wear a servant's clothes?!"

“让你要就去要，别管那么多。”
"Do as I say and don't fuss."

苦荞只好出去找下人要身男装。
Left no choice, Kuqiao went off to borrow a man's outfit from a servant.

夏果并不关心小姐要男装之事，今日的早茶由她来做，正专心体验做茶的快乐，她觉得做茶时自己仿佛变成大家闺秀的模样。
Unbothered by the clothes request, Xiaguo was busy whisking morning tea, savoring the feeling that whisking tea made her look just like a refined young lady.

茶做好后，给小姐端到跟前，夏果小心问：“小姐今日要见乔公子，我可否跟着伺候？”
Bringing the finished bowl over to her mistress, Xiaguo asked with care, "Miss is meeting Young Master Qiao today—may I come along to wait on you?"

“你是我的贴身丫鬟，当然得跟我一起。”茶到嘴边，千里寻不想喝一口，将茶盏放在桌上。
"You're my personal maid; of course you're coming." Raising the tea bowl to her lips, Qianli Xun lost the urge to drink and set it right back down.

夏果担忧地问：“小姐对我做的茶不满意？”
Alarm flashed in Xiaguo's eyes: "Isn't Miss pleased with the tea I made?"

“不是，你别多想，我只是突然不想喝而已。”
"No, don't overthink it—I just don't feel thirsty right now."

早饭时，小姐也没吃多少，夏果清楚一定是因与乔公子的婚事受阻，小姐心情不佳所致。
Her mistress had barely touched breakfast either; Xiaguo knew the wrecked marriage proposal had drained all appetite from her.

苦荞找来一身干净的下人衣裳给小姐换上，又把她头上的发饰取下，将头发绾成男子的发髻。
Kuqiao returned with a clean set of servant's clothes, helped her mistress change, removed her hairpins, and bound her hair into a man's topknot.

千里寻照照镜子，很满意：“我出去一趟，你们两个不许跟着。”
Checking the mirror, Qianli Xun nodded in approval. "I'm stepping out for a bit. Neither of you is to follow."

“小姐要去做什么？”夏果担心小姐偷偷去见乔公子。
"Where is Miss going?" Xiaguo worried her mistress was sneaking off to see Young Master Qiao alone.

“我去街上转转，听听别人怎么议论我。”千里寻扔下两个丫鬟独自走出望月轩。
"Taking a turn down the street to hear what folks are saying about me." Leaving both maids behind, Qianli Xun walked out of Wangyue Pavilion alone.

苦荞叹道：“小姐真可怜，难道以后出去都要女扮男装？外面议论的人实在可恶！”
Kuqiao sighed, "Poor Miss—will she have to dress like a man every time she steps outside now? Those gossipers out there are vile!"

夏果想不通，小姐为何要亲自上杆子去听：“是可恶，昨个我出去买东西，听到有人议论，可难听，我都听不下去，把议论的人臭骂一顿，小姐要是亲耳听到，岂不更难过？”
Xiaguo couldn't fathom why her mistress would go hunt for insults: "Vile is right! Yesterday when I went shopping I heard folks spewing filth so awful I cursed them out on the spot. If Miss hears it with her own ears, she'll only hurt worse!"

“那还用说。”苦荞瞧到桌上的茶盏，看样子里面的茶一口都没喝，“小姐竟然没喝茶就走，她得多伤心。”
"No kidding." Glancing at the untouched tea bowl on the table, Kuqiao sighed again: "Miss didn't even take one sip before leaving—she must be heartbroken."

千里寻步行来到街上，她不打算走得太远，附近逛逛即可，之所以换上男装，只是不想被人认出而已。
Walking out onto the avenue, Qianli Xun meant only to stroll nearby, dressed as a man just to avoid being recognized.

在大街上走了半晌，人来人往，没有听到任何议论朱淑真的言论，千里寻的心情变得轻松许多。
Walking for a good half-hour through bustling crowds without hearing a single syllable about Zhu Shuzhen eased the knot in her chest.

早晨未喝茶，多走几步后，倍感口渴，正好不远处有家茶肆，她决定进去坐坐。
Having skipped morning tea, thirst crept up after her long walk. Spotting a teahouse ahead, she stepped inside for a seat.

这家茶肆为招揽生意，也有说书表演。
To draw customers, this teahouse featured live storytelling too.

一走进茶肆，听到说话艺人说的正是她写的话本，心情不由自主好了许多，问伙计要壶茶，坐下边喝边听。
The instant she crossed the threshold, she heard the storyteller reciting her own huaben story! Her spirits lifted at once; ordering a pot of tea, she sat down to sip and listen.

只听说话艺人讲道：
The storyteller's voice carried across the room:

说也奇怪，自从华家生下女儿，地里便长出各色花卉，华家认为是祥兆。果然是祥兆，从此华家便以卖花为生，日子过得越来越好，成为乡下富户。
Strange to tell, the very day the Hua family welcomed their daughter, flowers of every hue sprang up across their fields—a sign of heaven's favor, they believed. And auspicious it proved! From then on the Huas sold flowers for a living, prospering year by year until they became a wealthy country household.

华红香长到十五岁时，城主的儿子武玄英快过十五岁诞辰，想要买些花卉装点宴席。派出去的人打听来，打听去，只有华家种植的花卉最好，而且传言只要华家姑娘在一旁，花不仅开得更艳，还不会凋谢，负责生日宴的人便向华家订下许多花卉……
When Hua Hongxiang turned fifteen, Wu Xuanying, son of the City Lord, was approaching his fifteenth birthday banquet and needed blossoms to deck the hall. His stewards searched high and low and found no flowers finer than the Huas'—and rumor said whenever the Hua girl stood nearby, blooms opened brighter and refused to wilt, so the banquet stewards placed a huge order with the Hua house...

千里寻瞧瞧周围听书的人，哪有人议论朱淑真？都在认真听，也是她写的故事太吸引人。
Glancing around the packed tables, Qianli Xun saw nobody gossiping about Zhu Shuzhen—everyone sat hooked on her story!

她向伙计招招手。
She waved a waiter over.

伙计来到跟前：“客官有什么吩咐？”
The waiter stepped up. "What can I get you, sir?"

千里寻装出男子的声音：“你们茶肆怎么也说千里寻的本子？”
Pitching her voice low like a young man, Qianli Xun asked, "Your teahouse performs Qianli Xun's scripts too?"

伙计夸道：“看来客官是爱听书之人，竟也知千里寻才子的美名，他写的故事很受欢迎，各大茶肆酒楼都在说。”
The waiter beamed: "Looks like young sir loves storytelling—you know the brilliant Qianli Xun's name too! His tales are all the rage; every big teahouse and tavern in town is telling them."

“千里寻现在很有名气吗？”
"Is Qianli Xun really that famous now?"

“小有名气。”
"Quite the rising name!"

听到小有名气，千里寻一开心，从怀里掏出两块碎银：“拿着，赏你的。”
Delighted to hear "rising name," Qianli Xun pulled two bits of loose silver from her sleeve: "Here, for you."

伙计更高兴：“谢客官！”
The waiter grinned wide: "Thank you, young sir!"

走进两名男子，瞧瞧周围没地方可坐，只有千里寻的桌子坐着一人，上前道：“小兄弟，我们能否跟您凑个桌？”
Just then two men walked in, scanned the packed room for empty tables, and stepped up to Qianli Xun's table: "Mind if we share your table, little brother?"

千里寻并不介意：“当然可以，二位随便坐。”
Qianli Xun waved them over: "Go right ahead, make yourselves at home."

两人坐下后，也要壶茶，品完几口，听会儿书，便没了兴致，轻声聊起天儿来。
Sitting down, the two ordered tea, listened for a few minutes, then grew bored and began chatting in low voices.

“这本子我听过好几遍，故事再熟悉不过，华红香在生日宴上第一次遇见武玄英，可惜前世记忆已无，两人都认不出对方，还闹出些误会。”
"Heard this script three times already—know it by heart! Hua Hongxiang meets Wu Xuanying for the first time at the birthday banquet, yet with past memories erased, neither recognizes the other and misunderstandings fly."

“不知接下来故事如何？只等千里寻才子写出来，我等才知。”
"Wonder what happens next—we'll have to wait for Master Qianli Xun to write more."

千里寻听后，更加高兴，谣言没听到，反而听到自己的大名逐渐被人所知：“两位朋友竟也知道千里寻？”
Delight bubbled in Qianli Xun: not a whisper of rumor, only praise for her pen name! "You two know Qianli Xun as well?"

“那是自然，喜欢听书的人都知道。”
"Of course—everyone who loves storytelling knows him!"

千里寻继续问：“他现在很出名吗？”
Qianli Xun fished for more: "Pretty famous, is he?"

“小有名气，不过，再有名也盖不过朱家姑娘，呵呵……！”
"Making a name, sure—though no fame beats Miss Zhu's right now! Haha!"

对方的笑声搞得千里寻笑容全无，她不再说话，继续喝茶听书。书没听进多少，二人的议论倒是全部灌进耳朵。
His laugh wiped the smile clean off Qianli Xun's face. Clamping her lips shut, she sipped her tea—ignoring the storyteller as every syllable from the two men poured straight into her ears.

“是啊，最近大家都在议论。说起朱家姑娘，实在大胆，一点儿大家闺秀的风范都没有。”
"Tell me about it—everyone's talking! That Miss Zhu sure has nerve, not an ounce of noble-lady grace."

“我家小妹以前很喜欢她的诗词，也不知从哪里抄来许多，听到街上的传言，伤心不已。”
"My little sister used to adore her poetry and copied heaps of it from who knows where—hearing the street talk broke her heart."

千里寻以为有人明事理，忍不住插嘴道：“你家小妹肯定觉得外面谣言是假，替朱家姑娘鸣不平。”
Thinking someone out there had sense, Qianli Xun chimed in: "Your sister must know those street rumors are lies and feel furious for Miss Zhu!"

那人摇摇头：“非也，我家小妹伤心自己多年喜欢幽栖居士的诗词，实在不该，早知她品性不端，就不应想尽办法抄来那么多诗词。”
The man shook his head: "Not at all! My sister's heartbroken she wasted years admiring Youqi Jushi's verse—said if she'd known her morals were rotten, she'd never have hunted down so many poems!"

另一位问：“不会一把火烧了？”
The other man asked, "Don't tell me she burned them all?"

“你怎知道？”
"How'd you guess?!"

“猜的！我听说知府大人把幽栖居士的手稿一把火烧毁，没想到你家小妹也如此，真是巾帼不让须眉！”
"Easy! Heard the Prefect torched all of Youqi Jushi's manuscripts—your sister did the same? Now that's a heroine to rival any man!"

千里寻觉得好笑，烧朱淑真的手稿与巾帼不让须眉有何关系？如此赞扬，实在不恰当。
Qianli Xun almost laughed out loud—burning Zhu Shuzhen's poems made a girl "a heroine to rival any man"? What twisted praise!

“二位有没有想过，大家议论的话只是谣言，朱家姑娘根本不像谣言说的那样。”
"Have you two ever considered those stories are just rumors, and Miss Zhu is nothing like what folk say?"

二人奇怪地盯着眼前的小子，一起摇摇头。
Both men stared at the strange youth and shook their heads together.

“没想过，我等不认识幽栖居士，谁知传言是真是假。”
"Never thought about it. We don't know Youqi Jushi—how should we know what's true or false?"

千里寻劝说道：“那就更不该胡乱议论别人，冤枉好人岂不罪过？”
Qianli Xun pressed: "Then you have even less business slandering her! Isn't wronging an innocent person a sin?"

“常言道：无风不起浪，何况她还是知府家千金，谁敢随意诋毁？肯定是她过于放荡，才臭名远扬，要怪只能怪她自己。”
"No smoke without fire! Besides, she's the Prefect's daughter—who'd dare slander her for nothing? She must be shameless to stink this far and wide; she has only herself to blame!"

千里寻已忍不下去，端起茶盏，将茶水泼在那人身上，大声吼道：“两个大男人跟个长舌妇似的，小心你生下儿子没屁眼！”
That snapped Qianli Xun's last nerve. Lifting her tea bowl, she splashed the tea straight across the man's chest and roared, "Two grown men wagging tongues like fishwives—may your sons be born without assholes!"

被眼前的小子侮辱，那人甚是气愤，站起来骂道：“臭小子，敢泼我茶水，看爷爷怎么教训你！”
Humiliated by a slip of a boy, the man jumped up cursing: "Little punk, dare splash tea on me? Watch your grandpa teach you manners!"

上前就要用拳头打千里寻，另一个也站起来帮忙。
He swung a fist at Qianli Xun, his companion surging up to help.

本想着两个大男人收拾一个臭小子应该不成问题，谁承想没两下被臭小子左右拳打倒在地，捂着被打成瘀青的脸直喊：“哎吆！”
Two grown men figured flattening one skinny lad would be a breeze—only for rapid left-right hooks to drop them both flat on the floor in two seconds, clutching bruised faces and howling, "Ow! Ouch!"

三人闹得太大，听书的没心情再听，艺人也停止说书，都看起热闹来。
The brawl brought the teahouse to a standstill; the storyteller froze and every customer turned to gawk.

千里寻骂道：“老娘……你爷爷我不好欺负！警告你们，以后再让我听到议论朱家姑娘，我的拳头绝不轻饶！”
Qianli Xun barked, "Your old la—your grandpa isn't someone to push around! Mark my words: catch anyone else slandering Miss Zhu, and my fists won't go easy!"

其他人听到这话，无不吐吐舌头，心想，刚才自己议论时多亏声音不大，没被那小子听到，否则被打的就是他们。
Every bystander stuck out their tongue in silent relief—thank goodness they'd kept their voices low while gossiping earlier, or they'd be eating dust too!

伙计上前扶起两人，劝道：“小哥，我们是小本经营，经不起折腾，您可别再乱打人。”
Hurrying over to haul the two men up, the waiter pleaded, "Young sir, we run a tiny shop and can't survive a brawl—please don't hit anyone else!"

千里寻瞧瞧周围：“也没砸坏店里的东西，再说我从不乱打人，打的都是该打之人，谁让他们胡乱议论朱家姑娘。”
Qianli Xun glanced around. "Didn't break a single table or chair. Besides, I never hit innocents—only scum who ask for it by slandering Miss Zhu."

伙计把碎银掏出来：“客官，还您，我也议论过，您不打我都是好的，小费我岂敢再要。”
Pulling out the two bits of loose silver, the waiter held them back: "Take your silver back, sir! I gossiped too—I'm lucky you didn't beat me up; how could I dare keep your tip?!"

千里寻瞧瞧两块碎银：“既给出，岂能要回？”
Qianli Xun waved the silver away: "Once given, I never take it back."

伙计又收起，劝道：“客官，不能怪议论的人，不信你问问，这里谁没议论过？”
Pocketing it again, the waiter sighed, "Can't blame folks for talking, young sir—ask around, who in this room hasn't talked?"

被打的人气愤道：“你打我们有什么用？有本事，把全城的人打一顿！我听说，乡下人也在议论，难道你也跑到乡下去一个个打不成？”
One bruised man yelled back, "What's beating us up prove?! If you've got guts, go beat up the whole city! Heard peasants in the countryside are gossiping too—going to hike out there and punch them one by one?!"

千里寻被问住，她的拳头再厉害，总不能见人就打，老百姓议论只图个茶余饭后开心，最可恶的是传播谣言之人。若抓住此人，定要将他大卸八块！
His retort stopped Qianli Xun cold. Tough as her fists were, she couldn't beat up all of Lin'an. Common folk only gossiped for idle amusement; the real villain was whoever planted the rumors. Once she caught that rat, she'd chop him to pieces!
