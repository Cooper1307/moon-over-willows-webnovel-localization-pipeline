## Chapter 307: Matron Wei’s Fruitless Plea

Guided into Princess Consort Yi’s courtyard, Matron Wei found no royal waiting—only a matron near her own age seated coolly below the head chair.

“Youqi Jushi’s matron?” Matron Zou didn’t rise. “Her Highness doesn’t see just anyone. State your business to me.”

Learning from a maid this was Prince Yi’s wet nurse, Matron Wei curtsied properly, took a seat, and cut straight to the bone. “Two things. First, tell Princess Consort Yi my miss returned safe from Mount Tianmu—cut foot, nothing serious.”

Seeing Matron Zou’s eyes dart toward the decorative screen confirmed Her Highness sat right behind it. Matron Wei pressed on bold: “Surviving a mess like that means King Yama isn’t ready for her—why waste effort on futile plots?”

“Don’t know what you’re babbling about,” Matron Zou feigned. “What’s her luck got to do with Her Highness?”

“Drop the act, Matron Zou—you wouldn’t be receiving me if you didn’t know everything. My miss only told me today Her Highness wants her dead, and out of love for her I came here to plead.”

Dropping the pretense, Matron Zou said, “Point one noted. What’s point two?”

“I’ve watched my miss every single day: she never crossed a line with Prince Yi, and since marrying she’s focused solely on her husband. Prince Yi isn’t fickle either—now wed, his heart belongs to Her Highness. Holding onto the sting of that wedding raid and targeting my miss only traps everyone in the past. Let bygones go and look forward!”

Matron Zou agreed inside, yet spoke up to test the screen behind her. “Easy for you to say—Prince Yi still drops everything whenever her name comes up! Look at today!”

“He’s Military Commissioner and Judicial Investigator—he’d ride out for any family in Lin’an! If anyone keeps handing Prince Yi chances to play hero for my miss, blame the ones cooking up attacks, not my miss!”

Behind the screen, a cold jolt hit Qiao Mengrong: the pamphlets, Chen Haodong’s stunts, Black-Fiend Tiger—every single trap she sprung had only handed Brother another excuse to rush to Zhu Shuzhen’s side! How stupid had she been—yet letting her live still stuck in her craw.

Matron Zou tried one last jab. “Gossip calls her a born flirt whose eyes steal men’s souls—I’d be mad too.”

“Street slander! Meet her and you’d know better.”

“I have met her—still can’t tell.”

“Because you never lived beside her.”

Unable to hold back, Qiao Mengrong stepped clean out from behind the screen. “I lived beside her—best friends once—and still only saw her true face too late!”

Matron Wei dropped to her knees to bow, rising at Her Highness’s cold wave. “Parts of what you said make sense, and I’ll keep them in mind,” Qiao Mengrong said icy-flat. “Still, don’t dream I’m letting Zhu Shuzhen go. Tell her luck runs out eventually.”

Both matrons’ hearts sank. Having said her piece, Matron Wei delivered her miss’s final word: “Target her alone—spare innocent people.”

“I’d love to spare others, yet she married into Chen Manor—collateral damage happens. Tell her I’ll try.”

Chilled by how unrecognizable kind Princess Jiahe had become, Matron Wei decided to tell a white lie back at Chen Manor rather than watch her miss abandon a thriving marriage out of fear.

Finding Chen Haoyu chatting warmly beside Shuzhen’s bed back in Moxiang Courtyard—and watching him politely excuse himself over “Zhu family business”—Matron Wei sat close and reassured her miss: “Better than expected! Her Highness swore she’ll live her life and let you live yours so long as you stay clear of Prince Yi—just avoid salons and banquets where they show up.”

Qianli Xun’s doubts melted away in relief, right before Chen Wenxin arrived to check on her for the frantic Zhu household.

Meanwhile, Zhu Xiyan marched straight to the Judicial Office and kowtowed three resonant knocks before Prince Yi—forgetting all his past irritation over losing graft from Lazybones Village. “Your Highness saved my daughter and my house—from this day on, I and every official in Lin’an serve at your command!”

“Even if I cut off your side income?” Shen Jianchen teased.

“Silver’s dust—doing real good for the people under Your Highness matters most!”

With Lin’an’s whole officialdom squarely in his pocket, Shen Jianchen set to work with full force.

Over the next twenty days—as Moxiang Courtyard’s lotuses bloomed and silk-tree buds swelled—the Judicial Office stacked five capital charges on Black-Fiend Tiger: arson, murder, robbery, pillage, and rape. Shen Jianchen sentenced him to autumn beheading, sorting his three hundred followers by guilt: death row for the worst, life behind bars for mid-tier, and parole for minor hands split between Lin’an’s garrison and Master Jiang’s river patrols.

Out of old brotherhood, Master Jiang got Prince Yi’s permission to bring wine and four dishes into death row for a farewell drink. Dragging heavy shackles to the wooden table, Black-Fiend Tiger had zero appetite—burning with envy at how Master Jiang rose to Ferry Superintendent while he waited for the axe.

Pouring drinks, Master Jiang said, “One last toast, Brother Black—be an honest ghost in the next life.”

Bitterness spilled out as Black-Fiend Tiger reminisced: ten years back they swore brotherhood, got rejected from Bianliang’s army for their looks, enlisted in Lin’an only to brawl their way out of a corrupt camp—then climbed fast on the dark side where honest paths stayed blocked.

“Still remember my real name?”

“Li Hu—nickname Little Tiger.”

“You killed men too—how’d you end up a shiny official visiting me on death row?”

“Good deeds balanced the bad, never crossed the yamen, and whenever folks paid for hits, I checked first—only whacked scum who deserved heaven’s wrath!”

“If someone paid you to kill me, would I make your list?”

Master Jiang blinked, then answered honest: “By my code, yes—yet we’re sworn brothers, so no silver buys your head.”

Touched, Black-Fiend Tiger clinked bowls and drained his cup.

Master Jiang sighed. “Robbing the Chens was one thing—why snatch Chen Haoyu’s wife?! Prefect’s daughter, Jiangnan’s poetess, and Prince Yi would die for her—she’s the very one who got me my official post! One quick word to me first and your whole stronghold would still be standing!”
