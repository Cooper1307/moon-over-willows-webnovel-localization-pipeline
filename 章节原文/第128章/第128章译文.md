## Chapter 128: Strolling the Market to Clear Her Head

Deep autumn settled in, bringing sharper chills as winter crept close. Wangyue Pavilion's chrysanthemums had faded, and the rest of the garden withered brown.

Every morning servants swept fallen leaves, only for one gust of wind to blanket the stones all over again; a few servants just grabbed tree trunks and shook them hard to bring every dead leaf down at once.

Qianli Xun had changed into lightly quilted autumn robes—thin padding warm enough against the late-autumn bite. She had turned into a bigger homebody than back when she wrote web novels.

One thought never left her: Wenyuan. How was he doing?

After sending Bai Feng twice with updates, Prince Yi seemed to vanish too, leaving both Kuqiao and Qianli Xun grumbling.

Half a year sounded neither long nor short, yet almost two months had already slipped by; Qianli Xun dreaded letting go without ever laying eyes on Wenyuan once more.

The three young masters hadn't called again, and Qianli Xun understood with gratitude: visiting once showed loyal friendship, staying away afterward showed wise friendship too, sparing her fresh gossip over male visitors.

Cyberbullied before crossing over, smeared by a whole city after—she knew firsthand how deadly wagging tongues could be. Was enduring mob gossip written into her destiny?

Huayu had dropped by a few times, collecting a handful of Zhu Shuzhen's ci lyrics. She could only write down the ones history preserved—who knew how many lost gems had perished in history's real bonfire?

Her mother and sister-in-law had visited twice more, and her brother Zhu Jingyuan came last week bearing treats. Warmed by family love, she asked how Father was doing.

Zhu Jingyuan told her Prince Yi had drafted a memorial calling for tax cuts; Father had carried it to Bianliang himself, returned beaming, and now spent all day at the prefecture office rolling out tax relief for Lin'an's commoners.

Taxes didn't interest Qianli Xun—yet that explained why Prince Yi hadn't sent Bai Feng over lately: he had real state business. Fair enough; he was an imperial prince and Military Commissioner of Lin'an, not her personal messenger.

Unable to hold back, she asked her brother about Wenyuan—a man out in town every day was bound to hear news.

Zhu Jingyuan had heard news, hesitated lest it break her heart, and finally gave in under her relentless questioning.

The Qiao family had already betrothed Qiao Wenxian to the Miao family, wedding set for spring.

Not quite a lightning bolt, yet the news froze Qianli Xun solid for a long moment.

Qiao and Miao united—Miao Zhiyu had got her wish. What about Qianli Xun? Maybe half a year really was too long—should she just let go now?

Sitting in the garden pavilion staring at the three osmanthus trees, she saw most golden blooms had fallen, only stray blossoms clinging amid green leaves as if loath to let autumn end.

Weeds crowded beneath the trunks—some yellowed, some clinging stubbornly to green—blanketed in fallen osmanthus petals like a warm quilt against the chill.

Shivering in the cold garden wind, Kuqiao and Xiaguo worried their mistress would catch a chill.

"It's getting chilly, Miss—let's go indoors," Xiaguo ventured, mostly because her own toes were freezing standing there.

Feeling for her maids, Qianli Xun stood without a word and headed back to her room.

Both maids hurried after her.

Xiaguo felt relieved: once back at her desk, Miss wrote nonstop—Xiaguo cared nothing for what she wrote, only that while Miss wrote, Kuqiao stayed to grind ink, freeing Xiaguo to grab a book and daydream about Young Master Qiao in the maids' quarters.

Hearing he was engaged had broken Xiaguo's heart for days before she talked herself round: born a maid, marrying him as wife was never in the cards anyway—being his maid or concubine would already be heaven's gift, so her shot wasn't dead!

Her mistress, though, had zero shot left—no wonder she sat mute in grief. Funny how low birth worked in your favor sometimes.

Back indoors, Kuqiao hung up her mistress's cape and laid out brush, ink, paper, and inkstone as usual; lately Miss barely spoke, yet her brush flew so fast Kuqiao trekked to West Third Alley every three or four days.

Without waiting to be told, Xiaguo picked up "Collected Poems of Wang Youcheng" from the desk, bowed, and slipped off to the maids' quarters.

Sitting at her desk, Qianli Xun lifted her brush—and froze. Today's chapter called for Hongxiang and Xuanying falling into sweet love. With Wenyuan engaged and bitterness filling her throat, how could she write sweet romance?!

Tossing the brush down, she wondered what on earth could blow the pent-up steam out of her chest. A brawl! She needed a good fight before she went nuts!

Chen Haoyu popped straight into mind—the one punching bag who never failed to fire up her fists! Never had she craved bumping into Chen Haoyu more; one combo of Qianli Fist punches knocking him flat would feel glorious!

Yet Sister-in-law claimed Chen Haoyu stayed holed up in Chen Manor now, returning home the same day even when he stepped out, winning praises from his sister. Had a leopard changed its spots? Qianli Xun doubted it.

She couldn't kick down Chen Manor's door to beat him up. Better stroll the streets and see if some jerk begged for a punch.

"Kuqiao, bring out my men's clothes—help me change."

Kuqiao had picked up the tailored men's robes from the tailor shop days ago, puzzling at first before figuring Miss wore them to dodge gossip outside.

Fetching a set, she helped her mistress dress.

Since the men's robe was thin, Kuqiao layered it over her quilted gown and bound her hair up. Cooped up indoors heartbroken over Young Master Qiao, Miss hadn't bothered with makeup anyway, saving time. Minutes later, a dashing young gentleman stared back from the mirror.

Ordering Kuqiao to stay put, Qianli Xun stepped out of Wangyue Pavilion alone.

Stepping onto the street after weeks indoors hit her with a strange sense of dislocation.

A quiet sigh rose in her chest: nobody to stroll with now. Wenyuan was gone; her maids could tag along, yet neither could fill the hollow space inside her.

Walking on, she laughed at herself—everyone on the avenue was shopping or hustling for a living; nobody was handing out free brawls! Dropping the fighting idea, she decided just to wander and clear her head.

Wangyue Pavilion was way too quiet, trapping her alone with her heartache; maybe noisy street life could drown it out for an hour.

Bustling crowds and ringing vendor cries washed over her, pulling her eyes outward to the lively stalls and shops—Song-dynasty street life rivaled any modern metropolis!

Passing a cosmetics stall mobbed by young women, Qianli Xun—still a woman inside—wandered up and inspected a sleek box of rouge: "How much for this, boss?"

Giving her an odd look, the vendor said, "Thirty wen."

Fishing out a thirty-wen coin, Qianli Xun handed it over while the vendor asked, "Buying for someone at home, young sir?"

Remembering her men's disguise—and catching puzzled stares from the girls around her—she covered fast: "For my little sister—she loves rouge." Tucking the box inside her robe, she turned to leave.

One girl stepped straight into her path: "How lucky your sister is to have such a thoughtful brother! A gentleman this sweet to his sister must spoil his wife rotten too."

Catching the starry-eyed blush on the girl's face, Qianli Xun suppressed a laugh—cross-dressing into an accidental romance! "Still unwed—single as can be."

The girl's eyes sparkled brighter: "Might young sir share your honorable name?"

She couldn't say Zhu Shuzhen—neither with rumors around nor dressed as a man! Why not drop "Qianli Xun" and test how famous her pen name really was?

"My humble name is Qianli Xun..."

Before the last syllable faded, another girl gasped: "Young sir—are you THE Qianli Xun who writes huaben stories?!"

Whoa—she really was famous! Nodding coolly: "The very one."

Every girl at the stall swarmed in at once: "Master Qianli Xun is so mysterious, never showing his face—running into you today is pure luck!"

"Not only brilliant, handsome as a painting too!"

Pulling out the thirty-wen coin, the vendor pressed it back into her hand: "If you're Master Qianli Xun, no charge—take it as a gift! Write us more great tales; I listen to every single one!"

Unable to turn him down, Qianli Xun pocketed the coin with warm thanks.

Blushing crimson, the first girl ventured, "A chance meeting beats any formal invitation—fate brought us together! Would Master Qianli Xun share a pot of tea at the teahouse next door?"

Instant regret hit Qianli Xun: flexing her fame had landed her in a pack of fangirls! Time to bail: "Kind of you, young lady, yet I have urgent business—excuse me!"

Moving to slip away, several girls blocked her exit: "At least tell us where you live, Master!"

Helpless, Qianli Xun improvised: "A drifter with no fixed address!"

Sizing up her fine clothes, the girls doubted: "Dressed like that with no address? Pulling our legs, aren't you?"

Refusing to stay trapped, Qianli Xun pushed straight through the girls and bolted like wind...

Gaping as the sleek figure vanished into the crowd, one pushed girl marveled, "Master Qianli Xun looks slender as a maiden, yet what a grip—and fast as lightning!"

The girl who had asked her name sighed, "Delicate as he looks, he's still a man! What luck meeting him today—wonder if I'll ever cross paths with him again?"
