## Chapter 350: Return to Lin'an, the Fate Ends

Once Mengrong's and Xilan's bones were laid in their coffins, Qianli Xun rode for Lin'an that same night.

The journey went smoothly enough. But set against two years earlier, there were far more refugees on the roads.

War between Song and Jin fell hardest on ordinary people. They lost their homes, and the property they had scraped together over years.

Against those wandering families, her own losses seemed small. No use clinging to what was gone. She had to look forward. A future that was truly hers was waiting.

Traveling north to south, she passed many strange places. Likely she would never see them again. She slowed the pace on purpose. At each stop she halted the carriage and described the view aloud—to the red wooden chest in the carriage behind her.

The red chest held Mengrong's bones. The gray one held Xilan's.

When Mengrong was alive, her brother fell ill and she stayed at Prince Yi's palace to nurse him. She never had a chance to leave the house. Later he recovered—but he was not her real brother. His heart belonged to someone else, and he left her at the edge of his life.

Now she had the chance. Qianli Xun wanted to lend Mengrong her eyes and show her what the wide world looked like.

She went in fits and starts. A journey of one month took her a full month and a half before she reached the border of Lin'an.

She was in no hurry to enter the city. She told the driver to take the bamboo grove first.

The carriage could not go into the village, so they stopped outside it.

As she stepped down, Ku Jue came walking out of the village.

Ku Jue knew her mistress at a glance but could hardly believe it. She came closer to look, then cried out, 'It's you, mistress! You're alive? My sister will be beside herself. I—I'll run to the city and tell her this instant!'

Qianli Xun caught her arm. 'Take me to Prince Yi's bamboo house first.'

Ku Jue flushed. 'I was so taken with my sister I forgot my mistress. Let me settle you first, then go to the city.'

Ku Jue led her to the bamboo house Shen Jianchen had built.

It stood at the far end of the village and took up a wide plot—the finest bamboo house in the place. The main hall and side rooms enclosed the whole courtyard, more like a manor than a cottage.

How did you know I wanted a house like this? she wondered.

In the courtyard Ku Jue shouted, 'Brother-in-law! Look who's come back!'

Bai Feng came out of a side room. Seeing Youqi Jushi standing there alive, he was overcome. He stepped forward and bowed. 'You've come back. At last the prince can rest easy.'

No prince in sight. Bai Feng asked, 'Why didn't His Lordship come back with you?'

'Prince Yi... he's staying on in Yanjing for a time. He can return in a year.'

Bai Feng worried for his lord, but could not press further. He did not need to guess why the prince had stayed: it was the price of getting Youqi Jushi back.

'The prince built this house for you. From now on it's your home. You've come a long way—come in and rest.'

'There's time. Mengrong's and Xilan's bones are still at the village gate. Let's bury them before we rest.'

Bai Feng thought of Yanjing and his face fell. 'I failed to protect them. Every time I think of it I blame myself...'

'Don't grieve, Captain Bai. The Jin man who killed Mengrong is dead at her grave. That debt is paid. But we still haven't found Xilan's killer.'

'You've done enough, Youqi Jushi. The prince had the tombs built already. I'll fetch men to carry the boxes over.'

'I'll go with you.'

'I'll give a hand,' Ku Jue said.

The three started outside. They had not gone far when Matron Zou met them head-on.

She saw Youqi Jushi and her nose stung, her eyes reddening. She came up and seized her hand. 'I'm so glad to see you alive. Now the prince can rest.'

'Matron... I... I didn't look after Mengrong properly. Do you blame me?'

Matron Zou let go, took out a handkerchief and dabbed her eyes. 'Ah, each of us has her fate. No blame to you. Your heart was in the right place. Blame these times. You've suffered enough. Things will get better. All I ask now is that you and the prince live out your days in peace.'

Matron Zou looked around. 'Where is the prince? Is he inside?'

Qianli Xun said, 'The young Jin prince admired Prince Yi's boxing. He kept him to trade techniques and promised to let him go in a year.'

'Let's hope the young prince keeps his word.'

'If he breaks it, I'll kill him.'

Matron Zou grabbed her hand again. 'Never say that! Stay safe. If anything happened to you, what would the prince do? He'll come back—he's not a man to be bullied. You're his one weak spot. As long as you're all right, he's free to act. I think he chose to stay in Jin for a while. And even if they won't let him go, he'll find a way home. He's that capable.'

Listening to her, Qianli Xun understood how much Shen Jianchen must have suffered in the days they thought her dead. That was why Matron Zou could not bear the thought of any harm coming to her—not for the prince's sake.

'Matron, come with us. We're burying Mengrong and Xilan.'

Tears poured down Matron Zou's face. She let go and wiped them again. 'They're home at last. Prince Yi's consort won't have to stay in a strange land any longer.'

The brick-lined graves lay below the grove, at the foot of the hill. When they arrived, Qianli Xun saw wildflowers of every color growing around them. No better place for Mengrong, with flowers for company.

With Ku Jue helping, Bai Feng carried the two chests into the waiting graves.

Qianli Xun brought out the spirit money and offerings she had bought on the road and laid them before the grave. She and Matron Zou crouched down and burned the paper money, then stayed a while talking to Mengrong before going back to the bamboo house.

The house inside suited her down to the last detail. A tea table, a writing desk, and a bookshelf built of bamboo—where he had found so many books she could not guess, but they filled the whole shelf. The inner room was to her taste as well, quiet and elegant. Only one thing was missing: Kuqiao.

She went into the outer room just as Bai Feng came in.

'I was about to look for you.'

'I have a favor to ask, Youqi Jushi.'

'Tell me first.'

Bai Feng spoke up. 'I can't wait any longer to marry Kuqiao. I started building this house before the prince left for Yanjing. It's finished now. Will you let me bring her home soon?'

Qianli Xun smiled. 'Granted.'

Bai Feng beamed. 'I'll fetch her at once.'

'Wait,' Qianli Xun said. 'Come with me to the Zhu house. We'll pick her up and visit my parents while we're there.'

'Kuqiao is still at the Chen house,' Bai Feng explained.

'Still at the Chen house?' She had thought that with the marriage ended and Chen Haoyu together with Xiangrong, his servants would have gone back to the Zhu house. Why was Kuqiao still there? Had the Chen elders kept her, or Chen Haoyu himself? 'We'll go to the Zhu house first, see my parents, then on to the Chens...'

The servants at the Zhu gate saw their young mistress standing there alive, and were so startled and glad they forgot to announce her to the master and mistress.

She paid them no mind and went straight to her father's courtyard.

Bai Feng stayed outside. He could imagine the family's joy at seeing their daughter returned whole—too happy to speak.

Just as he guessed, Zhu Xiyan dropped the teacup he had just lifted. Tears of joy ran down his face. It was a while before he could say, 'Go tell her mistress—quick!'

Qianli Xun knelt. 'Father, I've been a bad daughter. I worried you.'

He came forward and lifted her up. 'Alive is enough. Alive is enough...'

Lady Xiang, helped by Chen Wenxin, hurried in with her son Zhu Jingyuan. She saw her daughter alive and cried, 'Shuzhen! Is it you?'

Qianli Xun turned and took her arm. 'Mother, it's me. I'm not dead. I'm fine.'

Lady Xiang stroked her face. 'So thin. I knew my girl wasn't dead. One day she'd stand in front of me and call me Mother. Oh...'

Chen Wenxin was crying too, and comforted her mother-in-law. 'Shuzhen is home. You should be glad. Why these tears?'

'That's right, Mother,' Zhu Jingyuan said. 'Our sister is back safe and sound. We should all be happy. Come, sit with her and talk.'

Zhu Xiyan said, 'No more tears. Sit down and talk.'

Lady Xiang stopped crying. With her daughter's help she sat down with the rest. One after another they said how they had missed Shuzhen, and asked about her time in Jin and how the news of her death had reached them.

To spare them more pain, Qianli Xun told only what she could.

They cursed the Jin for their cruelty and told her never to go north again. By the look of things, the south was safer.

When they asked how she had come back safely, she said nothing of Shao Yueting—that was the Shao family's secret. She only said Prince Yi had ridden to Yanjing again to exchange himself for her, and the Jin had agreed to release him for Lin'an a year later.

The room went quiet. Zhu Xiyan and the others were thinking how deep the prince's feeling for Shuzhen ran. When he came home, there would be no reason to keep them apart.

Chen Wenxin thought of her brother. He had never forgotten Shuzhen and had kept Moxiang Yuan all this time. If he learned she was alive, what would he do?

Outside, Bai Feng grew restless. No telling how long Youqi Jushi would talk with her family. Better to go to the Chen house, tell Kuqiao the good news, and bring her back with him.

As he was about to leave, a servant stopped him. 'Captain Bai, our mistress is having a family dinner. The master says you must eat here too.'

'No, really, I have business to attend to,' Bai Feng said.

The servant took hold of him and would not let go. 'The master's orders: you stay today. Our mistress has come back after all this time. Share a little of the family's luck.'

Bai Feng could not refuse again. He went inside and ate at the place the servants had set for him.

After the reunion dinner the family sat talking. In the middle of it, Chen Haoyu arrived with Kuqiao, Matron Wei, and Xiangrong.

At the door he heard his wife's voice from inside.

'I'll stay with Father and Mother for a while. After that I'll live in the bamboo house. I'll wait there for him.'

Chen Haoyu shut his eyes. A few tears slipped out. He stepped into the room, and every voice stopped. Even the laughter died.

Zhu Xiyan signaled to the others. They all rose and went out.

Matron Wei and Kuqiao bowed to the master and mistress.

Lady Xiang's spirits had lifted with her mood. She took Matron Wei's hand. 'Come sit in my room a while.'

Matron Wei understood and helped her out after Zhu Xiyan.

Kuqiao started toward the inner room, but Chen Wenxin stopped her. 'What are you doing?'

'I haven't seen my mistress in over two years,' Kuqiao said. 'I miss her.'

'Not now. Bai Feng is here at the Zhu house. Go talk to him first.' She called a maid over. 'Take Kuqiao to see Bai Feng.'

Kuqiao glanced at the inner room, unwilling to go, but left.

Xiangrong watched Chen Haoyu's back as he disappeared into the room. Something sat bitter in her chest.

Chen Wenxin took her hand. 'Don't take it to heart. Haoyu had no choice but to end the marriage. He went to Bianliang to fetch her and missed her, then heard she was gone. Now she's back, safe. They have to have it out.'

Xiangrong made herself smile. 'I understand. Haoyu has always carried Shuzhen in his heart. If he kept those words locked up, they'd make him ill.'

Chen Wenxin glanced at the slight swell of her belly. 'Four months gone. You can't stand about. Come to my room and keep me company.'

Xiangrong nodded, stole one look at the room, and left with her.

Zhu Jingyuan also glanced in and sighed. 'Ah, Shuzhen's luck in love has never been easy. Let nothing else go wrong for her.'

With that, he followed Chen Wenxin and Xiangrong out.

When they were gone, Qianli Xun stood up. The sight of Chen Haoyu moved her so much she did not know how to begin.

He came closer, one step at a time, each step carrying both reproach and a joy he had not expected. When he was near enough, the tears fell. 'Why wasn't I the first to know? If Ku Jue hadn't come to the Chen house for Kuqiao, I'd have been the last.'

'I meant to come to the Chen house after seeing my family. I didn't expect you here first...'

He could hold back no longer. He stepped in and pulled her into his arms. 'I missed you so...'

She did not return the embrace. With tears in her eyes she said, 'I'm no longer... no longer your wife.'

He let her go little by little. 'Prince Yi lied to me. He's a scheming man. Step by step he took you from me...'

'That's not it. You've got him wrong.'

'Wrong? You were alive, and he acted the grieving man to make me think you were dead. He had an agenda!'

'He truly believed I was dead then. Someone had fooled him too. A poem of mine reached Lin'an. That's how he learned I was alive, and he rode to Yanjing to exchange himself for me.'

'Exchange himself? He's in Yanjing?'

'Wanyan Chi says he'll be released in a year.'

Tears and a bitter laugh mixed on Chen Haoyu's face. 'Hmf. He's always been shameless. Shameless enough to take every chance I had to be good to you. In Bianliang he knelt and wouldn't rise, and I lost my marriage. Then he sold everything he owned without a word and raised ten million taels of silver. Now he's gone alone to Yanjing and traded himself for you. Shameless, I tell you! So shameless I can't even bring myself to hate him. I don't want to lose you, and I have to lose you. Tell me—what am I supposed to do?'

'I hear... Xiangrong is carrying your child...'

'He told you that?' Chen Haoyu went on, tears in his voice. 'He's been plotting all along. If I'd known, I would have gone to Yanjing to find you. I was a fool to believe Matron Zou and run back to Lin'an. One wrong step, and then every other.'

'Chen Haoyu. It's done. All we can do is face it.'

He would not let go. 'The emperor who wrote that edict is no longer emperor. The edict should be void. If you're willing, we can restore our marriage tomorrow...'

'An imperial edict isn't so easily voided. And even if it could be—I'm afraid I... I can't go back...'

'Can't go back? What do you mean?'

'I intend to live in the bamboo house Prince Yi built, and wait for him.'

He had heard the same words at the door. Hearing them again from her lips finished him. He thought of that kiss in the prison cell and stared at her through tears. 'Answer me honestly. Did you ever feel anything for me?'

'I tried. I wanted us to have a good life. Things went against us. If none of this had happened, I might be carrying your child by now...'

'I asked whether you loved me.'

'I... I kept trying...'

'When you kissed me in that cell, was there love in it?'

Qianli Xun remembered how she had felt. It was the lowest point of her life. Chen Haoyu had stayed by her and would not leave, and that moved her. She had thought he would be the end of her road, that nothing between them could be wrong. That was why she had let him hold her, why she had kissed him.

His eyes were red with weeping, full of worry and longing. He had once been a wastrel, and she had hurt him more than he deserved. She could not bear to hurt him again.

'At that moment I... I think I loved you.'

At last Chen Haoyu smiled. 'I knew you had loved me...' He walked out of the room, satisfied and emptied by that love.

Qianli Xun said to herself, 'Chen Haoyu, thank you for coming into my life, thank you for loving me. Our fate ends here.'
