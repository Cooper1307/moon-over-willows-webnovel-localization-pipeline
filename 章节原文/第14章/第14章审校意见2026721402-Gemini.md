### 2. 表达是否正确且地道？（核心修改点）

*   **P17 致命的直译误区（十有八九）**：`nine out of ten people in the Zhu family couldn't be relied on.`
    *   **问题**：“十有八九”是一个表示概率的成语，意思是“大概率、极有可能”，而不是真的指“朱家10个人里有9个靠不住”。
    *   **修改**：`In all likelihood, no one in the Zhu family could be counted on.` 或 `Nine times out of ten, the Zhu family couldn't be relied upon.`
*   **P18 词汇过于学术（睡眠质量）**：`she had pretty good sleep quality that night.`
    *   **问题**：sleep quality 听起来像是在写医学报告。
    *   **修改**：`she slept soundly through the night.`
*   **P49 古诗词的口语化转换（柳暗花明又一村）**：`Just when the road seems to end, there's another village ahead.`
    *   **优化**：直译稍微有些生硬。女主在这里表达的是“天无绝人之路/事情有了转机”。
    *   **修改**：可以用更地道的英文俚语 **"Talk about a silver lining!"** 或者 **"When one door closes, another opens."**
*   **P55 渣男的深层痛骂**：`He's truly a sinister villain!`
    *   **优化**：villain（反派）略显书面。女主发现林觉明明没自己有钱，还厚颜无耻地答应给她钱花，这里应该骂他虚伪、厚脸皮。

*   **P77 情绪词的降维打击（谬论）**：`Fallacy! Fallacy! It's simply a fallacy!`
    *   **问题**：Fallacy 是一个非常高级的学术词汇（逻辑谬误）。女主作为一个现代暴躁大女主，听到“女子无才便是德”这种言论，第一反应绝对是骂脏话或者觉得荒谬。
    *   **修改**：`"Bullshit! Absolute bullshit! That is completely absurd!"`（如果你希望小说全年龄向，可以用 `Absurd! Absolute garbage!`）

    *   **优化**：“见外”不宜直译为 distant，通常表达为“太客气了、不像一家人”。

**Chapter 14: Wait, Turns Out I'm Loaded!**

When Qianli Xun walked out of the main hall, Kuqiao was already waiting outside, lantern in hand. Seeing her miss emerge, she hurried forward. "I thought I'd have to wait much longer. I didn't expect you to come out so soon, Miss."
Qianli Xun didn't respond. She just kept walking straight ahead. However, once she left the main courtyard, she realized she had no idea where she was going and was forced to stop.
Kuqiao could tell her miss was deeply upset. Not daring to ask too many questions, she had simply followed quietly. But seeing her stop, Kuqiao knew she was lost and quickly stepped to the front. "Just follow me, Miss."
And so began another round of weaving through winding paths.
Qianli Xun was in no mood to appreciate the secluded, moonlit trails of the Zhu estate. Her mind was entirely consumed with what to do next. Her father refused to step forward, which meant no one in the Zhu family would dare speak for her. Was this amicable divorce doomed to fail? Did transmigrating into Zhu Shuzhen's body mean she was actually forced to live out her days with Lin Jue?
Zhu Shuzhen had left very few historical records for later generations. Based on what little remained, she and Lin Jue never officially divorced but simply lived apart for years. Later, she met a man she truly loved and engaged in an extramarital affair, only to be separated from him by war. In the end, she died of a broken heart, passing away in her early forties.
Was she, Qianli Xun, destined to suffer the exact same fate? 
Absolutely not! She would change this destiny! And it had to start with ending this toxic marriage.
Lost in thought, she hardly noticed they had already arrived at her garden. The paths in the Zhu residence were truly bizarre—when you paid close attention, it felt like you'd never reach your destination; but when your mind wandered, you arrived in no time.
Kuqiao pushed open the garden gate, holding the lantern high to light the way. Not a single lantern hung in the courtyard; it was pitch black. Thinking of her father's brightly lit main courtyard, Qianli Xun said, "This courtyard is too dark. We need to hang more lanterns."
Hearing their voices, Lutao came out of the house to greet them. "You're back, Miss. I've already made the bed. You may rest whenever you wish."
"Lutao, have you always been in charge of this courtyard?" Qianli Xun asked.
"Yes, Miss."
"Tomorrow, find a few maids and hang more lanterns out here, just like in my father's main courtyard. Light them at night so the place feels brighter and more alive."
Lutao looked completely baffled. "But... we used to have lanterns hung in the courtyard. You disliked them, Miss, and ordered me to have the servants take them all down. Are you... absolutely sure you want to hang them back up?"
"Positive! Absolutely certain!" Qianli Xun declared, stepping into the room.
Kuqiao handed the lantern to Lutao. "Sister, just do as the Miss instructed."
Taking the lantern, Lutao muttered softly as she walked away, "I heard the other maids gossiping that the Miss is completely different now, and they were right. Whatever. Whatever the Miss says, goes. I'll have someone hang the lanterns up in a few days."

Under Kuqiao's care, Qianli Xun climbed into bed, her mind still racing. Judging from the current situation, in all likelihood, no one in the Zhu family could be counted on. She feared even her brother and sister-in-law would eventually cave to her father's authority. She had arrived today brimming with hope, only to have it all shattered by her cold, expressionless father.
Yet, despite her heavy thoughts, Qianli Xun had always been the type of person who knocked out the second her head hit the pillow. She slept incredibly soundly through the night.
...
When the first rooster crow of the morning echoed through the air, her eyes snapped open. It was the first time in her life she had ever been woken up by an actual rooster. The experience was quite novel.
Pushing aside the bed curtains, Qianli Xun slipped out of bed, threw on an outer robe, and pushed open the window. A burst of fresh, crisp morning air hit her face. She took a deep breath. "Smells so good. It smells like nature."
She spotted a rooster standing proudly on the courtyard wall. Its red comb was bright and eye-catching, its neck thick and powerful, and its feathers a glossy black with hints of crimson. It stretched its neck and let out another series of crisp, loud crows: *"Cock-a-doodle-doo..."*
Woken up by the racket, Kuqiao stumbled out of the maids' quarters, snatched a pebble off the ground, and hurled it at the rooster. "Keep that racket down!" 
Startled, the rooster flapped its wings frantically and flew over the wall, disappearing from sight.
"I chased it away, Miss," Kuqiao yawned. "Go back to sleep for a bit."
"I'm wide awake now. Might as well get up."
Kuqiao entered the room and began helping her miss dress and wash up.
"Why on earth is there a rooster roaming around the Zhu residence?" Qianli Xun asked.
"It could belong to one of the servants, or it might belong to the Buddhist shrine."
"The Buddhist shrine?" Qianli Xun was confused.
As she adjusted her miss's collar, Kuqiao explained, "There is a Buddhist ancestral shrine within the Zhu estate. I've heard there is a venerable ancestor living inside who is over a hundred years old. But neither you nor I have ever seen her. Mostly because she stays shut inside the shrine and never comes out."
"Has my father ever seen her?"
"I don't know. I've certainly never seen the Master go to the shrine."
"This Zhu residence really is full of deep, unfathomable secrets," Qianli Xun mused. "But what makes you automatically assume the rooster is from the shrine?"
"It's just a habit everyone in the manor has formed. Whenever we see something strange or out of place, we just say it 'might be from the shrine.' I'm just so used to it that I blurted it out."
"I see."
Qianli Xun wasn't in the mood to dwell on some mysterious ancestral shrine. Once Kuqiao finished styling her hair, she instructed, "Kuqiao, don't bother whisking tea for me this morning. Just get me something simple to drink. I want to go see my brother and sister-in-law early and find out if they can still support me."
"Did the Master refuse to back your divorce last night, Miss?" Kuqiao asked worriedly.
Qianli Xun nodded grimly.
"Then you shouldn't even bother asking, Miss. The eldest young master and young mistress definitely won't step forward for you now."
"Because of my father?" Even though she already knew the answer, Qianli Xun couldn't help but ask.
Kuqiao nodded. "It has always been this way in the Zhu family. If the Master says no, absolutely no one dares to defy him. They can only wring their hands in anxiety."
"But why?!" Qianli Xun demanded.
"The Zhu family rules are extremely strict. You could say the Master *is* the family law."
"Hmph! What family law! If you ask me, he's a damn fascist!"
Kuqiao had no idea what a "fascist" was. Her changed miss was always spouting bizarre words she couldn't comprehend.
"Let me ask you," Qianli Xun pivoted, "does the Zhu family have a bunch of clansmen and elders like the Lin family does?"
"Yes, there are five or six of them living right here in Lin'an City. They are all your paternal uncles."
Qianli Xun instantly perked up. "Talk about a silver lining! When one door closes, another opens. Today, I'll go visit every single one of these uncles. I refuse to believe I can't persuade at least one of them to take my side!"
However, she quickly realized that showing up empty-handed would seem insincere. To convince powerful elders to stick their necks out for her, she needed to bring valuable gifts—or better yet, just stuff their hands with silver. She patted her pockets. Completely empty. Having just transmigrated, she hadn't needed to buy anything yet, and she had completely forgotten to pack any money when she stormed out of the Lin residence.
"Kuqiao, how about you run back to the Lin residence and ask Lin Jue for some silver? He agreed to give me spending money."
"If you need silver, Miss, I can go back and fetch some. But there is absolutely no need to ask the young master."
"What do you mean? If I don't ask Lin Jue, who else would I ask?"
"Miss, when you married the young master, you brought an immense dowry with you, including massive tracts of farmland and several storefronts! The rent you collect every single year is more than you could ever spend. Lin Jue isn't nearly as wealthy as you are!"
"What?!" Qianli Xun shot up from her chair. "I'm *that* rich?! Then what the hell do I have to be afraid of?! The old saying goes, 'marry a man, get clothed and fed.' But I don't rely on Lin Jue for my clothes or my food! Why on earth should I keep suffering with him?! This marriage is over! And when I leave, I am taking every single penny of my dowry with me! Ugh, I was so stupid! When I made that three-point agreement with him and demanded he give me spending money, that bastard actually smiled and agreed! He's a two-faced, scheming hypocrite! Kuqiao, the second we finish breakfast, you are going to the Lin residence to fetch my silver. The more, the better!"
"You got it!"

Just as they were speaking, Lutao brought servants in to set up breakfast. Kuqiao stayed by her miss as she ate, then tidied up the writing desk. Just as Kuqiao was about to head to the Lin residence, Yinxing's voice called out from the courtyard. "The Madam has come to see the Miss."
"Hold off on going to the Lin residence for a second," Qianli Xun told Kuqiao. "Let's see what my mother has to say first."
Madam Xiang hurried into the room. Seeing her daughter standing there to welcome her, she stepped forward and clutched her hands tightly. "Shuzhen, I didn't sleep a wink last night. Even though you are different now, you are still my flesh and blood. I just hope... I hope you don't resent your father."
"If you, Brother, and Sister-in-law can still stand up for me during the divorce, I won't blame him at all," Qianli Xun replied honestly.
Madam Xiang released her daughter's hands, her face twisting in anguish. "As long as your father disagrees, no one in this family will dare to speak for you."
"If that's the case, Mother, then don't blame me for resenting him. My father is a cold-blooded animal. He doesn't care one bit about his daughter's lifelong happiness; he only cares about his so-called 'reputation.' I honestly don't understand—how much could my amicable divorce from Lin Jue possibly damage his precious reputation?!"
Madam Xiang sank heavily into a chair. "Sit down. Let Mother explain it to you slowly."
Qianli Xun obediently took a seat. "What do you want to tell me, Mother?"
Madam Xiang first turned to Kuqiao. "Go and whisk two bowls of tea for us."
Kuqiao nodded and headed to the tea table. Yinxing, who was fascinated by the art of tea-making, happily trotted over to watch.
Madam Xiang let out a long, heavy sigh. "You were incredibly brilliant from a young age. You loved reading, and you were entirely self-taught. By the age of ten, you were already composing poetry and *ci*. Your father boasted that you inherited his literary genius, so he allowed you to frequently showcase your talents at poetry banquets. Soon, your title as 'The Poetess of Jiangnan' spread far and wide. Back then, our family was very close to the Xu family. You and Young Master Xu were childhood sweethearts. You were innocently devoted to each other, and the adults saw the spark between you two. We were thrilled, fully intending to arrange a marriage between the two families..."
Qianli Xun had zero interest in Zhu Shuzhen's past romantic drama. "Why are you telling me all this, Mother? It has nothing to do with my divorce."
"If you still acknowledge that you are Shuzhen, then sit and listen to me."
Qianli Xun had no choice but to bite her tongue and listen.
"Then, one day, the Xu family suddenly announced they were moving north," Madam Xiang continued sadly. "They claimed the business climate in the south was poor and that they were relocating all their operations. But regarding your impending engagement to their son, they didn't utter a single syllable. After they left, your father and I lied to you. We told you their family had fallen into financial ruin and was forced to move. 
"Much later, while attending a gathering of official wives in Lin'an, I accidentally overheard the truth. The real reason the Xu family fled was that they desperately wanted to back out of the marriage. Apparently, a fortune-teller had read their family's *feng shui* and warned them that if their son married a 'talented literary woman,' the family would face absolute ruin and every business venture would fail..."
"Bullshit!" Qianli Xun cursed, unable to help but feel enraged on the real Zhu Shuzhen's behalf.
Kuqiao had finished whisking the tea. She and Yinxing brought the bowls over and placed them on the table.
Qianli Xun was in no mood to savor it. She picked up the bowl, downed it in one gulp, and slammed it down. "Mother, you and Father should be celebrating! Thank God you didn't marry me off to the Xu family. From the sound of it, they are shallow, superstitious fools not worth marrying into anyway!"
Madam Xiang delicately picked up her tea and took a small sip. "Your father is the Prefect of Lin'an. His rank is high. The Xu family couldn't afford to offend him outright, so they fabricated the relocation excuse. In truth, they still run businesses right here in Lin'an; they just moved their household north to avoid us. Later on, Young Master Xu married someone else. But my poor daughter... you waited for him for five or six years." Madam Xiang began to dab her tears again.
"Why didn't you and Father just tell me the truth?" Qianli Xun demanded.
Gathering her emotions, Madam Xiang replied, "Because we were terrified it would break your heart! Your father may seem cold to you, but he truly does care. He deeply regretted ever exposing you to poetry and letting you attend those banquets, but by then, it was too late to stop it. Your reputation was known throughout Jiangnan. You were surrounded by like-minded scholars, many of them talented young men. Your father would often hear vicious rumors and filthy gossip about you in the streets. 
"It was then he finally understood the old saying: *'A woman's virtue lies in her lack of talent.'* Right at that moment, the Lin family came to propose. After careful consideration, your father saw that Lin Jue was the exact opposite of you. He believed that marrying a traditional man like him would force you to wake up, abandon your poetry, and learn to simply support a husband and raise children—which he believed was the only way to save you from your own 'talent.' Besides, you were getting older. He was terrified that if you didn't marry then, you'd be ruined forever."
"Absurd! That is completely and utterly absurd!" Qianli Xun snapped angrily. "You can see the results for yourself, Mother! Marrying Lin Jue brought Zhu Shuzhen nothing but agony! Even *I* want to divorce him!"
Her phrasing instantly brought a fresh wave of sorrow to Madam Xiang's eyes. "You... you truly don't believe you are Shuzhen?"
Qianli Xun immediately realized she had gotten too worked up and broken character. "Mother, of course I am your daughter."
Madam Xiang stood up, walked over, and cupped her daughter's face. "Let Mother look at you. Yes... you are Shuzhen. My Shuzhen is still alive."
Moved by the woman's genuine love, Qianli Xun stood up as well. "No matter what I become, you are my mother."
"Mother knows," Madam Xiang said softly, gently tucking a stray hair behind Qianli Xun's ear. "I told you all this so you would understand that your father’s intentions, however misguided, came from a place of protecting you. Do not hate him. 
"Now, Mother has an idea for you. Since your father refuses to step forward, you must seek out the elders of the Zhu clan. You have six uncles living right here in Lin'an City. I have already sent word to your brother. He will escort you to visit them today. He is waiting outside the estate for you right now."
Overjoyed, Qianli Xun threw her arms around Madam Xiang. "Mother, you are the greatest mother in the entire world!"
Madam Xiang patted her daughter's back affectionately. "You're about to get a divorce, yet you're this happy."
Pulling back, Qianli Xun asked, "Mother, should I bring money or expensive gifts to bribe my uncles?"
Madam Xiang smiled and shook her head. "They are your own family. Bringing money or bribes would be far too formal and make you seem like a stranger."
Qianli Xun couldn't wait another second. "Mother, I'm going to find Brother right now! Kuqiao, what are you standing there for? Let's go!"
Watching her daughter's retreating back—a figure so deeply familiar yet entirely foreign—Madam Xiang let out a long sigh. "I just hope your uncles don't let your father's stance deter them."
