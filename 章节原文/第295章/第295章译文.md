## Chapter 295: Meeting a Famous Poet at the Salon

Dongfang Chenze had booked all of Juxiang Tower for the day, hanging a “Closed to Walk-Ins” sign out front beside a bold new banner: “Dongfang Poetry Salon.”

Qianli Xun stepped inside with Chen Haoyu and Chen Haofei. Along the main ground floor stood a tasteful stage crowned by a horizontal scroll reading “Dongfang Poetry Salon,” flanked by a couplet from Tang poet Bai Juyi: “At leisure we trade refined drinking games through classics and histories; tipsy, we hear clear chanting sweeter than pipes and strings.” [TN: Translator’s rendering] At center stage stood a carved rack for hanging poem scrolls beside a guqin zither.

Early as they were, scores of guests had already arrived—not a single woman in sight, confirming how smart bringing Chen Haoyu had been.

Spotting friends across the floor, Chen Haofei fidgeted. “Second Sister-in-law, mind if I go say hello?”

“Go right ahead.”

Haofei dashed off without a glance at his brother. Chen Haoyu pouted for show. “Look at him—totally ignores me and only asks you! Wait till we get home, I’m lecturing him.”

“Third Aunt worried he’d feel lost and asked me to look out for him—turns out he knows twice as many folks here as I do!”

Right then Dongfang Chenze, Cao Ban, and An Mingxuan hurried over.

“Youqi Jushi’s arrival makes the Dongfang Salon shine!” Dongfang Chenze beamed.

Keeping up her modest poetess front, Qianli Xun replied, “You flatter me, Young Master Dongfang. With this hall packed with distinguished scholars—and royals and officials due any minute—my humble light fades away. Better I tuck myself into a quiet corner.”

Cao Ban grinned. “If Youqi Jushi hides in a corner, the rest of us will have to dig holes in the floor!”

Laughter rippled through the group. Chen Haoyu had no patience for stuffy scholars, yet since his wife was trading banter, he plastered on a polite smile—no earthly clue what was supposed to be funny.

“Brought a fresh poem, Youqi Jushi?” An Mingxuan asked.

“Of course.”

“There’s a live brush-and-ink round later—we’re counting on you to dazzle us.”

Qianli Xun answered with a smile. Mindful not to snub her husband, Dongfang Chenze turned to Chen Haoyu. “Winning Youqi Jushi’s hand made half of Lin’an jealous, Young Master Chen—and attending a poetry salon under her influence shows true conjugal bliss!”

That hit the sweet spot—he loved the sound of “conjugal bliss.” “Right you are! Wherever my wife goes from now on, I stick like a shadow.”

The three young masters traded amazed glances—rumors of the wild playboy’s total overhaul were true after all!

Pointing to a table, Dongfang Chenze added, “We seated you over there. Only three women are attending today, so we paired you with Miss Xiangrong—hope you don’t mind.”

“Mind? Inviting Xiangrong was brilliant—haven’t caught up with her in ages! Wait, who’s the third woman? Why isn’t she seated with us?”

“Princess Consort Yi,” An Mingxuan answered. “She insisted on sitting beside Prince Yi.”

Mengrong really was coming. A wistful pang hit Qianli Xun—how she would’ve loved trading poems and laughs at the same table—yet those days were gone. “Her Highness is royalty, naturally she sits apart. Go tend your guests; I’ll head over and wait for Xiangrong.”

Taking seats at their table, Qianli Xun spotted a jug of rice wine, four cups, and—oddly—a plate of green stir-fried vegetables. “Why a dish of greens? Are we writing poems about dinner?”

Chen Haoyu peered at it. “Looks like wild mountain greens. Heard Lazybones Village’s soil only grows wild veggies, and Prince Yi asked Father-in-law to open a market—bet Father-in-law is promoting them here.”

Qianli Xun tilted her head. “Stuck in the silk shop all day—how do you know that?”

“Father stopped by the shop a couple times and chatted with me.”

Before she could reply, a knot of unfamiliar scholars—several graying at the temples—came over to pay respects. “Long admired Youqi Jushi’s fame—meeting you today is an honor!”

Careful not to tarnish Zhu Shuzhen’s reputation, Qianli Xun rose with a modest curtsy. “Sheltered in the inner quarters, my knowledge is narrow—forgive me for not recognizing you gentlemen.”

One older scholar chuckled. “Don’t mind us—yet you must meet Ye Shaoyun here.”

Blanking on “Ye Shaoyun,” Qianli Xun stared at the man in his forties, kicking herself for skipping literary history in college—unless he was a local celebrity lost to time.

The man smiled and recited softly, “Fallen petals have danced upon the wind, only to send off the dusk in rain; by dawn the courtyard lies half strewn with fading crimson...” [TN: Translator’s rendering]

Recognition snapped—she blurted before he finished, “You’re Ye Mengde?!”

“Indeed I am.”

She knew several of Ye Mengde’s lyrics—by the calendar, he’d be in his forties now, serving as a court official! “Wait—as an imperial official, shouldn’t you be arriving with Prince Yi and the Prefect?”

“Traveled from Yingchang to Huzhou for the completion of my retreat in Shilin Valley, then swung by Lin’an to see old friends,” Ye Mengde laughed. “Neither Prince Yi nor the Prefect knows I’m here—much freer this way!”

Doing quick math, she curtsied low. “Prefect Ye’s carefree spirit fills Shuzhen with admiration.” Chen Haoyu hopped up to bow too. “Lord Prefect!”

Ye Mengde waved it off with a laugh. “No prefects today—only fellow poets making friends through verse!”

Everyone chuckled warm and easy before Ye Mengde excused himself to greet other friends.

Sitting back down, Chen Haoyu asked, “Big celebrity?”

“Huge—his name goes straight into the history books.”

“What about you?”

“Youqi Jushi goes into history too, of course.”

“Think folks in future centuries will know my name?”

“Why ask that?”

“Because I’m your husband!”

Caught off guard, she offered a vague smile. “Maybe.”

Wave after wave of scholars stopped by next, Chen Haoyu bowing beside his wife, swelling with pride at her star power and scheming how to slip “husband of Youqi Jushi” into every hello.

Right then a vision of grace strolled up. In his old life, Chen Haoyu would have ogled and flirted nonstop; bound by his two-year vow, he didn’t feel a flicker of stray thought.

His wife beamed and jumped up to clasp the newcomer’s hands. “What took you so long? Sit—let’s catch up!”

Taking a seat, Xiangrong glanced curious at Chen Haoyu. “And this is...?”

Chen Haoyu chimed in proud, “She’s my wife, and I’m her husband!”

“Second Young Master Chen! A pleasure!”

“I’m that famous?”

“With Youqi Jushi’s fame, everyone naturally follows who she marries.”

“Too much fame brings nothing but gossip—better staying low-key,” Qianli Xun sighed, then introduced her. “My close friend Miss Xiangrong—unmatched on the zither and singing. Wait till she blows you away later!”

Recognizing her name from Huayun Pavilion—a refined venue he’d found too tame back in his rowdy Chunxiang Brothel days—Chen Haoyu admired her ladylike poise and decided to flex a little literary polish for his wife’s sake. “Li Bai wrote: ‘Her face is seen in flower and her dress in cloud.’ [TN: Translated by Xu Yuanchong] Miss Xiangrong’s name must come from ‘hua xiang rong’ (花想容, ‘flowers recall her face’), right?”

*Look at him pulling Li Bai out of thin air!* Qianli Xun marveled inside.

Xiangrong winked slyly at Qianli Xun. “It does come from that line, except my ‘rong’ is written with the character for floss-silk flowers (绒), not appearance (容).”

“Swapping appearance for silk-flower ‘rong’—even cleverer!” Chen Haoyu praised.

Qianli Xun shot him a mock glare. “She’s my friend—I barely got two words in and you won’t stop talking!”

Leaning close to her ear, he whispered, “Just trying to look sharp in front of your friend so she thinks I’m worthy of you!”

She whispered right back, “Save the act—all of Lin’an knows what you’re like.”

Shutting his mouth at once lest she think he was sweet on a pretty girl, Chen Haoyu sat quiet while Xiangrong hid a giggle behind her silk handkerchief.

“What’s funny?” Qianli Xun asked.

Lowering her handkerchief, Xiangrong smiled. “Street gossip claims Youqi Jushi is a fierce tigress who scared Young Master Chen straight. Nonsense—you two are in total harmony, the wife leading and the husband right behind!”

Savoring that phrase, Chen Haoyu glowed, while Qianli Xun felt the quiet truth beneath: hard as the road had been, she was willing to walk it with him.

Before they could chat more, Zhu Xiyan led Lin’an’s officials into the hall surrounding Prince Yi, Qiao Mengrong walking at his side wearing a proud, regal smile.

Every guest rose in unison. “Greetings, Your Highness Prince Yi and Princess Consort Yi!”

Prince Yi raised a hand. “Please be seated—no need for formality.”

Once the royals and officials took the center seats, Zhu Xiyan remained standing. “Gentlemen, thank you for coming! Prince Yi poured heart and silver into the Dongfang Salon, and Young Master Dongfang worked tirelessly—normally I’d step aside, yet for the people’s livelihood, allow me one brief word.” Sweeping the room, he pointed to the tables. “Notice the green dish before you? Please taste it.”

Chopsticks rose across the hall. “Crisp and refreshing—a superb appetizer!” Xiangrong praised, Qianli Xun nodding along as murmurs of praise rippled around.

“These wild greens come straight from Lazybones Village, cooked by Juxiang Tower’s chefs,” Zhu Xiyan announced. “At Prince Yi’s urging to give Lazybones Village and Lin’an’s vagrants an honest trade, I’ve decreed every tavern and inn in Lin’an serve this dish—only it needs a classy, memorable name! I’m imposing on your poetic genius today. Over to you, Young Master Dongfang!”

Stepping onstage for the shining hour of his life, Dongfang Chenze bowed to the royals, officials, and crowd. “Today’s opening round has a twist! Instead of recommending new verse by peers, nominate classical poems—about wild greens or anything fitting—and draw a dish name from the lines! Prince Yi will choose the winner!”

“Fresh and clever!” Qianli Xun praised—and caught Qiao Mengrong’s stare from across the floor, half boast, half warning. Qianli Xun returned a polite nod and smile; Qiao Mengrong froze her face and turned away.

Beside her, Chen Haoyu scanned the sea of scholars hunting for Qiao Wenxian—ready to put on a dazzling display of marital bliss the instant he spotted him.
