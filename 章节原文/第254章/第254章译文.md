## Chapter 254: Everyone Critiques the Poem and Reviles Shuzhen

The venomous edge in Bai Meiruo's voice pricked Qianli Xun's pride. She flicked a glance toward Mengrong—who sat sipping fermented rice wine with a cold smirk—and swallowed the curse perched on her tongue.

"Silence means consent," Bai Meiruo went on. "Still, why do you insist on writing nothing but gloomy, tear-soaked verses? Don't bother answering—I'll tell everyone for you. Ladies! Guess which sort of woman men adore most: a fragile, weeping beauty who looks ready to faint, or a fierce, gorgeous tigress?"

The young woman seated beside Qianli Xun answered, "The first kind, obviously."

"Wrong," Bai Meiruo declared.

"The second, then?" another guest asked.

Bai Meiruo shook her head. "Wrong again."

"Stop keeping us in suspense, Madam Bai—spit it out!" cried the third girl.

Rising, Bai Meiruo sauntered to the center of the hall, dipped a curtsy to Princess Consort Yi, and faced the room. "Men crave a woman who is *both* at once—and Youqi Jushi is the living embodiment! Everyone's heard how wicked her fists are, and Madam Miao has seen them firsthand. Even my own husband is no match for her—just yesterday he was griping again, scolding me for knowing nothing about poetry or martial arts! Imagine that: mutually divorced and still hung up on his ex-wife! Doesn't that prove how irresistible Youqi Jushi is?"

Qianli Xun snapped. "Bai Meiruo, this is a poetry salon—stop airing your family's dirty laundry in here!"

Qiao Mengrong stepped right in. "Why snap at her, Youqi Jushi? She's miserable at home because of you—letting her blow off a little steam here does no harm. My mistake, I forgot to mention today's house rules: everyone speaks freely, critiquing your poetry while getting whatever grievances they have off their chests. Really, as the famed Poetess of Jiangnan, how can you be so narrow-minded? You're allowed to wallow in fake anguish in your poems, yet nobody else is allowed to vent their real frustrations over them?"

The whole room chimed in at once. "Please, let us use your lyric to vent our frustrations!"

Holding up one corner of the scroll while everyone gang-attacked her mistress made Kuqiao itch to drop the roller, grab Miss by the hand, and storm right out. One look over her shoulder at the latched front doors reminded her why they had been shut. Catching Kuqiao tensing, Qianli Xun gave a slight shake of her head. Kuqiao tightened her grip on the scroll, seething right alongside her mistress.

So this whole spectacle was Mengrong using the other women's mouths to bleed off her rage. Fine—Qianli Xun would say one line, then zip her lips and take whatever venom they threw. "By all means, ladies. Help yourselves!"

Qiao Mengrong offered mock praise. "Spoken like the grand Poetess of Jiangnan—magnanimous indeed. Continue, Madam Bai."

Dipping another curtsy to the Princess Consort, Bai Meiruo pivoted back to her audience. "Word has it Youqi Jushi marched into the Chen household and whipped Second Young Master Chen right into line until he's practically a brand-new man—there's the tigress charm in action! Now look at this lyric: *my spirit hurts*, *a light cold*, *tears have washed off half my makeup*, *sorrow and sickness have joined hands*... tsk, tsk, tsk! What a pathetic, drooping Xi Shi—guaranteed to trigger every man's urge to sweep in and protect her! Especially that word *alone*, hammered home five times in a row. Care to guess what message Youqi Jushi is trying to broadcast?"

Smirks rippled around the tables as brushes scratched across paper.

Not waiting for an answer, Bai Meiruo pressed on. "This lyric is fresh off the brush, meaning she wrote it *after* marrying into the Chen family. You're already wed, and your husband has already reformed—what more could you possibly want? Yet here you are broadcasting your loneliness and playing the fragile invalid, making everyone think marrying into the Chen family is some sort of torture!"

Miao Zhiyu jumped in on cue. "From what I hear, Youqi Jushi is living like a queen over at Chen Manor. She took over Young Master Chen's courtyard, pocketed his assets, charmed Old Madam Xia, landed the kindest in-laws imaginable, and even whipped her husband into shape. Every woman in town envies her—what 'torture' could she possibly be suffering?"

"There is only one explanation," Bai Meiruo declared.

"What is it?" the others chorused.

"Picture yourselves as men for a moment," Bai Meiruo purred. "Reading a lyric like this from Youqi Jushi, wouldn't your hearts melt—and wouldn't all those whispers of being *alone* set your blood itching with desire?"

Tittering behind their sleeves, the guests hid their grins.

Qianli Xun's fists clenched white. Every muscle begged to stride over and smash Bai Meiruo's smug face flat—yet one glance at Mengrong's glowing satisfaction made her uncurl her fingers.

Kuqiao could hardly believe her ears. Turning a heartfelt lyric into cheap seduction—how shameless could this Madam Bai get? Her mistress had to be boiling inside! Yet glancing sideways at Qianli Xun, she saw the flash of fury ebb away into calm restraint. Miss valued Princess Consort Yi too much; that was the only reason she kept swallowing this abuse.

Bai Meiruo turned toward Miao Zhiyu. "Madam Miao, you got this poem from Young Master Qiao, which means he's already read it. Keep him on a tight leash, lest someone hook his soul right out of his chest."

Miao Zhiyu laid down her brush with cool poise. "My Wenyuan may have been fond of a few stray flowers in his youth, but that's ancient history. Ever since our wedding, he has had eyes only for me—hardly the sort to be bewitched by one lyric. He's simply a connoisseur of verse who collects fresh pieces so he never turns up empty-handed at literary gatherings."

"Congratulations on finding such a devoted husband, Madam Miao," Bai Meiruo simpered, then returned to her seat, glowing with triumph after dumping months of pent-up spite.

Miao Zhiyu tipped a glance toward the friend sitting diagonally across from her.

Catching the signal, that young woman set down her brush. "Princess Consort Yi, ladies—just now Her Highness used the phrase *wallowing in fake anguish*. I couldn't think of a sharper verdict, so let me use that as my theme to dissect Youqi Jushi's lyric." Raising her wine cup with theatrical mock-courtesy, she nodded at Qianli Xun. "My apologies in advance, Youqi Jushi."

Qianli Xun didn't spare her a blink. *Have your fun running your mouths,* she thought. *Once every last one of you runs out of spit and venom, I'll take you apart one by one.*

Draining her cup, the young woman set it down and launched in. "Late spring is still gorgeous weather, yet instead of celebrating the season's beauty, Youqi Jushi stands around moaning that *standing lone, my spirit hurts*, and whines about *a light cold*—how affected! With a reputation already in tatters—plopping herself right into a man's lap in public without a shred of shame—she still has the nerve to write, *Who can see how tears have washed off half my makeup*, acting as though she's the poor wronged victim! Strangers might think she's nursing a broken heart, whereas those of us who see right through her know she's a master of seduction with flirtatious eyes that steal a man's wits in a single glance. After breaking so many other women's hearts, why put on this simpering act in verse—claiming *sorrow and sickness have joined hands*? Look at her cheeks, ladies! Rosy and glowing with health! Pure fake anguish cooked up to fish for sympathy."

"Well said!" Qiao Mengrong applauded. "Especially *master of seduction*—fits Youqi Jushi to a tee! She hooks men's souls out of thin air without even leaving a mark. Madam Miao and Madam Bai had best watch their husbands closely—and you three girls, since Madam Miao tells me you've just been betrothed, had better keep a close eye on your fiancés too."

"Your Highness's warning is wise indeed!" the guests chorused. "We will take it to heart."

*Princess Consort Yi and this pack of vipers have gone way too far!* Kuqiao cursed silently. *How do such filthy words even leave their mouths?* Shooting a worried glance at her mistress, she saw calm on the surface, yet sensed a volcano banked underneath—if Miss blew her lid, even Princess Consort Yi's royal title wouldn't save them. Glancing next at Youfan, who wore the same smug smirk as her mistress, Kuqiao shot her a scorching glare; Youfan merely tossed her head and gave a haughty sniff. Stuck holding the scroll, Kuqiao silently cursed every soul in the room, the Princess Consort included.

As the others scribbled furiously, the young woman seated right next to Qianli Xun put down her brush and turned to face her. "Youqi Jushi, I used to enjoy reading your verses, yet today's piece leaves me speechless. Sure, the character count, rhyme scheme, and tonal patterns obey *Jianzi Mulan Hua*—but the artistic vision is pathetic, leagues beneath Yi'an Jushi or Dongpo Jushi. Strip it down, and it's just a bitter wife whining in her boudoir. What are you so resentful over, Youqi Jushi? Regretting who you married, or something else?"

Qianli Xun stayed silent, answering every jab with cool stillness.

The girl pivoted to address the room. "Regretting her husband, most likely! *I trim the lamp's wick till it's gone and dream still does not come*—married twice already and still mooning over dreams like a lovesick maiden! Who is it you're dreaming of—or longing to dream of and failing to reach? The man you couldn't resist *falling asleep fully dressed in his arms* for? Or the one from your line, *It's simple enough to watch the sky, hard to catch a glimpse of him*?" [TN: Translated by Sophie Volpp]

The final guest, who hadn't spoken yet, chimed right in. "Maybe it's the exact same man!"

"Possible," the girl beside Qianli Xun snickered, "though if it were only one man, how could Youqi Jushi showcase her irresistible charm?"

Snickers burst around the room.

Qianli Xun's patience hovered at the snapping point. One last speaker left—the second she finished, Qianli Xun was going to roast every single person in this hall till their ears smoked!

When the laughter died down, the last young woman stepped into her turn. "My turn to weigh in. Summing up everyone's critiques, one verdict is obvious: Youqi Jushi is an embarrassment to the title Poetess of Jiangnan. Better step down on your own before your verses sink even lower and you get chased out of literary circles with no face left. Look at this poem's subtitle—*Spring Resentment*—yet there isn't a breath of spring anywhere in it, nothing but *resentment*! Cover the title and anyone would swear it's about late autumn. Who'd believe a famous poetess goes off-topic like a clueless toddler? Yet here it is, right under our noses! Take a bit of friendly advice, Youqi Jushi: quit poetry while you still can."

Brushes flew across the remaining sheets, recording every barb.

Eyeing Zhu Shuzhen's unflappable mask with growing irritation, Qiao Mengrong cut in. "I showed better foresight than all of you. I saw ages ago that Youqi Jushi's verses lacked vision and depth, so I burned every single poem of hers I had ever collected. I suggest you all do the same."

Every guest dropped her brush and bowed in unison. "We will follow Princess Consort Yi's example!"

With a wave of Qiao Mengrong's hand, Youfan yanked the scroll out of Kuqiao's grip, rolled it shut, and stepped back behind Miao Zhiyu. Caught off guard by the rude snatch, Kuqiao bristled with pent-up rage, biting her tongue as she marched back to stand behind her mistress.

"Does Youqi Jushi have anything to say?" Qiao Mengrong asked.

Handing her the floor? Nothing could suit Qianli Xun better. Time to vent the bile churning in her gut—and more importantly, clear the real Zhu Shuzhen's literary name so her transmigration didn't trash the Poetess of Jiangnan's legacy!

Standing up, Qianli Xun strode to the center of the room and bowed to Mengrong—for the first time, fully accepting the cold gulf of the title *Princess Consort Yi*.

"Princess Consort Yi, I accepted your invitation expecting a friendly poetry gathering. Instead, every single speaker trained her knives on me, ripping me apart until I wasn't worth a copper. I've bit my tongue up to now, and I've reached my limit—if I don't fire back, I'll choke on it."

"Oh? Do you mean to fire back at *me* too?"

"Whatever Your Highness spoke true, I accept without complaint. Wherever you spoke false, allow me to clear my name."

"Humph! Clear your name, indeed! Say whatever you please." Qiao Mengrong flashed the guests a knowing smile, and the women smiled right back.

Qianli Xun swung straight onto Miao Zhiyu. "Madam Miao, you and I attended Young Master Dongfang's poetry salons together plenty of times. Why didn't you ever trash my poetry back then?"

"Back then, your poetry was decent enough," Miao Zhiyu countered.

"What a joke! My poetry has *always* been good—the only thing that changed is your attitude toward me! Just because Wenyuan and I had a past, you've been nursing a grudge! Don't forget this, Madam Miao: if I hadn't stepped aside and let Wenyuan go, you wouldn't even be sitting in the Qiao Manor as Young Madam today!"

"You—!" Flushed with rage, Miao Zhiyu grabbed her brush and scribbled aloud, *"Youqi Jushi flies into a shameful rage!"*

"Write whatever you want—today I'm tearing into you till I've had my fill!" Qianli Xun snatched up the wine cup on Miao Zhiyu's table and poured fermented rice wine straight across her fresh ink. "You call yourself a literary talent who's read loads of classics and attended dozens of salons, yet today you're talking like a complete idiot!"

"You—!" Miao Zhiyu slammed her brush down. "Youfan, clean this up right now and bring fresh paper!"

Youfan scrambled forward to whisk away the soaked sheets, mop the tabletop dry with a cloth, and lay out fresh paper.

Setting the cup down with a sharp *clack*, Qianli Xun turned to face the entire room. "The *Book of Songs* is packed with refrains and repeated stanzas—a classic device prized for its echoing musical beauty:

*The peach tree is young and elegant; / Brilliant are its flowers. / This young lady is going to her future home, / And will order well her chamber and house. / The peach tree is young and elegant; / Abundant will be its fruits. / This young lady is going to her future home, / And will order well her house and chamber. / The peach tree is young and elegant; / Luxuriant are its leaves. / This young lady is going to her future home, / And will order well her family.*" [TN: Translated by James Legge]

Bai Meiruo jumped in. "How dare you compare your little lines to the *Book of Songs*?"

Qianli Xun whipped around to lock eyes with her. "You'd better shut your mouth right now—your turn to cry is coming up next!"
