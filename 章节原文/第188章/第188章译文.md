## Chapter 188: Conversations Beneath the Divine Willow

Finding Matron Wei and the Zhu family waiting in Wangyue Pavilion's garden kiosk, Kuqiao relayed her mistress's message.

Anxiety creased Madam Xiang's brow. "Could Shuzhen be tricking us? What if she runs away and hides?"

"Rest easy, Madam," Matron Wei reassured her. "Miss never goes back on her word. Once she promises, she follows through. Put a hundred hearts at ease—tonight I will deliver her safe and sound to Zhu Manor."

"Trust Matron Wei," Chen Wenxin agreed. "Nobody knows Shuzhen better."

Zhu Jingyuan sighed. "Wasted a whole trip over here, then. Where did Shuzhen even go, Kuqiao?"

"Most likely to see the Divine Willow outside town."

"The Divine Willow?" Thought flickered across Zhu Jingyuan's face before he nodded. "Folks say that willow governs marriage ties. With her wedding tomorrow, paying it a visit makes sense. Looks like she is fine, Mother—let us head back and wait."

Madam Xiang nodded. "Let us go home, then."

Out in the bright third-month spring, the mountains burst with fresh green life, roadside grass and wildflowers standing tall. Walking alone along the foothill path, Qianli Xun spotted the Divine Willow from afar, veiled in tender green shoots swaying in the slanted afternoon sun, dotted with red ribbons glowing like sunset clouds.

Approaching with a chest full of unspoken feelings, her quiet moment shattered at the sound of loud grunting.

There beneath the willow stood Bai Feng, both big hands propping a piglet's four hooves against the rough bark. "Climb! Climb already! Don't climb and you go hungry tonight!"

Snout shoved against the trunk, the piglet snorted in protest. Bai Feng slid it a notch higher; its little hooves scrabbled uselessly as its squeals grew louder. Undeterred, he kept inching it up like coaching a toddler to walk. "That is it—one step at a time! Believe in yourself! I am going to make you the first tree-climbing pig in history! Come on! You got this!"

Clueless as to his pep talk, the piglet squealed weaker and weaker until its voice cut off and its head sagged limp.

Bai Feng panicked. "Hey! Pigs slack off too?!" Sensing something wrong, he lowered it onto the grass, where it lay motionless. "Did you pass out—or die?!"

Qianli Xun walked straight up. "You hounded it to death! Pigs cannot climb trees—forcing it to do the impossible, what did it have left to live for?"

Spinning around in shock, Bai Feng spotted her. "When did Youqi Jushi get here?! Standing there silent watching me make a fool of myself!"

"If you know it is a joke, why keep at it? Beats me whether you or your prince is crazier."

Bai Feng glanced past her shoulder, spotting no Kuqiao.

Reading his face, Qianli Xun said, "I came alone. Kuqiao stayed back."

"Well, since Kuqiao is not here, better I bury the poor pig and go report to His Highness." Scooping up the limp piglet, he wandered off to find soft soil, drew a dagger from his belt, and started digging a hole.

Paying him no further heed, Qianli Xun raised her eyes to the Divine Willow and spoke inside her heart: *Divine Willow, tomorrow I marry a man I cannot stand. Did crossing over condemn me to marrying men I never love? Master Jingguang told me changing fate takes Right Thought and Right Mindfulness—yet every step of this road has been brutal, and it feels like Right Thought just means swallowing my own misery to make everyone else happy. I do not want to swallow misery—I want to be with the man I love...*

A familiar voice spoke behind her: "Patron, enduring wrong for yourself IS fulfilling yourself."

*Master Jingguang?* Turning around, Qianli Xun saw a gentle-faced elderly nun. "Does Master cultivate at the nunnery up the mountain?"

"Indeed."

*The exact same voice—it has to be Master Jingguang!* "Are you Master Jingguang?"

Unwilling to claim the title, the nun smiled. "I am not the abbess—merely someone with a similar voice."

A pang of disappointment flickered—yet clearly this nun had read the exact cry inside her heart, no ordinary monk at all. Stepping forward, Qianli Xun bowed. "Master just said enduring wrong for oneself is fulfilling oneself. I do not understand—please enlighten me."

Pressing her palms together, Master Jingguang chanted, "Amitabha. Mortals are trapped in passion and craving, sinking deep without escape. Why do they suffer unfulfilled love and unmet desire? Only because Heaven cherishes life and hopes people turn back from stray roads to their true nature. Yet mortals stay blind—calling unmet desire 'wronging oneself,' when getting what you crave is often the truest wrong."

Unable to grasp or swallow that, Qianli Xun shook her head. "By Master's logic, giving up the man I love to marry someone I despise fulfills me? Anyone hearing that would call it nonsense."

"Tell me, Patron—why can you not marry the one you love?"

Why? Her whole romantic road since arriving felt cursed: longing for Shen Jianchen with no trace of him; falling for Wenyuan only for rumors to rip them apart; healing just enough to realize Prince Yi was Shen Jianchen, only for Mengrong to block every bold step. Convinced she had done nothing wrong, she blurted, "Fate playing cruel tricks, nothing more."

"Every person is born with a destiny," Master Jingguang said, "yet destiny is not unchangeable—change lies in the single word 'course.' Pleasing yourself while wronging others is one course; pleasing others while enduring wrong yourself is another. Two courses plant different causes and yield utterly different fruits. Which does the young lady wish to choose?"

Qianli Xun let out a sigh. "My heart wants the first, yet reason forbids it, so I swallow the wrong—only because Master Jingguang urged Right Thought and Right Mindfulness. Tell me, Master: does Right Thought ALWAYS mean sacrificing myself for others?"

A compassionate smile warmed the old nun's face. "Buddhist wisdom is boundless, never one-size-fits-all. Right Thought shifts with circumstance—at times enduring wrong to spare others, at times letting others bear discomfort to save yourself. For the dilemma facing you today, enduring wrong is the sole path. I leave you one verse: *Wait through the clouds, and you will see the bright moon.* Amitabha. Affairs await at the nunnery; excuse me."

Watching the old nun vanish up the mountain path, Qianli Xun turned back to the willow with a heavy heart. Sunset faded as the pale moon climbed the sky, yet she lingered, turning those seven words over and over: *Wait through the clouds, and you will see the bright moon.* Did that mean true love waited AFTER marrying Chen Haoyu? Hardly—when did Buddhist precepts ever endorse adultery?!

"Still here, Youqi Jushi?" Having buried the piglet, Bai Feng strolled back over.

Turning to him, Qianli Xun felt an unexpected urge to talk. "Bai Feng, I have a riddle in my head—mind helping me answer it?"

Bai Feng grinned sheepishly. "Youqi Jushi is a great scholar, and I barely know a handful of characters—how could I answer riddles for you? Too much credit!"

"That nun just fed me Buddhist philosophy I can neither fathom nor stomach. Maybe an ordinary mortal like you will cut straight to the bone." Having already agreed to marry Chen Haoyu tomorrow, she still craved confirmation that stepping away was right.

"Ask away—I will give it a shot."

Hesitating for a smooth way to phrase it and finding none, she laid it bare. "Princess Jiahe came to see me and said Prince Yi has someone else in his heart. You know about this?"

"Of course I know," Bai Feng answered plainly.

"And you know who is in his heart?"

"Need you ask? Who else besides Youqi Jushi?"

"If... if I insisted on being with Prince Yi, how would you look at me?"

"I would think Youqi Jushi was a terrible person!"

His blunt reply sent a bitter pang straight through her. "Why?"

"Need you ask?! His Highness is betrothed to Princess Jiahe by imperial decree—jumping in between them, how could anyone call that decent? Besides, Xilan tells me all the time that when His Highness lay near death, Her Highness nursed him night and day—where were you then? What gives you the right to claim him now that he is well? These last two days Her Highness cannot eat, cannot sleep, crying her eyes red—all because of Youqi Jushi..."

Unable to hear another syllable, Qianli Xun cut him off. "Enough! Do not breathe a word to Prince Yi that you saw me beneath the willow today. Tell him pigs will never climb trees, and tell him to cherish the girl right beside him. And swear you will never let Mengrong know who he loves! Rest easy—tomorrow I marry into the Chen family, and I will not become the villain in your eyes."

A cool evening breeze rustled the willow branches. With zero heart left to enjoy the spring night, Qianli Xun walked away with a desolate chest.

Left alone under the moonlit boughs, Bai Feng scratched his head. "Did I lay it on too thick? Youqi Jushi looked pretty gutted. Whatever—long as you stop wrecking His Highness and Her Highness's marriage!" Starting off, he paused to squint up at the towering willow in confusion. "Why do both His Highness and Youqi Jushi love hanging around this tree?" Scanning the trunk and swaying branches under moonlight and spotting nothing special, he concluded, "Both His Highness and Youqi Jushi must be cracked in the head, running out here to stare at a tree like it grows gold!"

Riding home inside her carriage, Qianli Xun replayed Bai Feng's raw, resentful verdict. Through every outside pair of eyes, walking far clear of Prince Yi was the sole honorable choice. Down the lonely road of swallowing her own heart she went.
