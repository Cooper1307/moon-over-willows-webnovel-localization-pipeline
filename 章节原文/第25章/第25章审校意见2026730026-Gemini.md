### 🌟 完整精修译文 (Final Polished Version)


Qianli Xun religiously applied the medicine she had bought from Jixiang Pharmacy. In just two days, the wound on her forehead had completely healed. She sat before the bronze mirror, fretting over the possibility of a scar, but after checking and rechecking, she found her skin as flawless as before. Finally, she no longer had to parade around with a bandaged head.

Yet, looking at her fair and delicate skin, she couldn't bring herself to smile. Following her failed divorce, it seemed she had truly inherited Zhu Shuzhen’s melancholic disposition.

Last night, she had dreamed of Shen Jianchen. In the dream, he had asked, "Qianli Xun, be my girlfriend!" To her own surprise, she nodded and agreed—something she had never done before. Shen Jianchen held her tightly, and she savored the sweet arrival of romance. His embrace was so warm that when she woke up, she could still feel the lingering pressure of his arms and the heat of his body.

Defeat in the divorce battle only fueled her longing for love. The more frustrated she felt, the stronger the desire grew. For the first time, Qianli Xun actually *wanted* to fall in love—a thought she had never entertained in her own era. But this marriage was like a thick, impenetrable wall, caging her heart and suffocating her freedom.

Qianli Xun couldn't help but let out a long sigh. "Ah!"

That sigh reminded her of a *ci* poem by Zhu Shuzhen, one that perfectly mirrored her current mood. "Kuqiao, prepare paper and ink. I want to write."

Kuqiao walked over, carrying freshly brewed tea. "It's all ready, My Lady. But drink your tea first; there's no rush."

Qianli Xun finished her tea in front of the bronze mirror. Setting the cup down, she rose, walked over to the desk, and took her seat. She dipped the brush deep into the inkstone and began to write:

*Slanting wind and fine rain make spring cold.*
*Before the wine cup, I recall past joys.*
*I used to hold pear blossoms,*
*My lonely tears flowing down.*
*On the South Port Road where fragrant grass and mist meet,*
*With parting tears, I look at the green mountains.*

*Last night I had a dream-link,*
*In the mist and water, silent and wordless.*
*Alas, upon waking,*
*Sorrow and regret remain the same.*
*Tossing and turning under the covers, in vain regret,*
*The sky is easy to see, but it is hard to see you.*
— "Jiangchengzi: Spring Cold" (tr. Xu Yuanchong) 
*[TN: This ci poem by Zhu Shuzhen expresses her deep longing for a lost love and the agonizing pain of separation.]*

After finishing, she remained utterly immersed in the lines. The brush still rested lightly between her fingers as she savored the words: *"Tossing and turning under the covers, in vain regret, the sky is easy to see, but it is hard to see you."*

Did Shen Jianchen transmigrate here with her? If so, where was he? As she was lost in thought, ink gathered at the tip of her brush and dripped. *Plop.* It landed squarely on the character "you," ruining her hard work in an instant.

Qianli Xun put down the brush and rolled up the paper. "Throw it away?"

Kuqiao took the scroll, dropped it into a nearby wooden bucket, and headed for the door. 

Seeing the bucket brimming with discarded scrolls, Qianli Xun grew curious. "Why are there so many in there?"

Kuqiao turned around. "These are all the drafts My Lady throws away. I save them up, and when the bucket is full, I leave them outside the front gate."

Qianli Xun blinked in confusion. "Outside the gate?"

"My Lady treats them as trash, but someone out there treats them as treasure," Kuqiao explained. "Every so often, people wait outside the Lin residence just to collect your discards! It was the same back at the Zhu residence."

Qianli Xun found it amusing, though she was too emotionally drained to dwell on it. Right now, her heart was weighed down by an inexplicable sadness.

Kuqiao carried the bucket outside the gates of the Lin residence and set it on the ground. Pulling out the *ci* poem her lady had just written, she held it up and called out, "Come out, all of you! Take them quickly so I can get back to work."

Barely had the words left her mouth when five or six young servants and page boys rushed out of nowhere. They swarmed the bucket, snatching up every last piece of calligraphy and painting. As they scattered, they didn't forget to shout back, "Thanks, Sister Kuqiao!"

Kuqiao stopped one of the young servants. "Nanshuang! Wait a moment!"

Nanshuang jogged back over. "What does Sister Kuqiao need?"

"I heard your young master is currently in Bianliang. Why didn't you follow him?" Kuqiao asked.

"Sister Kuqiao never used to ask about my young master. Why the sudden interest today?"

"I didn't think of it before, but now I did! Why ask so many questions?" Kuqiao retorted.

"Think about it, Sister. If I went with him, who would stay behind to collect Youqi Jushi's poetry for him?" Nanshuang unrolled the paper he had managed to grab and sighed. "Just my bad luck today. This sheet only has two lines on it."

Kuqiao handed him the paper she had kept aside. "My lady just wrote this. It's a complete piece. Give it to your young master."

"Why is Sister Kuqiao being so good to my young master?"

"Is that even a question? Young Master Qiao is the only one who truly understands my lady's poetry. They are kindred spirits."

"If that's the case, why doesn't your lady just give her poems directly to him? It would save me from having to squat outside the Lin residence all day."

"How could we do that? There are already enough nasty rumors flying around about my lady and your young master. People love making something out of nothing, so we have to be extra careful. Just keep waiting outside the gate. When there's something good, I'll naturally make sure you get it."

Nanshuang thanked her profusely and left. Kuqiao picked up the empty bucket and headed back, only to run into Lin Jue halfway through the courtyard.

Lin Jue glared at her. "Are people picking up useless trash again?" he demanded angrily.

Kuqiao replied coldly, "To answer you, Young Master: what you consider trash, others treat as treasure."

Lin Jue fumed. "I'll station soldiers outside the gates this very day! Let's see who dares to show up. If one comes, I'll arrest one; if two come, I'll arrest the pair!"

"I strongly advise Young Master not to provoke anything related to my lady. If she finds out, I'm afraid she'll let her fists do the talking again." Leaving that warning hanging in the air, Kuqiao walked right past him.

Lin Jue stared at her retreating back, furious. "That wretched girl never used to talk like that. She's getting more and more insolent! Like mistress, like maid!"

These past two days, Lin Jue had also been doing some soul-searching. Was refusing to let Zhu Shuzhen go the right choice? 
Rationally, it was a mistake. But emotionally, he couldn't bear to lose her. Even if he kept her locked in the Lin residence without ever touching a single hair on her head—as long as she carried the title of Lin Jue's wife, he felt a twisted sense of satisfaction. 
The thought that the arrogant scholars of Lin'an could only admire Zhu Shuzhen from afar but never touch her gave him an immense sense of pride. But above all else, the truest reason remained: he still had feelings for her.

When Kuqiao returned to Zhulan Residence, she found her lady sitting at the desk, staring blankly into space. Not daring to disturb her, she quietly set down the bucket and began tidying up the room.

Having transmigrated into Zhu Shuzhen's body, Qianli Xun was horrified to realize she was adopting her predecessor's sentimental habits. Suddenly, a chilling thought struck her: Zhu Shuzhen had died at the age of forty-five. Qianli Xun had absolutely no intention of dying young! If she wanted a different fate, she had to stop acting like Zhu Shuzhen. She had to remember who she really was.

She smacked her forehead. *That's right! I'm Qianli Xun. And Qianli Xun doesn't waste away feeling sorry for herself! Pull yourself together. Think about how you want to live the rest of your life. You are not going to die of depression in your forties like Zhu Shuzhen!*

Shaking off her gloom, Qianli Xun resolved to take action and change her destiny. But how? Since she couldn't think of a concrete plan just yet, maybe it was time to put a little faith in superstition.

"Kuqiao, do you know anyone who is exceptionally accurate at fortune-telling?"

Kuqiao stopped her dusting. "My Lady, you've asked the right person. Though I haven't met her myself, I know of someone. On a mountain just outside Lin'an City, there is a nunnery. The abbess there is known as Dharma Master Jingguang, and they say she can see both the past and the future. Even the Master went to see her once."

"My father?" 

Kuqiao nodded eagerly. "I heard that back when the Master was young, his career skyrocketed immediately after a single conversation with Master Jingguang. That's how he became the Prefect of Lin'an today."

"That amazing, huh?"

"However," Kuqiao added cautiously, "word has it that Master Jingguang doesn't read fortunes for just anyone. She has conditions. You can only meet her if you meet her requirements."

"What conditions?"

Kuqiao hesitated. "Um, I'm not exactly sure."

Intrigued, Qianli Xun stood up. "I want to meet this Master Jingguang. Pack plenty of silver."

"We're going right now?"

"Right now!"

Armed with silver, Kuqiao followed her lady out of the Lin residence. She instructed the coachman to ready the carriage, and soon they were speeding toward the outskirts of Lin'an.

Qianli Xun had no interest in the bustling street markets outside her window. All she wanted was to find a glimmer of hope for her future. She needed to know if there was a way to break free from this suffocating marriage—if she could somehow carve out a life for Qianli Xun in Zhu Shuzhen's era.

The carriage rattled on for what felt like hours, finally leaving Lin'an City behind and arriving at the foot of a towering mountain.

"My Lady, we're here," the coachman called out.

Qianli Xun was the first to leap down. A magnificent peak, lush and green as if coated in colored glaze, loomed before her.

Kuqiao followed suit, pointing up the slope. "My Lady, the nunnery is halfway up. The path ahead is too narrow for the carriage; we'll have to walk."

Without a word, Qianli Xun strode toward the mountain path. Having trained in the Qianli Fist since childhood, mountain climbing was a breeze for her. Even though this body wasn't originally hers, her subconscious had seemingly rewired Zhu Shuzhen's physique, making it just as agile as her old self. The fact that she could execute the Qianli Fist with zero loss of power was proof enough.

The dirt path winding up the mountain was narrow and steep. Judging by how solidly the earth had been packed down by foot traffic, the nunnery must be thriving with pilgrims.

Qianli Xun spotted five or six pilgrims making their way down. As the first one approached, she and Kuqiao stepped aside to let them pass. 

"Auntie, how much farther to the nunnery?" Qianli Xun asked politely.

"Not far, not far at all. Just a little further up," the woman replied without breaking her stride.

Qianli Xun and Kuqiao resumed their climb, flattening themselves against the hillside whenever they encountered others descending. The path was truly cramped, barely wide enough for a single person.

The scenery along the way, however, was breathtaking. Qianli Xun admired the views as she walked, and before she knew it, they had reached a flat plateau halfway up the mountain. The view suddenly opened up, revealing a serene nunnery. Pilgrims of all ages—men and women alike—flowed in and out of the gates, surrounded by small stalls selling incense.

Looking up at the gate's archway, Qianli Xun read the four characters inscribed on the wooden plaque: *'By the Power of Buddha's Compassion.'* 

"Kuqiao, buy some incense. We're going in to pay our respects."

Kuqiao bought three tall joss sticks, and the two stepped into the courtyard. 

Directly ahead was the main hall. Outside, dozens of people were lighting their incense and reverently planting the sticks into a massive bronze burner. Kuqiao mirrored their actions, and once finished, she followed her lady into the main hall.

The moment they crossed the threshold, rhythmic chanting drifted from both sides: *"Namo Amitabha, Namo Amitabha..."* 
The voices were melodic, almost like a song, instantly washing away worldly anxieties.

Qianli Xun glanced to either side. Six nuns sat cross-legged in perfect rows on the left and right. Their expressions were serene and untroubled as they chanted in perfect unison.

Three prayer cushions rested before a towering Buddha statue. Qianli Xun pressed her palms together and knelt; Kuqiao followed suit. With every bow they made, a sharp ***thwack!*** from a wooden fish rang out, blending harmoniously with the Buddhist chants.

After three bows, they rose and looked toward the source of the sound. Seated beside the great statue was a nun, a wooden fish and striker resting on the table before her.

Qianli Xun approached respectfully. "Little Master, I am looking for Abbess Jingguang. Might she be available today?"

The nun set down her striker and stood. "Follow me."

Qianli Xun breathed a sigh of relief. That was easier than expected. *Kuqiao’s information must have been exaggerated,* she thought, shooting her maid a side-glance. 

Catching her lady's gaze, Kuqiao flushed in embarrassment, though she was mostly just thrilled they could see the abbess so easily. 

Together, they followed the nun toward the rear of the main hall.
