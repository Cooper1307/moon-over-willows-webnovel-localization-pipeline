#TITLE: Huayu Comes for Poems and Eats Roast Chicken
They chatted a while longer until Madam Xiang realized they shouldn't leave Zhu Xiyan and Zhu Jingyuan sitting alone on Mid-Autumn night, and rose to go.
"Now that Mother knows the way, I'll come visit again soon."
Qianli Xun and Matron Wei saw them out to the gate and into their carriage.
Just as the driver flicked the reins, Qianli Xun called out, "Wait!"
When the carriage halted, she stepped up and asked, "Sister-in-law, there's a house right nearby called Yongning Residence—does it belong to the Chen family?"
Poking her head out the window, Chen Wenxin said, "The Chen clan owns so many houses I can't keep track—why ask?"
"Oh, nothing. My maid happened to see Chen Haoyu walk into Yongning Residence, so I got curious."
"If Haoyu went in, it's bound to be a Chen house. Having estates everywhere is why that rascal never sleeps at home! Lately he's turned over a new leaf, though—moved back to Chen Manor swearing he'll behave and stay home! Sun's rising in the west, haha..."
"Thanks for telling me, Sister-in-law." Hearing Chen Haoyu had holed up at Chen Manor eased Qianli Xun's mind; they probably wouldn't bump into each other anymore.
Madam Xiang dared not look back at her daughter lest her tears show and dampen Shuzhen's mood.
The Zhu carriage rolled off, wheels rumbling down the lane until it vanished into the night.
Just as the clatter faded and Qianli Xun turned to head inside, a voice hailed her: "Wait, Youqi Jushi!"
Stepping out of the Mid-Autumn moonlight came a familiar boy—Dongfang Chenze's attendant, Huayu.
"How did you track me down here?"
"My master swore Youqi Jushi would compose poems on Mid-Autumn night! Old Master locked him indoors with no freedom to come out himself, so he sent me to beg a poem. Didn't know where you moved, so I ran to Youlin Retreat to ask Young Master Qiao—didn't find him, got your new address instead, and rushed over to try my luck!"
"Why did your master get locked up?"
Huayu told the plain truth: "With everyone gossiping about Youqi Jushi, Old Master worried my master would get dragged in, so he locked him up."
So Dongfang Chenze got grounded over her! "Come inside—I'll write two poems for you to take back."
Huayu beamed—his trek hadn't gone to waste!
Unwilling to stay up late, Matron Wei turned in early. Kuqiao seated Huayu in the reception hall while Xiaguo followed her mistress to the bedchamber to tend ink and brush.
Walking back, Qianli Xun already knew which two Mid-Autumn poems of Zhu Shuzhen's to write.
Grinding the ink fresh and soaking the brush tip, Xiaguo presented it with both hands.
Taking the brush, Qianli Xun wrote down two Mid-Autumn poems:
Since autumn came I've lingered sick in bed, / No easy thing to reach Mid-Autumn's night. / To gaze upon the moon that shines tonight, / I climb the tower of last night once more.
Thick dew dims shadows of the wutong tree, / Fine breezes float the sweet osmanthus scent. / Do not regard it with a casual eye: / Even Chang'e knows what sorrow means. —"Mid-Autumn" [TN: Translator's rendering]
It claims the fullest splendor of the fall; / Heaven's hand does show favor, after all. / Its pure light reaches out a thousand li, / A bright white orb rounded in perfection.
Through chill the jade hare works its pestle still; / Grown old, the moon toad stands yet stronger still. / I fear I cannot drink my fill of it, / For once it passes, a whole year must turn. —"Enjoying the Moon at Mid-Autumn" [TN: Translator's rendering]
Watching her mistress write, Xiaguo recognized every character yet missed the deeper layers. She vowed to keep studying hard until she could grasp every poem and even offer critiques—worthy of Young Master Qiao someday.
Once her mistress laid down the brush, Xiaguo blew the ink dry, folded the sheets neat, and carried them to the reception hall.
Inside, Huayu was munching mooncakes while chatting with Kuqiao.
"Sister Kuqiao, heard you and Bai Feng are an item..."
Kuqiao flushed red: "You little kid—quit talking nonsense!"
"Bai Feng told me himself!"
"Why does that blabbermouth tell everyone?! Wait till I see him next—I'll teach him a lesson!"
"Judging by your face, Sister Kuqiao, it's true!" Huayu grinned through a mouthful of mooncake.
"What does a kid know? Eat your mooncake!"
"I'm no kid! Same age as Nan Shuang—if he can crush on Xiaguo, why can't I tease Sister Kuqiao and Bai Feng?"
Walking in just in time with a smile on her face, Xiaguo snapped cold at the mention of Nan Shuang: "Keep my name out of Nan Shuang's mouth! I wouldn't look twice at him—tell him to drop it!"
Swallowing his last bite, Huayu hopped up and sidled over with a cheeky grin: "What does Sister Xiaguo think of me, then?"
"Say one more silly word and you don't get Miss's poems!"
Huayu backed off at once: "Hand them over, Sister Xiaguo! My master's locked indoors moping—he's counting on Youqi Jushi's poems to cheer him up!"
"No more nonsense from you!"
"Never again, I swear!"
Xiaguo handed over the folded sheets: "Here. And if you see Nan Shuang, tell him to stop acting like a toad dreaming of swan meat."
Taking the papers, Huayu winced: "Can't deliver that message, Sister Xiaguo! How is Nan Shuang a toad? Bai Feng told me poor Nan Shuang is locked up in Qiao Manor right alongside his master—haven't even laid eyes on him."
"Enough chatter! Take Miss's poems and go—and don't you dare gossip about Miss to outsiders!"
"Not my style—besides, my master would skin me if he heard!" Huayu called back as he headed out.
After seeing Huayu off, both maids returned to help their mistress get ready for bed.
Warm family love, loyal friends sending for her poems even while the city jeered—Qianli Xun felt lucky all over again, refusing to stew in gloom beneath a full Mid-Autumn moon.
Only one ache lingered: her future with Wenyuan. Yet worrying changed nothing—she couldn't storm Qiao Manor and bust him out! Tempting as it sounded, fists couldn't solve everything; she had to wait on fate.
Once the maids tucked her in, they turned in too.
Lying in bed pondering destiny, Qianli Xun felt sure she was walking Qianli Xun's own path now. Even if fate denied her Wenyuan, she would summon the grit to accept an ending she never wanted.
Tucking the two poems inside his robe, Huayu strolled home on foot. Mid-Autumn night markets blazed all night every year—why not enjoy the bustle while he was out?
Mid-stroll, someone called out: "Huayu!"
Spinning around, he spotted An Mingxuan and Cao Ban each munching a candied hawthorn skewer.
"Greetings, Young Masters!"
An Mingxuan said, "Thought that was you! If you're out, where's Brother Dongfang?"
"Still locked up at home, unlike you two free birds! I don't get it—you two love Youqi Jushi's verse just as much, so why didn't your parents lock you up too?"
Popping the last candied hawthorn into his mouth, Cao Ban barked, "Watch it! Putting a curse on us?!"
Huayu bowed fast: "Wouldn't dare!"
An Mingxuan chided him: "Your master's locked up miserable, and here you are roaming the night market instead of waiting on him—isn't that awful?"
Huayu defended himself: "I'm not out playing! My master gave me a mission—swore Youqi Jushi would write verse on Mid-Autumn night and sent me to get some!"
Both spoke in unison: "Did you get any?"
Huayu puffed out his chest: "When I'm on the case, nothing fails! Scored two whole poems!"
"Two?!" An Mingxuan gasped. "Show us right now!"
Huayu named his price: "Sure, you can look—treat me to sour plum drink and roast chicken first!"
Cao Ban waved a hand: "Easy! Come on, let's get your drink and chicken!"
At a nearby stall, Cao Ban bought Huayu two bowls of sour plum drink, which Huayu downed like a man crossing a desert. Finishing their skewers, the two young masters then walked him over to pick out a roast chicken.
Chicken in hand, they ducked into a teahouse. Once Huayu pulled out the poems, each scholar grabbed a sheet and read like starving men, then paid the waiter for brush and paper to copy both regulated five-character poems down.
Chomping roast chicken as he watched them, Huayu shook his head: rich boys didn't know real life, fussing over useless poems when hot roast chicken beat verse by a mile!
Done copying, the two scholars returned the originals, told Huayu to send Brother Dongfang their regards, and dashed off in a rush.
Tucking the poems back in his robe, Huayu reached for his chicken—only for the waiter to step over: "Pot of tea, guest?"
"Nah, I've got roast chicken."
"You didn't buy that chicken here! Taking up a table without ordering tea—isn't that bad form?"
"Those two young masters didn't order tea either!"
"They paid for brush and ink—what did you pay? We're running a business, not a charity!"
Wrapping the rest of his chicken, Huayu huffed off muttering, "Money-grubbing snob—careful you don't fall down a coin hole and get stuck!"
Clutching Youqi Jushi's brand-new poems, the two young masters beamed—now they had a ticket to the Mid-Autumn Poetry Banquet!
With Brother Qiao and Brother Dongfang both locked up and Youqi Jushi under fire across town, they had planned to skip the banquet and just wander the streets; landing two fresh gems like these, skipping the banquet would be a crime!
Halfway there, Cao Ban hesitated: "A circle of literati chipped in to host this Mid-Autumn Poetry Banquet—we didn't pitch in a coin. Won't they kick us out?"
An Mingxuan waved it off: "Forgot we paid last year? They'll give us face—especially with new poems in hand!"
"True, yet what if folk turn up their noses at Youqi Jushi's poems with all the rumors flying?"
"Do you believe street gossip?"
Cao Ban shook his head: "Not a word."
"If you don't buy it, literati above our level certainly won't turn away masterpieces over market chatter!"
Convinced, both strode straight toward the tavern hosting the Mid-Autumn Poetry Banquet.
