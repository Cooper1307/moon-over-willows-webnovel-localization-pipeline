#TITLE: Prince Yi Yields to Reality
Once Chen Haoyu actually got serious, his wits sharpened, his tongue loosened, and his feet flew. Eager to attend to a private errand, he wrapped up his shop-clerk duties early, changed out of his clerk's tunic alongside Qinggua, and went to ask Steward Qiu for leave.
Steward Qiu had watched Second Young Master's transformation with quiet pride, though he kept a stern face behind his desk as he lifted his tea bowl. "Hmm. Barely made a dent and already slacking off? Don't tell me you're itching to slip back to some gambling den or brothel?"
*You aren't my father,* Haoyu muttered inwardly, though as a pupil he kept his tone deferential. "I'm a married man—why would I set foot in places like that? I'm heading to Yongtai Teahouse to study tea-whisking under a tea master. My wife loves tea, and her maid's brews are nothing to write home about, so I want to learn and whisk it for her myself."
*Now that's a true Chen man,* Steward Qiu thought. Since Haoyu had finished his shift and was off on wholesome business, he saw no reason to block him. "If it's honest work, I won't stop you. Still, our own Chen teahouse has fine tea masters—why travel all that way?"
"The Chen teahouse only employs male tea masters," Haoyu answered. "Skilled as they are, they won't understand a woman's palate like a female tea master. Since I'll only ever be whisking tea for my wife, learning from a woman makes more sense."
Taking a sip, Steward Qiu set his bowl down. "Fair enough. Your devotion to Young Madam is touching. Let me let you in on a secret: Yongtai Teahouse by West Lake will belong to the Chen family very soon, so studying there still counts as learning under our own roof."
Haoyu couldn't care less who owned the place; learning tea was all that mattered. "Thank you, Steward Qiu. I've got to run—excuse me." Before the steward could reply, Haoyu swept Qinggua out the door.
Down by West Lake, Qinggua parked the carriage and trailed his master toward Yongtai Teahouse, craning his neck at everything on his first visit. Stepping inside, Haoyu noticed business had slumped since his last visit—far fewer patrons, half the wall paintings gone, and a distinct chill hanging over the room.
A waiter hurried up with wary caution. "Long time no see, Young Master Chen. What brings you here today?"
"Save the small talk," Haoyu said curtly. "Bring out your female tea master. I've got business with her."
Last year's stunt when Haoyu had harassed the tea master flashed straight through the waiter's head. He rushed to plead for her. "With your status, Young Master Chen, you could have any woman you want—why keep hounding our tea master? She's had a hard life. Used to be a rich young lady in Bianliang before her family fell on ruin and she drifted down here with only her mother to lean on. Lucky she knew tea-whisking well enough to earn a crust here."
*Young Master's old rotten stunts really do follow him everywhere,* Qinggua sighed inwardly.
Stirred in spite of himself, Haoyu barked, "Enough! Still judging me by the old days? I'm here to apprentice under her, not harass her!"
"Truly?" the waiter blinked.
"Would I joke about it? Go fetch her!" Haoyu dropped into a seat at a tea table, and the waiter hurried joyfully to the back.
Moments later, the female tea master came out with her maid. "You asked for me, Young Master Chen?"
Sizing her up, Haoyu admitted she really was pretty—no wonder his foolish old self had flirted with her, absurd as it felt now. Feeling his gaze crawl over her, the tea master shivered and stepped back. "If Young Master Chen has no real business, I'll take my leave."
"Hold on!" Haoyu tossed a pouch of silver onto the table. "Of course I have business—I want to take you as my teacher. All that silver is yours. Well? Will you accept me as your pupil?"
Convinced this was just a slick new game to toy with her, she raised her chin. "If Young Master Chen truly wants to learn, I won't take a single copper. If you're merely bored and killing time, I wouldn't teach you for ten thousand taels."
Anxious seeing his master misjudged again, Qinggua stepped in. "Don't misunderstand, Miss! My master's turned over a new leaf. He genuinely wants to learn tea-whisking because our Young Madam loves fine tea."
Inspecting Qinggua's honest face—nothing like Haoyu's old gang of thugs—the tea master softened a fraction. "You truly mean to learn?"
"Of course! Tell you a secret—my wife is the very woman who beat me up on your behalf last time."
"Your wife is Youqi Jushi?"
"What, don't believe me?"
A warm smile finally touched her lips. "Marrying Youqi Jushi is a rare blessing, Young Master Chen. Since it's for her sake, I accept you as my pupil—free of charge. Only grant me one favor in return."
"Since you're waiving the tuition, name it."
Worry shaded her brow. "As you can see, Yongtai Teahouse has fallen on hard times, and word is the Chen family plans to buy it out. Everyone knows the Chen teahouse employs top-tier tea masters—once your family takes over, I fear I'll be cast out on the street. Could Young Master Chen put in a word to keep me on?"
Haoyu slapped his chest. "Easy! I'll tell my father tonight to keep you—and keep the entire original staff right where they are."
Dipping a relieved curtsy, the tea master smiled. "Thank you, Young Master Chen. Let's begin your first lesson right now."
Sitting down before the utensils, she demonstrated each motion while explaining every trick of wrist and water. Haoyu watched with total focus, picturing his wife's surprised delight at his first bowl—and how frantic she'd look once she got hooked on his tea and couldn't do without it.
Back at Youlin Retreat, Xilan had maids fill the wooden bath with warm water strewn with petals, helped Princess Consort Yi disrobe, and watched her step down into the steaming tub like a mermaid. She had bathed on her wedding night too, picturing herself cradled in Brother's arms under his gentle gaze, kissed until she became wholly his—only to spend that night weeping alone on a freezing bridal bed. Tonight Brother had explicitly announced he would sleep at Youlin Retreat. The signal was unmistakable. Reclining in the water, Qiao Mengrong stroked warm water along her bare arm, picturing Brother's touch; a blissful smile curved her lips, and for this brief hour, even her hatred for Zhu Shuzhen dimmed.
Out beneath the Divine Willow, Shen Jianchen stood alone. Having decided to accept Mengrong tonight, he had come here to bid farewell to his stubborn devotion. How laughable he felt—barely days after swearing to Qianli Xun he'd keep hunting for a tree-climbing pig, here he was saying goodbye.
Gazing at the willow as though it were Qianli Xun, he spoke silently in his heart. *Qianli Xun, I'm bowing to reality. Have you bowed too? Tonight I'll consummate my marriage with Mengrong. I'm terrified my stubbornness will hurt both you and her. I used to believe holding fast to love was right—yet Wenyuan's words yesterday and today's murder trial woke me up. Sometimes what you think is right only inflicts wounds on others. From today on, I'll lock you deep inside my heart and build a good life with Mengrong. I hope you find happiness in the Chen household.*
Thick evening clouds smothered the sunset, denying its last warmth to both the willow and Shen Jianchen's chest. Memory after memory rose as he forced himself to let go—and the ache cut deep enough to draw a single tear down his cheek. Wiping it away and forcing the rest back down, he turned to leave, only to spot two young Buddhist nuns strolling along the foot of the hill chatting.
"We bought heaps of red silk ribbons, brushes, and ink today—ought to last a good while."
"True enough. So many people hang ribbons every year we have to hike down to shop every month."
Shen Jianchen stepped forward with a courteous bow. "A moment, little masters."
"How may we help you, Patron?" one nun asked.
"I overheard you carrying red ribbons and ink. Might I ask for one ribbon to hang on the Divine Willow?"
"The ribbons only grant wishes after our abbess blesses them back at the nunnery," the other warned.
A shadow crossed his face. "What I wish for is already impossible—blessed or not doesn't matter. I just want closure for my own heart. Please grant me this small favor."
Without arguing, one nun untied her pack and drew out a strip of red silk, while the other handed over brush, inkstick, and inkstone. "Here's the inkstone and ink—you'll have to figure out water yourself."
Taking them to his favorite boulder under the willow, Shen Jianchen plucked a broad leaf from nearby brush, scooped water from the pond, and drew amused murmurs from the two nuns—"Clever fellow." "Quite resourceful."
Dripping water onto the stone, he ground fresh ink, smoothed out the red ribbon, dipped the brush, and wrote *Shen Jianchen* and *Zhao Pu* at the top, with *Qianli Xun* and *Zhu Shuzhen* beneath. Remembering Qianli Xun's own ribbon, he drew neat squares around *Zhao Pu* and *Zhu Shuzhen*.
Pinning the ribbon down with a pebble against the breeze, he shrugged off his fine outer robe, wrapped the wet brush, inkstone, and inkstick inside it, and presented the bundle back to the nuns. "Thank you both. Wet writing gear will stain your pack—wrapping it in my robe is cleaner."
Feeling the sumptuous silk, one nun gasped. "Such fine fabric—isn't that a terrible waste?"
"Wrapping the nunnery's brushes is no waste at all."
"Since Patron is so generous, I won't refuse! I'll wash it clean and gift it to Old Li who tends our vegetable patch."
Once the nuns walked off, Shen Jianchen picked up the ribbon, vaulted high into the canopy with light-foot technique, tied it tight to a lofty branch, and dropped back down to stare up at it. *With this unblessed strip of red silk, I tie you into the deepest vault of my heart where no one will ever find you. From tonight on, I walk the road reality laid out for me.*
Dusk deepened. After a final pause, he set his shoulders and strode back to Youlin Retreat.
Inside his courtyard, glowing red lanterns hung everywhere, every shade stamped with double-happiness emblems—plain testimony to how desperately Mengrong had longed for tonight. Showing good sense, the servants stayed out of sight, leaving a stillness so thick he could hear his own heartbeat. Each measured step across the threshold felt like laying the past to rest.
Red silk draped every wall and screen. Rounding the partition into the inner chamber, he found the wide marriage bed shrouded in crimson gauze, a slender silhouette reclining behind the sheer curtain. For a split second he pictured Qianli Xun and smiled as he stepped closer.
The gauze parted. Wearing a sleeping robe thin as cicada wings, blushing pink, Mengrong sat up and rose to meet him. "Brother, I've waited so long for this night."
Shen Jianchen cursed himself silently—he knew full well it was Mengrong, so why had Qianli Xun flashed across his eyes?
Mengrong wrapped her arms around him. "Brother, once you make me yours tonight, I'll be the happiest woman alive."
"Mengrong," he said at last, "I've thought things through. From now on I'll treat you right and make you the happiest woman in the world."
Tears of joy brimmed over her lashes. "Let me help you undress, Brother."
He did not stop her. Letting his outer robes pool onto the floor, she nestled back against his chest and lifted her flushed face. "Kiss me, Brother."
Qianli Xun's image flickered once more before he forced it away. Focusing wholly on Mengrong's trembling face, he bent down and kissed her.
Breathless when they parted, Mengrong cupped his cheeks. "You kissed me once when you were ill, and I nearly fainted from joy. Tonight I won't faint—I want to give all of myself to you." Pressing a kiss over his heart, she whispered, "Your chest is so broad and strong I feel like I'm melting. Carry me to bed, Brother—tonight is our real wedding night."
All cool restraint vanished. Having chosen to embrace her, Shen Jianchen scooped her up in strong arms and carried her to the crimson bridal bed.

#TITLE: Only When I Fall for You
Barely scratching the surface with the female tea master taught Chen Haoyu that whisking tea was no walk in the park—and painting pictures in tea foam was harder still. It would take at least ten days or half a month of steady drills before he could produce anything decent.
Darkness had fallen when he wrapped up, still far earlier than usual. Remembering his promise to the tea master, he headed straight to his parents' courtyard without a single detour.
Inside, Chen Jianglin and Yao Qingyan sat sharing tea on the daybed.
"Jianglin, are you really letting Shuzhen run the congee shed?"
"Mother already gave her word—hardly my place to overrule her."
"Still, Shuzhen could stay home writing poems and keeping the elders company. Why volunteer to run a congee shed? Showing her face out in public invites gossip—and what if street thugs cause trouble?"
"I felt the same at first, yet now I think it's a fine move."
Yao Qingyan blinked. "Because of her fists?"
"Partly. Back when we engaged Haoyu to her, gossips whispered he got stuck with a divorced woman because Prefect Zhu twisted our arm. Now that she's our daughter-in-law, tongues are still wagging. Letting her whip that congee shed into shape shows every gossip in Lin'an what a capable daughter-in-law we married—and makes everyone envy Chen women, loved by their husbands and free from stuffy rules."
Yao Qingyan laughed. "Fair point. I won't fuss about it anymore."
Stepping right through the door, Haoyu blurted, "What?! My wife is managing the congee shed? No way—I'm going to help her!"
"Walk right in without announcing yourself? Where are your manners?" Chen Jianglin teased.
"Just sneaking in to watch how Father spoils Mother so I can copy your moves on my own wife!" Haoyu grinned, bowing before dropping into a chair.
"A son who knows how to dote on his wife can't be lacking in manners," Yao Qingyan beamed.
"Mother understands me best!" Haoyu pressed his luck. "Father, give me a few days off so I can go with Shuzhen to the congee shed."
"Ask Shuzhen first," Chen Jianglin said. "If she agrees, take all the days you like. If not, stay at the counter. Steward Qiu tells me you've mastered shop-clerk duties already—seven more days of polish and you're moving to the counting house to learn bookkeeping."
Haoyu stared in disbelief. "That fast? Steward Qiu said two full months—was he bluffing?"
"He wasn't bluffing, silly," Yao Qingyan chuckled. "Your father's praising how fast you learn."
Chen Jianglin nodded. "Once you know the counter inside out, why waste another six weeks? Better move on to the next stage."
Haoyu grinned ear to ear—he'd wasted years playing around without realizing he actually had a head for trade. Remembering his errand, he shifted gears. "Father, I heard you're buying Yongtai Teahouse by West Lake?"
"Deal's sealed. Five days from now it's ours, and we'll give it a full overhaul. Literati used to flock there until the old owner got sloppy and stopped rotating the wall paintings. Once we take over, we'll swap in fresh art every month and restore its former glory."
"Could I ask a favor, Father? Keep the female tea master on—in fact, keep the whole original staff."
"Since when do you meddle in staffing?"
"I used to bully the folks there. Looking back, I feel awful about it and want to make amends."
Warm pride shone in both parents' eyes. "First time Haoyu's ever pleaded on someone else's behalf—say yes," Yao Qingyan urged.
"When my wife speaks, I obey," Chen Jianglin laughed.
Haoyu sprang up. "Thanks, Father! Thanks, Mother! Carry on—I'm off to Moxiang Courtyard!" Whisking Qinggua out, he bolted down the path, leaving Yao Qingyan smiling fondly: "Look at him dash—obviously missing his wife!"
The second they entered Moxiang Courtyard, Chouya pounced and hauled Qinggua off without a word. Paying them no mind, Haoyu saw lamps glowing inside Suxin Thatched Hall, leaned near the wooden door, and called in, "Wife, I'm home early! Got something to run by you—may I come in?"
Knowing tomorrow's congee-shed duty would cut into her writing time, Qianli Xun had been pushing out extra huaben pages. Hearing Haoyu outside, she reminded herself to soften her tone now that she'd decided to give him a fair shot. "That's enough for tonight," she told Kuqiao. "Pack up the pages—I'll get the door."
Kuqiao gathered the manuscript while watching her mistress walk over, pause a heartbeat, and open the door.
Haoyu froze in stunned delight—his wife answering the door herself! "Wife opening the door for me in person? Did luck just drop out of the sky?"
Kuqiao hid a giggle; ever since turning over a new leaf, the young master's tongue had grown sweeter by the day.
Swallowing a snappy comeback, Qianli Xun offered a faint smile. "You're back early. What's on your mind?"
"Let me come in—can't talk in the doorway." Without waiting for permission, he breezed inside and spun around. "Busy as I was today, my mind never left you. I heard you're taking charge of the congee shed—take me along! Father said if you say yes, he says yes."
Holding back her usual barbs, she replied, "Aren't you busy at the silk emporium? Where would you find the time?"
"Say the word and I'll ask Steward Qiu for a few days off to keep you company."
"I don't need company. Stick to your work at the emporium."
"What if I *want* to keep you company? Give me a chance?" Stepping closer, he gazed into her bright eyes until an itch to steal a kiss made his fingers twitch.
Any other day her fist would have flown; tonight her clenched fingers eased open. "Chen Haoyu, quit staring at me like that before my fist forgets my manners."
He blinked back with a cheeky grin. "You didn't hit me! Means you've already fallen for me, right? I'm your husband—no need to be shy! How about I stay the night right here?"
Her temper flared, then cooled at the memory of Kuqiao's warning. Swallowing her curses, she walked to her desk and sat. "Sit down. I have a few questions for you."
Knowing she wouldn't cave that fast—and counting no shouting as a huge win—Haoyu obediently pulled up a chair. "I'm all ears, Wife."
"How long since you last went to a brothel?"
"Not once since you tied me up and dragged me home."
"Before that, how often did you go?"
"Never counted. Whenever I was bored."
"Figures." Qianli Xun paused. "At night, do you itch to have a girl warm your bed?"
"I want *you* to keep me company."
"If you'd married some other woman, wouldn't you want her keeping you company too?"
Haoyu thought a second. "I suppose so."
"There you go. You're only pestering me and spouting sweet nothings because of plain physical itch. Tell you what—let me take in a concubine for you. You get company at night, and I get peace and quiet."
Haoyu's eyes popped wide. All those questions had been leading up to foisting a concubine on him! He shot to his feet. "I am *not* taking a concubine!"
"Why not? Someone to keep you happy—"
Stalking up to her desk, he planted both palms on the wood and glared down at her with blazing eyes. "One last time! I don't want any concubine! I want to consummate our marriage with *you*! You're the only woman I want beside me at night!"
*Who knew Young Master was this devoted?* Kuqiao marveled silently.
Startled speechless by his raw outburst, Qianli Xun stared up at him. Steaming so hard he forgot why he had come, Haoyu pushed off the desk and headed for the door—only for her voice to halt him after three steps.
"Chen Haoyu!"
He stopped. "What else, Wife?"
"If you want to consummate this marriage, you have to make me fall in love with you first."
"I'll work for it. One day you *will* love me."
"I'll be waiting."
At *I'll be waiting*, pure sunlight broke across his face. Spinning back, he grinned, "So—can I come with you to the congee shed tomorrow?"
"No. Stick to learning the business. I want a husband who can hold up the sky on his own."
Grinning like a fool, Haoyu left—the first time in his life being turned down had ever felt this sweet.
Once the door shut, Kuqiao beamed. "You handled that so well, Miss! Didn't scold him once, actually talked things through—you two took a giant leap forward!"
Yet a quiet ache lingered in Qianli Xun's chest. "Kuqiao... do you think I'll ever actually fall in love with Chen Haoyu?"
"If you open your heart to it, you will."
"If only loving someone were as simple as flipping a switch." She had already walked too far down the road of bending to reality to turn back now; all she could do was march forward and hope compromise eventually turned into genuine willingness.
Across town at Youlin Retreat, Shen Jianchen sat up at dawn, gazing down at Mengrong asleep beside him. Having taken her willingly last night, he resolved to treat her with wholehearted kindness. Her sleeping face looked softer, more radiant than ever; leaning down, he brushed a gentle kiss against her cheek.
*I will stay chaste for you.* His own bold vow to Qianli Xun echoed back, twisting a wry, self-mocking smile onto his lips. *Couldn't even keep it—why on earth swear oaths you can't honor?*
As he moved to rise, Mengrong stirred and wrapped her arms around his back. "Move back to Youlin Retreat, Brother? Or drop that Judicial Investigator job and stay with me every day—please?"
Without turning, he answered gently, "A man needs proper work. Military Commissioner bores me, whereas I actually enjoy running the Judicial Office. Don't worry—when work is light I'll come home every night, and even when busy I'll return every few days."
"May I visit you at the Judicial Office when I miss you?"
"You're Princess Consort Yi—my wife. Come whenever you wish."
Hearing him call her *my wife* made her hug him tighter. "You were so strong last night, Brother... I've never known bliss like that. Waking up, I still can't bear to let you go. Stay just a little longer—I still want..."
Unwilling to disappoint her, he turned back into her arms. "If it makes you happy, I'll stay a bit longer." Mengrong pulled him straight into a feverish kiss.
The sun stood high before Mengrong finally let him rise. Maids helped him wash and dress before he stepped out to the front hall, where Matron Zou waited.
"Where is the Princess Consort?" she asked.
"Mengrong's tired. Let her sleep in."
Taking a covered bowl from a maid, Matron Zou set it on the table. "After Your Highness's exertions last night, I had this nourishing tonic porridge simmered specially for you."
Shen Jianchen sat and spooned up the porridge while Matron Zou dabbed happy tears beside him. "At last Your Highness has come to his senses! I'm so glad for the Princess Consort—every year she poured into caring for you paid off!"
Unsure what to say, he ate in silence. Qianli Xun's silhouette still flickered behind his eyes; daily habits forged over months wouldn't vanish overnight. Finishing his bowl, he took Bai Feng back to the Judicial Office—no fresh cases today, so diving into old dossiers would help keep his mind occupied.
Left behind, Mengrong slept another hour before waking in a warm haze, replaying every second of last night. Even after Xilan dressed her and she ate Matron Zou's meal, she sat holding an unread scroll, daydreaming of how Brother had turned her from little sister into wife. His strength intoxicated her—and the deeper she craved him, the sharper a fresh anxiety stabbed her: what if there were other women hovering around the Judicial Office when he slept there?
"Xilan," she ordered, unable to bear the thought, "go straight to the Judicial Office and tell Brother he *must* come home tonight—tell him I've missed him the second he walked out. And look around every corner to see if there are any women working there."
"Right away, Your Highness."
Mengrong had assumed once Brother bedded her, he'd be wholly hers and her worries would vanish. Instead, tasting how extraordinary he was only made her terrified of losing him.

#TITLE: Trouble with Lazybones Village at the Congee Shed
With Xilan off to the Judicial Office, Qiao Mengrong tossed her book aside and sat beaming at the empty air, reliving Brother's touch.
Matron Zou stepped in, took one look at the glowing newlywed, and thought, *Look at her—hooked even deeper now. Better give her a word of advice.* Bowing, she smiled. "Missing His Highness already?"
"I started missing him the instant he stepped out the gate," Mengrong admitted without a blush. "I wish he stayed here with me all day like he used to."
"That won't do, Your Highness. A man needs affairs outside the home."
Noticing the older woman still on her feet, Mengrong gestured to a chair. "Sit and talk with me, Matron."
Settling down, Matron Zou spoke with motherly candor. "I'm overjoyed His Highness finally took you to bed, yet you must spare his health—don't wear him out too often."
Mengrong flushed. "I'll keep that in mind, Matron."
"His Highness is deeply devoted by nature. Now that you've consummated the marriage, his heart will belong to you. As for that Second Young Madam Chen, let her go—live your own lives and stay out of each other's path."
Mengrong straightened slightly. "My hatred for her has ebbed a little, yet it's still there. I'm not letting her off that easy."
"Then stir up a little trouble for her and leave it at that," Matron Zou suggested. "Drop the idea of making her vanish."
Mengrong frowned in fresh dread. "Matron... what if Brother sneaks off to meet her in secret? Plenty of people carry on illicit affairs."
Seeing that consummating the marriage had only sharpened her mistress's insecurity, Matron Zou sighed inwardly. "So you still won't let that poetess go?"
Mengrong hesitated. "First we'll make her life uncomfortable and see how she behaves. If she stays shameless and keeps eyeing Brother, I still won't spare her."
Having watched Mengrong devote her youth to nursing a dying prince, Matron Zou couldn't bear crossing her. "We'll do as Your Highness wishes. Only let me say one more thing: if His Highness remains devoted to you alone, that's your good fortune; yet even if he ever takes a secondary consort or two, Your Highness should stay magnanimous, keep the inner court in order, and guard your seat as primary consort rather than wasting energy on lesser women."
"You don't understand, Matron. Zhu Shuzhen isn't some passing fancy—she's a scar branded onto Brother's heart."
Realizing her mistress hadn't heard a word of her broader caution, Matron Zou sighed quietly. "Whatever Your Highness decides, I'll back you to the end." A pleased smile touched Mengrong's lips.
Over in Lin'an's east quarter, the Chen family's congee shed was a lifeline for every homeless pauper in town, famous for hot gruel and solid steamed buns that actually filled an empty belly. Word had spread so far that vagrants hiked in from rural counties just for a meal.
The compound held three timber canopies: a cooking shed with four massive cauldrons tended daily by two porridge cooks and two bun steamers—no watery swill here, only thick, tender rice porridge and hefty buns where one alone killed hunger; a serving shed with a long counter holding twin wooden tubs of gruel and twin bamboo baskets of buns, where clerks ladled two generous scoops and handed out a bun to every battered bowl held up in line; and a dining shed where vagrants sat on the dirt eating with relish, some even bedding down there at night so long as they didn't foul the floor.
Leaving Kuqiao back in Moxiang Courtyard, Qianli Xun arrived early with her ten martial servants to find the line running in decent order.
Steward Wang, the rotating manager on duty, hurried out in relief. "At last Second Young Madam is here! Now I can head back to the tavern."
"Not so fast," Qianli Xun barred his way. "That's pretty sloppy management, Steward Wang."
Blink-staring in bewilderment, he stammered, "Whatever do you mean, Second Young Madam?"
"I'm taking over fresh, and you're bolting without briefing me on a single detail of how this place runs—call that doing your job?"
"Don't misunderstand, Second Young Madam! There's nothing to brief—one look tells you everything—and the tavern's swamped today."
Seeing right through his half-hearted attitude toward charity work, Qianli Xun decided to teach him a crisp lesson: every Chen assignment deserved real effort. "Oh? Then tell me: how many vagrants eat here daily? How many men, women, elders, and children? How many measures of rice go into the cauldrons each day, and how many bowls does that yield? How many steamer tiers of buns, and how many buns total? And how many freeloaders are showing up to mooch?"
Hit by a rapid-fire barrage, Steward Wang gaped helplessly. "Second Young Madam has sharp eyes—I'm just a rough fellow who never tracked numbers, and I've only been here a few days. Every steward rotates through for a short stint; doubt a single one knows those figures. Please forgive me."
She wasn't out to punish him, just rattle his cage. "Remember from now on: big task or small, anything Master Chen puts in your hands gets done right. Go on back to your tavern—I've got it from here."
Thanking her hastily, Steward Wang scrambled into his carriage muttering to himself, *Second Young Madam is no joke! Hit me with a firing squad of questions right off the bat—spitting image of Old Madam Xia in her youth! No wonder the Chens ignored her divorce and snapped her up—and word is she whipped Second Young Master into shape too. Formidable!*
Watching the ragged, grimy line shuffle forward—men, women, young, old, nothing suspicious yet—Qianli Xun had Hongdou keep order and watch for troublemakers. Hongdou split the ten servants into two teams of five under herself and Asheng to patrol both lines.
Qianli Xun strolled through the cooking shed, returning the cooks' respectful greetings with a nod, then stepped into the dining shed. Huddled against a wall sat a gaunt, ghostly-pale old woman while a boy of seven or eight carefully spooned porridge from a chipped bowl to her lips.
"One more bite, Grandma—you'll get better."
Qianli Xun walked over and laid a hand on the old woman's forehead. "She's burning up with fever. Gruel won't cure this—she needs a doctor."
The boy burst into sobs. "We don't have a single copper! No doctor will look at her!"
Pity squeezed Qianli Xun's chest. "Chouya!"
Chouya jogged right over. "Yes, Miss?"
"Take this grandmother to a clinic right now." Pressing a one-tael ingot into Chouya's palm, Qianli Xun watched Chouya gently hoist the old woman up while the little boy trailed behind sobbing endless thanks.
No sooner did Qianli Xun turn to leave the dining shed than a dozen hands shot out around her.
"Help me too, lady!"
"Spare some silver!"

Seeing zero gloom on Zhu Shuzhen's face deflated Mengrong's spite—just as Zhu Xiyan asked routinely, "Does anyone present object?"
"I object!" Ye Anya shot to her feet!
Shock rippled across the hall. Ignoring Madam Bao's hiss and Haodong's boiling glare, Anya spoke clear as a bell: "Chen Haodong is unfit—because *I*, his wife, am unchaste and disgraced! I'm pregnant—and the child isn't his! Because Chen Haodong is *impotent*—here is his doctor's prescription to prove it!"
As Chen Jiangshu yelled for male servants to drag her out while she shouted about Haodong's brothel trysts and jealousy toward Haoyu, Old Madam Xia thundered *"Stop!"*—accepting the prescription bearing Haodong's own name, then gracefully excusing Princess Consort Yi and Prefect Zhu from messy family business.
With outsiders gone—Mengrong storming off empty-handed, Zhu Xiyan secretly thrilled for his son-in-law—Old Madam Xia resumed the chair, determined to dig out the truth while Haodong seethed, resolved to repudiate Anya on the spot to save his crown.
#CH 310
#TITLE: One Storm Barely Passes Before Another Strikes
Quietly weighing Anya's accusations against Haoyu's courtroom ordeal, Chen Jianglin kept silent while Old Madam Xia asked Anya what she truly wanted.
"Two things, Grandmother: freedom from Chen Haodong, and stripping him of the inheritance!"
Scolding Haodong for beating his wife until she snapped, Old Madam Xia didn't stop him when he called for brush and paper, scrawled a divorce letter (*xiushu*) on the spot, and flung it onto the floor—"Want it? Pick it up!"
Without a flinch, Anya calmly picked up her freedom, checked every character, tucked it safely into her waist pouch—and before leaving, fired one parting broadside: *"Chen Haodong is a hypocrite sleeping with brothel women—and I'll bet he was behind that courtesan raiding Chen Manor too!"* Eating one final vicious slap from Haodong, she spat back that her pregnancy proved *she* wasn't the barren one, spat at his boots, and strode out free beside Xiaocui.
An uncomfortable hush fell. Coughing softly, Granduncle Lu announced the elders felt fertility mattered to clan continuity—forcing cornered Haodong to confess his doctor had promised a cure within a year, while insisting fertility wasn't in the written rules!
Ruling they'd add fertility for future elections yet honor today's ballot since Anya was already gone, Chen Jianglin drafted the official appointment scroll, pressed his red thumbprint first, and invited the elders forward one by one—while Qianli Xun silently marveled at Anya's fierce gamble and laughed softly as Haoyu teased her about green hats.
Seventeen thumbprints down, only *three* elders left—when Han Gu's voice echoed across the hall:
*"The Judicial Investigator arrives!"*
Leaving Chen Manor earlier, Qiao Mengrong had dropped by the Judicial Office and gleefully dished the whole Chen scandal to Brother—prompting Shen Jianchen to realize there was zero Chen face left to spare, and march straight over before the clan crowned a rapist! (Riding home afterward, Mengrong sighed over losing another pawn, eyed Imperial Attendant Liang as a dream trump card—and suddenly wondered why Bai Feng had vanished for weeks, sparking fresh paranoia about Shuzhen!)
Striding into Xinglong Pavilion with Han Gu, Wen Xin, and armed constables, Shen Jianchen smiled coolly at stunned Chen Jianglin: "Looks like I arrived just in time."
Stopping straight before sweating, white-faced Chen Haodong, Shen Jianchen proclaimed loud enough for every elder in the room to hear:
"Remember Chun Jinlan? On the third night of the third month last year, maid Aye saw a man shrouded head-to-toe in a black cloak walk out of that room. That man was *you*—Chen Haodong, you are the rapist who violated Chun Jinlan!"
