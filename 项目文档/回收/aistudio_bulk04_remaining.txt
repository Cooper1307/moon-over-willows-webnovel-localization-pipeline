#TITLE: First Day at the Silk Shop
Still strolling the petal-strewn garden with Wenxin, Qianli Xun paused as a Chen maid hurried up. "Eldest Miss, Second Young Madam—Guard Bai from Prince Yi's household is at the gate asking for Kuqiao."
Chatting behind with Yuegui, Kuqiao had missed the news of Prince Yi's return and gasped bright-eyed: "Miss...!"
Eager herself to pick up scraps of news about Prince Yi and Mengrong, Qianli Xun waved her off. "Go on—I don't need you here."
"Thank you, Miss!" Kuqiao sprinted away.
"Look at that girl—one mention of Guard Bai and her mistress vanishes from her head!" Wenxin teased.
"My feet are tired, Sister-in-law—let's go sit at Grandmother's and catch the storyteller." Not really tired, just craving a noisy crowd to drown out Shen Jianchen's name.
"Heard hiring the storyteller by the month was your brainstorm!" Wenxin laughed. "Now half the rich houses in Lin'an hire him on retainer—sprints from manor to manor earning ten times what he made at Goulan Wasi! Chen Manor started a whole city trend!"
"Guess I ought to charge him a commission!"
"Second that!"
Arriving at Old Madam Xia's courtyard just as the gavel cracked—with maids taking Laibao off to play—the sisters-in-law settled in. Listening to her own plot performed live drowned out every stray thought until the tale ended and family chatter resumed, where Qianli Xun noticed Ye Anya missing once again.
Finding an opening, she asked, "Second Aunt, where's First Sister-in-law?"
Madam Bao's smile died. "Never likes crowds—prefers hiding in her room."
Madam Cui smirked inside: Everyone knows Anya only hides when Haodong's left fresh bruises!
Old Madam Xia turned stern on Madam Bao. "You and Jiangshu lecture Haodong—hitting a wife brings zero honor! Leading the Chen estate starts with putting your own house in order, and that starts with cherishing your wife. A wife is a man's treasure vault! Keep your vault bruised, weeping, and terrified, and how do you expect big fortune to stick?"
Unconvinced yet obedient, Madam Bao murmured, "Yes, Mother. We'll talk to him tonight."
"Not your ears—his heart needs to hear it!"
A wife is a man's treasure vault—Qianli Xun marveled at the phrase, picturing Chen Jianglin hovering devoted around Yao Qingyan while Chen wealth still swelled nonstop. A wild fleeting thought flickered—what if Chen Haoyu treated her like that? She snorted at herself at once; brothel-hopping slime turning into a dream husband? No thanks!
Once Old Madam Xia tired and Wenxin headed home with Laibao, Qianli Xun returned alone to Suxin Thatched Hall, where Kuqiao sat whisking tea with a glowing face.
Sitting on the floor cushion beside the tea table, Qianli Xun tested the waters casual. "Saw Bai Feng? Put on weight or lost some?"
Seeing right through the detour, Kuqiao handed over a bowl of tea. "Bai Feng says Prince Yi looks the same—only difference is he's wed to the princess now."
Taking a sip, Qianli Xun arched a brow. "Asked about Bai Feng—why bring up Prince Yi?"
Keep pretending! Kuqiao thought.
"Gone all that time—didn't Bai Feng bring you a souvenir?"
Hesitating—Bai Feng had brought a gift for her, nothing from Prince Yi for Miss, though both were married now anyway—Kuqiao admitted, "Yes—a Bianliang woodblock print."
"Let's see!"
Fetching it from Yuening Room, Kuqiao handed over a crisp carved portrait catching her own likeness to the life.
"Thoughtful guy," Qianli Xun murmured, struck by quiet envy—Kuqiao could love open and proud while she and Shen Jianchen stayed locked behind separate wedding vows.
Suddenly Kuqiao dropped straight to her knees, sobbing. "Miss... waaah!"
"Just saw your sweetheart—what's with the tears?!"
"Heartbroken for you, Miss! Waaah!"
"Save the tears—I'm thriving! Get up and sit!"
Wiping her eyes, Kuqiao sat down. "Terrified your Lin Manor misery is repeating all over again!"
"Not a chance—I'm not who I was back then."
Gathering her nerve, Kuqiao spoke straight. "Let Prince Yi go, Miss! You're both married—stop holding onto him. Chen Manor is wonderful; I'm praying you and Young Master work out!"
Realization hit Qianli Xun: she was standing one against the world—Chen elders, Zhu family, Matron Wei, every maid down to Kuqiao all rooting for Chen Haoyu! Resentment flared: why did two days of decent behavior turn everybody into his fan club while she got pressured to hand herself to a man she didn't love?!
"Meddling busybody—stay out of my heart!"
"I'm just a maid, Miss, yet I care about you too much to stay quiet! Even setting everything else aside, think of Princess Jiahe—what wife wants another woman holding her husband inside her heart?"
That arrow hit dead center. Qianli Xun's face turned grave as she stood up. "Pack that peeing clay doll out of my sight. I won't touch it again."
Nodding, Kuqiao felt a stab of guilt for snuffing out her mistress's last tiny keepsake.
Meanwhile, Chen Haoyu had stepped down at the silk shop with Chen Haodong, greeted by Manager Qiu. Showing off his turf, Chen Haodong headed straight into the guest room for a big southwest merchant deal while tossing a smooth nod to Manager Qiu: "Take good care of Haoyu for Eldest Uncle!" (sneering inside: learn all you want, still zerohow could they give me a hard time?"
Old Madam Xia gave him a laughing scold. "Talking nonsense again. Forgot why your father-in-law forced you to marry the Zhu girl in the first place?"
"A real hero doesn't bring up past glories! Don't mention that again, Grandmother, or if that tigress hears it, your grandson will be in for a world of pain."
To coax more silver out of her, he needed her in high spirits, so he switched tracks at once. "Grandmother, I always pray for your long life and good health. Today at Zhu Manor, I dug up the longevity secret of their Old Ancestor. Want to hear it?"
Certain he was just buttering her up, Old Madam Xia played along. "Of course I do."
Chen Haoyu leaned in with an air of mystery. "I paced outside the Zhu Buddhist shrine for ages and heard nothing but wooden fish tapping and sutra chanting inside. That must be why their Old Ancestor has lived so long. How about I buy you a wooden fish and a string of prayer beads tomorrow so you can give it a try?"
"Save it. Your grandmother isn't cut out for chanting sutras. Just come visit me every day and keep me company. When I'm happy, good health and long life take care of themselves."
"Deal, I'll spend more time with you from now on." Casting about for a way to sic his grandmother on Zhu Shuzhen, he put on his best pitiful face. "Grandmother, you have to help me."
"Help you with what?"
"Zhu Shuzhen is bullying me."
"How is she bullying you?"
"She kicked out my maids, seized my courtyard, hogged all my properties, and gives me only twenty taels of silver a month! I'm practically a pauper now. Nobody out on the street believes I'm a Chen."
Old Madam Xia chuckled. "That bride of yours has a razor-sharp tongue. Even I can't talk her down. Besides, who told you to get on your knees and beg her to marry you, then sign some prenuptial contract? Blame yourself for gambling with Master Jiang and near losing the whole Chen estate."
Chen Haoyu blinked in shock. "How did you know about that?"
"Your new bride told me. Your father and mother know too."
Zhu Shuzhen! You didn't leave me a shred of face in front of my own family! Just you wait! he fumed inside. Aloud, he said, "Gambling was my fault, Grandmother. I'll never touch it again. Still, you have to teach Zhu Shuzhen a lesson for me. She's walking all over my head now."
"How should I teach her a lesson?"
"Find excuses to give her a hard time whenever you get the chance."
"She's your wife now. Giving her a hard time means giving you a hard time."
"Grandmother, we're husband and wife in name only."
Old Madam Xia's face clouded with sorrow. "My grandson gets married at last, only to live like this. Just thinking about it breaks my heart."
Chen Haoyu rushed to cheer her up. "Don't be sad, Grandmother. Sooner or later I'll bring home a wife you approve of."
Her reply came straight from the heart. "I'm quite pleased with the wife you have right now."
With his scheme to set Grandmother on Zhu Shuzhen dead in the water, Chen Haoyu pivoted straight to silver. "Grandmother, Zhu Shuzhen won't give me spending money, and I don't dare ask Father or Mother. Could you spare me a little silver?"
Old Madam Xia never stinted on her grandson. She ordered a maid to fetch a wooden box and pulled out a five-hundred-tael silver note. "Spend it with care. Not a copper on courtesans or gamblers, you hear me?"
Overjoyed, Chen Haoyu snatched the note and planted kiss after kiss on her cheek until she beamed from ear to ear. After chatting a few minutes longer, he tucked the note into his sash, bolted out of the manor, and swept Scarface and his gang off for another round of carousing.

#TITLE: Starting Out as a Shop Clerk
Steward Qiu did not rush to answer. He walked over, took the main chair behind the desk, and let his smile vanish into stone-cold gravity. "Since Eldest Master placed Second Young Master in my charge, you will follow my orders to the letter. Until you finish your apprenticeship, I won't bow to the King of Heaven himself. See that spot over there? That seat is yours."
Chen Haoyu glanced over. It was plainly a subordinate desk—smaller than Steward Qiu's, with a stiff, uncomfortable chair. He had assumed the steward's chair was meant for him, yet here was his setup. Fine, no point picking a fight over a chair. Pushing down his irritation, he started toward the desk to sit.
Steward Qiu halted him on the spot. "Not so fast, Second Young Master. Before you earn the right to sit at that desk, you have some seasoning to go through."
Qinggua, visiting the silk emporium for the first time, had tagged along behind his master taking everything in. He had assumed Haoyu would now kick back and rest in this nice room; instead, Steward Qiu had claimed the seat of honor and started throwing his weight around. *Young Master is going to blow his top and walk straight out,* Qinggua thought.
To his shock, Haoyu paused for a moment—annoyance flashed across his face, then vanished under a composed mask as he turned back to the steward. "What kind of seasoning?"
"First, you'll work two months up front as a shop clerk," Steward Qiu declared. "Then three months learning the books in the counting house. After that, you'll close three major deals on your own. Pass all of that, and you may sit at that desk and learn how to manage the shop under me."
"So I'm not even qualified to sit in that chair yet?"
"Second Young Master catches on fast."
"Then why bother bringing me in here at all?"
"If I didn't bring you in here, how else would you see my authority as steward?"
A flicker of regret pricked at Chen Haoyu. Had he known it would be this much of a headache, he never would have volunteered to join the family business. Then he thought of his feelings, and of the wife waiting at home, and hardened his resolve. "Did Brother Dong learn the same way?"
"Eldest Young Master started selling bolts up front like a common clerk when he was seven or eight. He's worked his way through just about every Chen shop in Lin'an."
Chen Haoyu set his jaw. "Then I'll learn the same way Brother Dong did. Since my father put me under your wing, Steward Qiu, assign me whatever you see fit."
Steward Qiu nodded in approval.
Qinggua stared in quiet awe. *Guess Young Master really has turned over a new leaf.*
Back at Youlin Retreat, Bai Feng returned from meeting Kuqiao to find Prince Yi sitting alone atop the raised wooden platform. Guessing his master was waiting for word of Youqi Jushi, Bai Feng climbed the steps.
Shen Jianchen spotted him. Every fiber of him yearned for news of Qianli Xun, yet the reality of their marriages forced him to keep his impatience leashed. "You saw Kuqiao? Is she well?"
Bai Feng knew full well the prince wasn't asking about Kuqiao. "Kuqiao told me it was Young Master Chen who spread those rumors about Youqi Jushi last year."
Shen Jianchen murmured as if to himself, "So she knows after all. I kept it from her because I didn't want to hurt her, yet she found out anyway. Discovering she married the very man who slandered her—how could she bear it?"
"Kuqiao says Youqi Jushi isn't holding the rumors against him anymore."
"Not holding it against him?" Shen Jianchen could not tell whether that should bring relief or sharper dread.
"Your Highness... there is one more thing, though I'm not sure I should say it."
"Speak."
"Kuqiao says Young Master Chen has reformed. He's fallen for Youqi Jushi, and he went straight to the Chen Silk Emporium first thing this morning to learn the business."
"Reformed? Fallen for her?"
Shen Jianchen could sit still no longer. He pushed to his feet and started down the steps, only for his stride to falter halfway down. What good would rushing off do? She was married. Chen Haoyu falling in love with her and mending his ways for her sake—nothing could be more natural. The strength drained out of his legs, and he sank down right onto the steps.
Seeing his master sit, Bai Feng dared not keep descending and sat on a step beside him.
They stayed there in silence until Matron Zou approached and bowed at the foot of the stairs. "Does Your Highness have a moment? I have something to discuss with you."
Shen Jianchen stood and walked down. "Say whatever you have to say right here, Matron."
Matron Zou glanced around. "This isn't the place for it. Better we speak in Your Highness's quarters."
Without a word, Shen Jianchen led her toward his courtyard. His brows stayed knitted in gloom the entire way, leaving Matron Zou reluctant to make small talk.
Once they were seated inside, Bai Feng had the sense to wait outside. He already guessed the matron was here to press the prince about Princess Jiahe—hardly a conversation for a guard to overhear. And if even Bai Feng could guess, Shen Jianchen knew it as well. Since the wedding, Matron Zou had held her tongue; today had to be about him and Mengrong.
When the prince stayed silent, Matron Zou rose from her seat to bow again. "Your Highness has likely guessed what I'm here to say. If I speak out of turn, please forgive an old servant."
"No need for ceremony, Matron. Sit and speak. Whatever you have to say, I will listen."
Matron Zou sat back down and spoke with bold candor. "Princess Jiahe is now Princess Consort Yi. Your Highness shouldn't leave her sleeping in an empty bed night after night. Why not spend tonight with her?"
Shen Jianchen said nothing.
"Back when she was still Princess Jiahe, I watched every bit of devotion she poured into caring for you, and it moved me to tears. Marrying a woman like her is Your Highness's blessing. You can't get your health back, come to Lin'an, catch sight of a few stray blossoms outside, and toss aside everything she sacrificed for you."
Shen Jianchen could not stop himself from replying. "I've told you before—I am not Zhao Pu..."
"Please stop saying such nonsense, Your Highness! If you aren't His Majesty's son Prince Yi, who else could you be? I know coming back from death's door wiped your old memories clean, but you're still a red-blooded young man. How can you stay cold to a wife as lovely as a flower? It must be because you've never tasted a woman's sweetness yet. Give it a try tonight, and by tomorrow you'll be stuck to the Princess Consort like glue! Hehe..."
Her chuckle died away when Shen Jianchen's face stayed utterly impassive. She pressed on in a more practical vein. "The Princess Consort told me you fell for a female poet after coming to Lin'an. You're a man, and a son of the emperor at that—taking a fancy to a woman or two is nothing unusual. Bring her into the prince's household as a concubine if you must, but don't let some ordinary woman ruin things between you and your primary wife. Favor the Princess Consort as you ought, and enjoy your outside fancy too—wouldn't everyone be happy? Why make yourself miserable and break the Princess Consort's heart too? Besides, I hear that poetess is already married. All the more reason not to hurt your wife over her."
Shen Jianchen remained silent.
Finding she was talking to a brick wall, Matron Zou stood up. "I've said my piece. Your Highness can decide for yourself."
Once she left, Shen Jianchen walked out into the courtyard and gave Bai Feng a crisp order. "Come with me to the Chen Silk Emporium."
Bai Feng knew at once who his master was going to see.
Over at the silk emporium, Chen Haoyu had changed into a clerk's tunic and taken his post on the front sales floor. Watching for a few minutes, he noticed that every single clerk was a man, while almost all the shoppers streaming in and out were women. He crooked a finger, and Qinggua stepped right over.
"Tell me," Haoyu asked, "if most of the customers are women, why are all the clerks men?"
Qinggua blinked, stumped. "I'm just a house servant—what do I know about running a shop? Don't put me on the spot, Young Master."
"What good are you, then?"
"Running errands for you—I can handle that just fine!"
A clerk who had just wrapped up with a customer noticed an unfamiliar face in a clerk's tunic and walked over. "Hey, you new here?"
Qinggua opened his mouth to announce that this was the Chen family's Second Young Master, but Haoyu cut him off. "Yeah, I'm new."
"Then what are you standing around gawking for? Go wait on the customers!" The clerk barked, then eyed Qinggua. "You new too?"
"I'm his servant," Qinggua explained.
The clerk stared, baffled and irritated. "Since when does a shop clerk bring a personal servant to work?"
Chen Haoyu couldn't keep the act up any longer. "I'm the Chen family's Second Young Master. Why can't I bring a servant?"
The clerk deflated on the spot, plastering on an apologetic grin. "Oh! Second Young Master! Forgive my blind eyes—please carry on, I'll go see to the customers."
Now that the clerk was all bows and scrapes, Haoyu lost the thrill of it and wished he had kept his mouth shut. Still, Steward Qiu had paraded him around the workshops earlier; half the staff probably knew who he was already, and the rest would find out soon enough. Looking at Qinggua beside him, he decided working as a clerk while trailing a servant did look silly. "Qinggua, why don't you head back and leave me to it?"
"No way!" Qinggua dug in his heels. "I'm sticking by you, Young Master. Worst case, I'll put on a clerk's tunic and be a clerk too."
Haoyu laughed. "Fair point. Go get changed, then."
While Qinggua went to change, Haoyu studied how the other clerks handled buyers. The moment someone walked in, they stepped forward with a warm smile, led the shopper to the silk racks, and talked through each fabric one by one. Looked simple enough. He might as well try his hand.
Just then a young woman walked in. Haoyu hurried up with a bright smile. "What can I help you find today, Miss?"
She walked deeper into the shop as she answered, "I'm looking for cloth to make an everyday robe for my father."
"What a filial daughter!" Haoyu praised her, adding with a wave of his hand, "Our Chen emporium has every fabric under the sun. Take your pick!"
She paused, looking around with a knit brow. "I don't know which one to choose. Give me a recommendation."
Haoyu pointed to a nearby bolt that looked fine to him. "How about that one, Miss?"
She shook her head. "That's women's silk. Hardly suitable for my father."
Mixing up men's and women's cloth on his very first customer sent heat rushing to Haoyu's ears, though he had no time to kick himself. "Over there, Miss—those are for men."
She walked over, fingered several bolts, and stayed undecided. "Tell me about these fabrics so I can compare them and pick the right one for my father."
Haoyu froze. All his life he had only worn clothes—he had never paid attention to what they were woven from. "There's... not much to explain. If the texture feels nice and you like the color, just buy it."
The customer's face soured. "Whenever I've shopped at the Chen Silk Emporium before, the clerks always explained every bolt in detail. How do you not know a single thing?"
Another clerk hurried over with an apologetic smile. "Don't be upset, Miss. He's brand new and still learning the ropes. Let me walk you through them."
Forced to step aside, Haoyu stood back and listened closely.
"Everything in front of you is silk gauze," the clerk said with smooth ease. "Light in weight, fine in thread, tight and durable in the weave, yet airy with tiny open pores—ideal for summer robes. We carry weft-ribbed gauze, warp-ribbed gauze, and figured gauze in every pattern you could want. I overheard you say it's for your father's everyday wear—this plain-weave gauze without brocade patterns would suit him best."
The young woman beamed. "Wrap up the plain-weave gauze for me, please."
"Right away, Miss! Just a moment."
Working the counter was no joke after all, Haoyu realized. He had a lot of learning ahead of him.
Qinggua, already changed into a clerk's tunic, had watched his master get edged aside and sidled up to console him. "It's your first day, Young Master—nobody expects you to know it all. Give it some time and you'll outshine all of them."
"There," Haoyu grinned. "Finally said something I like hearing."
The clerk tied up the parcel and handed it over. "Turn right when you step outside, Miss, and you'll find the Chen tailor shop. Since you bought your fabric here, tailoring is half price. And if you ever want ready-made garments, our ready-to-wear shop is right next door."
Taking her bundle, the young woman walked past Haoyu and tossed him a sharp glance. "Take notes!"
Once she stepped outside, the clerk bowed to Haoyu. "Forgive me for taking your customer, Second Young Master."
Haoyu waved it off. "You did the right thing. What's your name?"
"Ergui, Second Young Master."
"From now on, you're in charge of training me. Teach me everything a shop clerk needs to know."
"I wouldn't dare—"
"What's there to dare or not dare? I'm ordering you to teach me, so teach me well—or I'll have Steward Qiu fire you."
"I'll teach you, I'll teach you! Please don't have Steward Qiu fire me, Second Young Master!"
"That's more like it."
In the reception parlor, Chen Haodong finished his negotiation and walked the visiting merchants out. Passing the sales floor, he caught sight of his cousin dressed like a common clerk, listening meekly while Ergui lectured him on silk weaves. A sneer curled Haodong's lip.
With shoppers everywhere, he held his tongue until the merchants departed, then walked over and tugged Haoyu's arm. "Stop letting these clerks feed you nonsense. Come walk around town with me—I'll teach you as we go."
Part of Haoyu itched to slip outside for a stroll, yet if he hadn't even mastered what a junior clerk knew, how could he follow his cousin's lessons? He shook his head. "You know way more advanced stuff than I can handle right now, Brother Dong. Once I get the basics down, I'll come learn from you."
Afraid pushing harder would make Haoyu suspicious of his motives, Haodong dropped the offer and addressed the nearby clerks instead. "Listen up, all of you. He's my younger brother—don't you dare neglect him."
"Rest assured, Eldest Young Master," the clerks chorused. "We'll look after Second Young Master."
Stepping out of the emporium in a foul mood, Chen Haodong spotted Scarface and his cronies loitering across the street. *Right on time,* he thought. *With them dangling temptation under his nose, Haoyu won't resist for long.* He shot Scarface a knowing glance, climbed into his carriage, and rolled away.

#TITLE: How Hard It Is to Let Someone Go
Scarface swaggered into the silk emporium with a handful of lackeys at his heels. Thinking ruffians had come to stir up trouble, the clerks hung back while shoppers, men and women alike, edged toward the walls. Only when Scarface grinned ear to ear and greeted Chen Haoyu as "Young Master Chen" did everyone exhale and go back to their business. Seeing his pupil had visitors, Ergui excused himself to greet incoming customers.
Annoyed that they had spooked the shoppers, Haoyu barked, "What are you doing barging in here? If you've got something to say, take it outside."
Once they stepped onto the street, Haoyu tore into them before Scarface could get a word out. "Look at the lot of you, slouching around like street bums—and you still have the nerve to come looking for me? Don't come hanging around me anymore. I'm busy!"
Scarface blinked. "Only a few days since we saw you last, and you're a whole different man, playing shop clerk at the silk emporium? Don't tell me the Young Madam beat the sense clean out of you?"
"Watch your mouth!" Haoyu snapped. "My wife treats me great."
Qinggua bristled with old resentment. These were the very loafers who used to drag his master off to carouse while leaving him behind. Since Young Master wanted nothing to do with them now, why not pile on? Stepping forward, he puffed out his chest. "Hey! Clear off, before I tell the Young Madam and she comes out to beat you all crying for your mothers!"
Scarface and his crew ignored Qinggua as though a mosquito had buzzed past. One of the cronies called out, "Wait—don't you miss Chun Jinmo at all, Young Master Chen?"
A twinge of guilt tugged at Haoyu at the sound of her name. "How is she?"
Scarface pounced on the opening. "How could she be, without you watching out for her? Cries her eyes out every single day. The very day after your wife hauled you home, Master Jiang marched into Chunxiang Brothel. Chun Jinmo swore she'd rather die than yield, and even the madam couldn't budge her. Lucky for her, Master Jiang didn't force her—he just bought out her exclusive patronage and says he'll redeem her contract once she has a change of heart. As long as silver keeps rolling in, the madam leaves her alone, but she's still pining for you morning and night."
Thinking of his wife back home, Haoyu steeled his heart. "Pass a message to Chun Jinmo for me. Tell her I used to be bored out of my mind with nobody I truly cared for, so I was only fooling around with her. Tell her not to take it to heart. I won't be setting foot in Chunxiang Brothel again, so she'd better stop pining for me."
Scarface saw then that Chen Haoyu meant it; the days of freeloading off his purse were over.
Another lackey whined, "Young Master Chen, if you stop taking us out, we won't have anyone left to lean on."
An idea struck Haoyu. "Instead of loafing around doing nothing all day, why not earn honest silver and feed yourselves? I can put in a word with Brother Dong and get you boys heavy labor hauling freight."
Accustomed to eating and drinking for free, every single one of them pulled a sour face at the mention of honest sweat.
Haoyu sneered. "Look at your lazy mugs. Don't bother me again unless you've got real business."
He pivoted to head back inside to his clerk duties, only for a voice to stop him in his tracks.
"Young Master Chen."
Turning around, he spotted Guard Bai—and right beside him sat Prince Yi's carriage. Haoyu blinked. He hadn't expected the prince back from Bianliang so soon. "Oh, Guard Bai. What brings you here?"
"His Highness invites you into the carriage for a word."
Who dared refuse Prince Yi? Haoyu obediently climbed aboard.
Scarface and his crew had not cleared out yet. They didn't recognize Bai Feng, but hearing the word *Prince* was enough—who in Lin'an didn't know Prince Yi had taken up residence in town? Sticking out their tongues in fright at the thought of offending royalty, they scattered in a hurry.
Qinggua, who had seen firsthand how lethal Bai Feng was, stayed well back at a safe distance while waiting for his master.
Inside the carriage, Chen Haoyu sat down and bowed. "Had I known Your Highness had returned, I ought to have paid a formal call myself. Making you come all this way to see me is unforgivable. Still, how did you know I was at the silk emporium?"
"When I want to find someone or learn something, no obstacle stays difficult for long," Shen Jianchen said. "Besides, the Chen Silk Emporium is famous enough in Lin'an—hardly a secret."
"I've already seen what Your Highness is capable of. May I ask what brings you here today?"
Shen Jianchen looked over Haoyu's plain tunic. "Why are you dressed like a shop clerk?"
"To be honest with Your Highness, I've decided to turn over a new leaf. I'm going to help run the Chen family business, starting from the bottom as a clerk."
"You truly mean to change?"
"Would I lie about that? That gang outside just came trying to lure me back to Chunxiang Brothel, and I tore strips off them until they tucked their tails and ran."
"And her... is she happy?"
Realization dawned on Haoyu—Prince Yi had come solely for Zhu Shuzhen. "She... I can't tell yet. Still, I believe if I keep at it, she'll come around one day. She's married to me, after all."
Studying the prince's face as he spoke, Haoyu noticed his lowered eyes and heavy gloom.
After a long pause, Shen Jianchen asked, "Do you remember what you said to me outside the Qiantang Gang before I left for Bianliang?"
Haoyu had banished those words clean from his mind, and the reminder hit him like a thunderbolt—he had promised to hand Zhu Shuzhen back to Prince Yi! Regret flooded him, followed by panic that the prince might charge him with breaking his word. He slid off the bench onto his knees—the first time in his life he had ever knelt to anyone inside a carriage.
"I hadn't fallen for Shuzhen back then, so I was just running my mouth," he pleaded. "Please don't hold me to it, Your Highness! With your noble status, you only have to raise a hand and any girl you want will come running. Why fight a commoner like me over my wife?"
"Is that how you see me?"
*Isn't it true?* Haoyu fumed silently. *You crashed my wedding to steal my bride, and even after marrying Princess Jiahe you're still hung up on my wife! If you weren't a prince, I'd brawl with you right here. Look at Qiao Wenxian—ever since Shuzhen married me, he's settled down and left all that old romance behind!*
"Chen Haoyu."
"Yes, Your Highness."

Usually the teahouse drew artsy scholars; a royal matron stood out like a crane in a chicken coop. The manager pointed toward a secluded corner. "Right over there, Eldest Young Master."
"Put her tea on my tab."
"Yes, Eldest Young Master."
Striding straight to Matron Zou's table, Haodong bowed with smooth courtesy. "May I ask what brings a distinguished visitor to seek me out?"
Glancing up at a tall, well-built man with decent features, Matron Zou knew she had Chen Haodong. "I'm no distinguished visitor—merely a servant to royal masters. Please sit, Eldest Young Master Chen."
No sooner had Haodong sat across from her than the male tea master arrived, set down two bowls of whisked tea, bowed, and withdrew. Admiring the delicate pattern drawn across the creamy foam, Matron Zou murmured, "Who knew the Chen teahouse's tea masters could create art right inside a tea bowl?"
"If Matron enjoys it, I'll have him whisk several more bowls."
"No need. One bowl is enough to savor its finest flavor; too many only dull the palate."
"Wise words, Matron."
Beneath the polite banter, Haodong's mind worked overtime—surely she hadn't summoned him just to chat over tea! Reading his thoughts, Matron Zou steered gently toward her goal rather than blurting it raw.
"Back in Bianliang, I saw the Chen family's tea shop too—nowhere near as grand as this one, and business looked pretty slow. I always wondered why the Chen family doesn't thrive in the capital."
Was she probing their capital operations on Prince Yi's behalf? Either way, Haodong answered straight. "Bianliang is packed with noble patrons. We Chens can't muscle in on aristocrats' turf, so we keep a low profile."
"Does your family have any ambition to expand in Bianliang?"
Chen Jianglin had mentioned that exact dream to Haodong once, only to shelve it for lack of backing in the capital. Haodong had suggested asking Zhu Xiyan, since the Prefect had court ties, yet his uncle had shaken his head—Zhu Xiyan helped them in Lin'an, sure, yet his capital connections were strictly guarded for protecting his own official seat, never loaned out to merchants. Could Prince Yi be offering to back the Chen family in Bianliang?! Nothing could beat that!
"To be candid with you, Matron Zou, we do hope to expand there—only lacking a patron in the capital has kept those plans on ice."
"Eldest Young Master may not know this," Matron Zou said smoothly, "but under His Majesty's original decree, Prince Yi and Princess Consort Yi weren't to wed until Mid-Autumn Festival. Yet the wedding moved all the way up to spring, simply because the Princess Consort wished to marry sooner. How many souls in the empire can alter an imperial decree? Princess Consort Yi did. Care to guess how?"
Baffled why she was rambling on about the royal wedding, Haodong still listened with sharp ears and shook his head. "How could an ordinary commoner like me know the workings of royalty?"
"Because the patron behind Princess Consort Yi is Imperial Attendant Liang—His Majesty's right-hand man. One letter from her, and Imperial Attendant Liang had His Majesty amend the decree, then rode all the way to Lin'an himself to deliver it! That's how Princess Jiahe became Princess Consort Yi in the blink of an eye."
Half the picture clicked into place—she was flaunting Princess Consort Yi's powerhouse backer, Imperial Attendant Liang. Still, why pitch this to *him*?
Catching the question in his eyes, Matron Zou laid her cards on the table. "If Eldest Young Master links up with Princess Consort Yi, scaling up the Chen family business in Bianliang won't be a problem at all."
So the tie was Princess Consort Yi, not Prince Yi—either way, royal backing was royal backing! Having a golden ladder dropped straight into his lap thrilled him; if he cracked open the Bianliang market, the helm of the entire Chen empire was guaranteed to be his! Still, Haodong wasn't naive enough to believe free pies fell straight from the sky onto his head. Nobody handed out favors this big for free.
"Earning Princess Consort Yi's favor is a dream come true," Haodong said smoothly. "May I ask what Her Highness wishes me to do in return?"
A satisfied smile curved Matron Zou's lips. "Dealing with a sharp mind saves so much breath! There is someone inside Chen Manor who is a thorn in Princess Consort Yi's flesh and eye. Until that thorn is pulled out, Her Highness will know no peace—and she needs Eldest Young Master's help."
Haodong knew in half a second she meant Haoyu's wife—who in Lin'an didn't know Prince Yi had crashed the wedding over her and wounded Princess Jiahe? Still, plucking that thorn wouldn't be a walk in the park. "That won't be easy—her fists are wicked."
"No rush—we take it step by step." Matron Zou crooked a finger; as Haodong leaned across the table, she dropped her voice to a whisper. "First, stir up a little trouble like this, and then..."
By the time she finished outlining the plot, a grin spread over Chen Haodong's face. "Splendid! I won't let Her Highness down."
Sitting back upright, Matron Zou concluded, "Once it's done, Her Highness will grant your deepest wish. Don't jump the gun yet, though—wait patiently for her signal."
"Deal!" Haodong crowed inwardly. What luck—Princess Consort Yi's goals dovetailed straight into his own! Good thing business had kept him too busy to launch his own scheme these past few days; now his ready-made plan would kill two birds with one stone!
Back at the Judicial Office, Shen Jianchen sat high behind the magistrate's bench presiding over court. Bai Feng and Wen Xin flanked him, Han Gu sat at the clerk's desk taking notes, and constables stood lined along both sides of the hall.
Wen Xin had hauled Sun Er down from the hills; bound with ropes, the prisoner knelt on the flagstones with his head hanging low.
"State your name!" Shen Jianchen barked.
"Sun Er, Your Honor."
A constable stepped forward holding the murder weapon on a wooden tray before Sun Er's eyes. One glance told Sun Er the game was up, and his chin sank back to his chest.
With a wave of Shen Jianchen's hand, the constable withdrew the dagger. "This is the blade you used to murder Fang Jiyuan's wife. Do you confess?"
Sun Er didn't even try to squirm. "I killed Auntie—only I had no choice! Blame Fang Jiyuan!"
An effortless confession lifted a load off Shen Jianchen's chest. "Explain why you killed her and how it happened."
"Your Honor, could you call Fang Jiyuan in? I want to say it straight to his face."
Fortunately, Fang Jiyuan was already waiting outside—no way he was missing the trial of his wife's killer.
"Summon Fang Jiyuan!" Shen Jianchen called out.
A constable at the doors shouted outside, "Fang Jiyuan, His Honor summons you!"
Stepping in behind the runner, Fang Jiyuan spotted Sun Er kneeling in ropes and surged with the urge to beat him bloody, holding himself back only out of awe for the court as he knelt and kowtowed.
"Fang Jiyuan is here, Sun Er," Shen Jianchen said. "Speak."
Sun Er began his confession. "I worked at Uncle and Auntie's house, and they treated me well. I was grateful and truly meant to repay them with honest work. One day Auntie handed me a bundle of clothes and silver and asked me to carry it to her parents' house behind Uncle's back—said Uncle was too tightfisted to let her help her own family. Feeling sorry for her, I took it over."
Fang Jiyuan pounded his own head and wailed, "Her family's a bottomless pit! No amount of silver ever filled it—how dare she call me tightfisted! *Sob*..."
Sun Er went on. "Another time, when Uncle was out, she asked me to make another run. As I took the bundle, my hand brushed hers, and dirty thoughts crept into my head. When I got back from the errand, Uncle still wasn't home and Auntie was tending the kitchen fire. I grabbed her from behind. She fought me off, so I threatened to rat her out to Uncle about the secret bundles. She had no choice—and I was stronger than her anyway—so that's how we had our first time..."
Snapping clean past his limit, Fang Jiyuan sprang to his feet and tackled Sun Er with flailing fists. Nearby constables moved to pull him off, yet Shen Jianchen raised a hand to hold them back—after a betrayal like this, letting the grieving husband blow off steam was only human.
"You ungrateful, cold-blooded beast!" Fang Jiyuan screamed, swinging wildly. "Pulling filth like that under my own roof! I'll beat you to death right here!"
Still, Fang Jiyuan had neither the muscle nor stamina to kill anyone; within minutes he collapsed gasping onto the floor, sobbing his heart out.
Bruised black and blue across the face yet oddly unbothered, Sun Er kept talking. "After that I forced Auntie whenever the urge hit me. Finally she couldn't take it anymore and threatened to tell Uncle everything I'd done. Scared out of my wits, I pulled a dagger and swore if she breathed a word, I'd stop helping her parents and gut her on the spot. She said she'd had enough of living like that—she'd stop helping her parents and tell Uncle the truth even if it killed her! Half terrified, half furious, I plunged the blade straight into her chest..."
With another anguished howl, Fang Jiyuan scrambled up to hammer at him again. Bound hand and foot, Sun Er couldn't swing back, yet his tongue stayed venomous. "It's all on *you*! If you hadn't been such a cheapskate, none of this would've happened! At the end of the day, *you* killed Auntie!"
Fang Jiyuan slumped back onto the stones, broken. "Wife... I was wrong! I shouldn't have been so stingy... *sob*..."
"Afterward," Sun Er finished, "I hid the dagger in the firewood stack, rented a carriage, stuffed Auntie's body inside, and lied to Uncle that she'd gone to her parents' house. Once he fell asleep, I drove out to the bamboo grove, dumped her by the stream, and fled back to the hills the next day."
Case closed. Murder carried no pardon; Sun Er was hauled off to the cells to await execution after the autumn assizes. Clerk Han escorted Fang Jiyuan to claim his wife's body, everyone else returned to their posts, and silence settled back over the Judicial Office.
Shen Jianchen meant to review old case dossiers, yet the trial lingered heavy in his gut, leaving him too restless to read. Fang Jiyuan's petty stinginess over his wife's family had planted the seed of domestic ruin—and what unseen disaster was Shen Jianchen planting by clinging to Qianli Xun and refusing to consummate his marriage with Mengrong?
Yesterday he had promised Wenyuan he would return to Youlin Retreat and consummate the marriage once this case closed—purely to stop his stubbornness from bringing harm down on Qianli Xun. Since he had given his word, why put it off any longer?
Handing off his court affairs, he ordered Bai Feng to hitch the carriage. Hearing His Highness was heading home to see the Princess Consort filled Bai Feng with quiet relief on her behalf.
Just before the carriage reached Youlin Retreat's main gate, Shen Jianchen called a halt and hopped down. "I'm going to walk under the Divine Willow for a bit," he told Bai Feng. "Go inside and tell the Princess Consort I'm staying the night at Youlin Retreat." Without looking back, he disappeared into the woods.
Bai Feng blinked after him for several moments, stumped. What was strange about His Highness sleeping at his own villa? Why make a special point of announcing it to the Princess Consort?
Only after stabling the horses and stepping inside did the penny drop: Princess Consort Yi had decked out the prince's quarters like a wedding chamber and moved right in—saying he would spend the night meant he meant to consummate the marriage at last! Grinning in relief for both of them, Bai Feng hurried inside to relay the message.
Hearing Brother's exact words from Bai Feng, Qiao Mengrong lit up with radiant joy and ordered Xilan to draw hot water at once—she was going to bathe and dress for her husband.
