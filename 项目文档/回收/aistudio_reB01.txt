#CH 172
#TITLE: Run-In Outside Wangyue Pavilion
Back at Wangyue Pavilion, Kuqiao laid out everything she had overheard from Master Chen and his wife down to the last detail.
Qianli Xun grinned from ear to ear. “Looks like making a scene at the Chen estate was a brilliant move. Who knows, the engagement might fall apart today. Once that’s settled, I’ll go talk to my dad and get him to promise he won’t just marry me off to whoever he feels like next time.”
In the old days, Kuqiao would have begged her mistress to drop such reckless talk. Now, though, her Miss had nerves of steel and spine to match; once she set her mind on something, no obstacle could budge her.
“I’m sure you’ll talk Master Zhu round, Miss,” Kuqiao said. Then another thought struck her—spending a whole lifetime alone sounded far too lonely. “Still, I’d feel a lot better once you find someone who likes you back and whom you can rely on for life.”
Qianli Xun fell quiet, thinking it over. Wenyuan would have fit the bill, yet he was getting married today. A good match, gone just like that. Who else out there could ever feel the same way about her?
Out of nowhere, Prince Yi’s face drifted into her mind. Qianli Xun shook her head hard to chase the image away.
The moment she shook him out, he popped right back in.
Seeing her mistress lapse into silence, Kuqiao stepped over to the tea table and began preparing tea.
Qianli Xun told herself it was only because Wenyuan’s wedding had left an empty spot inside her, making her reach for someone else to fill it. Ever since learning that Prince Yi was Shen Jianchen, her feelings toward him had shifted bit by bit—perhaps because, when she first woke up in this body, Shen Jianchen was the only one on her mind.
Then she thought of Mengrong, and guilt hit her at once. Letting her thoughts wander toward her best friend’s fiancé was inexcusable.
Even so, she couldn’t stop herself from asking silently: if Mengrong weren’t in the picture, would she accept Prince Yi’s feelings? She forced herself to murmur, “Maybe,” though deep down she feared the honest answer was a resounding yes.
Before she crossed over, whenever Shen Jianchen confessed his love, she had brushed him off every single time—even throwing out, “Only when pigs climb trees will I ever marry you!”
Now, in this lifetime, if she and Shen Jianchen ever wanted to be together, they really would have to wait for the day pigs learned to climb trees. Thinking back, people ought to watch what they said—one careless remark could turn into a prophecy.
Her thoughts drifted again to Prince Yi’s proposal to take her as a concubine. In this era, for a daughter of Zhu Shuzhen’s background, becoming a prince’s concubine wouldn’t be considered beneath her. Yet Qianli Xun couldn’t get past the hurdle in her own heart.
For one thing, she refused to be anyone’s concubine. Sharing one husband with other women and scheming every day for a man’s favor sounded exhausting—and utterly beneath her dignity.
For another, she couldn’t bear to hurt Mengrong. Mengrong loved Prince Yi to the marrow of her bones; she looked so frail and devoted it broke one’s heart. Qianli Xun felt dirty just for letting her mind stray toward him at all.
To stop brooding, she sat back down at her desk and picked up a brush to work on her huaben tale. The more tangled her thoughts grew, however, the harder it became to spin a story. The huaben wouldn’t flow, yet Zhu Shuzhen’s own poems rose unbidden in her head.
She gave a faint smile. Poetry really was the distillate of emotion; with feelings pressed tight in her chest needing release, Zhu Shuzhen’s verses surfaced all on their own.
Dipping her brush, she wrote out all five poems of Hating Spring:
Poem I: Cherries are just offered up, apricots and plums still sour; / Tender scholar-trees, brisk wind, wheat blades green in the chill. / Melancholy that the Lord of Spring is so fickle of heart— / Even to detain him a little while must prove hard indeed.
Poem II: A fleeting burst of spring bloom passes in so brief a space, / Hard to find fine lines enough to record this longing. / Though spring’s light is fair, it brings too much wind and rain; / Love has only just grown deep—alas for parting! / Tear-filled eyes thank the flowers for wrapping me in their embrace; / My sorrowing heart has only wine to lean on for support. / Orioles and swallows, cease your mocking laughter— / Try roosting alone yourselves, and each will know the ache.
Poem III: Heavy-headed from last night’s wine, the sun climbs high and bright; / A single bird cries out from the tip of a flowering branch. / Startled back from sweet dreams just budding into bloom, / Fresh sorrow wakes as the calyx splits wide open. / Secretly I mark our future tryst wherever I may turn; / At leisure I borrow verses to mock this pure regret. / From this day on, I believe that kindness turns to grievance— / For now I’ll trade idle talk only with orioles and flowers.
Poem IV: Slowly the flower-dappled sun climbs to the curtain hooks; / All day long, with no one near, I lean alone on the upper rail. / Butterflies as envoys and bees as go-betweens bring only a traveler’s grief; / Oriole threads and willow strands weave the sorrow of spring. / Word from azure clouds is severed—nothing left but fragrant dreams; / Red leaves turned to verse carry thoughts all the way to autumn. / How many partings, how many tears— / Unbearable to recall again, unbearable to let fall.
Poem V: One coil of incense smoke fades from the arm-scent sachet; / Idly reading a scroll, I lean beside my ivory-inlaid bed. / Oriole notes drift lingeringly into the deep courtyard; / Willow shadows fall dark across the painted wall. / Before my eyes, crimson petals scatter in ten thousand specks; / Down my cheeks, fresh tears run in two or three thin tracks. / Pear blossoms and fine drizzle after dusk— / Even one who knows no sorrow would break his heart at this. [TN: Translator’s rendering]
While she wrote, Kuqiao brought over the freshly whisked tea. Seeing her mistress deep in concentration, she didn’t disturb her, setting the tea bowl down with a feather-light touch and standing beside the desk to watch.
Line by line, poem by poem, Kuqiao marveled in silence. Since coming back from death’s door, her mistress hadn’t lost a shred of her poetic gift—turning out five full poems in a single sitting was nothing short of astonishing.
Once Qianli Xun finished, the tightness in her chest eased. She set down the brush, lifted the tea bowl—now at the perfect temperature—and drained it in one go.
Setting the bowl down, she glanced around. “Where’s Hongdou?”
“Did you forget, Miss? You ordered Hongdou to drill the other nine servants in fist techniques every day. She’s out in the back courtyard right now.”
“Look at my memory.”
From outside the door came a page boy’s voice. “Young Lady, Young Master Dongfang’s servant Huayu is here again.”
Qianli Xun laughed. “Huayu has good timing. Give him all five.”
Kuqiao balked. “You worked so hard writing those, Miss. Why hand over all of them? Two would be plenty.”
“Poems are meant to be read and savored. Keep them locked up next to me and they’ll just grow moldy.”
Seeing the sense in that, Kuqiao gathered all five sheets, stepped out of the boudoir, and walked to the main gate.
Huayu was standing there obediently, craning his neck for a peek inside. The moment Kuqiao appeared, his face broke into a grin. “Youqi Jushi and Sister Kuqiao are both good souls—you never keep me waiting long.”
Kuqiao handed over the stack of paper. “You sure keep a strict schedule, coming every few days. The Qiao family is holding a wedding today—why didn’t you go with your young master?”
Beaming, Huayu accepted the pages. “My master meant to take me along, then remembered today was the day to come to Wangyue Pavilion for new poems, so he left me behind.”
“Didn’t you miss out on a grand feast?”
“Doesn’t matter! My master promised that as long as I bring back a fine piece by Youqi Jushi, he’ll take me to Juxiang Tower.” Noticing the stack felt thicker than usual, Huayu counted the pages—five whole sheets! “What a haul! Youqi Jushi gave me five poems today. My master’s going to be thrilled—Juxiang Tower is a sure thing now!”
After Huayu left, Kuqiao cast a glance toward the spot where Bai Feng usually met her, figuring he must have accompanied Prince Yi to Qiao Manor to offer congratulations and wouldn’t be coming today.
Just as she turned to head back inside, the rumble of carriage wheels reached her ears. Looking closer, she recognized a Chen family carriage. It rolled to a halt, and Chen Haoyu stepped down alone without a single attendant.
Guessing he had come to bring the happy news that the engagement was off, Kuqiao stepped forward with a welcoming smile.
Chen Haoyu stepped down, spotted Kuqiao, and opened his mouth to call out—but before a single word left his lips, a swarm of men surged out from nowhere and boxed him in on all sides.
The sight scared Kuqiao half to death. She froze for a heartbeat, then bolted back inside Wangyue Pavilion, shut the gate, and—unable to resist her curiosity—cracked it open a sliver to peek outside.
A stout, dark-skinned man with a bulging belly waddled through the crowd with narrowed eyes, closing in on Chen Haoyu.
Kuqiao muttered under her breath, “Young Master Chen never does anything decent—no wonder trouble found him! Serves him right.”
She kept watching through the crack in the gate.
As the stout man approached, Chen Haoyu put on a slick, cheerful grin. “Master Jiang! What brings you all the way over here with such a crowd?”
Master Jiang let out a snort. “The Chen estate sits right next door, and you little punk hang around this neighborhood all the time. Think I didn’t know?”
“Master Jiang truly lives up to your name as Lin’an’s know-it-all. Nothing gets past you.”
“Didn’t know Eldest Young Master Chen had such a smooth tongue. Since your manners are so nice today, give me your word right here and now: stay away from Chun Jinmo. I’ve set my sights on her.”
Chun Jinmo had been the very first woman Chen Haoyu turned to after his rumor-mongering scheme was exposed and he broke his fast. From then on, Chun Jinmo had eyes only for Chen Haoyu, who lavished silver on her and looked after her every whim.
“Chunxiang Brothel has plenty of women, Master Jiang. Why make things hard on me? Just name your price in silver, and it’s yours.”
Master Jiang had no intention of backing down. He gave a cold laugh. “Think the Chen family’s stinking pile of cash makes you special? Today I’ll teach you there are things in this world silver can’t buy. I want Chun Jinmo, and I don’t want your money. Let’s see what you can do about it!”
Chen Haoyu cursed himself for bringing no bodyguards today. These men meant business; there was no talking his way out of a brawl. Since he wasn’t about to hand over Chun Jinmo, he might as well fight.
“You’ve got the numbers on your side, Master Jiang, so I’m in no position to stop you. Still, I’m not handing Jinmo over to anyone just like that.”
“Beat him!” Master Jiang barked, stepping back. “Beat him until he agrees!”
His thugs lunged at Chen Haoyu all at once.
Kuqiao slammed the gate shut. “Serves you right! That’s what you get for chasing brothel women!”
She sprinted back to the boudoir. “Miss! Trouble! There’s a fight outside!”
Having poured her feelings into the poems, Qianli Xun felt much calmer and had returned to writing her huaben, paying no attention to Kuqiao.
Kuqiao stressed it again. “Miss, they’re fighting right outside!”
“Let them fight. What’s that got to do with us?” Qianli Xun kept writing.
“They’re beating up Young Master Chen!”
“Good. He had a beating coming anyway.”
Qianli Xun finished a character, then stopped her brush mid-air. “Wait—Chen Haoyu came to Wangyue Pavilion?”
Kuqiao nodded.
“Could he have come to tell me the good news about breaking off the engagement?”
Kuqiao nodded again. “Looked like it! Before he could say a word, a gang surrounded him. They exchanged a few words, and then a brawl broke out.”
Qianli Xun tossed her brush down and stood. “Considering he might have brought me good news, I suppose I’ll lend him a hand. Run to the back courtyard and tell Hongdou to bring everyone here. We’ll use that lot outside for target practice.”
Much as Kuqiao disliked Chen Haoyu, her mistress’s sister-in-law was a decent woman; for her sake alone, they couldn’t leave Young Master Chen to get pummeled. She hurried out to the back courtyard, called Hongdou, and rounded up the nine other servants to meet her mistress in the front yard.
Hearing the racket outside, Qianli Xun ordered the page boy to unbar the gate and charged out with her crew right behind her.
Facing a mob alone, Chen Haoyu was outmatched. Pride kept him swinging, though his half-baked martial arts did no good whatsoever. Before long he ran out of steam, and the thugs pinned his arms and legs so he couldn’t move an inch.
Master Jiang strolled over and slapped Chen Haoyu hard across both cheeks, left then right. “I paid for Chun Jinmo first, you little brat, and the madam only gave her to you because you flashed more silver! Today you learn what happens when you steal a woman from me!”
Chen Haoyu refused to grovel. “Blame the brothel madam for chasing money! If you’ve got the guts, go hit her—why pick on me?”
“Once I’m done teaching you a lesson, she’s next.”
No sooner had Master Jiang finished speaking than the gate behind him swung wide. Three women and seven men poured out, followed by a striking young lady.
Master Jiang’s eyes lit up at the sight of her. “Well now, where did such a pretty little miss pop out from?”
Glancing up, he read the three characters on the plaque: Wangyue Pavilion. “Could this beauty be the mistress of Wangyue Pavilion?”
Before Qianli Xun could open her mouth, Chen Haoyu cried out as if he’d spotted a savior. “Zhu Shuzhen! Help me! These men are all scoundrels!”
At the name Zhu Shuzhen, realization dawned on Master Jiang. “Aha! So you’re that Poetess of Jiangnan! Not bad to look at, not bad at all…”
Sauntering closer, he leered at her. “Word is you’re betrothed to Chen Haoyu. He’s no good—spends all his days sneaking off to Chunxiang Brothel. Why not dump him and come home with me instead? Hehehe!”
Qianli Xun wasn’t about to stomach an insult like that, and his sleazy grin made her skin crawl. Without wasting a syllable, she drove a fist straight into Master Jiang’s face. “Time you learned who you’re messing with! Speak politely to me from now on!”
His cheek stinging like fire, Master Jiang bared his teeth in fury. “What are you idiots gawking at? Get her!”

#CH 304
#TITLE: Kidnapped to Mount Tianmu in the Dead of Night
The masked men clad in night-prowler black were bandits brought down from Mount Tianmu by the brigand chief Black-Fiend Tiger. Slipping into Chen Manor, they ransacked chests and cabinets for gold, silver, and valuables while everyone in the household slept like the dead, none the wiser.
Black-Fiend Tiger led several brothers straight into Moxiang Courtyard as though he knew every turn by heart. Whoever had spread the knockout incense had already unlatched the courtyard gate, giving them free run of the place. With a deft flick of his blade, he slid back the door bolts of each room, had his men strip every chamber of anything worth silver, and only then stepped into the main hall and entered the bedchamber.
Drawing a gleaming broadsword from his waist, he approached the canopy bed. Instinct told him his target was the person lying right here.
Under the lingering spell of the knockout incense, Qianli Xun slept so deeply she sensed no danger creeping near.
Black-Fiend Tiger raised his broadsword to bring it down on the sleeper—just then Qianli Xun rolled over, turning face-up toward him. His blade faltered and dropped of its own accord. He murmured, “What a stunner. Pity to kill her. Might as well haul her up Mount Tianmu to be my bandit bride.”
Sheathing his sword, Black-Fiend Tiger rolled her tight in the quilt, hoisted her onto his shoulder, and led his men out of Chen Manor.
Outside the estate, accomplices already waited beside hitched carriages and tethered horses. Black-Fiend Tiger shoved his bundle inside one carriage while the others crammed looted treasures into the rest.
Once loaded, Black-Fiend Tiger vaulted onto a horse and barked, “Move out fast—city patrol soldiers will be sweeping through soon.”
Some piled onto carriages, others mounted up, and the gang galloped at full speed back toward Mount Tianmu.
Lately, Lin Jue spent most of his days supervising labor at Lazybones Village, returning home around dusk. More recently, he had begun sending a subordinate in his place, going out himself only once every few days.
Tonight happened to be that subordinate’s night watch on street patrol. On a whim, Lin Jue decided to take over the man’s patrol route to repay the favor—and to relive the old rush of serving as a military training officer.
As Lin Jue led his men past the perimeter of Chen Manor, the clatter of carriage wheels and drumming hooves cut through the quiet. Strange—why were so many wagons rolling out at this hour of the night? He spurred his troops after them.
Passing Chen Manor’s south gate, he saw the massive doors standing wide open. Knowing something was wrong, Lin Jue ordered two soldiers to stay behind and guard the entrance while he led the rest in pursuit of the receding rumble. Soon they spotted the convoy ahead and kicked their horses into a gallop.
Hearing government troops closing behind them, Black-Fiend Tiger’s men whipped their horses harder. Even so, Lin Jue cut them off just short of the west city gate.
Lin Jue hauled back on his reins and shouted, “Who are you? Why are you sneaking through the city in the middle of the night? Pull off those masks and let this officer inspect your carriages!”
Black-Fiend Tiger wasn’t about to let some patrol officer bark orders at him. He whipped out his broadsword. “Cut them down!”
The bandits struck first, brandishing steel blades and charging at the soldiers like leopards. Caught on edge, the troops reacted fast and drew their swords to meet them.
Steel clashed against steel in the middle of the street, ringing out across the night sky.
Lin Jue squared off against Black-Fiend Tiger. The brigand possessed brute strength and a vicious edge; realizing he was outmatched, Lin Jue decided that if he couldn’t win, he should at least find out who they were.
Parrying a heavy slash with his sword, Lin Jue demanded, “Who the hell are you? Did you just come from the Chen estate?”
Black-Fiend Tiger spat three words: “Mount Tianmu.” With one sweeping strike, he knocked Lin Jue clean off his horse. The remaining soldiers were beaten back as well, and Black-Fiend Tiger’s gang bolted straight out through the west gate.
Recognition hit Lin Jue a beat too late. “That was Black-Fiend Tiger! Quick—to the Chen estate!”
Vaulting back into the saddle, Lin Jue raced to Chen Manor. Inside, everything had been turned upside down, though no one appeared harmed; it looked as if only money and valuables had been stolen.
Lin Jue wanted to check whether Zhu Shuzhen was safe, yet couldn’t very well barge into her private bedchamber. Besides, the Chen compound was sprawling and crowded, and he didn’t even know which courtyard she lived in. Since everyone else seemed unhurt, she was most likely safe.
Given the gravity of the raid, Lin Jue dared not handle it on his own. He headed for Secluded Forest Villa to report to Prince Yi and await his orders.
At Secluded Forest Villa, Shen Jianchen was sound asleep beside Mengrong when Butler Li’s voice drifted through the haze: “Your Highness, Commander Lin is here. Something major has happened at the Chen estate—he needs your instructions.”
At the words “Chen estate,” Qiao Mengrong snapped wide awake. Assuming Black-Fiend Tiger had done the deed, she burned to hear what had become of Zhu Shuzhen. She whispered in her brother’s ear, “Brother, something big happened in the city. Commander Lin is looking for you.”
Shen Jianchen opened his eyes and asked in a voice thick with sleep, “What happened?”
Lin Jue called from outside the door, “Your Highness, Black-Fiend Tiger raided Chen Manor. Everyone inside has been knocked unconscious.”
Sleep vanished in an instant. Shen Jianchen sat straight up. “Mengrong, I have to go to the Chen estate right now.”
He swung out of bed and dressed himself in seconds.
A sour ache twisted inside Qiao Mengrong—seeing him panic so openly proved his heart was frantic over Zhu Shuzhen.
Still, if today meant he would never lay eyes on that woman again, her mood brightened considerably.
“Go right ahead, Brother. I’ll wait right here at home for you.”
Touched by how understanding and gentle she seemed, Shen Jianchen looked back and gave her an approving smile. “Be good and wait for me.”
Qiao Mengrong put on a worried face. “I’m so anxious about Shuzhen. No matter what state she’s in, Brother, promise you’ll tell me first thing.”
Glad to see Mengrong showing concern for Youqi Jushi, Shen Jianchen believed his efforts were helping heal the rift between the two women. He nodded and gave his promise.
Outside in the courtyard, Lin Jue and Butler Li were waiting. The door flew open, and Prince Yi stepped out.
Before Lin Jue could utter a word, Shen Jianchen ordered, “Commander Lin, ride with me to Chen Manor at once.”
The two galloped back to Chen Manor. Checking the gatehouse first and seeing the servants out cold, Shen Jianchen sent a soldier to fetch a physician from the nearest clinic.
The physician arrived, checked a few servants, and diagnosed sleeping smoke right away. Conveniently, his shop stocked an antidote incense.
Shen Jianchen sent men back with the physician to bring plenty of the antidote incense. They lit it in the servants’ quarters first; once the staff stirred, the male servants went to wake the Chen masters while the maids roused the mistresses.
Sitting in the main hall of Chen Manor, Shen Jianchen worried sick about Qianli Xun, praying she had only fainted from the smoke and nothing worse had happened.
Lin Jue worried a little too. After all, they had once been husband and wife, and she still crossed his mind from time to time.
Two men fretted over the same woman—one seated, one standing—leaving silence as the only thing passing between them.
Before long, Chen Jianglin, Chen Jiangshu, Chen Jiangmu, Chen Haodong, and Chen Haofei staggered into the hall. Seeing Prince Yi, all of them dropped to their knees. “Thank you, Your Highness, for coming to save the Chen family.”
Shen Jianchen cut straight to the point. “Is everyone safe?”
Chen Jianglin answered, “I’ve sent servants to check every courtyard. From what we can tell so far, the bandits only came for silver and didn’t harm anyone. Thankfully, what they took won’t shake the family’s foundation.”
Both Shen Jianchen and Lin Jue breathed easier.
“No need to kneel, gentlemen,” Prince Yi said. “Get up and take a seat.”
At his word, they rose. The three elder brothers sat while Chen Haodong and Chen Haofei stood behind their respective fathers.
Chen Haodong was squirming with regret now. He never should have handed over the layout map of the estate—not only had the family lost a fortune, everyone had been drugged, and who knew what could have happened if they’d breathed that smoke much longer?
Regret came too late, though. All he could hope now was that his sister-in-law had been wiped out; otherwise, if Princess Consort Yi took offense and refused to show up next month to lend him face, how was he supposed to dazzle the clan?
Noticing Chen Haoyu was missing, Shen Jianchen felt a spike of alarm. “Where is Second Young Master Chen?”
Chen Jianglin said, “Haoyu went to check on Shuzhen, so he didn’t come with us.”
The words had barely faded when Chen Haoyu burst into the hall, panic etched across his face. “Father! My wife isn’t in her room! I searched every inch of Moxiang Courtyard and couldn’t find her! Her daytime clothes are still lying right there, and the quilt on her bed is gone!”
Before Chen Jianglin could speak, Shen Jianchen shot to his feet. “Commander Lin, I’m taking a squad up Mount Tianmu tonight. Go to the Judicial Office, grab Chief Constable Wen, and bring more men up behind me.”
“Yes, Your Highness!”
Shen Jianchen strode straight out the door.
Chen Haoyu sprinted after him. “Wait for me, Prince Yi! I’m coming with you!”
By the time Shen Jianchen and his riders reached the foot of Mount Tianmu, dawn was breaking. Leaving the horses below, the group pushed up the mountain trail on foot.
...
Meanwhile, after escaping out the west gate, Black-Fiend Tiger raced his men back up Mount Tianmu. Tonight’s haul was huge—enough gold and silver to keep the mountain brothers living high for a whole year.
Even better, he’d gone down the mountain on a murder job and stumbled onto a breathtaking beauty he simply couldn’t bear to put under his blade.
Setting the quilt-wrapped beauty onto a couch, he lit a lamp and stared down at her sleeping face. The longer he looked, the more smitten he grew—every curve of her brows, eyes, nose, and lips delighted him. Never in his life had he set eyes on a woman so exquisite.
He thought about taking her while she was still out cold, then figured having his fun with someone dead asleep took half the sport out of it. Better to wake her up, give her a good scare, then sweet-talk her a bit—to save her own neck, she’d cave in soon enough.
He ordered a henchman to bring reviving incense, lit it from the candle flame, and waved it under her nose. The beauty stirred, still sunk deep in dreams.
Black-Fiend Tiger shoved the incense back at his man. “Get lost! I’m about to enjoy this beauty—what are you still standing around for?”
Through her haze, Qianli Xun heard a man’s voice humming near her ear. Assuming it was a dream, she ignored it and kept sleeping.
Black-Fiend Tiger leaned right in. “Wake up, pretty thing. Take a look who’s here.”
Hearing someone call her “pretty thing” jolted Qianli Xun awake—this was no dream. Was it Chen Haoyu? No, Chen Haoyu always called her “Wife,” never “pretty thing,” and that voice didn’t sound like him at all.
Sensing trouble, Qianli Xun snapped her eyes open—and found herself staring into a dark-skinned, coarse-featured face she had never seen before in her life. Dread sank into her stomach: had she transmigrated again in her sleep?
She shoved the stranger’s face away, sat up, and scanned the room. Neither Suxin Thatched Hall nor Chen Manor. Eight times out of ten, she’d jumped bodies again.
“Who am I?” she demanded.
Sitting on the edge of the couch, Black-Fiend Tiger blinked. “What kind of fool question is that? You don’t know who you are?”
“Who are you?”
“I’m the famous Black-Fiend Tiger.” He waited for the beauty to turn white as a sheet and tremble with terror.
Instead, she didn’t flinch an inch. Her voice turned sharp as steel. “I don’t care if you’re a black tiger or a white tiger—tell me where the hell I am right now!”
Amused, Black-Fiend Tiger answered, “Mount Tianmu.”
“Mount Tianmu?” Qianli Xun muttered. “Which Mount Tianmu?”
“How many Mount Tianmus do you think there are? There’s only one in Lin’an, and it’s mine. I snatched you right out of Chen Manor tonight. Scared yet? Just submit to me and be my bandit bride, and I’ll treat you real nice.”
At last Qianli Xun pieced it together. She hadn’t transmigrated a second time; while she slept, this Black-whatever-Tiger had hauled her off to the mountains.
She was a martial artist, always alert even in sleep—he must have used some dirty trick to knock her out cold and bundle her all the way up here without her knowing.
Checking herself, she was relieved to find her sleeping clothes intact and tight. Seeing her own quilt beside her, she deduced he had rolled her inside it to carry her out of Chen Manor.
Qianli Xun glared at Black-Fiend Tiger. “Listen, Black-whatever-Tiger, I don’t know you and I’ve never crossed you. Why kidnap me up this mountain?”
“Black-Fiend Tiger!” he corrected, then explained, “Someone paid me to go down the mountain and kill you. Trouble is, you’re too gorgeous—I couldn’t bring myself to slice you up, so I hauled you back up here tonight instead.”
Mengrong flashed through Qianli Xun’s mind. To think she would stop at nothing to have her killed—a wave of sorrow washed over her chest.
Thinking the beauty had lost her nerve, Black-Fiend Tiger leaned in to coax her. “Don’t be scared. Once you give in and belong to me, I won’t kill you. Now come on, let’s have ourselves a good time!”
He reached out to grab her—and caught a fist square in the face, tumbling straight off the couch onto the floor.
“Try getting fresh with me! Not in this lifetime!”
Rubbing his throbbing, already-swelling cheek, Black-Fiend Tiger bared his teeth and scrambled up off the dirt. “Hah! Got some fight in you, eh? Rare! Never had myself a fiery spitfire before—today I’ll break you in!”
Black-Fiend Tiger lunged at the couch. Unafraid, Qianli Xun met him head-on with her fists, trading bare-knuckle blows.
Mid-brawl, Qianli Xun vaulted off the couch onto the floor, moving light on her feet as she fired off punch after punch. Black-Fiend Tiger staggered backward, blood oozing from the corner of his mouth.
Dozens of his bandits rushed into the room to back him up, yet Qianli Xun’s fists and kicks sent every single man sprawling to the floor one after another.
By the time daylight broke outside, Black-Fiend Tiger’s gang still couldn’t take her down.
Noticing their captive was barefoot, one bandit hatched a sly trick. While she tangled with the others, he tossed a jagged, sharp-edged stone across the floor.
Coming down from a sweeping kick, Qianli Xun planted her bare heel dead onto the shard. It sliced into her sole, drawing blood and sending a lance of blinding pain up her leg. Distracted for one fatal second, she was seized by Black-Fiend Tiger’s men and lashed tight to a chair with heavy rope.
Unable to move while blood dripped from her sole, she gritted her teeth through the pain. “You’d better untie me right now! Otherwise the yamen won’t let you walk away from this!”
“What can the yamen do to me?” Black-Fiend Tiger wiped the blood off his lip with the back of his hand. “Pulling you clean out of Chen Manor proves Lin’an’s yamen isn’t worth a fart. Truth be told, I even fought a squad of government soldiers inside the city—those weaklings folded in a couple of moves and let me ride straight out the gate.”
“Who hired you to kill me?” Though Qianli Xun already suspected Mengrong, she wanted to hear it straight from his mouth.
Black-Fiend Tiger had only ever dealt with Chun Jinmo and knew nothing about Princess Consort Yi behind the scenes—and he wasn’t stupid enough to rat out Chun Jinmo either.
“What’s the point asking? Someone paid good money for your head, so I took the job. Only spared you this long because of your pretty face. Since you’re so eager to die, I won’t waste any more mercy—time to finish you off. Bring me a blade!”
A henchman tossed over a broadsword. Black-Fiend Tiger caught it, gripped the gleaming steel, and stalked toward Qianli Xun.

By the time Bai Feng returned with Physician Guo, Qiao Mengrong had calmed down, lying flat on her back with eyes stretched wide open, staring blindly at nothing at all.
After taking her pulse, Physician Guo shook his head with a sigh. “My skill falls short—I cannot cure an affliction this deep. I’ll write a calming tonic to settle her spirits for now, though I advise taking her back to Bianliang. The capital has far better master physicians.”
Shen Jianchen had already meant to head back to Bianliang—and now that Qianli Xun had been hauled there in a prisoner cart, staying in Lin’an made zero sense. He had to go to Bianliang and save her.
Once the physician wrote the prescription and left, Shen Jianchen told Matron Zou, “We leave for Bianliang tomorrow.”
Then he sent Bai Feng straight to Chen Manor to tell Chen Haoyu that if he wanted to save his wife, they could travel north together.

Reaching Chen Manor’s south gate, Bai Feng saw the Qiao carriage parked nearby, with Qiao Wenxian and Nan Shuang standing outside peering eagerly toward the entrance.
Just as Bai Feng walked up to greet them, Chen Haoyu stepped out the main gate.
Without saying a word, Qiao Wenxian charged straight up and started swinging at him, yelling between blows, “If I’d known marrying you would bring Shuzhen to this, I never would’ve let you wed her!”
Standing numb, Chen Haoyu didn’t even lift a hand to block, letting Qiao Wenxian hammer away against his chest.
Not that Qiao Wenxian’s soft scholarly fists hurt much; panting once he tired out, he seized Chen Haoyu’s shoulders and shook him hard. “What are you going to do?!”
“I’m going to Bianliang to save her,” Chen Haoyu answered flatly.
“At least you still act half a man.” Letting go, Qiao Wenxian added, “I lived in Bianliang a spell and know a few influential folks there. Once I get home, I’ll write down every name and address for you. Show up mentioning my name, and they’ll grant you a hearing—might even help get Shuzhen out. If my wife and concubine weren’t both pregnant right now, I’d ride north with you myself.”
Chen Haoyu condensed every ounce of gratitude into two words. “Thank you.”
Stepping over, Bai Feng announced, “Gentlemen—my master bids me inform you he sets out for Bianliang tomorrow. If Young Master Chen wishes to save Youqi Jushi, you’re welcome to ride along with His Highness.”
Qiao Wenxian brightened at once. “Riding with Prince Yi gives you solid protection—and you won’t be groping blind in Bianliang. Makes saving Shuzhen far easier!”
Seeing the wisdom in it too, Chen Haoyu nodded. “Thank you for bringing word, Guard Bai. Tell His Highness I’ll meet him bright and early tomorrow without fail.”
With a firm answer in hand, Bai Feng lingered anyway, craning his neck past the gateway.
Catching on fast, Chen Haoyu called to a gatekeeper, “Go tell Kuqiao to come out.”
Bai Feng grinned wide. “Many thanks, Young Master Chen!”
Drawing Chen Haoyu aside, Qiao Wenxian rattled off careful instructions on court etiquette and which officials to tread lightly around so he wouldn’t stumble blindly around the capital.
Meanwhile, Nan Shuangsidled over beside Bai Feng. “Heading back to Bianliang?”
“Yep—leaving tomorrow.”
“Haven’t caught up in ages. Once you finish talking with Kuqiao, drop by Qiao Manor and let’s have a real chat.”
“Deal.”
Finishing his briefing, Qiao Wenxian departed with Nan Shuang.
Cupping his hands gratefully to Bai Feng, Chen Haoyu hurried back inside to pack.
At last Kuqiao hurried out the gate. One look at Bai Feng, and tears burst right down her cheeks. “My Miss was hauled off to Bianliang in a prisoner cart—no telling if she’s dead or alive! What do we do?!”
Bai Feng hurried to reassure her. “His Highness leaves for Bianliang tomorrow—and if I’m not wrong, he’s only rushing north this fast to rescue Youqi Jushi! I just came to invite Young Master Chen to ride with us, and Young Master Qiao even promised letters introducing big shots in the capital! Don’t cry—Youqi Jushi’s going to be fine!”
Hope lit a tearful smile on Kuqiao’s face. “Really?”
“With this many people tearing down Heaven to save her, how could anything go wrong?”
“Where were you these past two days?”
“Locked up inside Liu’an Villa with His Highness.”
“Are you hurt?!”
“Not a scratch!” Terrified she’d ask about his wound and worry sick, Bai Feng switched subjects fast. “Why don’t you come along to Bianliang with us?”
Kuqiao agreed without blinking. “I’m coming! For Miss, I’d go anywhere!”
“Brilliant! I’ll go beg His Highness to bring you along!”
“Why ask Prince Yi? My Young Master is going too!”
“Oh—right, right! Just ask Young Master Chen! Haha, got so excited I forgot you’re a Chen maid now!”
Their flicker of cheer faded fast as worry for Qianli Xun returned. After soothing her a bit longer, Bai Feng parted ways with Kuqiao and hurried off to Qiao Manor for one last catch-up with Nan Shuang before leaving town indefinitely.

#CH 324
#TITLE: Bidding Farewell to Prince Yi
Arriving at Qiao Manor looking for Nan Shuang, Bai Feng learned Young Master Qiao had taken him straight out to Secluded Forest Villa—so back Bai Feng rode.
Reaching Secluded Forest Villa, he found Nan Shuang sitting at the foot of the wooden platform while Prince Yi and Young Master Qiao sat drinking and talking up top, facing toward the Divine Willow.
Plopping down beside Nan Shuang, Bai Feng complained, “Just rode all the way to Qiao Manor looking for you, and here you are sitting in my backyard!”
“Been waiting here a good while already.”
“You’re my only real friend in Lin’an—hate leaving you behind.”
“Why not ask Prince Yi to stay then?”
“No chance. His Majesty stripped his Military Commissioner post—what’s left keeping him here? Besides, with Youqi Jushi hauled to Bianliang in a prisoner cart, His Highness has zero heart left for Lin’an…”
“Prince Yi really is faithful to the bone—still can’t let go of Youqi Jushi after all this time.”
“Keep your voice down.” Tapping a finger against his temple, Bai Feng whispered, “Princess Consort Yi’s head isn’t right anymore. Local doctors can’t cure her, so His Highness has to take her to Bianliang for treatment.”
“Lost her marbles?”
“Out of her senses.”
Nan Shuang’s mouth fell open. “How on earth did that happen?!”
“Some big fright, plus grief—and she just snapped.”
“Whoa! Being scared too hard can wreck your head? Better watch myself from now on!” Shaking off the shock, Nan Shuang grinned shyly. “My master says I get to marry Youfan next spring—wonder if you’ll be back in Lin’an by then?”
“Congrats first off—though no telling if I’ll be back by spring.”
“What about you and Kuqiao? When’s the wedding?”
“Beats me. With Youqi Jushi in trouble, how could Kuqiao even think about marrying? And with Princess Consort Yi like this, I wouldn’t dare bother His Highness about my own plans—have to keep pushing it off.”
Nan Shuang sighed in relief. “Lucky my master didn’t marry Youqi Jushi after all—otherwise he’d be the one sweating bullets right now, and my wedding would be pushed off too!”
Irritated by Nan Shuang’s smug look, Bai Feng snapped, “Don’t think your master’s living some dream life—he only looks fine on the outside.”
“Still beats marrying someone hauled off to Bianliang in a prison cage!”
Bai Feng opened his mouth to curse Nan Shuang out, then bit it back knowing how long it might be before they met again—besides, who could blame Nan Shuang for thinking like an ordinary bloke? Youqi Jushi’s life really had been bumpy as cobblestones, dragging his master along for the ride.
“Knock it off—don’t talk about Youqi Jushi like that. She’s a good soul, just hit some rough luck.”
The two friends sat chatting over ordinary trifles, masking the sting of parting beneath casual banter.
...
Up on the platform, Shen Jianchen and Qiao Wenxian drank steadily, trading farewells of their own.
“Who could have guessed how fast things turn? Barely over a year in Lin’an, and you’re already heading back. Feels hard watching you go.”
Remembering those early months sharing Secluded Forest Villa with Wenyuan, Shen Jianchen sighed. “Same here—yet no feast lasts forever.”
“Is it because of Shuzhen?”
“Not *only* because of her—though she’s why my mind locked firm and why we’re leaving early.”
After a quiet pause, Qiao Wenxian spoke softly. “At last I see you love her deeper than I ever did.”
“With her, it goes well beyond a single word like *love*.”
“What is it then?”
Shen Jianchen shook his head. “Can’t even explain it. Only know wherever she goes, I have to go—otherwise I wouldn’t even know why I’m drawing breath.”
“She’s Chen Haoyu’s wife now.”
“Which is why I’m taking Chen Haoyu to Bianliang to help save her.”
“And once you rescue her, she’ll ride back to Lin’an with Chen Haoyu. Are you planning to come back too?”
Gazing toward the distant Divine Willow, Shen Jianchen murmured, “The Divine Willow stands in Lin’an. If she returns to Lin’an too, sooner or later I’ll be back.”
Too heavy a topic to linger on, Qiao Wenxian steered away. “How is Princess Consort Yi holding up?”
“Not well at all.”
“What do you mean?”
“Her mind’s slipped—keeps giggling blankly and muttering things nobody makes sense of.”
“How did it come to this?”
“Suffered a fright while out, finally conceived only to miscarry, got crushed by grief, then learned she can’t bear children again. All those blows shattered her—now she alternates between laughing and weeping or spouting gibberish.”
Qiao Wenxian shook his head in pity. “Who’d have thought trouble would hit you too in just a few days? Bianliang boasts master physicians—someone there might cure Her Highness.”
“That’s my hope too. Everything piled together leaves no choice but going back to Bianliang.”

Bright and early the next morning, Chen Haoyu rode up to Secluded Forest Villa with Kuqiao and Qinggua to meet Prince Yi’s party.
Pulling up outside the gate, he found a sizable crowd gathered to see Prince Yi off.
Prince Yi stepped through the gate trailed by Matron Zou and Princess Consort Yi. Even bystanders couldn’t miss how glazed and vacant the princess consort’s eyes looked, devoid of her old spark.
Once the women climbed inside their carriage, well-wishers stepped up one by one to bid Prince Yi farewell.
Zhu Xiyan spoke first. “To see Your Highness go to such lengths for my daughter’s sake moves me beyond words. Having nothing worthy to repay you with, let me kowtow in gratitude.”
As Zhu Xiyan dropped toward his knees, Shen Jianchen caught his arms. “I’m saving Youqi Jushi of my own free will—no need for you to kneel to me. If you kneel, it only sits wrong on my conscience.”
Everyone standing by understood perfectly what Prince Yi meant.
Watching from inside his carriage, Chen Haoyu’s jaw clenched tight: *Saying that right in front of everybody—doesn’t put me in his eyes at all!*
Seeing a line waiting to speak with the prince, Zhu Xiyan excused himself politely. “You’re busy, Your Highness. Let me climb into my son-in-law’s carriage for a word.”
With Prince Yi’s nod, Zhu Xiyan stepped inside Chen Haoyu’s carriage.
Next up stepped Master Jiang. “Your Highness, a word in private?”
Though Ferry Superintendent was only a minor post, it was still a government post. Seeing Master Jiang brave Liang Shicheng’s wrath to show up warmed Shen Jianchen’s heart; he nodded readily.
Stepping aside together, Shen Jianchen teased lightly, “Coming to send me off—aren’t you afraid the new Prefect will make life miserable for you?”
Master Jiang grinned. “Only virtue I’ve got is sticking by friends. You helped me out before—even if it costs me my post as Ferry Superintendent, I don’t turn my back on a debt.”
“Got a real conscience on you.”
“Came for two reasons, Your Highness: first, to wish you safe travels; second, to drop a hint—my wife suspects what happened to Youqi Jushi ties back to Princess Consort Yi. You know she and Her Highness pulled a dirty stunt against Youqi Jushi once before, so she reads Her Highness pretty well. Thinking it over, her hunch rings true—wanted to tell you straight before you left.”
Noticing Prince Yi’s expression stiffen instantly, Master Jiang wrapped up fast. “That’s all I had to say, Your Highness. Smooth roads north—farewell!”
After Master Jiang stepped away, Shao Zhengye and his son Shao Zhize walked up carrying two jars of wine.
“Two jars of fine vintage for Your Highness to drink along the road,” Shao Zhengye offered warmly.
Pushing down the dark churn stirred by Master Jiang’s warning, Shen Jianchen cupped his hands. “Thank you, Master Shao! Bai Feng, take Master Shao’s gift!”
Bai Feng stepped forward, accepted both jars from Shao Zhize, and stowed them neatly inside the hitched carriage.
An escort guard from the Shao Family Escort Agency followed with a tray holding three bowls of wine.
“Drink up, Your Highness,” Shao Zhengye beamed. “Everything goes unsaid in a bowl of wine!”
Clinking bowls together, the three men drained theirs dry in one gulp, Shao Zhengye and his son bursting into hearty laughter.
Before he knew it, Shen Jianchen laughed along with them, setting his heaviness aside for a moment. Grateful to father and son, he marveled how one hearty laugh of theirs could sweep away gloom and rekindle nerve to face whatever lay ahead.
Once the laughter settled, Shao Zhengye and his son cupped their hands. “Safe journey, Your Highness—farewell!”
Last in line stood the Judicial Office crew—Han Gu and Wen Xin leading several constables forward.
Before they could cross the grass, Shen Jianchen strode straight out to meet them.
“Thank you both for coming to see me off.”
The two officers dropped to their knees alongside every constable. “We pay our respects to Prince Yi!”
“We’re all brothers here—why kneel? Get up quickly!”
Rising, Han Gu sighed, “Only a few days gone and we already miss Your Highness running the Judicial Office. The new Judicial Investigator cares only for lining his pockets—wouldn’t lift a finger for common folk.”
Wen Xin spat, “Liang’s lapdog too! Can’t stomach staying Chief Constable under him anymore—soon as I finish today’s shift, I’m quitting the Judicial Office and heading home to farm. Anything beats serving a crooked boss.”
“I’m heading home to the countryside too,” Han Gu nodded. “The new guy knows how close we stood with Your Highness and squeezes us every turn. Zero point sticking around.”
“Hate seeing you both dragged down because of me.”
“Never crossed our minds, Your Highness,” Han Gu protested. “Blame a rotten court and treacherous ministers who can’t tolerate honest men speaking for the people. Not even an imperial prince could hold the line.”
Wen Xin added fiercely, “Why serve a court like that? Better go home and tend our own integrity.”
“Well said! ‘When successful, benefit all under Heaven; when thwarted, cultivate one’s own virtue in solitude.’ [TN: Translator’s rendering] In a fading age, keeping your own hands clean is no small thing.”
Both Han Gu and Wen Xin nodded firmly at the prince’s words.

Inside the carriage meanwhile, Zhu Xiyan pressed crucial instructions onto Chen Haoyu—most importantly a list of his own contacts in Bianliang to call on right after arriving, any one of whom might hold the key to freeing Shuzhen.
Memorizing every detail alongside the names and addresses Qiao Wenxian had sent via Nan Shuang last night, Chen Haoyu felt confident they stood a solid shot at rescuing his wife.
Suddenly remembering his sister Chen Wenxin, he asked anxiously, “How are my sister and Mother-in-law holding up?”
Zhu Xiyan let out a ragged sigh. “Coming home from Liu’an Villa yesterday, I told them what happened to Shuzhen. Your mother-in-law collapsed sick in bed from heartbreak. Between worrying over Shuzhen and nursing her mother-in-law, Wenxin’s barely holding together herself. Jingyuan and I have to stay strong as men. Every last hope in both households rests on your trip to Bianliang to bring Shuzhen home.”
“Liang took so much silver from our family—surely he won’t torment my wife too badly.”
“I know Liang Shicheng inside out: greedy for silver *and* hungry for women, wanting both ends at once. First time you brought silver, he turned it down because lust came first. When Shuzhen marched in, refused him, and even beat him up, he locked her up and slapped a crime on her head out of spite. Only when you came back the second time couldn’t he bear turning down five hundred thousand taels a second time. Now that the money’s in his pouch, no telling if he’ll lift a finger—don’t pin too much hope on *him*.”
“So five hundred thousand taels went straight down the drain?!”
“Straight down the drain—with nowhere on earth to appeal.”
“Liang Shicheng is pure scum! Why would His Majesty ever put a monster like him in power?!”
“An incompetent emperor naturally surrounds himself with treacherous officials.”
Chen Haoyu stared in surprise. “When you still served in office, Father-in-law, you sang His Majesty’s praises every chance you got—how’d your tune flip overnight?”
“In office, a man’s hands are tied—you say what the role demands, even when your heart sees clear as glass. Funny how what men think inside and what leaves their mouths are two completely different things.”
Chen Haoyu teased lightly, “So are your mouth and heart saying the same thing right now, Father-in-law?”
“Every single word.” Glancing out the window, Zhu Xiyan saw Prince Yi stepping toward his carriage. “Enough talk. One last reminder: every single contact I gave you—don’t miss a soul. Knock on every single door.”

Hurrying straight up to Prince Yi, both men cupped their hands and bowed.
“Young Master Qiao sent word yesterday that Your Highness departs for Bianliang today,” Dongfang Chenze said warmly, “so we rushed out to bid you farewell.”
Pulling his foot back down from the carriage step, Shen Jianchen smiled. “Thank you both for coming all this way to see me off.”
An Mingxuan sighed, “Lin’an will feel much emptier with Your Highness gone.”
“You flatter me, Young Master An. To Lin’an, I was only ever a passing traveler—no loss at all.”
“No matter what anyone else says,” Dongfang Chenze replied earnestly, “in our eyes, what Your Highness did meant everything to the people of Lin’an.”

Together with everyone else, they watched Prince Yi’s and Chen Haoyu’s carriages roll away.
Three carriages moved out in convoy: Qiao Mengrong, Matron Zou, and Xilan in one; Chen Haoyu with Qinggua and Kuqiao in the second; Shen Jianchen and Bai Feng in the third.
As the wheels rolled down the highway, farewells faded behind them, and Master Jiang’s warning crept straight back into Shen Jianchen’s mind, darkening his mood toward Mengrong once more.
Over the forged scrolls and the Mount Tianmu kidnapping, because Qianli Xun had come out unscathed, he had forgiven Mengrong—even blamed himself. Yet if she had engineered *this* disaster too, he doubted he could ever forgive her again.
Some distance beyond Secluded Forest Villa, a cluster of figures flagged down the convoy—Qiao Shouren waiting alongside Madam Qian, Concubine Mi, and Wenyuan to say goodbye.
Though Wenyuan had already seen Prince Yi off yesterday, his parents insisted on coming, leaving him no choice as a dutiful son.
Climbing down from his carriage, Shen Jianchen received the Qiao elders’ bows.
Steadying Qiao Shouren’s arms, Shen Jianchen urged gently, “Once I leave, Grandfather, Grandmother, and Aunt Mi must take good care of yourselves. Soon as Wenyuan’s children arrive, you can enjoy bouncing your grandchildren on your knees.”
That warmed every Qiao face at once.
Still, Qiao Shouren couldn’t fully smile—knowing Prince Yi was only returning to Bianliang stripped of his Military Commissioner post, and having heard from Wenyuan yesterday that Princess Consort Yi had lost her wits, Qiao Shouren had lain awake hurting for the prince.
Mental ailments rarely healed; sooner or later, Prince Yi would likely wed again or take a concubine anyway. Better to get the heavy secret off his chest now.
Not wanting Wenyuan to hear, Qiao Shouren told Madam Qian, “Take those two over yonder for a moment—I need a private word with Prince Yi.”
Strolling aside, the three watched from afar as Qiao Shouren leaned close to murmur in the prince’s ear. Wenyuan frowned, wondering what his father could be whispering about, though Madam Qian guessed easily enough it concerned Princess Consort Yi.
Staying inside his carriage, Chen Haoyu stared sharply at the pair, too far to catch a single syllable.
Matron Zou had thought about climbing down to exchange pleasantries with the Qiaos, yet one glance at Princess Consort Yi’s vacant stare drained every ounce of heart from her. Seeing Qiao Shouren pull Prince Yi aside in private out the window sent a cold knot twisting in her gut—praying silently he wouldn’t spill Her Highness’s secret.
Once his family stepped out of earshot, Qiao Shouren lowered his voice. “I’ve swallowed something for months, afraid saying it might ruin things between Your Highness and Princess Consort Yi. Yet ever since Mount Tianmu bandits raided the Chens, my heart’s turned cold with dread. Now that the poetess’s been hauled off on some trumped-up charge, I can’t keep quiet anymore…”
Already guessing the rest, Shen Jianchen finished calmly, “Grandfather suspects Mengrong is behind all of it.”
Relief washed over Qiao Shouren’s shoulders. “So Your Highness already knew.”
“About the earlier plots, yes. This time I can’t be certain yet. Why did Grandfather suspect Mengrong?”
Qiao Shouren held nothing back. “Right after you and Princess Consort Yi returned from your wedding in Bianliang, Matron Zou came calling at Qiao Manor on Her Highness’s behalf—asking our house to find a way to make the poetess disappear. The Qiao family has never touched murder! How could I take that on? So I pointed Matron Zou toward Master Jiang instead. I spent months sweating in fear afterward—thank Heaven word never came that Master Jiang killed the poetess, or my conscience would never let me sleep!”
Now Shen Jianchen knew with absolute certainty: Liang Shicheng’s trap around Qianli Xun traced straight back to Mengrong. Worse, Matron Zou knew everything and had actively aided her—likely Xilan knew the whole story too! Fool that he had been to forgive Mengrong out of guilt last time, landing Qianli Xun straight in mortal peril!
Bidding the Qiaos goodbye and climbing back aboard, Shen Jianchen spoke not a single word the rest of the road—whatever thread of affection still lingered between him and Mengrong had snapped clean for good.
