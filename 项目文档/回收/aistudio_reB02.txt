#CH 325
#TITLE: The Prince Departs, and Winds Shift
Three days later, Liang Shicheng’s bruised face had healed considerably. Eager to return to Bianliang and settle the matter of the female ci poet, he planned to linger in Lin’an for four or five days to wrap up his business there.
At the top of his list was replacing any official who had been close to Prince Yi yet failed to offer him a bribe.
Word traveled fast. Officials big and small across Lin’an flocked to his door, catering to his tastes with silver or women. Many kept the black gauze caps on their heads that way.
There were always a few stubborn souls who offered neither coin nor women and resigned their posts of their own accord. Liang Shicheng only mocked them for failing to ride the tide of power.
Master Jiang did not escape scrutiny either; Liang even knew he had gone to see Prince Yi off on the day of his departure. To hold onto the post he had worked so hard to gain, Master Jiang went to the madam of Chunxiang Brothel, fetched three commercial courtesans dressed as married women, and presented them to Imperial Attendant Liang.
Liang praised Master Jiang for knowing how the world worked, let him keep his post, and warned him to stay clear of Prince Yi from now on. Master Jiang agreed to his face, while scoffing in private.
The biggest winners in Lin’an were Lin Jue and Chun Jinqiao. Lin Jue leaped two ranks at once to become Commander-in-Chief of All Garrisons, holding total military authority over Lin’an.
Chun Jinqiao visited Liu’an Villa twice more and pleased Imperial Attendant Liang better than any other woman he had summoned. Liang Shicheng went so far as to throw a banquet in her name, and to curry favor with him, every official in Lin’an brought his family.
At the banquet, Liang Shicheng boasted without shame that Chun Jinqiao was a paragon among Lin’an women, blessed with both beauty and a kind heart. He paid two paupers from somewhere to testify before the crowd about how she had helped them. Coached beforehand, the pair lavished praise on Jinqiao until even she found her own goodness hard to believe.
Next, they praised her for living in harmony with the primary wife despite being a concubine, keeping peace within the household. Under pressure from Liang’s power, Bai Meiruo forced herself to invent a few tales before everyone about how she and Jinqiao treated one another like sisters and how Jinqiao always kept the bigger picture in mind, laying the flattery on thick. Once she finished speaking, her stomach churned so hard she nearly threw up.
For days afterward, nausea dogged her. When she called a physician to take her pulse, she learned she was one month pregnant. The child saved her place as primary wife; otherwise, riding this wave of favor, Jinqiao could have seized that seat with ease.
After the banquet, Jinqiao basked in glory, watching the noblewomen of Lin’an fawn over her with cool eyes. At last, she thought, she had held her head high before the very people who used to look down on her.
Her one bitter disappointment was seeing her dream of replacing Bai Meiruo as primary wife fall through. Imperial Attendant Liang told her there had never been a precedent since ancient times for a concubine to take the primary seat while the primary wife lived. He could grant anything else, he said, just not a breach of that age-old rule.
Jinqiao begged Lin Jue to help, leaving him torn, until Meiruo’s pregnancy ended that hope for good. Out of deference to Liang, Lin Jue promised Jinqiao she would stand on equal footing with Meiruo in the Lin household from then on, and Jinqiao dropped her demands.
To all appearances, Jinqiao shone in glory, yet behind closed doors the noblewomen of Lin’an cursed her up and down.
Bai Meiruo met Miao Zhiyu at Yongtai Teahouse, one newly pregnant and the other already showing at three or four months. After exchanging congratulations on a pregnancy that strengthened a woman’s standing at home, they sat in a private booth, sipped tea prepared by the tea master, and began trading barbed remarks.
“A concubine out of a brothel, good at nothing but selling her flesh, actually thought she could snatch the primary wife’s seat,” Bai Meiruo said. “She doesn’t know her own place. Too bad Heaven shut the door on her. Not only did Imperial Attendant Liang refuse, I went and got pregnant too.”
“You praised her to the skies at the banquet like some rare blossom,” Miao Zhiyu teased. “Why look down on her now?”
“You know full well why. Why ask?”
Miao Zhiyu set down her tea bowl and sighed. “Ever since Imperial Attendant Liang set foot in Lin’an, decency has gone to the dogs. A shameless woman like that riding roughshod over us! Yesterday when I had to bow to her, my skin crawled.”
“Tell me how the others curse her,” Meiruo urged. “Give me something to smile about.”
“Nothing but filth. Calling her a ‘cheap slut,’ a ‘worn shoe,’ and dragging Commandant Lin through the mud alongside her. Nobody dares curse aloud, of course, so everyone vents behind her back. When I chatted with Xiaguo yesterday, she cursed nonstop too, saying Chun Jinqiao disgraced women everywhere.”
“Good!” Meiruo’s smile faded as her thoughts turned to Youqi Jushi. “Now I understand why Youqi Jushi left the Lin household. A man who hands his own woman out to another cannot be trusted. I expect nothing from him anymore. From now on I care about two things only: holding my seat as primary wife, and raising this child well so it doesn’t turn out like its father.”
Miao Zhiyu sighed in admiration. “Youqi Jushi is the true model for women. That trumped-up charge Imperial Attendant Liang pinned on her must have come because she refused to bow to his power.”
“My husband told me Liang likes married women. He must have set his sights on Youqi Jushi, and when she refused, he framed her.”
“Which proves all the more what a model she is.”
“Still, I suspect there is more to it.”
Miao Zhiyu caught her meaning at once. “You think Princess Consort Yi had a hand in it?”
Meiruo nodded. “You and I joined her a few times to move against Youqi Jushi. Even then I knew Princess Consort Yi would never let her off.”
“Wenyuan told me she lost her mind before leaving for Bianliang. It couldn’t be her.”
“She seemed fine before. How did she lose her mind?”
“I heard she suffered a bad fright, then miscarried, and the grief and shock pushed her over the edge.”
Bai Meiruo lifted her bowl, sipped in silence, and let out a long breath. “Good thing you and I pulled back in time instead of following her down that road. Otherwise we would have ruined others and ourselves alike.”
“So you still believe Youqi Jushi’s trouble ties back to Princess Consort Yi? If it does, she brought this ruin on herself.”
“I can’t be sure about this time, yet Mount Tianmu was her doing without a doubt.”
“Commandant Lin told you?”
Meiruo shook her head. “Woman’s instinct. We’re both carrying children now. For their sake, we can’t act as we did before.”
“You’ve grown up a lot.”
“Once you let go of things, you grow up on your own. I used to resent Youqi Jushi for holding my husband’s heart. Looking back now, what is Lin Jue worth? Not worth my envy of anyone, least of all a woman fearless enough to defy Liang Shicheng.”
“That’s the first time I’ve heard you call Commandant Lin by his plain name. You really have changed. Truth be told, I don’t hold a grudge against Youqi Jushi anymore either. I knew her long before and understood her temper best. Only because of Wenyuan did I turn jealous and refuse to be close to her. Now that I’m carrying Wenyuan’s child, what is there left to envy? Once that jealousy faded, everything became clear. I’m grateful she walked away from Wenyuan and made room for me. Putting myself in her shoes, she must have hurt terribly back then.”
“When Youqi Jushi makes it back safe to Lin’an, let’s pay her a visit together. I want her as a close friend.”
“Agreed.”
Having reached the same mind, the two women sighed a bit more over life, cursed Chun Jinqiao one last round, and went their separate ways home.
Along the road out of Lin’an, whether traveling by day or resting at inns by night, Shen Jianchen ignored Qiao Mengrong. She stayed lost in her fog, vacant and simple-minded, laughing one moment and crying the next. His cold shoulder meant nothing to her now.
As Matron Zou and Xilan tended the Princess Consort along the route, both saw the change in Prince Yi. Matron Zou suspected he had guessed her mistress’s hand in the poetess’s plight, and braced herself for his reckoning once they reached Bianliang.
Traveling alongside them, Chen Haoyu learned of Princess Consort Yi’s condition and wondered what could have broken a sane woman like that. Seeing Prince Yi’s dark mood kept him from asking, though he marveled that the prince paid his wife no mind at all. Haoyu assumed Prince Yi shunned her because she had gone simple, which fed his dread that the prince might try to take his own wife instead. He resolved that once they reached Bianliang, he would avoid leaning on Prince Yi; the high officials Qiao Wenxian and Zhu Xiyan had recommended should carry enough weight to save her.
After more than ten days on the road, the party reached Bianliang at last.
Haoyu looked to take rooms at an inn, but Shen Jianchen refused and insisted he stay at Prince Yi’s Manor. Haoyu went along reluctantly. By the time they arrived, dusk had fallen, leaving no time to visit anyone on his list until the next day.
Once everyone settled in, ate supper, and Mengrong fell asleep, Shen Jianchen sent Bai Feng to summon Matron Zou and Xilan. Bai Feng delivered the message and slipped off to find Kuqiao, leaving only the prince, the matron, and the maid inside.
Guessing what awaited her, Matron Zou knelt as soon as she stepped in, and Xilan dropped down beside her. Seated behind his desk, Prince Yi made no move to bid them rise.
“Since you knelt on your own, Matron, you must know what I mean to ask,” Shen Jianchen said. “No more hiding anything. Tell me the whole truth from start to finish.”
Matron Zou confessed. She told how Princess Consort Yi grew frantic after learning of his love for the poetess and losing face when he crashed the wedding, bringing Matron Zou to Lin’an to ruin Youqi Jushi’s name. When that failed—and he used Dongfang’s poetry banquet to restore her honor—Mengrong’s heartbreak hardened into murder. Master Qiao refused to help, so she struck a deal with Chen Haodong, setting off the Chen family troubles and the plot with Master Jiang, followed by her alliance with Master Jiang’s wife that led to Mount Tianmu.
Matron Zou slanted the truth slightly—Mengrong had wanted blood even before visiting Qiao Manor—hoping the prince would feel the weight of his own role and spare a broken woman his wrath.
Shen Jianchen cut her off. “You are older, Matron, seasoned in the ways of the world. You knew right from wrong. Why fail to counsel Mengrong and become her accomplice instead?”
“At first I pitied her,” Matron Zou answered. “When Your Highness lay sick, she nursed you night and day. Anyone seeing it would have been moved, let alone me watching every hour. When you returned from death’s door, forgot your bond with her, and set your heart on another, nobody felt her torment more than I, so I helped her. Later I saw Youqi Jushi kept to her own life with no improper designs, and I tried to dissuade the Princess Consort, yet she acted possessed, bent on killing her. What choice did I have?”
Shen Jianchen drove straight to the vital question. “Did Princess Consort Yi cause Youqi Jushi’s current disaster?”
Both Matron Zou and Xilan kept silent.
“Xilan, speak the truth. Did you lie to me that day about attending a banquet? You never went to a banquet. You went to Liu’an Villa.”
Xilan pressed her forehead to the floor, terrified. “Y-Your Highness, we did go to a banquet.”
Shen Jianchen slammed his fist onto the desk. “One more lie and I’ll have you dragged outside for fifty strokes of the heavy bamboo!”
Shaking from head to toe, Xilan begged for mercy. Shen Jianchen demanded the truth, and Matron Zou urged her to confess since they could hide it no longer. Bursting into tears, Xilan poured out every detail of what happened.
Enraged, Shen Jianchen overturned the desk. “I’ll butcher Liang Shicheng with my own hands!”
Having never seen him erupt like that, both women trembled in terror.
Shen Jianchen reined in his fury and spoke in an icy voice. “From this day on, the two of you will look after Princess Consort Yi. Out of regard for how she cared for me while I was sick, I will continue to support her and summon doctors to treat her. Just don’t expect me ever to lay eyes on her again.”
After they withdrew, Shen Jianchen stepped outside to walk off his foul mood. In Lin’an he used to go to the Divine Willow; here there was no willow, only biting late-autumn wind and dead leaves scattering across the yard. Oddly, the chill helped clear his head.
Before, his thoughts had been tangled with how to care for Mengrong, settle into marriage, and grow old with her. Now only one goal remained: saving Qianli Xun. That clarity brought a strange relief.
Every now and then passing guards bowed and greeted him as Prince Yi. To transmigrate into an imperial prince only to be helpless before a treacherous eunuch was pathetic, and the root of the problem sat on the dragon throne. He resolved to see His Majesty the next morning and request an order for the Court of Judicial Review to free Youqi Jushi.
With his mind made up, he stayed out crunching over fallen leaves instead of turning in. Ahead came the voices of Bai Feng and Kuqiao.
“I grew up in Bianliang and know every corner,” Bai Feng said. “Tell me wherever you want to go.”
“With Miss still in trouble, how could I be in the mood to stroll around?”
“Don’t worry. With His Highness and Young Master Chen here, Youqi Jushi will be fine. Once we get her out, promise me one thing?”
“What thing?”
“Come see my old neighborhood. I want the neighbors and everyone I grew up with to see that Bai Feng is about to take a wife.”
Kuqiao flushed shyly and said she wouldn’t go.
“You look adorable when you’re shy. Let me steal a kiss?”
“How can you see me blushing in pitch dark?”
“I can feel it. I’m kissing you now—don’t push me away.”
Silence fell over their spot. Picturing them holding each other in the dark, Shen Jianchen felt a pang of envy. Servants though they were, Bai Feng and Kuqiao got to be with the one they loved, while he had no such luck.
Not wanting to intrude on the pair, Shen Jianchen turned down another path—and caught sight of Chen Haoyu walking toward him in the moonlight.

#TITLE: Busy Rescue in Bianliang
Settled into a guest room at Prince Yi’s Manor, Chen Haoyu had wanted to call on those influential officials that very night, though late-night visits were impossible. Tossing and turning in bed, he got dressed and wandered the manor grounds to pass the hours.
In the dark, another figure strolled along the garden path. Drawing closer, Haoyu recognized Prince Yi and hurried forward to greet him.
“Are the quarters to your liking, Young Master Chen?” Shen Jianchen asked politely.
“The manor lacks nothing, and the room is comfortable. Out of deference to Your Highness, the servants look after me well. It feels no different from home.”
“Good. Tomorrow I will seek an audience with His Majesty about Youqi Jushi and get her out no matter what.”
“Thank you, Your Highness.” Still uneasy, Haoyu added, “Tomorrow I will also visit the high court officials Wenyuan and my father-in-law recommended. With luck, we may free my wife without troubling Your Highness.”
Sensing his guard, Shen Jianchen said, “We will work from both ends. Whichever path works, getting her out is all that matters.”
“Your Highness speaks the truth.”
Brought together by one woman and having spoken of tomorrow’s errands, the two men ran out of words. Haoyu bowed, excused himself to sleep, and disappeared into the night.
Still wide awake, Shen Jianchen kept walking until he found himself outside Mengrong’s courtyard. Stopping for a moment, memories of their time together surfaced. With a bitter pang, he wondered if keeping another woman in his heart had pushed a once innocent, gentle girl into cruelty. Refusing to shoulder every shred of blame, he let go. She had disappointed him too far; he would make no more allowances and try no longer to mend what lay broken.
He would stake everything on saving Qianli Xun, even if she chose to walk straight back to Chen Haoyu the moment she walked free.
At daybreak, Haoyu ate a quick breakfast, packed gifts into a carriage, and set out with Qinggua to visit every name and address on his list one by one.
Shen Jianchen kept busy too, telling Bai Feng to stay behind and bring Bianliang’s top doctors to examine Princess Consort Yi—an errand Bai Feng gladly used as an excuse to take Kuqiao out shopping.
Riding alone to Xihua Gate, Shen Jianchen dismounted and showed his Prince Yi waist token to the guards. After inspecting it and returning it, one guard had another take the prince’s horse and bowed. “His Majesty should be in Zichen Hall right now, Your Highness. To seek an audience, you will need the approval of the Lord of Chu.”
“Lord of Chu?” Shen Jianchen knew little of court titles, having transmigrated in haste and left for Lin’an soon after.
“Grand Preceptor Tong,” the guard clarified.
“Tong Guan?”
“The very man.”
“Since when do I need his consent to see His Majesty?”
“You may not know, Your Highness. Since Imperial Attendant Liang went to Lin’an, His Majesty ordered the Lord of Chu to attend him personally. His Majesty trusts him implicitly and confides in him on everything.”
Helpless that an emperor’s son needed a corrupt favorite’s permission to see his own father, Shen Jianchen told the guard to report that his thirteenth son, Prince Yi, sought an audience.
“No need to stand on ceremony with us, Your Highness, and we have no standing to face the Lord of Chu anyway. Please step inside and wait in the side hall. I will go to the Office of Imperial Attendants and have a junior eunuch pass word up.”
Shown into an unnamed side hall built for high-ranking visitors—furnished comfortably with tea, fruit, pastries, and palace maids—Shen Jianchen waited in silence.
About an hour later, a junior attendant entered and bowed. “I went on your behalf, Your Highness. After consulting His Majesty, the Lord of Chu bade me relay this: if you come regarding the female poet, there is no need for an audience. First, His Majesty is weighed down with state affairs; second, Imperial Attendant Liang has not yet returned to Bianliang, and since this case concerns him, it must wait for his return. Furthermore, the Lord of Chu said His Majesty reminds you that now you are back from Lin’an, you should stay quietly inside your manor, stop roaming about, and stay out of other people’s affairs.”
Dismissed in a few curt sentences, Shen Jianchen left the palace seething yet powerless. Unwilling to go straight home, he led his horse aimlessly through the streets until the sound of a storyteller outside a teahouse caught his ear—reciting one of Qianli Xun’s huaben tales. Even here in Bianliang, her stories had spread.
Unable to see her, he sat down at the teahouse, ordered a pot of tea, and forced himself to listen. Yet thoughts of her locked inside a filthy prison, tormented by jailers, soon broke his patience. Leaving his cup unfinished, he tossed down silver, mounted up, and asked directions to the Court of Judicial Review.
Informed of Prince Yi’s arrival, Chief Minister Chen came out with his staff to welcome him inside. Shen Jianchen dispensed with pleasantries before even sitting down. “I came to ask about the southern poetess Youqi Jushi, brought here from Lin’an for your court’s review. Where does the case stand?”
“Thank you for your concern, Your Highness,” Chief Minister Chen answered. “She arrived two days ago and is held in the prison. His Majesty ordered us to hold the trial until Imperial Attendant Liang returns.”
“When will he be back?”
“If I am not mistaken, he is already on the road to Bianliang.”
Counting the days, Shen Jianchen judged it would take at least seven or eight more days. “I have a favor to ask, Minister Chen. May I visit her in prison?”
Chief Minister Chen smiled. “You are Prince Yi. With your waist token, the gates of every prison in Bianliang open to you.”
Shen Jianchen felt foolish for begging a minister when his own rank held that authority. Still, he had learned what he needed: nothing could move until Liang Shicheng arrived. Thanking the minister, he rode back to his manor.
That evening, Haoyu trudged back exhausted and defeated. Finding Shen Jianchen just lifting his chopsticks for supper, he dropped straight to his knees. “Your Highness, please, you must save my wife! You are my only hope left!”
His appetite gone, Shen Jianchen set his chopsticks down. “I told you my stance last night. I won’t repeat myself. Qinggua, help Young Master Chen up and have him dine with me.”
Too frantic after a wasted day to remember Prince Yi’s own devotion to her, Haoyu let Qinggua pull him into the opposite chair.
“Shut out at every door?” Shen Jianchen asked.
Haoyu sighed. “I visited every single name on the list. Some refused to see me outright; others gave gentle excuses that nobody can challenge Imperial Attendant Liang, only His Majesty. You are the only path left. Did you see His Majesty today?”
“He refused me. Told me to wait until Liang Shicheng returns to Bianliang.”
“When will that be?”
“Another seven or eight days.”
“So long! My wife will be stuck in that filthy, stinking cell for another week or more. How will she bear it?”
“Tomorrow I will take you inside to see her.”
Hope broke across Haoyu’s face. “I can really see her?”
“I’m still a prince. Getting into Bianliang’s prisons is well within my power.”
Relief awakened Haoyu’s hunger; he picked up his chopsticks and began eating. Watching his rival tuck into dinner, Shen Jianchen reflected on how strange it felt to share an ordinary meal with the man who held what he wanted most. He didn’t hate Haoyu—only envied him, wondering silently if Heaven would ever grant him a second chance, as the nun under the Divine Willow had hinted with her words about waiting for the clouds to part and reveal the moon. Listening to Haoyu talk of “my wife,” he thought the nun had only meant to comfort him, and let out a low sigh.
Haoyu looked up in alarm. “Why sigh, Your Highness? Is her case too hard to fix?”
“Don’t jump to conclusions. It’s tricky, not hopeless.”
Relieved, Haoyu ate faster, eager for tomorrow’s visit.
After supper, Shen Jianchen sparred with Bai Feng until tired, then let Bai Feng help him undress. Noticing the prince hadn’t asked once about his wife, Bai Feng tested the waters. “Your Highness, I summoned two physicians to examine the Princess Consort today.”
“What did they say?” Shen Jianchen asked evenly.
“Both said they could not cure her. They claim they are among the finest street physicians in Bianliang, so if they fail, only the palace’s imperial physicians might stand a chance. If even they cannot cure her, she may stay like this for life.”
“Go to the Imperial Medical Bureau tomorrow and summon a skilled imperial physician.”
“The bureau is inside the palace. Will the guards let an attendant in?”
“You aren’t asking to see His Majesty. Show my waist token and they will oblige.”
“Right! I have a token I’ve never used. Tomorrow it earns its keep.”
Once Shen Jianchen lay down, Bai Feng ventured one more question. “Why didn’t you go visit the Princess Consort after returning today?”
Shen Jianchen rolled over, facing the wall. “Stop meddling in what isn’t your concern.”
Taking the hint, Bai Feng blew out the lamp, stepped out, and pulled the door shut.
Bianliang’s prison stood in the loneliest corner of the east quarter, bleak enough from outside to warn of the squalor within. Following Prince Yi out of the carriage, Haoyu asked Qinggua to fetch two bundles of food and supplies.
At the gate, Shen Jianchen flashed his waist token. “I heard the poetess from Lin’an is held here. Lead the way.”
The jailer’s manner flipped at once to deference, ordering the gate guards to watch the entrance while he escorted the prince inside. When guards moved to block Haoyu and Qinggua, Shen Jianchen turned back. “They came with me. Are they barred too?”
The jailer beamed. “Not at all, though rules allow only one extra guest for Your Highness.”
Haoyu slung both bundles onto his own shoulders, leaving Qinggua to wait outside as the heavy iron gates slammed behind them.
A foul stench hung over the corridor lined with women’s cells, where disheveled prisoners in rags stared out. Haoyu dreaded seeing his wife like them. Deeper inside, the jailer unlocked a cell, and Shen Jianchen pushed the door open.
Inside sat only a wooden bed topped with a straw mat and a low table. Qianli Xun sat on the bed’s edge, pale, iron chains shackling her wrists and ankles. At the sight of Shen Jianchen, delight lit her face, and she rose. “Why are you here?”
Then she spotted Haoyu behind him with two bundles on his back, and tears welled in her eyes.
“My wife, look how you’ve suffered!”
Her own eyes grew moist. “Why did you come all this way too?”

Chen Haoyu brushed everything else aside and swept his wife into an embrace, tears spilling from his eyes. Qianli Xun hugged him back naturally, the iron chains clinking between them.
“I never thought I’d see you here,” she said. “Is everyone at home well?”
Standing beside them, Shen Jianchen felt a dull ache in his chest. Having no right to part them, he turned his back so he wouldn’t have to look, though every word reached his ears clearly.
“Everyone is fine, just sick with worry over you.”
“It’s my fault for stirring up trouble and costing the family their peace.”
“Nobody blames you—not me, not anyone at home. Quite the opposite. Because of this, everyone respects you even more as a worthy wife. Before I left for Bianliang, they told me over and over to bring you back safe.”
Qianli Xun caught the deeper meaning behind his words. By standing up to Liang Shicheng and taking his false charge, she had proved her innocence to the Chen household beyond doubt. Feeling the bundles against his shoulders, she asked, “Why carry packs in here? You can’t use anything inside a prison.”
Haoyu let go, slipped the bundles off, and laid them on the straw mat. “Just food and daily things. Out of respect for Prince Yi, the jailer let them stay.”
He reached to hug her again, only for Qianli Xun to step back, chains dragging, and drop into a formal bow before Shen Jianchen. “Your Highness has spent great care and effort on this humble subject’s behalf. I have no way to repay you save this bow of gratitude.”
Shen Jianchen turned around. “Why speak of thanks between us? Seeing you with my own eyes sets my mind at ease. I cannot get you out just yet—the Court of Judicial Review won’t open your case until Liang Shicheng returns to Bianliang. Once he arrives, I will do everything in my power to free you. Bear with it a few days more. I will see that the jailers look after you.”
Every word carried personal devotion, souring Haoyu’s mood. Needing the prince’s help yet unable to swallow his pride completely, he cut in. “If Your Highness wants the jailers to care for my wife, start by taking off her wrist and ankle chains so she can breathe easier.”
Without hesitation, Shen Jianchen called out for the guard. When the jailer stepped up, the prince ordered him to unlock Youqi Jushi’s shackles.
“She is a capital prisoner, Your Highness,” the jailer hedged. “If something happens, this lowly servant cannot bear the blame.”
“If anything happens, I will bear it. Unlock them.”
Powerless to refuse a prince, the jailer took out his keys, freed her wrists and ankles, and stood up. “Your time is almost up, Your Highness. Say what you must quickly.”
Hurried by the guard, both men fell strangely tongue-tied. Qianli Xun rolled her shoulders and flexed her ankles. “Much better! Today I finally see the perks of having a prince for a friend.”
Haoyu pulled her back into his arms. “You fool. If you had fled to the countryside with me earlier, none of this would have happened.”
She patted his back gently. “Hiding would only have delayed my arrest and dragged the Chen family down too. Isn’t this better? As long as everyone at home is safe and the family business untouched, a little hardship means nothing. I brought this on myself, and I’ll answer for it.”
“Wait—do you know why Liang Shicheng demanded to see you?” Shen Jianchen asked.
To Haoyu, the answer seemed obvious—Liang’s lechery. Hearing an unspoken layer in the prince’s question, he let go of his wife. “Liang Shicheng is a shameless lecher after her looks. What other reason could there be?”
Instead of answering Haoyu, Qianli Xun looked at Shen Jianchen. “You already found out?”
He nodded. “Only after they sent you off to Bianliang.”
“How is Mengrong?”
“She did wrong and reaped the bitter fruit. She has lost her mind.”
“Lost her mind? How did that happen?”
With Haoyu standing right there, Shen Jianchen kept his answer guarded. “As I said, she acted wrongly, and the consequences broke her mind.”
Taking the hint, Qianli Xun asked no more.
Watching the two of them trade coded words he couldn’t follow left Haoyu frantic. Before he could press for answers, the jailer called time. Shen Jianchen rested one long look on Qianli Xun, full of pain and reluctance, then walked out in silence.
Haoyu hugged her a third time. “What other reason? Tell me.”
“What other reason could there be?” she replied, patting his back. “His Highness already left. Hurry out so the jailer can lock up.”
Haoyu pulled away at last, promising that he and Prince Yi would free her, and stepped out as the cell door clanged shut.
Outside the prison, Haoyu chased after Shen Jianchen. “What was that other reason you and my wife spoke of?”
“What reason? You’re baffling me.”
Haoyu followed him right into the carriage and pressed harder. “Don’t play dumb with me, Your Highness. I’m no fool. You asked straight out whether she knew why Liang Shicheng summoned her. Tell me what else happened!”
Shen Jianchen refused to speak, both to guard the last shred of Mengrong’s dignity and to keep that secret thread shared solely with Qianli Xun intact. “Even if there were one, I wouldn’t tell you. Stop asking.”
Dependent on the prince to save his wife, Haoyu swallowed his frustration, though that private exchange inside the cell left a bitter taste in his mouth.
Back at Prince Yi’s Manor, Bai Feng was just seeing an imperial physician out the gate. The doctor bowed respectfully to the prince, who walked straight past without asking a word about his wife—sending a chill down Haoyu’s spine.
Once the doctor left, Bai Feng hurried after Shen Jianchen to report. “The palace physicians truly know their craft, Your Highness! He named the exact cause and symptoms of the Princess Consort’s illness down to the word. Sadly, he can’t cure her either. He said this sickness tends to run in bloodlines—someone in her family likely passed it down to her, and any child she bore might inherit it and break down under outside strain too.”

Too spent to rise, Shen Jianchen watched through blurring eyes as Lu Yuanfeng lifted Qianli Xun into a waiting carriage, shutting his eyes silently as steel closed in, hoping only that once he died, she might keep him in her heart.
Hours earlier, after weeping himself dry over Mengrong and Xilan, Bai Feng had gathered every scrap of strength to carry both women off the freezing stones onto the bed inside the abandoned home—collapsing beside them dizzy from blood loss.
As the western sun faded, his eyes fell on a dead Jin officer slumped against the wall. Crawling over on a prayer, he searched the corpse’s robe and pulled out two medicine bottles—swallowing a pill from one, pouring powder blind across the stinging cuts on his back.
Within half an hour, the pain dulled and his head cleared. Lighting the oil lamp with his fire-striker, he knelt beside the bed, promising Princess Consort Yi and Xilan to wait while he found their prince, and limped down the darkening alleys toward the sounds of battle—reaching the rear street just as raised blades hovered above Shen Jianchen’s chest.
Forgetting his own wounds, Bai Feng leaped square into the ring with *Thunderbolt Chain Kicks*, knocking the nearest killers flying, and threw himself flat over Shen Jianchen. “Your Highness! I’m here to save you!”

When Shen Jianchen said one piece of unfinished business kept him here, Cai Jing caught his drift at once and asked quietly what held Prince Yi behind.
