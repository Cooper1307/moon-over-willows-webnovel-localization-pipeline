#CH 311
#TITLE: The Heir Is Decided
Shock and disbelief froze every face in the hall.
Chen Haodong made one last desperate grab. “Your Highness must be mistaken—how could I ever do such a thing?!”
“Scarface already confessed everything, so drop the act.” Shen Jianchen held the silver note up before the room. “Fresh out of jail, Scarface collected a hundred and fifty taels from Chen Haodong. Admitting he covered it up for silver, he spilled every detail of the night of the third day of the third month last year. Clerk Han, read out the full record.”
“Yes, Your Highness.” Han Gu addressed the hall. “By Scarface’s confession, he fed Chen Haodong regular reports on Chen Haoyu’s doings for silver. On the third day of the third month last year, once Chen Haoyu left the Chens’ country residence, Scarface ran straight to Chen Haodong’s private tryst house nearby—the place he used to meet brothel courtesans. Hearing about the girl, Chen Haodong cooked up a foul plot: first he went back to town and had Butler Wang pull every servant away on the pretext of cleaning city properties, while Scarface sent the rest of his crew off and drugged the caretaker’s wine. After dark, Chen Haodong slipped in shrouded head to face in a black cloak, violated Chun Jinlan, and ordered Scarface to sell her to a Yangzhou brothel—then spread rumors that Chen Haoyu made a habit of selling abducted girls to brothels to smear his cousin’s name.”
Every last defense stripped away, Chen Haodong felt his legs go boneless beneath him.
Before he could sink, Chen Jiangshu lashed across his son’s face with a resounding crack. “Wretched beast! How dare you pull something this vile after all the years your uncle and I poured into raising you?!”
Sent sprawling across the flagstones, Chen Haodong watched what should have been his crowning day crash into the darkest hour of his life.
Chen Jiangshu dropped into a crouch clutching his head and sobbed aloud. “It’s all my fault! All my fault!”
Madam Bao threw herself to her knees before Prince Yi weeping. “Spare Haodong, Your Highness! I beg you!”
“This hundred and fifty taels will be donated to the poor in the Judicial Office’s name.” Tucking the bank note away, Shen Jianchen told Madam Bao, “The realm has laws, and every clan has rules. Chen Haodong broke Song law and must pay—only then can Chun Jinlan’s spirit rest. Chief Constable Wen, arrest him and bring him back to the Judicial Office.”
Wen Xin signaled constables to bind Chen Haodong in ropes and haul him upright.
Shen Jianchen cupped his hands to the hall. “Farewell, everyone!”
Even after Prince Yi marched out, Chen Haoyu stared stunned. “How could it be Brother Dong?”
“Never expected him either,” Qianli Xun sighed. “Took Prince Yi to turn the whole board upside down.”
“What do you mean?” Chen Haoyu blinked.
Too tired to explain, Qianli Xun watched Madam Bao crawl on her knees toward Old Madam Xia sobbing uncontrollably. “Mother! Save Haodong—please!”
Everything stood crystal clear to Old Madam Xia now. Narrowly dodging handing the clan empire to her eldest grandson was a blessing, yet seeing any Chen blood hauled off in ropes tore at her heart.
Eyes brimming, she announced, “What’s done can’t be undone. Once the Judicial Office sentences him, I’ll have Jianglin appeal to Prince Yi—no matter how much silver it takes, we’ll buy his life.”
Hearing that, Madam Bao fainted dead away. Qianli Xun darted forward and pinched the philtrum under her nose until she gasped awake shrieking “Haodong, my son!” Yao Qingyan signaled two maids to help her back to her quarters.
Bracing herself upright, Old Madam Xia addressed the room. “Unforeseen as today’s shock is, we started this selection and can’t leave it hanging. Settle the heir first, deal with the rest after. Two grandsons remain—who do we choose?”
Rising from the floor where he had wept, Chen Jiangshu spoke first. “Mother, pick Haoyu. Wild as he was, his heart has changed. Business skills can be taught—between Eldest Brother, Third Brother, and me, he’ll master it. And his wife Shuzhen has proven herself right before our eyes—nothing like those street rumors.”
Utter disillusionment drove Chen Jiangshu’s turnaround. He had backed petty maneuvering, never a capital crime pinned onto a cousin’s neck; remorse burned him for feeding his son’s ruthless greed since childhood.
Chen Jiangmu stood next. “Haofei is bent on court office and heads to Bianliang next spring for the palace exam—no time or head for trade, plus he’s still an unmarried lad. Pick Haoyu! Steward Qiu says he already outperforms veteran clerks and picks up bookkeeping fast—born for business.”
Old Madam Xia nodded and turned to the elders. “Uncle Lu, what say the clan?”
“Give us a minute to confer.” Chen Lu huddled with the other elders.
Qianli Xun nudged Chen Haoyu with a smirk. “No escaping your fate now.”
Still heavy-hearted over Brother Dong—still blaming himself for abducting Chun Jinlan in the first place—Chen Haoyu muttered, “The family heir falling onto my lap? Still can’t believe it.”
“Like they say: life loves plot twists.”
“True,” he sighed, then added, “Back then, if Brother Dong wanted a girl, one word to me and I’d have helped him—why sneak around and sell her off?”
Catching his wife’s razor glare, he backpedaled fast. “I meant *back then*, not now! With you, I’d never touch dirty business!”
“Think everyone sins out in the open like you? Plenty of folks wear saint masks by day and crawl through gutters by night.”
Finishing their huddle, Chen Lu spoke for the clan. “We’re unanimous: on paper Haofei fits best, yet his heart is set on civil service. Someone has to step into Jianglin’s shoes, so Haoyu it is. Let both brothers put the Chen clan first, whether in office or in trade.”
“Then Jianglin, draft a fresh appointment scroll,” Old Madam Xia decreed.
Never dreaming the mantle would land on his own son—and too weighed down by Haodong’s fall to celebrate—Chen Jianglin unrolled a blank scroll and brushed out the decree anew.
One by one, everyone pressed red thumbprints, silently praying no fresh thunderbolt would strike. When the final print dried without incident, a collective breath escaped the hall.
Scanning the document and rolling it tight, Chen Jianglin summoned his son. “Step forward, Haoyu.”
With no way out, Chen Haoyu stepped up ready to shoulder the weight.
“Take this,” Chen Jianglin said, holding out the scroll. “Once I pass on the Chen trade secrets, the entire house rests in your hands.”
Feather-light scroll, mountain-heavy burden. Chen Haoyu bowed low. “For our clan’s future, I accept this duty. I’ll hold myself to every rule of an heir, better myself every day, and give my all to the Chen name.”
Since two of those six rules spoke directly to the heir’s wife, Qianli Xun rose and curtsied to the elders. “As Chen Haoyu’s wife, I owe my pledge too. My natal family’s standing is clear; never mind who I was before, from this day on I’ll guard my words and deeds and run the inner household well so my husband can devote himself to the business without worry.”
Warm nods of approval swept the elders.
“Settled and sealed,” Old Madam Xia concluded. “Jiangmu, report the outcome to Prefect Zhu and the Lin’an Chamber of Commerce. Jianglin, handle Haodong—spend whatever silver it takes to spare his neck.”
“Yes, Mother!” both answered in unison.
Under Song law, Chen Haodong faced hanging, yet Chen wealth—working within legal redemption rules—bought his life at the price of permanent imprisonment. Devastated, Chen Jiangshu and Madam Bao both fell ill and wept anew every time they visited their broken son in jail.
Sunk to rock bottom, refusing any self-reflection, Chen Haodong blamed every ounce of his ruin on Prince Yi—and determined to drag the prince down into misery with him, sent word through a jailer demanding to see him.
Hearing the jailer’s message at the Judicial Office, Shen Jianchen figured it couldn’t hurt to hear him out. Just back from Zhuli Village after finishing the bamboo cottage, Bai Feng followed close behind as personal guard.
Pinching his nose against the prison stench, Bai Feng griped, “Your Highness shouldn’t set foot in a dump like this.” Rounding a corner into a noticeably cleaner block, he blinked. “How come these cells look so much nicer?”
“Got enough silver, you can live like this too,” Shen Jianchen joked.
“Hey—sounds like you’re wishing me behind bars!”
A jailer unlocked a cell revealing a disheveled figure sitting motionless on the bunk. Stepping in, Shen Jianchen asked, “What did you want to tell me, Chen Haodong?”
Remaining seated without a shred of deference—what was left to lose?—Chen Haodong sneered. “You wrecked my life, Your Highness. Can’t sleep easy in here unless I share a few little secrets about Princess Consort Yi.”
Shen Jianchen tensed. “What secrets?”
“Princess Consort Yi wants Haoyu’s wife dead...”
“Lies!” Bai Feng barked. “How dare you slander Her Highness?!”
Cold dread tightening his chest, Shen Jianchen raised a hand. “Let him talk.”
“I was Her Highness’s errand boy,” Chen Haodong smirked. “Hired Master Jiang for her—his thugs blew it. Approached Shao Escort Agency next—they turned me down. Then somehow she bought off Black-Fiend Tiger and had me hand over Chen Manor’s floor plan for that raid. You know the rest.” Watching Prince Yi’s face turn stony dark, he chuckled nasty. “Quick to lock me up, Your Highness—why not throw your own Princess Consort in a cell too? She’s got plenty of dirt on her hands!”
“Say another word and I’ll bust your jaw!” Bai Feng snapped.
Ignoring him, Chen Haodong pressed the knife in. “Failed three times, yet her malice runs deep—she won’t quit. The day she finally succeeds, will Your Highness sentence *her* to autumn beheading too?”
Unable to stomach another syllable, Shen Jianchen wheeled out of the cell as Chen Haodong’s raw laughter echoed behind him. Skipping the Judicial Office, he rode straight for Youlin Retreat.
Meanwhile, after months of sharing a bed with no sign of pregnancy, Qiao Mengrong had Matron Zou summon a skilled physician, who diagnosed a chill womb and wrote a three-month warming tonic guaranteed to bring a little prince.
Just as the physician departed, her brother strode in—thrilling her until she saw the thundercloud across his brow. “Home so early, Brother! Just saw a physician who says three months of tonic will give us a baby—wait, what’s wrong?”
Studying her sweet, gentle face, Shen Jianchen struggled to picture her plotting murder. “Xilan, wait outside. I need a private word with Her Highness.”
Sensing danger as Xilan shut the door, Qiao Mengrong asked timidly, “What is it, Brother?”
Locking eyes with her to read every flicker, he began, “Chen Haodong told me a few things about you in prison today...”
Knowing the game was up, Qiao Mengrong dropped straight to her knees in a flood of tears. “I know what you’re going to say, Brother, and knew you’d find out sooner or later! Hatred for Zhu Shuzhen blinded me into doing awful things—I regret it to death and swear I’ll never do it again! Thank goodness she’s safe; if anything had happened to her, I’d never have known peace!”
Watching her weep so piteously melted every harsh rebuke on Shen Jianchen’s tongue—turning the blame right back on himself for driving her to desperation. Stepping forward, he lifted her up. “Knowing you were wrong is enough. Never harbor thoughts like that again.”
“I’ll listen to you, Brother,” she sobbed, dabbing her cheeks.
Still, he laid down one steel warning. “If not for Youqi Jushi’s martial arts, she’d have died on Mount Tianmu. Had she died, as Judicial Investigator I would have dug straight to you—and enforced Song law without sparing kin.”

#TITLE: Time Flies Like a White Colt

Master Jiang’s wife—that ex-courtesan Chun Jinmo—hated Zhu Shuzhen for stealing Chen Haoyu and climbed Mount Tianmu herself to talk Black-Fiend Tiger into it! The instant he heard Master Jiang’s wife was involved, Shen Jianchen dropped his lecture and strode straight for the door.
“Where are you going, Brother?!” Qiao Mengrong cried after him—receiving no answer except his shout summoning Bai Feng to ride to Master Jiang’s house.
Sinking onto the couch, Qiao Mengrong clenched her fists: smooth promises meant nothing when Zhu Shuzhen’s name came up!
Stepping back inside, Xilan urged softly, “Heard everything outside, Your Highness—please quit trying to kill the poetess.”
Wiping her dry-again cheeks—she had rehearsed that tearful confession days ago—Qiao Mengrong narrowed her eyes. “Quit?! Saw him bolt straight out the door for her again! When has he ever done that for me?! No more clumsy amateurs—next time I pick the right blade, stay spotless before Brother, and finish Zhu Shuzhen in one blow.”
“Picked someone already, Your Highness?”
“Picked him—just waiting for the opening. I’m writing Imperial Attendant Liang right now; send a courier straight to Bianliang.”
Over at the riverfront manor, a month locked indoors had finally broken Chun Jinmo’s illusions. Sold into a brothel by kin, robbed of Young Master Chen, stuck with a homely husband, sacrificing her body to Black-Fiend Tiger for nothing—where else could she go if Master Jiang tossed her out? Better make peace with reality and hold onto the one man who prized her.
Summoning Master Jiang, she dropped to her knees in tears, confessed everything, and swore to be his faithful wife from then on. Too in love to stay hard, Master Jiang forgave her—and had barely lifted her to her feet when a fist slammed into his back and floored him!
Scrambling up to face a furious Prince Yi, Master Jiang hit his knees with Chun Jinmo shaking beside him. “Calm your wrath, Your Highness—how did I offend you?!”
“Sending thugs to ambush Youqi Jushi, then colluding with Black-Fiend Tiger and wrecking Chen Manor—and you ask how you offended me?!”
Seeing Prince Yi knew the whole mess, Master Jiang hurried to explain how he’d sent bottom-tier scrubs on purpose so she’d walk away unscathed—and Chun Jinmo cut in owning up to Mount Tianmu entirely on her own, while Master Jiang explained he’d beaten her and locked her up a full month the day he found out.
Seeing their genuine remorse—and grateful Qianli Xun was unharmed—Shen Jianchen reined his anger in with one parting warning: “Outside real bouts, my fists don’t hit women. Catch your wife scheming once more, though, and I won’t guarantee she dodges them!”
Crumpled flat on the floor, Chun Jinmo trembled speechless while Master Jiang swore it would never happen again.
Once Prince Yi left, Master Jiang hauled his weeping wife up. “Told you! He’d kill for Youqi Jushi! Settle down with me and forget Young Master Chen—none of them treat you half as good as me.”
Throwing her arms around him sobbing, Chun Jinmo cried, “I was blind, Husband—nobody in this world loves me like you! I’ll never call you ugly again!”
Pulling her close, he chuckled warm. “Real men aren’t judged by pretty faces—you stay pretty, I’ll earn the silver, and in a year we’ll have a fat baby boy with your looks!” Smiling through her tears, Chun Jinmo finally let go of the past.
Riding home in his carriage, Shen Jianchen blamed himself again—since Mengrong repented, turning cold would only push her darker. Better wipe the slate clean and cherish her properly.
Sensing his master’s heavy mood, Bai Feng tried steering him to brighter news. “Our bamboo cottage is the prettiest in Zhuli Village—when’s Your Highness coming to see it? Clerk Han said you planned to invite Young Master Chen and Youqi Jushi over...”
“Thought about it—forget it now,” Shen Jianchen murmured.
“When are you moving in, then?”
“Leave it sitting for now.”
Baffled why his master dropped heaps of silver on an empty cottage, Bai Feng said he’d have Kujue keep watch over it.
Peace settled over Lin’an for the months that followed. As the heir’s wife, Qianli Xun learned household management beside Yao Qingyan each day and wrote huaben tales in her spare hours—stories now printed in bookshops and sung across every street under the name Scholar Qianli Xun, including a tale inspired by Ye Anya, who had eloped away from Lin’an with Cao Ban to start fresh. Chen Haoyu trained hard under his father and uncles, making rapid strides while never missing a single day whisking his wife’s morning and evening tea.
Meanwhile, Qiao Mengrong drank her warming tonic daily, basking in her brother’s attentive visits and West Lake outings—yet still feeling his heart remained out of reach, with zero reply yet from Imperial Attendant Liang in Bianliang.
Autumn chill arrived right as both Qiao Wenxian’s wife and concubine turned up pregnant. Following Black-Fiend Tiger’s autumn execution, Lazybones Village’s housing project wrapped up. Shen Jianchen relocated every vagrant in Lin’an there: wild greens sold fresh in warm months and dried for winter made the able-bodied self-sufficient, while elderly and infirm newcomers received monthly stipends funded by the yamen, wealthy donors, and confiscated illicit silver like Scarface’s hundred fifty taels. Chen Manor dismantled its congee shed, beggars vanished from Lin’an’s streets, and citizens sang Prince Yi’s praises.
Then an imperial edict dropped like a bolt out of a clear sky: Zhu Xiyan was stripped of Prefect of Lin’an and forced into retirement, replaced by a newcomer named Li Ke—known crony of Imperial Attendant Liang.
Too young to retire, Zhu Xiyan knew the score immediately: backing Prince Yi and quitting bribes had painted a bull’s-eye on him—killing the chicken to scare the monkey.
When Shen Jianchen hurried to Zhu Manor in guilt, Zhu Xiyan sighed calm. “In a rotten court, doing right is swimming upstream. I’m retiring clean ahead of you—feels lighter already! Watch your back, Your Highness: the new Prefect didn’t even pay respects to the Military Commissioner, clearly backed by Imperial Attendant Liang.”
“Let him ignore me—I’ll sit tight at the Judicial Office and see what Liang Shicheng pulls next.”
Once Prince Yi left, Zhu Xiyan urged his son Zhu Jingyuan to resign his yamen clerkship before Li Ke squeezed him out and run the family estates instead.
Arriving from Chen Manor to check on her father, Qianli Xun met her cheerful brother outside and walked in on Zhu Xiyan scolding tearful Madam Xiang—“Lost an office, not my head!”—ordering a full year’s severance paid to dismiss every singing and dancing girl in the house so he could write poetry in peace.
“Already famous, Father—saw two of your poems in a collection yesterday!” Qianli Xun laughed, cheering both parents at once—Madam Xiang thrilled both to see her daughter and to finally clear the back courtyard of entertainers.
Teasing him further, Qianli Xun let a modern slip fly: “Better out of office anyway—odds are the guy sitting on the throne ends up a prisoner in a few years!”
Zhu Xiyan and Zhu Jingyuan jumped in alarm—“Never say that outside or lose our heads! New Prefect Li Ke answers straight to Liang Shicheng and doesn’t even care about Prince Yi!” Chastened, Qianli Xun promised to bite her tongue.

#TITLE: An Oral Edict at the Judicial Office
Zhu Xiyan sighed. “Without my prefect seal, I can’t shield the Chens anymore. Liang Shicheng sent Li Ke to scrub Lin’an clean—everyone close to Prince Yi and me will feel heat, Chen Manor included. Tell your father-in-law to lie as low as possible.”

Dread gripped Qianli Xun for Shen Jianchen—doing real good in a doomed dynasty only invited blades. She yearned to visit and comfort him, yet thoughts of Mengrong and her own marriage held her feet back, leaving only silent prayers.
Over at the Judicial Office, Shen Jianchen drank alone in the courtyard, bracing for the axe—in a court ruled by an inept sovereign, an unfavored prince counted for less than a favorite eunuch’s lapdog.
Pouring him a fresh cup, Bai Feng scoffed, “An imperial attendant is just a palace servant—how dare he outrank a prince? Don’t sweat him!”
Snapping out of his slump—true, the crook only had a few years left in history anyway!—Shen Jianchen downed his cup crisp and told Bai Feng to put the wine away—right as Han Gu announced a richly dressed visitor straight from Bianliang.
Stepping in alone dressed like a wealthy squire, wearing polite smiles over venom, was Imperial Attendant Liang Shicheng himself.
Dismissing Bai Feng—who gladly dashed off to see Kuqiao—Liang Shicheng waited until they were alone before asking slyly, “Does Your Highness intend to stay seated for His Majesty’s oral edict?”
Jaw tight, forced to swallow modern pride and kneel before a seated eunuch, Shen Jianchen listened to the axe fall: “His Majesty named you Military Commissioner of Lin’an merely out of delight at your cuju skills—don’t let it go to your head. All lands belong to the throne, leaving no room for private agendas. Effective today, your Military Commissioner title is revoked; stay an idle prince.”
“Your son obeys the edict,” Shen Jianchen said flatly.
Helping him up with phony warmth, Liang Shicheng delivered the real sting over tea: “Bypassing His Majesty’s own Juyang Yuan, Anji Fang, and Louze Yuan to build your own earthen huts and give vagrants jobs? You practically slapped His Majesty’s face in public! Worse, stooping to a shabby Judicial Investigator’s post shames the imperial house. A replacement Judicial Investigator arrives tomorrow—pack out and go back to playing the quiet sickly prince. You can find me at Liu’an Villa north of town if you ever need me.”
Saying not one word back, Shen Jianchen sat still long after Liang Shicheng swaggered out—then walked straight out of the Judicial Office without even saying goodbye to Han Gu or Wen Xin.
Roaming the bustling streets alone only sharpened his hollow ache. Crossed over chasing Qianli Xun, lost her, tried leaving a mark for the common folk, and got shut down by one whim from the throne. Living idle beside a wife he didn’t love felt bleak as ash.
Then Qiao Wenxian’s voice hailed him from a passing carriage, pulling him into a nearby tavern over a window table and a pot of Capital Spring wine.
Hearing the news, Qiao Wenxian poured cup after cup. “Doing normal work in an abnormal age is impossible—yet you still have hope! You’ve got Princess Consort Yi, and soon a child.”
Seeing Wenxian beaming over his own two coming babies, Shen Jianchen envied him aloud. “How do you look so content wed to women you didn’t love?”
“Life’s never flawless,” Qiao Wenxian said softly. “Make peace with what Heaven puts in your lap instead of torturing yourself. And since you’re idle now, we’ll visit Youlin Retreat often—minus Cao Ban, of course. Brave guy, eloping with Ye Anya—though she paid a steep price, severing ties with the Ye clan so her sisters’ marriages tanked. Every choice carries a bill. Shuzhen chose the bigger picture, forcing me to let go—yet look at her doing well with Chen Haoyu, and me content here. Once you step onto a road, walk it forward; there’s no turning back.”
Three pots later, leaving a dead-drunk Wenxian to his servants, Shen Jianchen walked out of town straight to the Divine Willow.
Stripped of red ribbons now, shedding yellow leaves into the chill autumn breeze beneath a half-moon, the willow stood quiet as his heart. Standing beneath it only proved one thing: losing Qianli Xun hadn’t dimmed his love one flicker. Afraid of bringing that ache home to upset Mengrong, he leaned against the gnarled trunk to clear the wine—and drifted straight off to sleep.
#CH 314
#TITLE: Delivering Bank Notes to Liu’an Villa
A bite of autumn wind woke Shen Jianchen beneath a high moon. Time to head back before Mengrong panicked.
Back at Youlin Retreat, Qiao Mengrong had indeed spent the evening pacing on pins and needles—chewing Bai Feng out for sneaking off and losing track of his master until the contrite guard went hunting beneath the Divine Willow, came up empty, and camped on a stone block outside the front gate until Prince Yi emerged from the woods at last.
Indoors, Qiao Mengrong threw herself into his arms the instant he appeared. Hearing he’d been drinking with Wenxian and resting by the willow—not seeing Zhu Shuzhen—eased her panic, right before his casual announcement stunned her: Zhu Xiyan replaced by Li Ke, his own posts revoked, Liang Shicheng in town to stay a while.
A secret smile flashed across her face behind his back. Asking where Liang Shicheng stayed under the pretext of bribing him to restore her brother’s post, she got brushed off—and resolved to send Matron Zou hunting the address at dawn.
Next morning, while Shen Jianchen sparred with Bai Feng in the yard, Qiao Mengrong visited Matron Zou privately—hiding her murder plot even from the matron and spinning a dutiful story about bribing Liang Shicheng to win back Brother’s post as a surprise. Moved by her devotion, Matron Zou went into town and came back by noon with the address—picked up straight from Bai Meiruo, since turncoat Lin Jue was already sucking up to Liang Shicheng—and reported during an after-lunch garden stroll: Liu’an Villa, north side of town.
Lying to her brother that noble ladies invited her to a tea party and politely turning down his offer to escort her, Qiao Mengrong rode straight to Liu’an Villa with Xilan and twenty thousand taels in bank notes—nearly her entire savings.
Stung when Liang Shicheng didn’t even bother greeting an imperial daughter-in-law at the gate—and humiliated seeing his sprawling villa rival Prince Yi’s Bianliang palace—she swallowed her pride and walked into the hall, only to find Liang Shicheng lounging barefoot on a couch in loose house robes with disheveled hair while maids massaged his legs and fed him grapes!
“No ceremony needed,” he chuckled when Xilan announced her, dismissing the maids without rising. “I dress like this chatting with His Majesty himself—no need to be stiff.”
Biting down fury, Qiao Mengrong cut straight to business when he confirmed receiving her Bianliang letter and boasted nothing lay beyond his reach. Having Xilan present the brocade box stuffed with bank notes, she added her second request: restoring her brother’s Military Commissioner post.
Scanning her up and down with a gleam of naked greed, Liang Shicheng ordered Xilan outside. Once the maid retreated, he padding barefoot to the door, shut it tight, and slid the wooden bolt home—dimming the room and sending cold dread down Qiao Mengrong’s spine.
Picking up the brocade box, he pressed it straight back into her arms. “Keep your silver—silver’s the last thing I’m short on.”
“Won’t you help me?” she stammered.
“Oh, I’ll help—depends if Your Highness gives me what I really want.”
“What... what do you want?”
“I may not be a whole man, yet I adore women—most of all married women. Give me one afternoon’s pleasure, and I’ll do anything you ask.”

#TITLE: Mengrong’s Humiliation
The brocade box clattered to the floor as Qiao Mengrong stumbled back. “You loved silver back in Bianliang!”
Closing in with a hand reaching for her cheek, Liang Shicheng leered. “Back then you were unwed—zero appeal! Took your silver just to string you along. Now you’re married, lovelier than ever, and walked right into my lap!”
Slapping his hand away shaking, she gasped, “I’m Princess Consort Yi! How dare you! I’m leaving—forget everything!”
Darting for the door, she hit his chest like a wall. “Think I’m letting this prize slip away? And don’t threaten me with Prince Yi—you’d never tell him for fear he’d despise you!”
Fighting with all her might only got her wrist wrenched and an arm locked around her throat as his hands roamed her body. Tears spilled over as she begged for mercy—only to hear him gloat over how he’d plotted this for years, fast-tracked her wedding just to ripen her, and twisted Lin’an’s reports to get His Majesty to strip Prince Yi’s post so she’d come crawling straight to Liu’an Villa!
Realizing she’d wrecked her own brother’s post—trapped behind a bolted door with nowhere to run—all her terror twisted into venomous rage aimed at one target: Zhu Shuzhen.
“Swear you’ll kill her?” she choked out.
Kissing her wet cheek, he promised, “Pleasure me today and I’ll wipe her off the earth—not a word to Prince Yi, ever.”
Shutting her eyes as tears rolled down, Qiao Mengrong whispered, “Fine... just never breathe a word to Brother.”
Shrugging off her outer robe, Liang Shicheng poured himself wine and called four scantily clad women in, ordering them to strip her naked and make her dance while he circled leering and drinking. Once the pot ran dry, the four women pinned her limbs flat to the floor—and Liang Shicheng squatted beside her holding a gleaming sewing needle.
“Not a whole man, so I take my pleasure my own way: wherever I kiss, I drive a needle in. The harder you scream, the sweeter my bliss!”
Shrieking “Pervert!” only drew his bitter laugh—“Blame His Majesty for turning good men into freaks!”—before his lips pressed her shoulder and sharp steel jabbed straight in, tearing an agonized scream from her throat.
Outside, Xilan hammered the locked door screaming until a palace guard pressed a blade to her throat and forced her back into the yard, where she crouched clamping hands over her ears while her mistress’s cries echoed on and on.
When the door finally opened, Qiao Mengrong staggered out pale, disheveled, and hollow-eyed, muttering only “Take me home... take me home,” while one of the women handed Xilan back the untouched box of bank notes.
Crumpled in the carriage whispering “Don’t tell Brother,” Qiao Mengrong shook silently in Xilan’s arms all the way to Youlin Retreat, heading straight to her old side courtyard instead of her brother’s rooms. Stripping her to apply salve, Xilan gasped in horror at dozens of bloody needle pricks dotting her shoulders and back—swearing silence even to Matron Zou as Mengrong buried her face in her pillow weeping: “She *has* to die, or I threw myself away for nothing!”
