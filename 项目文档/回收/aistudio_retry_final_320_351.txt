
#CH 320
#TITLE: Resolved to Face It Head-On
After stepping down from the carriage with her son and grandson, Old Madam Xia knocked on the main gate of Liu’an Villa and told the servant who answered to report one name alone: “Liang Shihui.”
Inside a garden pavilion, Liang Shicheng sat engrossed in a musical performance, tapping the beat against his thigh and humming low along with the singer. The servant approached, wary of disturbing him aloud, leaned in, and murmured the name into his ear.
Liang Shicheng’s eyes snapped open. He waved the singers away and asked, “Are you certain you heard right?”
“Your humble servant heard every word clear as day. An elderly woman is at the gate, and that is the exact name she gave.”
“An elderly woman?” Moisture welled in Liang Shicheng’s eyes. He rose and hurried out to meet her at the gate.
Chen Haoyu and Chen Jianglin stared in astonishment as Liang Shicheng came out in person, his face brimming with emotion, as though greeting long-lost kin.
Before him stood a silver-haired old woman leaning on a walking cane. Liang Shicheng searched her features for some trace of a familiar face, yet found none. Even so, he called out in a tender voice, “Older Sister, is that you?”
That name worked like a charm, Old Madam Xia thought. She hastened to reply, “I hate to disappoint you, Imperial Attendant Liang. I am not your older sister, Liang Shihui. I am from the Chen family. However, Sister Shihui and I were close confidantes, and she left a few things with me to place into her younger brother’s own hands.”
Only then did father and son understand what trump card the old madam had been holding.
Disappointment washed over Liang Shicheng, and he knew at once they had come on account of the Chen family and the poetess. Still, since she held his sister’s belongings, she had to know what had become of her. He would have to invite them inside.
“I see. Please come inside so we may talk.”
Meanwhile, Qianli Xun had told Kuqiao to wait in their carriage while she stayed out of sight and peered toward the villa’s front gate. She spotted Chen Haoyu and her father-in-law standing there beside Grandmother. Moments later, a man came out, exchanged a few words with them, and invited all three inside. Qianli Xun guessed the man who had come to greet them must be Liang Shicheng.
“What are they doing here?” she wondered. “Does the Chen family’s business have ties to Liang Shicheng?”
Determined to slip inside and find out, she glanced up at the courtyard wall of Liu’an Villa. Not too high—an easy hop with her light-foot technique. Gathering her breath, she launched herself up and landed inside without a hitch.
A row of trees provided good cover, keeping her out of view. Peering through the gaps between the trunks, she had a clear sight of her family trailing Liang Shicheng deeper into the estate. Taking a roundabout route, she shadowed them on silent feet, her eyes scanning every corner in constant alert.
The group rounded a rockery of stacked boulders. She crept up to the stones, about to follow, when a squad of patrolling guards marched past. She tucked herself out of sight with care.
The last guard in the file caught a flicker of movement, halted, drew his sword, and edged toward the back of the rockery. Fearing discovery, Qianli Xun held her breath and pressed her body flat against the rock. The instant the sword-bearing hand came into view, she snatched his wrist, yanked him behind the boulders, and knocked him out cold before he could react. Then she sprang onto the roof with her light-foot technique.
From her perch above, she watched the three of them enter a building below. With guards patrolling the yard, tailing them on the ground was too risky, so she chose to eavesdrop from the rooftops. Skimming across the roof tiles as light as a dragonfly touching water, she glided from eave to eave.
Her path carried her right over the building where Shen Jianchen was held captive. Catching the faint sound, Bai Feng alerted him, “Your Highness, someone is on the roof.”
Shen Jianchen had heard it already. Eyeballing the sturdy iron cage enclosing him, he muttered, “Here’s hoping whoever it is will do me the favor of killing Liang Shicheng.”
On second thought, history recorded that Liang Shicheng met his end at the hands of Emperor Qinzong, so whoever had just crossed the roof could never be the one to kill him. Shen Jianchen consoled himself. “He has three years of life left. I have the patience to wait for that day.”
Reaching the roof above the room where the others sat, Qianli Xun eased onto her stomach, shifted a tile aside, and pressed her ear to the gap. She could make out most of what was being said below.
Once everyone had taken their seats inside, Liang Shicheng pressed without delay, “Elder Madam, do you know where my sister is?”
Old Madam Xia let out a sigh. “Alas, she passed from this world over fifty years ago. Before she went, she asked me to place this into her younger brother’s own hands, telling me he had entered the palace to serve the nobles within.”
As she spoke, she held out a large purple silk pouch. A servant received it and carried it to Imperial Attendant Liang.
With trembling hands, he undid the pouch and drew out the letter. He unfolded it and read every line with full attention, his eyes swimming with tears by the end. When he pulled out the jade piece carved with his sister’s name, all doubt vanished—these were indeed his sister’s keepsakes.
“No wonder all my years of searching turned up nothing,” he said. “She has been gone all this time. Yet why did you not bring these things to me sooner?”
Old Madam Xia explained, “It is not that we never tried to find you. We went to the capital several times, only for the guards at the palace gates to tell us no such person existed. Later, once you won His Majesty’s favor and your name began to spread, I worried that seeking you out would look as though the Chen family were trying to curry favor with the mighty, so I kept putting it off. Had it not been for our family’s current trouble, I doubt I would have brought this pouch out in my lifetime.”
“Where is my sister buried?”
“In the Xia family cemetery.”
“How did you come to know my sister?”
“I was not yet married back then. Out on the street one day, I saw a young woman kneeling by the roadside offering to sell herself. My heart went out to her, so I bought her and brought her home. That young woman was your sister, Liang Shihui. Shihui and I hit it off from the start, swore sisterhood, and kept her right there in the Xia household. After I wed, my parents adopted her as their own daughter, hoping to find her a good husband in time. Who could have known she would fall prey to a strange, incurable illness and pass away not long after? Before she died, she gave me that pouch, told me she had a younger brother who had been forced into the palace, and begged me to find him and deliver it into his own hands. Today, at long last, I have fulfilled her dying wish.”
Grief tore at Liang Shicheng, fat tears plopping down one after another. “My sister was eight years older than me. We lost our parents as children and scraped by in misery. She raised me as both father and mother. I could not bear to watch her toil, so I had myself castrated and entered the palace of my own accord, hoping to give her a comfortable life. Yet by the time I had the means to give her that life, I could not find her anywhere...”
Imperial Attendant Liang began to sob. The appeal to his emotions had reached its peak. Seizing the moment, Old Madam Xia dropped to her knees, and Chen Haoyu and Chen Jianglin knelt right behind her.
“For the sake of the bond I once shared with Sister Shihui, I beg you, Imperial Attendant Liang, to show mercy to the Chen family and spare Shuzhen.”
Listening from above, Qianli Xun grasped at last that the trouble weighing down her father-in-law and Chen Haoyu was all about her. She had never crossed paths with Imperial Attendant Liang—why on earth was he targeting her? Her thoughts jumped straight to Mengrong. Did Mengrong still refuse to let her go, going so far as to enlist someone of such immense power? To be sure of her hunch, Qianli Xun kept listening with rapt attention.
Imperial Attendant Liang checked his sobs, lifted his gaze, and dabbed his tears with a handkerchief. “Please rise at once, Elder Madam. You showed great kindness to my sister, so of course I will not lay a finger on the Chen family. On your account, I will even help the Chens expand their business in Bianliang. From now on, with me standing behind you, no one will dare touch the Chen family.”
Overjoyed at his words, the three of them gave thanks in unison. “Thank you, Imperial Attendant Liang!”
Father and son helped Old Madam Xia back to her seat, all three convinced that Shuzhen was safe now.
Yet Liang Shicheng went on, “As for the poetess, I would still like to meet her once. Rest assured, it is just a meeting—nothing more.”
The smiles vanished from all three faces in an instant. If all he wanted was a simple meeting, Chen Jianglin thought, why go to such drastic lengths?
Unable to hold back, Chen Haoyu spoke up. “If Imperial Attendant Liang wishes to see my wife, I will accompany her to pay a formal call tomorrow.”
The smile paired with Liang Shicheng’s red-rimmed eyes looked unsettling and out of place. “There is no need for Young Master Chen to escort her in person. Since I will no longer make trouble for the Chen family, the two-day deadline is off. Today during the hour of wei, between one and three in the afternoon, Commander Lin will call at Chen Manor to invite the poetess to Liu’an Villa as my guest.”
Hearing that, Qianli Xun felt even more certain Mengrong was behind this. A trip to Liu’an Villa would bode far more peril than good. Part of her itched to drop straight down and beat Liang Shicheng to a pulp. Still, the Chen elders had treated her with loyalty and warmth; storming in now would only drag the Chen family into disaster. Besides, the villa teemed with guards, so the odds were not in her favor. Using her light-foot technique, she bounded across the eaves and walls and sped away.
Inside, Chen Jianglin opened his mouth to protest, but his mother stopped him. Old Madam Xia spoke up instead. “Since Imperial Attendant Liang insists on meeting my granddaughter-in-law, we shall await his envoy at the hour of wei.”
After leaving Liu’an Villa, the three boarded their carriage and rode back the way they had come.
Inside the carriage, Chen Haoyu asked in alarm, “Grandmother, do you really intend to let Imperial Attendant Liang’s people come to Chen Manor and haul my wife away?”
The old madam explained, “What sort of person do you take your grandmother for? Imperial Attendant Liang has his heart set on seeing Shuzhen. Nothing you or your father said would have swayed him—better to hurry home and devise a plan. We no longer face an impossible choice between the family and her; now our sole challenge is protecting Shuzhen, which is far easier to handle. We still have a full shichen—two hours—before the hour of wei. I have thought it through. Once we get back, we will send Shuzhen into hiding in the countryside. When Lin Jue arrives at Chen Manor and finds no one there, he will have no choice but to leave empty-handed.”
Chen Jianglin frowned in worry. “I fear that if he cannot find Shuzhen, Imperial Attendant Liang will take it out on the Chen family all over again.”
“I was his sister’s benefactress. He will never bring harm to the Chens. Thank goodness I did not hand that pouch over years ago, or how else could I have saved this family today?” Old Madam Xia felt a measure of relief over her past choice.
Knowing his mother well, Chen Jianglin hazarded a guess. “I suspect Mother had another reason for holding back the pouch all those years—or perhaps you were waiting for the right moment to bring it out.”
Old Madam Xia nodded in approval. “You are half right. There was indeed another reason. At first I could not track him down, so I set it aside. By the time word of him reached me, he had risen in His Majesty’s favor and become a treacherous official reviled by the common people. Wanting no ties whatsoever between the Chen family and a man like that, I hid the pouch away. I thought I would never deliver it into his hands, yet when he backed the Chens into a corner, I had no choice.”
Chen Haoyu voiced a fresh worry. “Now that the Chen family has a connection to Liang Shicheng, people will find out sooner or later. I am afraid our family will be condemned for it down the line.”
The old madam gave them a strict warning. “Both of you remember this well. Today we leaned on that tie only to save the Chen family and Shuzhen. Going forward, keep your heads down and guard the foundation the Chens already have. Do not dream of expanding into Bianliang, and never go begging at Imperial Attendant Liang’s door. Only then will our family stay safe.”
The two answered together, one saying, “Your son will remember,” and the other, “Your grandson will remember.”
By the time the three returned to the manor, Qianli Xun had been home for a while. Her mind was made up: she would face whatever was coming on her own. Even death did not scare her. She had died once already, so every extra day she had lived was a stroke of luck. Let her see what Liang Shicheng thought he could do to her.
To avoid arousing Shuzhen’s suspicions, Old Madam Xia and Chen Jianglin stayed away from Moxiang Courtyard, leaving the crucial task to Chen Haoyu.
Putting on an air of casual ease, Chen Haoyu stepped into the courtyard and called out at the top of his lungs, “Wife! I’m back! There’s something I have to tell you!”
Before he even reached the doorway, his wife stepped out in front of him. “What is it?”
Chen Haoyu did his best to sound breezy and carefree. “Business requires me to make a trip out to the countryside. You have nothing keeping you at home anyway—why not come along with me?”
“Are we going to stay at the country house?”
“Not that place. This one is farther out—two full days and nights on the road.”
So Chen Haoyu meant to spirit her away. That had to be the elders’ plan.
“Too far. I’m not going.”
Chen Haoyu dropped the act. “You’re going whether you want to or not. That’s an order.”
“What a joke. Since when can you give me orders?”
“On this matter, I call the shots, and you don’t get a say.” Casting all caution aside for his wife’s sake, Chen Haoyu shouted, “Kuqiao!”
Kuqiao emerged from the room. “Young Master, what is it?”
Panic crept into Chen Haoyu’s voice despite himself. “Pack some clothes for your mistress! Right now! This instant! I’m taking her to the countryside!”
Clueless as to what was going on, Kuqiao went back inside to pack all the same.
Next, Chen Haoyu bellowed, “Hongdou!”
Hearing him shout, Hongdou ran over from the west side of the yard. “What are your orders, Young Master?”
“Gather every last servant! You’re all escorting me and your mistress to the countryside!”
Even more bewildered, Hongdou glanced at her mistress standing off to the side in silence, then went off to obey.
Chen Haoyu muttered to himself, “Right—almost forgot Matron Wei.”
He was about to bolt toward the back courtyard when his wife wrapped her arms around him from behind.
Held like this by his wife for the first time, Chen Haoyu felt an inexplicable rush of happiness flood his chest. Yet the thought of Lin Jue arriving with his men any minute snapped him out of the spell.
“Wife, I need to go find Matron Wei. Let go for a moment.”
Qianli Xun kept her arms right where they were. “Matron Wei isn’t in the back courtyard. She took two servants and went out first thing this morning.”
“Then we’ll have to leave without her. Let’s go first.”
“What’s the big rush?”
“No choice—some urgent business came up.”
“I already know it has nothing to do with business. Imperial Attendant Liang wants to see me, and because you’re worried sick, you want to spirit me off to the countryside to hide.”
Chen Haoyu could not fathom how his wife knew the truth, yet he kept trying to cover it up. “Where did you hear such nonsense? Who do you think Imperial Attendant Liang is? Why would a man like him ask to see you? Besides, even if he did, what would be the harm in meeting him? Stop letting your imagination run wild—it really is just business.”
“I tailed you, Grandmother, and Father-in-law all the way to Liu’an Villa. I was up on the roof and heard every word you said inside.” Qianli Xun let go of him. “You said it yourself—what’s the harm in a meeting? Fine, then. I’ll go meet him.”
Realizing the charade was over, Chen Haoyu spun around, urgency pouring from his voice. “Since you already know the truth, there’s no point hiding it. Imperial Attendant Liang’s demand to see you is nowhere near that simple. To force your hand, he sent men to stir up trouble at our tea shop, and he has both Prince Yi and my father-in-law locked up. Going to him puts you in grave danger. Father told me your dad said Liang Shicheng is a lecher—with a special taste for married women. How could I ever let you walk into that trap?”
Qianli Xun let out a cold laugh. “Him? A treacherous toad like that thinks he can get his hands on me? Not a chance! One punch from me and I’ll crack his skull open like a melon!”
Seeing his wife’s fearless expression eased his heart a fraction, yet he still tried to reason with her. “Rather than clashing with him head-on, why not go into hiding where he can’t find you? Give it time, and maybe he’ll lose interest and forget.”
Qianli Xun thought Chen Haoyu was being far too naive. Liang Shicheng had locked up both Prince Yi and her father, Zhu Xiyan. Until he laid eyes on her, he would never back down. What good would hiding do?
“Hiding solves nothing. A man like Liang Shicheng has plenty of ways to hunt me down. I’ve made up my mind. I’ll walk right in and face him in the open—and see what he thinks he can do to me.”

#TITLE: Moonlight on the Divine Willow, a Lover’s Return (The End)
Chen Haoyu left Zhu Manor alone, needing some quiet time by himself to think things through. Chen Wenxin urged Xiangrong to leave him be; men needed to be alone from time to time, and if they left him to himself for a while, he would come right back home come nightfall.
Qianli Xun, Xiangrong, and Chen Wenxin shared a carriage back to Chen Manor.
Xiangrong broke the silence first. “Shuzhen, I’m so glad you made it back safe and sound—for Haoyu’s sake, too. Ever since he thought you were... well, he hasn’t been happy much at all.”
Qianli Xun looked at the gentle swell of Xiangrong’s belly. “Thank you, Xiangrong.”
“What are you thanking me for?”
“You did what I once tried so hard to do, and by his side you’re bound to do a far better job than I ever could. Chen Haoyu once told me he liked women who were beautiful and gentle, and you fit that to a T. All at once, I feel like everything Heaven arranged turned out for the best. You are the best match Chen Haoyu could ask for.”
A flicker of sadness crossed Xiangrong’s face. “He might not see it that way.”
“I’m sure he will. He’s about to become a father—of course the mother of his child will matter more to him than anyone else.”
A smile broke over Xiangrong’s face at last. “You’re so kind, Shuzhen. No wonder so many people adore you.”
“Yet I only ever wanted one person. I’m not as lucky as you, with the man you love right beside you. I still have a year to wait, and once that year is up, I’ll be just as happy as you are.”
The last of Xiangrong’s guard melted away. “You will. You’ll be even happier than me.”
Chen Wenxin could not help chiming in, “Shuzhen is my sister-in-law on my husband’s side, and you’re my younger brother’s wife—you’re both family to me. Both of you deserve every bit of happiness.”
The three women shared a warm, knowing smile.
Upon arriving at Chen Manor, Qianli Xun paid her respects to Chen Jianglin, Yao Qingyan, and the other elders. Everyone rejoiced to see her alive and sighed in disbelief over all she had endured. Though it pained them to part with Shuzhen, they knew they had to let her go.
After catching up with the family, Yao Qingyan led Shuzhen to Old Madam Xia’s memorial tablet.
Qianli Xun dropped to her knees and kowtowed, tears spilling down her cheeks unbidden. “Grandmother, Shuzhen came back too late and missed your final moments. I have been an unfilial granddaughter. From now on, I’ll visit your grave often and sit with you to talk.”
Alone with her in the ancestral hall where the tablets stood, Yao Qingyan spoke straight from the heart. “If the old madam knows beneath the Nine Springs that she lost such a fine granddaughter-in-law, it is bound to grieve her.”
At a loss for words, Qianli Xun stayed kneeling before the tablet, overcome with sorrow.
Yao Qingyan stepped forward and helped her to her feet. “Get up, child.”
Rising, Qianli Xun answered, “Even though I’m no longer a daughter-in-law of the Chen family, I hold all the Chen elders dear. If you don’t mind, I’d like to take you as my adoptive mother.”
Yao Qingyan could not have been more delighted. “What adoptive mother? I’m your real mother! From today on, you have two real mothers.”
“All right—my real mother.” Smiling through her tears, Qianli Xun called out with warmth, “Mother.”
“Yes, my girl!” Yao Qingyan answered without a split second’s hesitation, then turned toward the old madam’s tablet. “Mother, did you hear that? Shuzhen is still one of the Chens. From now on, you’re her own grandmother.”
After paying her respects at Old Madam Xia’s tablet, Qianli Xun returned to the reception hall, knelt before Chen Jianglin, and called out, “Father.”
Chen Jianglin blinked in bewilderment for a moment, then caught on before Yao Qingyan could even explain. “Since you have taken me as your father, I must give you a proper kinship gift. Before the New Year, without my knowledge, Matron Wei went to Steward Fang in private and returned the estates, storefronts, and Zuiyun Tower—all the properties once transferred to you from Haoyu’s name—back to Haoyu. I was too heartbroken over you at the time to bother intervening. Today, now that you call me Father, every last one of those properties goes right back to you. Don’t think it too meager.”
“Thank you for your generosity, Father. How could Shuzhen ever call that meager?”
“Get up now. Don’t stay on your knees.”
Yao Qingyan had already sent her maid Yingxue to fetch a brocade box. Lifting an emerald-green jade bangle from inside, she stepped up and slipped it onto Shuzhen’s wrist. “A gift from Mother. From now on you have two homes. Whenever you have the time, make sure you come stay with me for a stretch.”
“Shuzhen will remember.”
Xiangrong felt more at ease than ever, gratitude toward Shuzhen filling her heart. If not for Shuzhen, how would she ever have escaped Huayun Pavilion and married into a household as decent as the Chens?
Aside from catching up with the Chen elders after their long separation, Qianli Xun had come on another important errand. She brought it up herself. “Father, Mother, I’d like to move my people and belongings out of Moxiang Courtyard and leave Chen Manor.”
Silence fell over the room, uneasy looks crossing every face.
Yao Qingyan broke the hush first. “By all rights your request could not be more reasonable, yet we have no say over Moxiang Courtyard. We would need Haoyu’s consent...”
Before she could finish, Chen Haoyu’s voice rang out from outside. “I agree!”
Chen Haoyu strode in from the courtyard. All the way home on foot, he had forced himself to come to terms with a great many things, resolving to let go for good and let the wife he loved live the life she wanted.
Xiangrong rose to meet him. “Haoyu, you’re... back.”
Spotting the shimmer of tears in Xiangrong’s eyes, Chen Haoyu spoke with concern. “You’re with child—sit back down.”
He guided Xiangrong back to her chair, put on an easygoing front, and turned to his former wife. “Move whenever you want.”
“I’d like to move today.”
Holding down the reluctance aching in his chest, Chen Haoyu said, “I’ll have Qinggua round up extra servants to help you.”
“All right. I’ll leave it to you.”
His former wife had so seldom agreed with him without a fight—and now that she did, it meant walking out of his life for good. Beneath Chen Haoyu’s breezy expression lay a sharp pang of heartache.
By nightfall, everything meant to leave Moxiang Courtyard was loaded onto the carts. Bai Feng and Kuqiao drove the lead carriage, escorting the nine servants back to the bamboo cottage in the countryside.
Qianli Xun did not head to the bamboo grove just yet. Instead, she rode in a carriage back to Zhu Manor with her sister-in-law Chen Wenxin, intending to spend a few days with her parents before settling into the bamboo cottage. Along the way, Chen Wenxin happened to mention that the Old Ancestor had passed away, sending another wave of sadness over Qianli Xun.
The next day she went to the Buddhist hall, where only Matron Xiuzhen remained. Guided by the matron, Qianli Xun knelt and kowtowed before the Old Ancestor’s memorial tablet. For some reason, as her forehead touched the floor, sorrow refused to come. Matron Xiuzhen told her the Old Ancestor had sensed her hour approaching and departed without a trace of pain. How many people in this world got to leave life with such ease? She ought to feel glad for the Old Ancestor.
Several days later, Qianli Xun set off for the bamboo cottage with Matron Wei. Throughout those days at Zhu Manor, the masters of the house had hovered around their daughter day in and day out. Even Master Zhu, aloof as he had always been, seemed like a changed man, insisting the whole family gather around one table for every meal and smiling far more than usual—leaving Matron Wei no chance to chat with her mistress.
Now, with just the two of them inside the carriage, Matron Wei had her chance for a heart-to-heart at last.
“Over two years apart, and you’ve grown so much more mature, Miss. I ought to be glad, yet my heart aches instead. Whenever I think of all the hardship you went through, I just...” Tears spilled down Matron Wei’s cheeks.
Even if Matron Wei had not said a word, one glance at her streaked gray hair told Qianli Xun how sick with worry the matron had been during her absence. Unwilling to watch Matron Wei grieve, Qianli Xun told a white lie. “I didn’t think it was rough at all—in fact, I enjoyed it plenty! I’m a poetess, and I write huaben tales on the sly, too. You can’t write without real-life experience. Now that I’ve got it, who knows—I might turn out a masterpiece that gets passed down for centuries. Just picturing people in future ages remembering my name makes me grin in my sleep. So don’t feel sorry for me, Matron. Besides, it’s all behind us now.”
Seeing her mistress brush it off with such ease, Matron Wei let her grief go. “You’re right, Miss. It’s all behind us now.”
Recalling a piece of happy news, Qianli Xun flashed a sly smile. “Mother tells me you’ve got some joyous news of your own, Matron?”
Matron Wei blushed, yet made no attempt to hide it. “While you were gone, Miss, I was lonely and miserable. Steward Fang was the one who comforted me and talked me through it. Before we knew it, we had grown fond of each other.”
“I’m going to throw you a grand wedding to marry Steward Fang in style.”
“I appreciate your kindness, Miss, but at my age I don’t care for grand ceremonies. What matters is having two people who can lean on each other. Steward Fang and I have worked it all out: he will keep tending to his work at the Chens’ Xinglong Pavilion, while I keep looking after your household and estates just as before, returning to our home in the city for a few days each month. When the day comes that he can no longer work and you grow tired of an old woman like me, I’ll go home with him to our own place.”
“What nonsense is that, Matron? You’re family to me—how could I ever grow tired of you getting old? I’ve already got it planned out: once Steward Fang retires from the Chen family, the two of you move right into the bamboo cottage with me. It’ll be much livelier with us all together.”
Unable to bear parting from her mistress either, Matron Wei smiled. “All right. Whatever you say, Miss.”
Once back at the bamboo cottage, on top of arranging the weddings for Kuqiao and Bai Feng as well as Asheng and Hongdou, Qianli Xun also wed Chouya to Qinggua. Word that her mistress had returned alive brought Amei out to the bamboo grove to pay a visit, too. Judging from her fine clothes and bright smile, Master Jiang was treating her well.
Friends dropped by the bamboo cottage all the time. The three young masters often came with their wives in tow. Qianli Xun saw Ye Anya on a regular basis; her complexion looked far healthier than before, proof enough that she had chosen the right path in life. Wenyuan also brought his wife and concubine over for visits, and his two children were so adorable that Qianli Xun could not help feeling a pang of envy.
Bai Meiruo came by as well with her child—and on more than a few occasions, Lin Jue tagged along right beside her. After the first Song-Jin war ended in a peace settlement, Lin Jue had returned to Lin’an. Living through the horrors of war had transformed him into a different man. Perhaps witnessing so much death and bloodshed had given him a new outlook on life; he had grown far more humble and restrained, and he even knew how to speak words of care to Bai Meiruo now. People really were the most changeable creatures on earth, Qianli Xun mused.
What delighted Qianli Xun most of all was that Miao Zhiyu and Bai Meiruo came forward on their own to ask if they could be her close confidantes, confessing together all the wrongs they had done her in the past. Qianli Xun was never one to hold a grudge and forgave them both. From then on, the three met once a month at Yongtai Teahouse, chatting about everything under the sun in high spirits.
Qianli Xun also paid frequent visits to the Divine Willow and stopped by the nunnery to see Shao Yueting—both out of gratitude and to ask after news of Shen Jianchen through her. Shao Yueting had returned from Yanjing only after Qianli Xun made it back to Lin’an. Because she exchanged letters with Wanyan Chi, she managed to glean bits and pieces of news from Yanjing. Knowing full well why her benefactress came calling, Shao Yueting found subtle ways in every letter to inquire after Prince Yi. Whenever she saw a happy smile light up her benefactress’s face, Shao Yueting felt as though the weight of her son’s sins grew a fraction lighter.
A few months later, the Jin marched south against the Song once more. Sweeping forward with unstoppable momentum for more than three months, Wanyan Zongwang stood outside the walls of Bianliang yet again. The relentless fighting in the north sent shockwaves through the south as well, driving ever larger waves of refugees fleeing southward.
In the tenth month, Xiangrong bore Chen Haoyu a son, bringing joy to every soul in the Chen household. Growing ever more attentive to Xiangrong, Chen Haoyu sought his family’s blessing and promoted her to primary wife.
Amid the turmoil of war, panic gripped the realm and Song law grew lax. Seizing the opening, the Chen family spent generous sums of silver and bought Chen Haodong’s release from prison. Years behind bars had worn away Chen Haodong’s old edge. Unwilling to meddle in the family business, he spent his days at home keeping his parents company. Now and then, Chen Haoyu used business matters as an excuse to ask Brother Dong for advice, and Chen Haodong always offered sharp pointers in return. Whenever Haoyu urged him to join in running the Chen enterprises, however, Chen Haodong turned him down with gentle grace, saying he already counted himself lucky that Haoyu and the rest of the family were willing to tolerate his presence.
In the first month of the second year of the Jingkang reign, Wanyan Zongwang breached the capital, taking both Zhao Ji and Zhao Huan captive. In the second month, the Northern Song fell. Emperor Taizong of Jin issued an edict deposing Emperors Huizong and Qinzong of Song, stripping them down to commoners, tearing the dragon robes from their backs by force, and holding them prisoner in Jin territory. That sixth month, Zhao Gou, Prince of Kang, ascended the throne in Yingtian Prefecture—the start of what history would call the Southern Song.
Even as the upheaval of the era weighed on Qianli Xun’s mind, she missed Shen Jianchen more than ever. Over a year had gone by—why was he still not back?
One day, while visiting Youlin Retreat, she walked beneath the Divine Willow with Miao Zhiyu and Bai Meiruo to watch their children play. The two youngsters chased butterflies in high glee, while Miao Zhiyu and Bai Meiruo each tied a strip of red silk onto the willow branches.
Gazing up at the canopy of green willow fronds and fluttering red ribbons, Qianli Xun prayed in her heart, “Divine Willow, please watch over him and bring him back to my side soon...”
On her tenth silent prayer, a familiar voice sounded behind her. “I guessed you’d be right here beneath the Divine Willow, and sure enough, I guessed right.”
Convinced her longing for Shen Jianchen was playing tricks on her ears, Qianli Xun did not turn around and kept on praying.
Miao Zhiyu called out to remind her, “Youqi Jushi, turn around and look! The man you’ve been waiting for is back!”
Heart leaping, Qianli Xun whirled around. It was him. He had come back at last, his skin tanned far darker than before...
With tact and grace, Miao Zhiyu and Bai Meiruo gathered their children and slipped away. Moments this precious belonged to the pair who had endured so much hardship just to find their way back to each other.
“You lied to me! You promised one year—why are you more than two months late?”
“I got held up on the road. When I passed through Yingtian Prefecture, His Majesty asked me to stay for a spell. With everything that has happened to the imperial house, I couldn’t well refuse.”
“Fine, you’ve got a fair excuse. Still...”
“Don’t tell me you still plan to turn me down?”
“Who says I...” Qianli Xun ached to throw herself into his arms, yet the urge to tease him won out. She turned her back on him on purpose. “And what if I do turn you down?”
Behind her, she heard Shen Jianchen call out, “Chubby!”
Next came a loud snorting and huffing, as if something were charging up from behind. Before she could even react, a sleek, jet-black piglet darted past her legs, bolted straight for the Divine Willow, dug in all four hooves, and scrambled right up the trunk as if trotting over flat ground, settling down on the sturdiest bough.
Qianli Xun stared, struck speechless. There really was a pig in this world that could climb trees?
Shen Jianchen stepped up beside her. “Let’s see you try turning me down now.”
Unable to hold back a second longer, Qianli Xun threw her arms around the man beside her. “How can you be this foolish? I was just joking—you really took me at my word? Even if pigs couldn’t climb trees, I still would have said yes.”
At last, Shen Jianchen could gather the woman he loved into his embrace without holding a single thing back. “Only with you do I turn foolish—foolish enough to hope that on the road of your life, I am the sole path you walk.”
Caught up in the tide of feeling, they held each other close and kissed.
The setting sun sank into the west, and the moon climbed above the boughs of the Divine Willow. A full moon hung in the sky tonight, bathing the ground around the tree as bright as day, throwing the silhouettes of the two lovers into sharp relief.
“Where on earth did you dig up a tree-climbing pig?”
“I ran into a man from a foreign tribe by chance in Huining. He made silver by having Chubby perform tree-climbing tricks, so I paid a handsome price to buy him.”
“How much silver did you spend?”
“A hundred taels.”
“Since when does a pig cost that much?”
“He’s a pig that climbs trees. He isn’t like other pigs.”
Qianli Xun let it drop. What was a hundred taels of silver to a man who had sold off his entire fortune for her?
“Weren’t you in Yanjing? Why did you go to Huining?”
“Nahegu took me there.”
“Nahegu?”
“Wanyan Chi’s master. I found out at last that he was the one who created Shen Fist. Its history goes back four or five hundred years before Qianli Fist.”
“So what if it’s older? Aren’t you still the man I beat?”
“And I’ll be your defeated opponent with all my heart—forever...”
(The End)
