#TITLE: Visiting His Beloved to Speak His Heart
While the clerk fetched a small wooden box to pack the peeing-boy figurine, two young women strolled in, chatting as they browsed the clay dolls.
"This shop's clay figurines are wonderful! Which one should I buy for him?"
"Have you no shame? Careful folks don't start gossiping about you the way they do about Youqi Jushi!"
"How can you lump me in with her? My parents arranged this betrothal, with every matchmaker and betrothal gift in place!"
"Sigh. Who knew Youqi Jushi writes great poetry while her conduct leaves so much to be desired?"
"Tell me about it! Nothing matters more to a maiden of the inner chambers than her chastity, yet she's bold enough to put it in verse and act on it, putting every sheltered young lady to shame! Makes you wonder what the men she traded secret glances and had affairs with think..."
Shen Jianchen could not listen to another word. Hearing "traded secret glances"—wasn't that talking about him?
Qianli Xun had not spared his own yearning glances a second thought, so how could he let two strangers smear her?
He marched right up to them. "Two young ladies blessed with faces fair as flowers—pity your words don't match your looks. Keep running your mouths behind people's backs and you might never find a husband's house. Even if you do, once your in-laws learn their future daughter-in-law is a gossipmonger, they'll regret the match and might even call off the wedding."
Flattered at first by his praise of their looks, both girls bristled at the sting that followed.
One snapped, "What's the use lecturing us? Everyone on the street is talking about her! Are all the girls in Lin'an going to end up unwed?"
The other grabbed a lucky-doll figurine and handed it to the clerk. "Wrap this up. What rotten luck today, running into a meddler!"
Taking her doll, the clerk handed the wooden box holding the peeing boy to Bai Feng.
Bai Feng paid the silver, annoyed at how rude the girls had been to His Highness, and snapped back, "Keep badmouthing folks behind their backs and watch your mouths rot off tomorrow!"
Both girls flared up hotter, one barking, "What business is it of yours if we talk about Youqi Jushi? Dogs catching mice—mind your own business!"
Bai Feng yelled back, "Gossipmongers! A pair of gossipmongers!"
Shen Jianchen did not stop him; they had it coming for slandering the woman he loved.
The clerk jumped in as peacemaker: "Now, now, folks—harmony brings wealth! Sir, it isn't right for the young ladies to gossip, sure, yet the whole city's buzzing right now. Can you police every last person? Blame the Prefect's daughter for crossing lines no sheltered lady should cross and inviting talk."
Shen Jianchen asked, "You gossip about her too?"
The clerk chuckled. "Who doesn't? Everybody's talking. Heard the Prefect even threw his own daughter out to save the Zhu family's name."
Taking her wrapped figurine, the second girl snorted, "Even her own father doesn't want her—what's wrong with us saying two words? Hmph!"
Paying up, both girls flounced out in a huff.
Dread deepened in Shen Jianchen's chest; how hard must Qianli Xun be hurting right now? Something about these rumors smelled fishy, as if engineered on purpose.
"Clerk, how did you hear these rumors about Miss Zhu?"
"Running a shop, folk stream in and out all day; hear enough talk and it sticks. Honest truth, I'm busy minding the counter and never cared a fig about poets. Don't laugh, sir, I don't know a single poem by heart, yet now even I remember 'falling asleep in his arms, fully dressed'! Haha—the only difference between refined scholars and common folk like us is dressing up dirty deeds in fancy words, haha..."
Unable to stomach another word, Shen Jianchen walked out without reply, boarded his carriage, and headed east.
He kicked himself for holing up in Youlin Retreat for over a month. Had he strolled the streets sooner, he might have caught the rumors early and snuffed them out before they swept the whole city.
Yet without those rumors, the proposal would have gone through, Qianli Xun would belong to Wenyuan, and he would not be riding east right now with a gift in hand.
Shen Jianchen gave his own cheek a light slap and muttered under his breath, "How can you think like that? You're an engaged man! Wenyuan is suffering, Qianli Xun must be hurting worse, and here you are feeling glad—shame on you!"
Yet he could not crush that stubborn spark of gladness—glad that one sliver of hope still survived between him and Qianli Xun.
Reaching the east quarter, it took no effort to find the wealthy neighborhood and locate Wangyue Pavilion.
Compared with Zhu Manor, Wangyue Pavilion was tiny, yet its layout held an understated grace.
Matron Wei had picked the house straight to her mistress's tastes: graceful orchids and slender bamboo filled the grounds, and chrysanthemums of every hue stood in full bloom across the yard, vying in beauty. It combined the best touches of Zhulan Residence and her old courtyard at Zhu Manor.
Qianli Xun loved it; above all, her bedroom was arranged identical to her old room at Zhu Manor, wrapping her in the comfort of home.
The maids busied themselves unpacking clothes while Matron Wei took charge of household affairs and laid down rules for the servants.
With nothing to fuss over, Qianli Xun collapsed onto bed to sleep. One day had dragged her through wild highs and lows; she needed to collect her thoughts, hoping sleep might wash every misfortune away.
Deep in slumber, she dreamed her huaben stories had taken Song readers by storm—books selling out the instant they left the press, a copy in every hand down every lane, her name headed straight into the history books.
"Miss! Miss, wake up! Prince Yi is here to visit!" Kuqiao's voice yanked her out of her dream.
Opening her eyes and sitting up, she glanced out the window at gathering dusk. "Who's here?"
Kuqiao repeated, "Prince Yi."
"Prince Yi? How did he know I moved to Wangyue Pavilion? Don't tell me you leaked it to Bai Feng!"
"You wrong me, Miss! However long you haven't seen Prince Yi, I haven't seen Bai Feng either."
Remembering her vow never to meet Prince Yi alone again, Qianli Xun said, "Go tell Prince Yi I'm in a foul mood and have no time to see him."
"Miss, he's Prince Yi—turning him away like that might not go over well."
Qianli Xun felt stuck: see him, and he would start tossing sparks at her again; refuse him, and she risked offending a royal prince.
Kuqiao handed her the ideal excuse: "Better see him, Miss—you can ask him how Young Master Qiao is doing."
Qianli Xun swung out of bed at once. "Help me freshen up! I want Prince Yi to see me looking my absolute best."
Dressed and polished by Kuqiao, she stepped into the main hall, where Prince Yi sat waiting with Matron Wei keeping him company.
Seeing her mistress enter, Matron Wei rose. "Since Prince Yi has words for our Young Lady, I'll take my leave."
Shen Jianchen nodded, his gaze lingering on Qianli Xun. She had taken great care with her dress and makeup—had she really dressed up just to see him? Could she have feelings for him after all?
A second later he saw through it: she was only using bright makeup to mask the ache inside.
On the ride over, Bai Feng had figured out the clay figurine was for Youqi Jushi. Baffled at first, he had rationalized it as His Highness visiting on Young Master Qiao's behalf and not wanting to arrive empty-handed—still, what on earth was the idea behind a peeing boy?
Spotting Kuqiao, he dropped the puzzle, set the wooden box on the table, tossed Kuqiao a wink, and slipped out in silence with her.
Alone at last, Shen Jianchen felt satisfied and stood up. "How is Youqi Jushi holding up?"
Skipping an answer, Qianli Xun bowed. "How did Prince Yi find Wangyue Pavilion?"
"Anyone I want to find can't stay hidden from me."
Right—he was a royal prince; what a pointless question.
"How is Wenyuan?"
Two sentences in and she was already asking about Wenyuan. Stung, Shen Jianchen answered anyway: "With the proposal off, Wenyuan is taking it hard. He begged me to go persuade Grandfather at Qiao Manor, yet Grandfather swore he'd give up his life before allowing your marriage. There was nothing I could do."
A wave of bitterness choked Qianli Xun silent.
Spotting the bruise on her hand, Shen Jianchen said, "Youqi Jushi needs to take better care of herself."
Swamped in heartache, she had no urge to talk, yet etiquette before a prince demanded a reply.
"Thank you for your concern, Prince Yi. Please pass word to Wenyuan to meet me beneath the Divine Willow tomorrow."
To Shen Jianchen, the Divine Willow belonged to him and Qianli Xun alone. "Why meet beneath the Divine Willow?"
"Why not beneath the Divine Willow?" Qianli Xun thought he was meddling way too much—now policing where she and Wenyuan met? What a busybody!
"Fine, suit yourself." Shen Jianchen picked up the wooden box. "Brought you a gift. Guaranteed to lift your spirits once you open it."
"Did Wenyuan send it?"
"Can't it be from me?"
Qianli Xun hesitated to accept. "Prince Yi shouldn't spend money on me."
Shen Jianchen pressed the box into her arms. "Hardly any money at all—buying you a gift makes me happy. Take it, or I won't pass your message to Wenyuan."
Shen Jianchen turned to leave.
Clutching the wooden box, tangled emotions churned inside Qianli Xun. "Prince Yi!"
Shen Jianchen halted without looking back. "Yes?"
"Wenyuan is the one I love."
"I know."
"Princess Jiahe is a wonderful girl. Don't you dare let her down."
"No need for you to remind me."
With that, Shen Jianchen strode out of the hall and across the courtyard. Seeing him leave, Bai Feng bade Kuqiao a hasty farewell and followed his master out of Wangyue Pavilion.

#TITLE: Family Warmth on Mid-Autumn Night
On Mid-Autumn Festival, wealthy households decked out their pavilions and towers, and every major tavern in Lin'an had been booked full long ago for evening drinking and moon-viewing.
Once night fell, music of strings and reed pipes drifted from every tavern onto the street; crowds thronged beneath dazzling lanterns, flutes and pipes echoed from home after home, and children chased each other laughing down the lanes.
Wangyue Pavilion, by contrast, felt quiet and forlorn.
The kitchen had baked mooncakes, an altar to the Moon Deity stood set in the courtyard with decorations all around, and the maids and page boys were in good spirits, gathering to play and admire the full moon overhead.
Yet Qianli Xun lay sprawled across a bamboo chair, unable to summon a shred of cheer; her gloom drained the color from every corner of Wangyue Pavilion.
Mid-Autumn meant family reunion, yet she had no home to return to. Wangyue Pavilion did not feel like home—where was home, anyway? That twenty-second-floor apartment before crossing over? Qianli Fist Manor? Or Zhu Manor? Every one had been home, yet none was home now.
With no answer in sight and the Qiao family blocking her and Wenyuan, sorrow weighed on her all the heavier amid the festival cheer outside.
A drift of osmanthus scent floated into her chamber, reminding her several osmanthus trees grew in Wangyue Pavilion's garden. That rich fragrance lifted her spirits a fraction.
"Fresh out of the oven, baked by the kitchen on Matron Wei's orders—still piping hot!" Carrying in a plate of mooncakes, Kuqiao urged, "Have one, Miss."
"Set it down. I want to walk by the osmanthus trees." With zero appetite for mooncakes, Qianli Xun rose from the bamboo chair and stepped outside.
Setting the plate down, Kuqiao grabbed a cape and followed her mistress out.
Out in the yard, Xiaguo knelt with the other maids and page boys bowing to the Moon Deity, whispering a silent prayer for a chance to be with Young Master Qiao.
Rising from her bow to see Miss and Kuqiao walk past, she scrambled up to follow: "Where is Miss headed?"
Qianli Xun stayed silent.
Kuqiao answered for her: "For a walk by the osmanthus trees."
Seeing her mistress mute in gloom, Xiaguo dared ask no more and trailed behind in silence.
Three osmanthus trees stood in Wangyue Pavilion's garden, branches crowded tight with golden blossoms pouring out rich perfume—sweeter with every step closer.
Qianli Xun circled the three trees, hoping the thick scent might wash away her low spirits.
She had never been the sentimental type before; why had crossing over turned her into such a softy? No answer came.
Completing a lap around the trees, she spotted a pavilion beside them and stepped inside. From the pavilion she could take in all three trees at a glance while waves of osmanthus fragrance drifted straight across her face.
Since moving in, it was not that she had lacked time to explore the garden—she just had not had the mood. Without that osmanthus scent, she would not have left her room tonight.
Standing in the pavilion, she lifted her eyes to the night sky. What a huge, round moon! Only now did she realize this pavilion was the prime moon-viewing perch—and why the house bore the name Wangyue Pavilion, the Pavilion for Gazing at the Moon.
"Put out the lanterns."
Exchanging a puzzled look, the two maids asked no questions and snuffed the pavilion's lamps.
With the glare gone, moonlight spilled twice as bright across the garden. Heaven had blessed this festival night with clear skies. What were the folks at Zhu Manor doing right now? And was Wenyuan still locked up?
Just then Matron Wei hurried up calling, "Been hunting everywhere for you, Young Lady! A maid said you headed this way, and here you are—come to the front courtyard! Guests are here to see you!"
Guests? Who? Not Prince Yi—he had promised never to come back. Could it be her family?
Qianli Xun hurried down the pavilion steps: "Who came?"
Matron Wei kept the surprise: "You'll see in a minute, Young Lady!"
Quickening her steps out of the garden, Qianli Xun tried guessing who waited ahead...
Entering the reception hall in the front courtyard, she saw Madam Xiang and Chen Wenxin sitting there, and joyful tears sprang straight to her eyes as she bowed: "Mother, Sister-in-law, thank you for coming to see me..."
Allergic to tearjerkers, Chen Wenxin teased, "Why act like strangers—bowing and thanking us as if Mother and I aren't family! If we weren't family, why on earth would we trek over here at night?!"
Laughing through her tears, Qianli Xun rushed up to hold Madam Xiang's hands: "I missed you so much, Mother! Haven't been out long, yet it feels like ages since I saw you."
Tears shimmered in Madam Xiang's smiling eyes: "You don't blame Mother for taking this long to visit, Shuzhen?"
"How could I? A daughter ought to go home to see her mother—I'm the unfilial one making Mother trek all the way out here."
Madam Xiang stood and looked her over: "You've gotten thinner! Eat more—with Mother not beside you, take good care of yourself."
"Don't fret over me, Mother—I eat great and sleep great. Look at you, though, more silver hairs at your temples! Quit worrying so much."
Chen Wenxin chuckled: "Mother, Shuzhen, quit standing around—sit down so we can have a proper chat!"
Everyone took seats.
Matron Wei signaled a maid to bring out the fresh mooncakes.
Madam Xiang beckoned: "Sit with us too, Matron—it's Mid-Autumn, merrier with the whole family together."
Taking a seat, Matron Wei said, "Try these mooncakes, Madam and Young Madam, and bring our Young Lady good fortune! Living out here alone on Mid-Autumn, I knew she felt lonely, yet baking mooncakes was all I could do. While baking, I wished that whoever visited tonight would eat a mooncake so our Young Lady's life ahead turns round and complete!"
Without a second thought, Madam Xiang and Chen Wenxin each picked up a mooncake and ate every bite with heart, silently blessing Shuzhen's future with fullness and peace.
Watching her two loved ones eat those mooncakes thawed Qianli Xun's chest, making all her earlier gloom look silly. Where was home? She had her answer now: wherever family stood, there was home.
Finishing first, Chen Wenxin dabbed her lips with a silk handkerchief: "Shuzhen, guess what I was thinking while eating that mooncake?"
"How could I guess Sister-in-law's thoughts?"
"I was thinking maybe the Qiao match falling through is a blessing in disguise—a better match is waiting for you! The Qiaos aren't the only fine family in Lin'an; our Chen house isn't shabby either!"
"Sister-in-law wants me to marry into the Chen family?"
"Why not?!"
"Quit joking, Sister-in-law—a guy like Chen Haoyu couldn't pass my inspection in a million years!"
"Haoyu isn't the only Chen boy! My third uncle's son Haofei is a gem—hates business, loves books, well-read as can be. Worth considering!"
"Sounds like Sister-in-law is bored stiff, playing matchmaker!"
Chen Wenxin appealed to Madam Xiang: "What do you think, Mother?"
Finishing her mooncake, Madam Xiang laughed while chiding her: "I'm taking it as a joke—your third uncle's boy is five whole years younger than Shuzhen! Haha!"
Dropping the match, Chen Wenxin grinned: "Just trying to make Shuzhen laugh!"
"Leave it to Sister-in-law to joke about something like that—aren't you afraid I'll take you up on it?"
"Even better if you did!"
Bad match aside, her sister-in-law's warm intent cheered Qianli Xun up more: "Coming over here tonight, Mother and Sister-in-law—won't Father be mad?"
Madam Xiang smiled: "Silly child, if your father objected, could we have walked out the gate? Acting stern and sending you out was all a show for outsiders—he worries about you every single hour."
Chen Wenxin chimed in: "True! Cut Father-in-law some slack—he's the Prefect of Lin'an and head of the Zhu house. With a scandal this loud, he had to put on a stern stance to answer to outsiders."
Qianli Xun had long stopped blaming Zhu Xiyan, though she put on a mock pout: "Father only cares about answering to outsiders—doesn't he owe his own daughter an answer too? Throwing me out just gave folks more to laugh at."
Hearing no real grudge left in her voice, Matron Wei beamed: "As the ancients say, fortune and misfortune lean on each other. Maybe this storm is Heaven's exam for you, Young Lady! Facing a whole city's gossip without flinching shows true grit; handling the Qiao mess with a cool head instead of weeping and threatening suicide shows real wisdom. If I were the examiner, I'd give you top honors! Haha!"
Half joke, half wisdom—Matron Wei's words struck home. Qianli Xun vowed inside to tackle every test life threw at her with clear judgment. Right now, the best answer for Wenyuan was patience.
Bringing up the Qiao family with care, Madam Xiang asked, "What are your thoughts on Young Master Qiao, Shuzhen?"
"I'm giving myself half a year. If nothing moves in half a year, I'll... let go."
Madam Xiang nodded: "Sensible—just hard on you."
"I'm glad you're keeping a cool head, Shuzhen. A gem like you—the Qiaos are the losers for backing out! Lucky whichever boy wins you next." Chen Wenxin silently sighed: if only her own brother had half a spine she would pitch him right now, yet with Haoyu's wild streak and the way those two clashed, she could not even open her mouth.
"Sister-in-law's family—of course you praise me."
Wangyue Pavilion no longer felt cold or empty; family warmth filled every corner.
Kuqiao felt happy too—Miss was not alone, and the Zhu family still cared for her.
Only Xiaguo could not raise a smile. Half a year—what if nothing happened in half a year and Miss gave up Young Master Qiao? What on earth would Xiaguo do then?

#TITLE: Fame of the Storyteller Scholar Spreads
Just as An Mingxuan predicted, the literati at the Mid-Autumn Poetry Banquet refused to let street gossip cloud their judgment, pouring praise on Youqi Jushi's poems instead.
One scholar marveled that no storm of rumors could break Youqi Jushi's spirit, letting her pen a line as transcendent as "Do not regard it with a casual eye: even Chang'e knows what sorrow means."
Another praised how, while market folk wagged tongues, Youqi Jushi met the world with a smile and enjoyed the Mid-Autumn moon—a breadth of spirit to envy.
Above all, the couplet "Through chill the jade hare works its pestle still; grown old, the moon toad stands yet stronger still" drew raves: "chill" and "old" mirrored harsh reality, yet the jade hare pounded its elixir undaunted by autumn frost, and the moon toad grew firmer with age, radiating an indomitable grace.
Both young masters showed off their critical flair too, reveling until the small hours before exhaustion sent them home to bed, while other scholars kept drinking, gazing at the moon, trading verse, and debating until dawn.
Stepping out of the tavern, they heard Lin Zixiao hail them: "Hold on, you two young masters!"
Recognizing him, both bowed: "Greetings, Second Uncle Lin. What brings you to stop us?"
Lin Zixiao spoke straight: "Have either of you seen Youqi Jushi?"
Both shook their heads: "Not in person."
"Then where did you get her poems?"
Cao Ban smiled: "No need for Second Uncle to worry over that."
"I'm not prying—just want to ask you to pass a message. She lived under the Lin roof once, so we were family. Hearing how folk smear her, how the Qiao match fell through, and how the Prefect cast her out—life's hard for a young woman on her own. Reading her poems tonight showed me Youqi Jushi isn't someone hardship can break. I admire her."
Lin Zixiao's words moved both young masters.
An Mingxuan said, "Tell us whatever you'd like to pass along, Second Uncle Lin, and we'll deliver it."
"Tell Youqi Jushi this: no matter what outsiders say, neither I nor anyone in the Lin family will ever breathe a single bad word behind her back. Tell her too that rumors are like a gust of wind—here one moment, blown clean away the next time the wind shifts."
Cao Ban teased, "Can you speak for the whole Lin clan?"
"With my eldest brother away, I'm the senior elder of the Lin house—which junior dares say 'no' to me?"
Sharing a smile, both young masters promised, "We'll deliver every word. Farewell, Second Uncle Lin."
...
In the days that followed, Qianli Xun did not set foot outside Wangyue Pavilion. Between eating, sleeping, and daily routines, she poured herself into writing huaben scripts, jotting down a poem or ci of Zhu Shuzhen's now and then to pass the hours.
Prince Yi sent Bai Feng over twice, both times visiting Kuqiao while delivering updates on Wenyuan. The first time, Wenyuan was still locked indoors; the second, the Qiaos let him step out into his courtyard, though not beyond its gate.
Worry mingled with relief in Qianli Xun: stepping out into his yard meant progress. Soon he would step past the gate, out of Qiao Manor, and straight to Wangyue Pavilion. She decided to wait with patience for the day he showed up at her door.
Wenyuan did not arrive; instead, the three young masters paid a call.
After Dongfang Chenze's parents investigated and confirmed their son had kept spotless propriety with Youqi Jushi, they pressured him into accepting a betrothal, releasing him only once the match was sealed.
Guilt tugged at Qianli Xun: "Getting Young Master Dongfang dragged into my mess—I'm truly sorry."
Dongfang Chenze seized the opening: "Empty apologies don't count, Youqi Jushi—give me something real!"
"Name it, Young Master Dongfang—anything within my power, you've got it."
"From now on, every new poem or ci you write comes to me first."
"Deal! I'll send a maid over to your manor whenever I finish one."
"No need—I'll have Huayu come collect them on a schedule."
An Mingxuan laughed: "Scoring a bargain like that, Brother Dongfang—being locked up a few extra days would've been worth it!"
Cao Ban added, "Brother Dongfang ought to thank Youqi Jushi! Without this fuss, would you have gotten betrothed to the girl you love so fast?"
A rare, bright smile broke across Qianli Xun's face: "What? Young Master Dongfang got engaged to the girl he loves? Tell me everything!"
An Mingxuan filled her in: "It's Brother Qiao's eldest younger sister! Brother Dongfang and she have had feelings for each other ages without daring to tell their parents—who knew his parents would march straight to Qiao Manor to propose, and the Qiaos said yes on the spot! Isn't that grand?"
Qianli Xun felt genuine joy for Dongfang Chenze—parental blessing and mutual love, a rare prize! Yet mention of Qiao Wenxian sent a quiet pang through her chest.
Catching the shadow on her face, Cao Ban shifted gears fast: "On Mid-Autumn night, An Mingxuan and I got hold of your two new five-character poems and brought them to the Mid-Autumn Poetry Banquet—everyone raved! On our way out, Second Uncle Lin even asked us to pass you a message."
An Mingxuan picked it up: "Second Uncle Lin said no matter how folk gossip, neither he nor the Lin family will ever badmouth you behind your back—and that rumors are just a passing gust of wind, gone the second the next breeze blows."
"How kind of Second Uncle Lin. Next time I see him, I'll thank him in person."
Warmth swelled inside Qianli Xun at Lin Zixiao's kindness, grateful fate had placed so many good, loyal people around her: Lin Zixiao, these three young masters, and Miao Zhiyu too—she wondered if Miao Zhiyu had seen Wenyuan yet?
"Have any of you seen Lady Miao lately?"
Dongfang Chenze said, "I just got let out of lockup—no idea how she's doing."
Cao Ban replied, "I spotted her on the street once with a cluster of female friends; looked in good spirits."
An Mingxuan said, "Without you and Brother Qiao, our poetry salons fell apart. Lady Miao only mixed with us at salons anyway, so we haven't heard much."
"No matter—just asking in passing since we all met through salons."
Dongfang Chenze urged, "Don't stay cooped up inside Wangyue Pavilion all day, Youqi Jushi—go out for a breath of air! Even head down to watch the Qiantang River tidal bore; crowds have been flocking there lately."
"I'll go take a look when I get the chance." Qianli Xun agreed out of politeness, though she had zero mood for tide-watching.
Out of the blue, Cao Ban changed the subject: "Have you guys heard of a scholar named Qianli Xun lately?"
An Mingxuan lit up: "Of course! Writes huaben scripts—gripping plots! Huge crowds flock to hear them; I went myself and loved it! Right now every storyteller in Lin'an is performing his story, and listeners come back again and again dying for the next installment!"
That was the sweetest news Qianli Xun had heard in weeks.
Dongfang Chenze chimed in: "While I was locked indoors, my cousin—a storytelling fanatic—kept retelling Qianli Xun's tale, and it really is full of twists! Shame everyone knows his name while nobody's seen his face—I'd love to meet him one day."
Cao Ban laughed: "Prepare for disappointment, Brother Dongfang! Qianli Xun is mysterious as a ghost—sends every new script through a maid, so not even the storytellers have ever laid eyes on him!"
Dongfang Chenze's eyes sparkled: "That just makes me itch to dig him out even more!"
Qianli Xun jumped in fast: "If you ask me, Master Qianli Xun likely hates the spotlight. Better respect his wishes and just enjoy his stories! I've listened to his scripts too and loved them—I'll go listen again when I can."
All three young masters gaped at Youqi Jushi.
Dongfang Chenze blinked: "Youqi Jushi used to turn her nose up at lowbrow stuff—since when did you take a shine to it?"
"People change! I used to look down on lowbrow works, too. Now I figure whatever ordinary folk love is good work—highbrow 'Spring Snow' versus lowbrow street art, what's the point drawing such snobbish lines?"
Back before crossing over, Qianli Xun wrote web fiction—a world where elitist critics argued over whether web novels counted as literature. In her book, elitist opinions meant nothing; the verdict of time and everyday readers was what lasted.
Just like here in the Song dynasty: Zhu Shuzhen's verse lived among elite scholars, yet common folk never spared her a thought until rumors broke—and even then only knew one line, "falling asleep in his arms, fully dressed."
Qianli Xun was different: ordinary folk talked about her because her stories spoke straight to their hearts.
Her words left all three young masters nodding in genuine admiration.
After chatting a bit longer, they took their leave, returning quiet to Wangyue Pavilion.
Back at her desk writing huaben scripts, knowing readers loved her work fired up her pen—giving her something meaningful to fill the hours, push sorrow aside, and wait for brighter days ahead.

#TITLE: Princess Jiahe Calls on Wangyue Pavilion
Pulling up outside Wangyue Pavilion, Qiao Mengrong saw no sign of Prince Yi stepping down and asked in puzzlement, "Isn't Brother coming in with me to see Shuzhen?"
Shen Jianchen burned to go in, yet he had promised Qianli Xun never to enter Wangyue Pavilion again; how could he break his word?
"You girls have girl talk—me tagging along would be awkward. Besides, I'm an unmarried man; visiting Youqi Jushi wouldn't look right for her reputation. I'll wait right here in the carriage. Take all the time you want; my whole day is yours."
Such thoughtfulness—above all, keeping proper distance from another woman—melted Qiao Mengrong's heart, leaving her at complete ease about marrying him.
Asking Wangyue Pavilion's servants not to announce her so she could surprise Shuzhen, Qiao Mengrong followed a servant straight to the bedchamber courtyard.
Spotting Xiaguo in the yard about to bow and announce her, Qiao Mengrong pressed a finger to her lips with a quiet "Shhh!" Catching on, Xiaguo bowed in silence and lifted the door curtain for her.
With winter nearing, heavy cloth curtains hung over every doorway in Wangyue Pavilion, both neat and snug against drafts.
Inside, Qianli Xun sat writing her huaben script while Kuqiao tended ink beside her.
After that market stroll—hearing rumors had died and beating up an arrogant bully—her gloom had lifted enough to start writing sweet romance for her hero and heroine again.
Lost in her script, she heard Qiao Mengrong's bright voice break the hush.
"What are you busy with? You never came to visit me, so I had to come hunt you down!"
Qiao Mengrong's arrival injected instant warmth into Wangyue Pavilion.
Laying down her brush, Qianli Xun jumped up to greet her: "You didn't even send word so I could come welcome you at the gate!"
"Where's the fun in formal welcomes? Much better popping in as a surprise!"
While they greeted each other, Kuqiao gathered her mistress's unfinished manuscript, walked over to the bookshelf, opened a wooden box, and tucked it safe inside.

Frozen where he stood, Chen Haoyu's taunt—"Shuzhen belongs to me now, keep your eyes off her"—hammered through his skull, wiping out every earlier complaint Chen Haoyu had made about hating the match until the whole world drained gray...
Rising from her chair, Miao Zhiyu stepped beside Qiao Wenxian and faced Chen Haoyu with calm poise: "Young Master Chen is mistaken. Young Master Qiao's feelings don't hurt me at all—quite the opposite, I understand entirely. I know every chapter of Youqi Jushi and Young Master Qiao's bond and wished they could end up together; only because our families sealed this betrothal were our hands tied."
Her generous, gracious speech drew admiring glances from every corner of the room—even Qiao Wenxian looked at her with fresh respect, a quiet wave of guilt stirring in his chest.
Watching from the side, Xiaguo beamed inside: reasonable and gracious—Miao Zhiyu would make an easy primary wife to live under once Xiaguo became Young Master Qiao's concubine!
Knowing she had won every heart in the hall, Miao Zhiyu turned warm toward Qiao Wenxian: "Sit back down and finish lunch, Young Master Qiao. Youqi Jushi only stepped out to freshen up and will be right back—whatever you need to tell her, tell her once she returns."
Backed into grace by Miao Zhiyu, Qiao Wenxian sank back into his chair—a thousand words that had burned to burst out at Shuzhen now caught silent in his throat.
