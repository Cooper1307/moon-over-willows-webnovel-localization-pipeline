#CH 338
#TITLE: An Under-Manor Hidden Beneath the Estate
Shen Jianchen spoke without hesitation. “Wanyan Chi took someone of mine…”
“You mean the woman who helped you rescue the Song general?” Cai Jing asked.
“The very same.”
“Set your mind at ease, Your Highness. I heard the young prince—no, Wanyan Chi—fancies that girl. He will not kill her lightly.”
“Which is the exact reason I must rescue her all the sooner.”
Catching the depth of Prince Yi’s bond with her, Cai Jing asked how he meant to get her out.
“I meant to slip her out tonight, yet I searched the entire royal manor and found nobody. Running into you was pure luck, Lord Cai—I hoped to ask what you know.”
Cai Jing thought a moment. “The Liao built that manor, and rumor says they constructed another estate underground beneath it—what people call the Under-Manor. Back in the fifth year of Xuanhe, after the Jin seized Yanjing from the Liao and returned it to the Song, Grand Preceptor Tong lived there. He sent men hunting everywhere for that underground estate and never found it. Later, a geomancer warned him an underground chamber beneath the manor brought ill luck, so Tong Guan left the compound empty. When Jin troops rode in two days ago, Wanyan Chi picked that estate of his own accord.”
“So the Jin know about the Under-Manor?”
“Having defeated the Liao, Wanyan Chi likely knows.”
“Thank you, Lord Cai. I’ll go seize a few manor servants and wring the truth out of them.” Shen Jianchen moved to step out of the carriage.
Cai Jing caught his sleeve. “Hold a moment, Your Highness!”
“Why stop me?”
“Manor servants are unlikely to know—only Wanyan Chi would. Going back now brings danger for nothing.”
“I can’t sit here waiting for disaster either.”
“If Your Highness trusts me, stay at my residence while I make inquiries for you.”
Weighing the odds, Shen Jianchen agreed and thanked him.
Tears welled in Cai Jing’s eyes again. “As a Song subject who bowed to the Jin, I dread the scorn of future ages. Doing this small service for Your Highness lets my conscience catch its breath.”
Sensing his tears came from real remorse, Shen Jianchen asked why he had stayed at the yamen past midnight.
Wiping his eyes, Cai Jing explained Wanyan Chi had given him two days to write a thorough report on every detail of Song governance in Yanjing, due first thing tomorrow morning.
“Is Wanyan Zongwang still in Yanjing?”
“Already marching south to strike Bianliang.” Cai Jing’s eyes reddened anew. “I heard another Jin column has surrounded Taiyuan. I fear Great Song may perish at Jin hands.”
“Jin troops fight fierce, yet Song has plenty of brave sons too,” Shen Jianchen said in calm reassurance. “Bianliang will not fall so easy—and even if it falls, Song will not vanish overnight. A learned man like you knows history is just one dynasty giving way to the next. If another dynasty ever replaces the Song, it only marks history moving forward. Ants like us cannot steer the tide; better to take it as it comes. Mark my bold guess: even if Song falls, it will not be the Jin who take its place.”
Moved to deep respect, Cai Jing murmured that as long as the Jin could not wipe out Great Song, his heart could rest. Surrender had been forced upon him to stay alive, shaming him day and night.
Though barely acquainted with Cai Jing, Shen Jianchen judged him sincere after their talk—and knew one man’s fists alone stood poor odds against an army. He decided to trust him.
The next morning, Cai Jing presented his neat report booklet at the royal manor. Scanning a few pages, Wanyan Chi nodded in satisfaction. “Well done, Lord Cai. There is much here to digest at leisure.”
Seizing his chance, Cai Jing asked politely how the young prince liked his new manor.
“It lacks nothing. Quite comfortable.”
“Perhaps Your Highness does not yet know—a hidden Under-Manor lies beneath these grounds.” Cai Jing dropped the bait to test whether Wanyan Chi knew of it.
A flicker of wariness crossed Wanyan Chi’s face before he feigned ignorance. “An Under-Manor? Hidden where?”
Certain now that Wanyan Chi knew—and very likely kept the poetess locked down there—Cai Jing pressed further. “Rumor says the Liao stashed vast treasures below. Years ago, Grand Preceptor Tong searched high and low for it without luck and had to give up. Since you live here now, I could help you locate it and dig up the treasure…”
A sly smile curved Wanyan Chi’s lips. “How would you find it?”
“The architect who designed this manor was surnamed Jia, and his descendants are said to live still. Track them down, and nine times out of ten they’ll reveal the entrance.”
In a flash, Wanyan Chi snatched an arrow and thrust the point toward Cai Jing.
Jumping back by reflex, Cai Jing stammered, “What does Your Highness mean by this?”
Pointing the arrowhead at his chest, Wanyan Chi snapped, “Think I don’t know? Last night the Song prince broke into my manor searching for someone and left empty-handed. Then you hid him in your house and came fishing around about the Under-Manor today—all to help him find her!”
Cold sweat broke down Cai Jing’s back; he hit his knees at once begging mercy, pleading that he only helped out of pity.
“Pity?” Wanyan Chi tossed the arrow back into its quiver. “How is a prince pitiable?”
“You may not know, Your Highness: his mother died when he was tiny, and because he stayed sick for years, the Song Emperor paid him little mind. Somehow he recovered by sheer miracle, won the post of Military Commissioner of Lin’an for his skill at cuju, then offended the Emperor and lost his office in short order. Now he’s just a powerless idle prince—no different from any rich commoner’s son.”
“Motherless from childhood too…” Wanyan Chi murmured. “A poor wretch nobody loved, just like me.”
Seeing sympathy strike home, Cai Jing pressed his plea. “Seeing how nobody ever loved him until one girl finally cared for him—how could I turn him away? Compared with you, surrounded by beauties, one woman more or less means nothing to Great Jin. Why not hand her back?”
Leaning back into his seat without anger, Wanyan Chi smirked. “You spin a fine plea for him, yet I refuse. Out of kinship for growing up motherless just like me, I’ll cut him one break: let him bring ten million taels of silver within two months, and I’ll give her back. Tell him to quit hunting blind and go scrape up the silver! Hahaha!” Laughing aloud, he walked into his inner chamber.
Wiping sweat off his brow, Cai Jing sighed in relief that at least he had a straight answer—though where on earth anyone could scrape up ten million taels of silver was another matter. Best advise Prince Yi to let the girl go.
Inside his inner room, Wanyan Chi halted over one spot, stomped his right foot three times, and stomped his left foot three times. With a low click, the wooden bed lifted upward, revealing a square shaft downward.
“When we seized Yanjing from the Liao, the Liao prince handed me the secret to this Under-Manor himself,” Wanyan Chi muttered. “Think I’d ever share it with a Song rat?”
Descending the shaft, the bed lowered shut behind him without leaving a trace.
Though dim and lit throughout by oil lamps, the underground estate matched the rooms above in every detail.
A maid bowed at his approach. “Young Prince.”
“How is our beauty?”
“Hardly obedient. She keeps shouting to be let out.”
Waving the maid away, Wanyan Chi stepped into the chamber—and met Qianli Xun’s fist flying straight at his nose.
“Let me out right now, or I’ll beat you till you kneel and beg!”
Parrying two blows, he smirked. “Stay nicely with me, and I’ll take you straight upstairs.”
“In your dreams! I’d take anyone before you!”
After a few exchanges, clearly outmatched, Wanyan Chi stepped back. “Easy, beauty! Beat me to death and nobody opens the doors for you.”
Dropping her fists, she snapped, “Quit calling me ‘beauty’! I have a name!”
“I know—Zhu Shuzhen, a female ci poet. Poets are supposed to be gentle and sweet. How did you turn into such a tigress?”
“For scum like you, tigress mode is the only setting I use.”
“Meaning you *do* have a sweet, gentle side?”
Seeing no intent to kill her in his eyes, she eased her stance to reason with him. “Wanyan Chi, you’re decent-looking and hold high rank in Jin. Plenty of women throw themselves at you. Your maid told me you already have a primary consort and several secondary consorts. Why waste time on me?”
“Because I fell for you at first sight.”
“Tch! I feel zero attraction to you—and I would never fall for a Jin.”
“Not now doesn’t mean never. I have all the patience in the world.”
“I will never like you.”
“Do you like that Song Prince Yi?”
“Yes! I like him!”
Stung into rage, Wanyan Chi shattered a porcelain vase on a nearby stand with one punch. “If I can’t have you, neither will he!”
“Haha!” Qianli Xun laughed bright and defiant. “He lives in my heart forever, and I live in his. Nobody can tear us apart!”
Wanyan Chi lunged and clamped a hand round her throat. “I’ll scrub his name clean out of your heart!”
Her lips curved in a cold sneer. “Keep dreaming.”
Drawn by her proximity, Wanyan Chi dipped down to kiss her—and took a thunderous punch square across the face that knocked him staggered backward.
“A rose with thorns,” he rasped, releasing her throat. “I like it even better.”
“Watch those thorns prick you dead one day!”
Nursing a swelling cheek, Wanyan Chi shot back through his pain, “Since you love him so much, let me test his devotion for you. I sent word offering to trade you back for ten million taels of silver. Let’s see what weighs heavier in his heart—ten million taels or you.”
“Shameless!”
“Scared he’ll decide ten million taels is too steep and dump you?”
A flicker of doubt crossed her mind before vanishing. “Truth be told, I *hope* he gives me up over ten million taels! I have a husband, and he has a wife—his consort happens to be my best friend. He ought to drop me and build a good life with Mengrong.”
“Ah, right—you married into the Chen family of Lin’an. Spare me your tangled history. All I want is him wiped out of your heart. I can’t wait to see your face when two months pass without a single silver note—or when he walks away on his own.”
With that, Wanyan Chi turned and strode out.
Stealthy on light feet, Qianli Xun shadowed him to the exit, peeking just half a head round a pillar in time to see him pull a slender bamboo flute from his sash, play a short tune, and watch the shaft swing open.
She bolted out to rush the opening—only for a solid stone wall to slam down from above right in her face!
“I can’t beat your fists,” Wanyan Chi’s voice drifted from beyond the wall, “yet getting out isn’t so simple!”
Trapped as the ceiling sealed shut above, she sighed in exasperation. “Barely out of prison and straight into an underground vault. What rotten luck.”
The maid hurried over urging her back to her room.
“Sitting buried underground all day—doesn’t it suffocate you?” Qianli Xun grumbled.
“I worked upstairs before, Miss,” the maid answered. “His Highness sent me down only after you arrived.”
Walking back, Qianli Xun probed casually whether the Under-Manor had any other exit. The maid shook her head—she didn’t know. Deciding to search every wall the moment the maid looked away, Qianli Xun held her peace.
Back at Cai Manor, Cai Jing relayed the ransom demand and counseled against it. No woman alive was worth ten million taels of silver—even if Prince Yi could raise such a sum, anything else made better sense than buying one woman back.
Shen Jianchen read the trap clear as day: Wanyan Chi never intended to release her, money or not; yet failing to try gave Wanyan Chi ammunition to slander him before her.
Weighing it for a single heartbeat, Shen Jianchen answered without blinking. “Trouble yourself to carry word back to Wanyan Chi, Lord Cai: in two months, all ten million taels will arrive on time.”
Refusing to waste another hour, he bade Cai Jing farewell and walked out with Bai Feng.
Watching him leave, Cai Jing shook his head. “Too young, valuing a woman’s heart above heaven and earth. He’ll regret it soon enough.”
Outside the gate, Bai Feng gasped, “Where on earth do we get ten million taels, Your Highness?”
“Back to Bianliang. Sell Prince Yi’s Manor, sell every acre of farmland and every shop in my name—I refuse to believe it won’t add up to ten million taels.”
“Then Your Highness becomes a pauper!”
“Silver is just worldly dust.”
“Why not ask the Chen family in Lin’an for help? They’re rolling in silver!”
“This is my business. It has nothing to do with the Chen family.”
Bai Feng held his tongue. Princess Consort Yi lay dead, an Imperial Decree stood written, and Young Master Chen had annulled his marriage. By every right, Youqi Jushi belonged to his prince—never the Chen family.
#CH 339
#TITLE: Silver for a Corpse
All along the road back to Bianliang, streams of refugees fled southward. Calculating the days, Shen Jianchen reckoned Wanyan Zongwang’s armies sweeping through Hebei would cross the Yellow River within three months to hammer the capital itself. Every day counted if he meant to sell off his holdings before war closed in.
Riding day and night with Bai Feng, they reached Bianliang ahead of the Jin vanguard, though panic already gripped the Eastern Capital as wealthy households escaped south. Court bans barred noble officials from leaving while fresh regiments marched north toward the Yellow River.
Back at Prince Yi’s Manor, Shen Jianchen broke the news of Mengrong and Xilan’s deaths to Matron Zou. After weeping through her grief, Matron Zou backed him without reserve, urging him to follow his heart.
Putting the manor and every estate on the block proved brutal during wartime panic; an entire month dragged past without a single buyer. Busy scrambling against invasion, neither Emperor nor ministers cared what an idle prince sold off—one small mercy.
At last, as winter’s first snow drifted over Bianliang, a buyer knocked at the manor gates—Lady Wei! Learning Prince Yi was liquidating everything to ransom Youqi Jushi, she bought Prince Yi’s Manor on the spot without bargaining down a single copper. Soon other buyers snapped up his lands and shops too, every last one sent by Lady Wei, leaving Shen Jianchen overwhelmed with gratitude.
Once every note of the ten million taels lay packed, he ordered Bai Feng to escort Matron Zou safely back to Lin’an and wait at his bamboo house. Alone, carrying ten million taels in silver notes, Shen Jianchen rode straight north into worsening war toward Yanjing.
By twists and back roads, he reached Yanjing at last. Jin decrees had coaxed locals back onto the streets alongside Jin civilians, restoring surface commerce—yet winter’s biting wind and silent dread hung over everyone huddled in thick coats, much like Great Song itself braced against ruin.
Waste no breath on sorrow, Shen Jianchen sought out Cai Jing to notify Wanyan Chi that all ten million taels waited ready.
Dazzled, Cai Jing hurried to the royal manor to report. Hearing it, Wanyan Chi thought jealously of how precious she must be to the Song prince—would he himself trade ten million taels for one woman? Pride and envy burned away any thought of surrendering her, yet ten million taels delivered to his doorstep was too sweet to turn down.
“Tell him to come tomorrow,” Wanyan Chi smirked. “Silver in one hand, the person in the other.”
Unable to read his mind and convinced a release was set, Cai Jing flattered him on choosing ten million taels over one woman, and hurried back to tell Shen Jianchen.
The next day, after setting his trap, Wanyan Chi received Prince Yi in the main hall. At the sight of the massive wooden chest set down on the floor, his eyes shone. “You actually raised ten million taels?”
Throwing back the lid, Shen Jianchen gestured inside. “Every tael is here. Inspect them—and release her now.”
Stacks upon neat stacks of thick silver notes gleamed inside, making Wanyan Chi’s breath catch. “You’d hand all this over to me without blinking?”
“Not handing it over. Buying her freedom.”
“You love her even deeper than I thought.” Wanyan Chi raised a tea bowl and sipped unhurried. “What a pity—she died last night. All I asked was one night in my bed, and she drove a dagger straight into her heart and dropped dead on the spot. Broke my heart all morning.”
“Lies!”
“Beauties or silver, I prefer silver—especially ten million taels!” Wanyan Chi smashed the porcelain bowl against the flagstones with a loud crash.
On that cue, armed Jin soldiers poured out from behind screens, swords raised.
Realizing Wanyan Chi meant to rob him blind and keep her hidden, Shen Jianchen met the steel with bare fists. These manor guards were no match for him; within fifteen minutes, every attacker lay groaning across the floor.
Just as Wanyan Chi braced for his contingency, Shen Jianchen bolted across the hall, punched him out of his chair, and hauled him upright by his lapels. “Give her back to me!”
“I told you already. She’s dead.”
A brutal punch flattened Wanyan Chi’s cheek purple before Shen Jianchen dragged him closer. “Tell me she’s alive!”
Using the lie to distract him while waiting for his hidden striker, Wanyan Chi spat through swollen lips, “Silver beats beauty any day—if I had her alive, why wouldn’t I trade her for ten million taels right here? Look at the mass burial mound outside the city if you don’t believe me. I had her corpse tossed there last night!”
Despite how convincing it sounded, Shen Jianchen refused to swallow it—yet dread clutched his throat. Catching the eye of a lurking Jin soldier raising a sword behind the prince’s back, Wanyan Chi twisted the knife deeper.
“Locking her up all these months taught me one thing, though. Married into the Chen family of Lin’an, yet every day her heart pined for one man alone. Want to know who?”
Pain tightened Shen Jianchen’s chest. “Who?”
“You.”
Tears spilled down Shen Jianchen’s face at that confirmation. Clenching Wanyan Chi’s collar tighter, he swore, “If she died because of you, I’ll kill you right here to avenge her!”
Before his fist struck bone, steel pierced deep into Shen Jianchen’s back. Blood gushed across his robes, and he pitched forward unconscious.
Straightening his tunic, Wanyan Chi looked down in contempt. “So what if your fists beat mine? Still lost the girl and lost your silver! Guards—drag him out and dump him beyond the city gates!”
When Shen Jianchen drifted back to consciousness, he lay inside a peasant cottage with bound wounds, Cai Jing standing beside the bed in plain commoner clothes.
“Thank Heaven you woke,” Cai Jing breathed. “Your life is out of danger. Stay here and heal, then hurry back to Bianliang.”
“Lord Cai… find out for me… is she…”
Thinking the prince had ruined his fortune and nearly lost his life over one woman—and hoping to break his fixation so he’d flee south alive—Cai Jing lied smoothly. “I asked manor guards in secret. She really died, tossed onto the mass burial mound. My men searched till they found her body, and I took the liberty of burying her in waste ground outside the city.”
That lie snuffed out the last spark in Shen Jianchen’s chest. Tears burst forth in ragged wails mixed with self-blame, almost moving Cai Jing to blurt the truth—until Shen Jianchen dragged himself upright swearing to kill Wanyan Chi, collapsing right back onto the bed from agony.
“Wanyan Chi doubled his guards around the manor and laid traps everywhere waiting for you,” Cai Jing pleaded. “Going back is suicide! Living through that blade was Heaven’s grace. Even her spirit in heaven would weep to see you throw your life away. Visit her grave once you heal, then leave Yanjing at once.”
“Where did you bury her? Take me there right now.” Propping himself upright through sheer will, Shen Jianchen refused to budge. Seeing him past persuasion, Cai Jing bade two servants help him out to a carriage.
Out at the fresh mound Cai Jing had ordered piled up on empty ground, raw yellow earth offered no tombstone. Shen Jianchen’s legs gave out; two servants caught his arms before he hit the dirt. Too broken for sound, silent tears streamed down his face as he staggered forward and waved them back, collapsing face-down onto the bare soil in bitter sobs until no tears remained.
Exhausted and steadied, he sat up on the grass. “Have your men dig up her body and coffin it. I’m taking her home to Lin’an myself.”
Terrified his empty grave would be exposed, Cai Jing invented a rule on the spot: opening a fresh grave violated the dead—if he must move her, wait forty-nine days!
“Why?”
“After death, the intermediate spirit wanders forty-nine days before seeking rebirth,” Cai Jing improvised. “Dig up the body early, and you disturb her spirit’s crossing. Besides, flesh takes three or four months to turn to clean bone.”
Hearing “forty-nine days” and “rebirth,” Shen Jianchen panicked that Qianli Xun’s soul might pass back through the Divine Willow into their modern world! Sitting up sharp, he demanded a carriage straight to Lin’an right now—ignoring Cai Jing’s warning about bumpy roads tearing open his stitches.
Over nearly a month of grueling travel south, the driver helped change dressings along the way until his wounds closed, and at last Shen Jianchen stepped back into Lin’an.
Skipping friends and manor alike, he walked straight to the Divine Willow—stripped bare by deep winter, lifeless as his own heart. Treading dead grass to its thick trunk, eyes brimming, he murmured, “Two years since we came to see you. How have you been?”
Only wind answered.
“She’s dead—did you know? You brought us both here. Can’t you bring her back to me one more time?”
The old willow stood mute.
“Why won’t you answer?!” Wrapping both arms tight round the rough bark, he pleaded aloud. “Forty-nine days aren’t up yet! Give her back to me! Bring her back alive, and you can have my royal title, every copper left to my name—even my life!”
Silence.
“Is my death the only way to find her again? Your silence means yes!” Stepping back, Shen Jianchen drew back his fist and gathered every ounce of strength to smash his own chest. “Wait for me—I’ll see you soon!”
“Stop, benefactor! Never throw away your life—one who takes his own life cannot enter the cycle of rebirth!”
Frozen with his fist inches from his ribs, Shen Jianchen spun round to see Master Jingguang standing behind him.
“Don’t lie to me, Master!”
“Amitabha. Monks speak no falsehood.”
“If I live, how do I ever see her again?”
“And does dying guarantee you meet the one you seek?”
“At least I won’t endure living alone in agony!”
“Our Buddha teaches how rare a human birth is—our precious vessel for spiritual practice,” Master Jingguang answered evenly. “Destroy it by your own hand, and you forge heavy fresh karma atop old debts, trapping yourself across lifetimes without release.”
“I respect the Dharma, yet that brings me no comfort.”
“Unwittingly I overheard your words. You seek death solely to chase after one who departed—yet how selfish that wish is! Leaving aside the dark karma you heap upon yourself, its backlash falls on her too, leaving the dead no rest!”
Lowering his fist, Shen Jianchen stared through wet eyes. “Why would my death keep her from resting?”
“Without her death, there would be no suicide of yours. She is cause, and you are effect; karma binds you both. Desecrate your living vessel, and the foul fruit rebounds onto her soul. You call it devotion, yet you simply flee your own pain—caring only for quick release while bringing torment upon her in the world beyond.”
Like morning bells shattering sleep, Master Jingguang’s words struck Shen Jianchen awake. Remembering how clinging to her had sparked his wedding crash and landed her in prison for two years, he saw the truth clear at last.
Bowing deep, Shen Jianchen swore he would live on—not for himself, yet for her.
Pleased by his sudden clarity, Master Jingguang offered one parting counsel: since he could not put her out of mind, do good deeds and build merit in her name, bringing peace to the living and blessings to the dead in the world beyond.
Warm tears filled Shen Jianchen’s eyes once more; at last he had a path through the years ahead without her—living in her name until the world kept speaking of her as if she stood right beside him.
“Thank you, Master.”
“Amitabha.” With a gentle smile, Master Jingguang turned away down the hill.
#CH 340
#TITLE: News of Death Reaches Lin’an
Stepping away from Master Jingguang’s counsel with suicide put behind him, Shen Jianchen walked heavy-hearted to the gates of Chen Manor.
Hearing Prince Yi waited outside, Chen Haoyu rushed out full of dread and rage. Barely glancing at the prince’s gaunt, haunted face, Haoyu swung a fist straight at him. “Bai Feng told me my wife was captured by Jin troops in Yanjing! It’s all your fault—who told you to take her there?!”
Shen Jianchen stood still and took blow after blow without raising a hand. Haoyu grabbed his lapels and shook him hard. “Bai Feng said you went to save her! Where is she? Why did you come back alone?!”
“She… is dead.”
Haoyu shoved him backward. “Liar!”
“She lies buried outside Yanjing’s walls. I saw her grave with my own eyes.”
“You’re lying just to keep her from me!”
“I didn’t lie. Nobody wanted her alive more than I.”
Seeing Prince Yi’s swollen red eyes, sunken cheeks, and face aged years overnight, Haoyu felt his raised fist go limp. Slowly crouching onto the flagstones, he buried his face and wept aloud.
After a long moment, Haoyu pushed himself up and turned back toward the gate. “I’ll pack right now and ride to Yanjing myself to bring my wife’s bones home to the Chen family graveyard.”
“She belongs to the Chen family no more. You have no right to fetch her bones.”
Whirling back, Haoyu seized Shen Jianchen’s collar again. “I only dissolved our marriage under an Imperial Decree! In my heart, she is my wife forever!”
“No matter what’s in your heart, she became mine.”
“What do you mean? Did you tell her about the decree?!”
Wanting Qianli Xun to belong solely to him even in death, Shen Jianchen lied straight to his face. “Yes. I told her. Relief washed over her—she confessed she had only suffered staying with you out of marital duty, and once free, she could finally be with the man she truly loved. She followed me to Yanjing of her own accord, never intending to come back to Lin’an for you.”
“You’re lying! Jin armies are marching south on Bianliang right now—who knows what becomes of the man on that throne? Once that decree turns into scrap paper, she’s my wife all over again!”
“No matter what becomes of His Majesty, it’s too late. In Yanjing… she and I had already consummated our union.”
Before the final word fell, Haoyu cracked a fist across Shen Jianchen’s jaw, painting fresh purple over his weary face. “Liar! Liar!” Haoyu’s voice broke near hysteria.
“I’ve said my piece,” Shen Jianchen whispered as tears spilled down his own cheeks. “You’re luckier than me—at least you had her beside you while she lived. My time alone with her was far too brief. Still not satisfied? Still fighting me for her even after death? Don’t you feel even a shred of pity for me?”
Stung silent, Haoyu dropped his fists and trudged back through Chen Manor’s gates like frostbitten stalks. Stopping just across the threshold with his back turned, he muttered, “I won’t fight you anymore. What’s the use fighting when she can’t come back alive?”
Only after Haoyu’s slumped back vanished inside did Shen Jianchen turn away—suddenly adrift with nowhere to go. His Bianliang manor was sold, Secluded Forest Villa belonged to another, and soon Bianliang might fall to Jin hands anyway. Only the bamboo house outside town felt like home—every bamboo beam built for him and Qianli Xun.
Walking out past the city gates with nowhere to hurry, letting empty miles eat away the hollow ache inside, he spotted a carriage stopping ahead—and Qiao Wenxian stepped down.
“I heard you returned and was just coming to find you.”
“How did you know?”
“Servants from Secluded Forest Villa spotted you heading toward Chen Manor. I drove straight there and caught you on the road.”
Before Wenyuan, Shen Jianchen dropped all pretense and collapsed onto his friend’s shoulder sobbing aloud. “Wenyuan… she’s gone! I’ll never see her again!”
Clapping his shoulder gently, Wenxian blamed himself for not eloping with her back then, then guided the prince back to Secluded Forest Villa.
Since Prince Yi had returned to Bianliang two years ago, Wenxian had moved into the villa with his wife and concubine. Miao Zhiyu had borne him a son and Xiaguo a daughter—both now lively toddlers past two racing across the yards. Elder Qiaos couldn’t have been happier, thanking Heaven they hadn’t chosen Zhu Shuzhen and brought this heartbreak upon Qiao Manor—though Wenxian himself had prayed for her safety every day.
After a hot bath, fresh clothes, and supper, Shen Jianchen looked less haggard, though his eyes remained hollow. Knowing better than to parade his own happy toddlers before a grieving man, Wenxian kept his family tucked away for a few days and sat across a table beside a glowing brazier pouring warm fermented rice wine.
Winter’s road-chill melted out of Shen Jianchen’s bones, leaving only the frost inside his chest.
“When Bai Feng brought Matron Zou back, he told me you sold your Bianliang manor and everything in your name for ten million taels,” Wenxian sighed, filling both cups. “I couldn’t hold a candle to you.”
“Without her, what good was silver?” Shen Jianchen drained his cup in one swallow. “And now she’s gone, silver means even less.”
“Over two years apart, and everything has shifted. Our court looks spent—I fear the whole realm may fall to the Jin.”
“The Jin aren’t fit to rule the whole realm,” Shen Jianchen said. “At best they’ll split the map with Song for a time, then meet ruin themselves. Shame neither of us will live to see that day.”
“How can you be so sure?”
A faint, rare smile touched Shen Jianchen’s lips. “Just a guess.”
“Right or wrong, you spoke straight to my heart!” Raising his cup, Wenxian toasted that distant day, and Shen Jianchen drank with him.
Setting his cup down, Wenxian asked what he planned next.
“Go back to the bamboo house built for her and me, and live out the rest of my days there,” Shen Jianchen answered softly, remembering Master Jingguang’s counsel. “I’ll do charity and build merit in her name, so whenever folks speak of her, it’ll feel like she’s still right beside me.”
Moved, Wenxian asked, “Count me in?”
“You already stole her first love. Trying to steal charity from me too?”
“Young Master Xu took her first love, not me.”
“As far as I’m concerned, it was you.”

Keeping her arms at her sides, she reminded him gently through tears, “I’m not… not your wife anymore.”
Releasing her slowly, Haoyu burst out bitterly against Prince Yi’s scheming—claiming the prince had faked grief to trick him into thinking her dead. Patiently she explained Cai Jing had fooled Shen Jianchen too until her new poems reached Lin’an, driving him straight back to Yanjing to swap himself for her life.
A bitter laugh broke through Haoyu’s tears. “Always that shameless… shameless enough to steal every chance to treat you right! Kneeling outside Zichen Hall till my marriage broke; secretly selling everything for ten million taels; riding alone to trade himself for your return… So shameless I can’t even hate him! Can’t bear losing you, yet forced to lose you—tell me what I’m supposed to do!”
“I heard… Xiangrong carries your child…”
“He told you that too?” Haoyu wept helplessly, cursing himself for listening to Matron Zou and returning to Lin’an. Refusing to surrender, he pleaded that with Zhao Ji off the throne, old decrees meant nothing—they could restore their marriage tomorrow!
“Decrees aren’t undone so easily,” she murmured softly. “And even if they were… I can never go back. I’m going to live in the bamboo house he built and wait for him to come home.”
Crushed, Haoyu locked his red, pleading eyes onto hers, recalling their prison embrace. “Tell me the truth—did you ever feel love for me?”
Seeing how deeply love for her had scarred this once reckless rake, Qianli Xun remembered those hopeless prison days when his unwavering loyalty warmed her heart and she thought him her final harbor—unwilling to deal him one final wound.
“In that moment… I think I must have loved you.”
A soft, satisfied smile broke through Haoyu’s tears. “I knew you loved me once…” Carrying that single memory, content yet defeated, he turned and walked slowly out the door.
Watching him go, Qianli Xun whispered into the quiet room, “Chen Haoyu, thank you for stepping into my life, and thank you for loving me. Here, our fate together comes to an end.”

中阴身 | intermediate spirit | 保留佛教轮回“中阴”概念，既不西化为 ghost，又能让英语读者秒懂蔡靖借口。
正思正念 | Right Thought and Right Mindfulness | 沿用术语表既定八正道核心译法，与第51章及前文主旨形成呼应。
